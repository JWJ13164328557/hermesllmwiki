## 摘要
We studied cone photoreceptors in adult mouse and baboon retinas for their structure and diversity with immunomarkers and confocal microscopy. In mouse retinas, L/M-opsin and S-opsin antibodies labeled regular cones primarily in the outer segment (OS). In dark-adapted conditions, S-opsin-positive OSs (152,941/retina) showed a Gaussian distribution with a density range of 40,245-150/mm2 ventrodorsally and 19,072-184/mm2 temporonasally. ~ 10% somas of L/M-opsin-positive cones were attached as pairs. 7.6% of L/M-opsin-expressing cones and 5.7% of S-opsin-positive cones showed higher opsin levels in soma and axon, and we named them lm-cones or ss-cones, respectively, which possessed single or dual OS(s), indicating the presence of double-cones and more color-sensitive subtypes. Unlike regular cones, lm-cones had significantly smaller OSs and shorter inner segments (ISs), and ss-cone somas resided in rod soma rows with a much longer IS-soma distance. Retrogradely labeling of retinal ganglion cells (RGCs) revealed two photoreceptor-RGC hybrids with OSs. Peanut agglutin (PNA) identified two OS calibers: 1) 1-2 μm with two intertwined continuous PNA-positive strings and 2) 2-3μm with 3-4 PNA-positive strings, which preferred the central or peripheral retina and matched one or two OS compartments, respectively. PNA-positive strings were thinner in ISs. In monkey cones, the OS disk region was positive for PNA (89.5%) or calcium-binding protein 5 (10.5%). OS surface was covered by vertical PNA-positive grids, which were thinner in ISs, absent in fovea cone OSs, and multi-barrel-like in other cones. PNA-positive sheath contained up to ~10-12 parallel grids of similar thickness, distance, and immunoreactivity, likely serving as the wall of cones.
