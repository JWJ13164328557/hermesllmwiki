# Role of LGALS9 and human milk oligosaccharides in modulating immune responses in food allergy: Insights from network pharmacology and molecular simulations.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Biochemical and biophysical research communications (2026)
- **PMID**: [42987599](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42987599/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154764](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bbrc.2026.154764)
- **第一作者**: Siyi Chen
- **作者**: Siyi Chen, Kun Ai, Yan Liu, Ziyi Xiong, Wei Zhong, Anshu Yang, Zhihua Wu, Hongbing Chen

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Food allergy is a growing global health concern, and effective strategies for restoring immune tolerance remain limited. Human milk oligosaccharides (HMOs) have potential immunomodulatory effects, but their molecular targets and mechanisms in food allergy are not fully understood.

OBJECTIVE: This study aimed to identify potential protective genes associated with food allergy and to investigate the possible role of LGALS9 in HMO-related immune regulation.

METHODS: Network pharmacology was used to identify overlapping targets between food allergy-associated genes and four selected HMOs: 2'-fucosyllactose, 3'-sialyllactose, lacto-N-neotetraose, and lacto-N-tetraose. Mendelian randomization analysis was performed to assess the association between candidate gene expression and food allergy. Single-cell transcriptomic analysis was used to examine LGALS9 expression across immune cell populations and treatment-response groups. Molecular docking and molecular dynamics simulations were conducted to evaluate predicted interactions between LGALS9 and the four HMOs. In vitro BMDC-naïve CD4+ T-cell co-culture assays were further used to assess T-cell differentiation and cytokine secretion.

RESULTS: Network pharmacology identified 16 overlapping targets between the four HMOs and food allergy-associated genes. Mendelian randomization analysis showed a significant inverse association between LGALS9 expression and food allergy, supporting a potential protective role. Single-cell analysis of a peanut allergy oral immunotherapy cohort showed that LGALS9 expression was significantly higher in healthy controls than in the patient groups, particularly in Treg cells. Molecular docking suggested that all four HMOs could interact with the selected LGALS9 binding site, with lacto-N-tetraose showing the most favorable docking-derived binding score. Molecular dynamics simulations indicated that the predicted complexes remained structurally stable over the simulated period without major conformational changes in LGALS9. In vitro assays showed that HMO-treated BMDCs promoted CD25+Foxp3+ Treg differentiation and increased IL-10 secretion.

CONCLUSION: The integrated computational, transcriptomic, genetic, and in vitro findings identify LGALS9 as a potential HMO-associated immunoregulatory target in food allergy. However, direct functional studies are still required to determine whether the immunomodulatory effects of HMOs are dependent on LGALS9.
