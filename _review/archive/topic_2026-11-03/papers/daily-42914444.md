# Visible-light photoactivation enhances the antimicrobial efficacy of biomass-derived carbon quantum dots: Temperature-dependent inactivation kinetics and application in enoki mushrooms.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Food research international (Ottawa, Ont.) (2026)
- **PMID**: [42914444](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42914444/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.foodres.2026.120758](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2026.120758)
- **第一作者**: Seul Bin Baik
- **作者**: Seul Bin Baik, Ajahar Khan, Jong Whan Rhim, Ki Sun Yoon

## 摘要
Valorization of biomass into functional antimicrobial nanomaterials offers a sustainable approach to food preservation. This study developed carbon quantum dots (CQDs), including rice wine sediment-derived CQDs (RWCD) and onion peel-derived CQDs (OPCD), and evaluated the effects of visible-light photoactivation on their antimicrobial efficacy, temperature-dependent inactivation kinetics, mechanisms, and performance on enoki mushrooms. Physicochemical and optical characterization confirmed stable, surface-functionalized CQDs. Photoactivation enhanced activity against Listeria monocytogenes and Salmonella Typhimurium, with photoactivated RWCD (RWCD-P) showing the greatest efficacy. Against L. monocytogenes, photoactivation reduced RWCD minimum inhibitory concentration (MIC) and minimum bactericidal concentration (MBC) values from 600 and 1600 to 200 and 400 μg/mL, respectively, corresponding to 3- and 4-fold reductions. Modified Gompertz and Weibull models effectively described pathogen growth and inactivation at 10-60 °C. Arrhenius analysis revealed higher activation energies for S. typhimurium (112.3-118.5 kJ/mol) than for L. monocytogenes (70.3-71.3 kJ/mol), indicating greater temperature dependence of S. typhimurium inactivation. RWCD-P generated the highest hydroxyl radical levels, while TEM revealed extensive cell-envelope disruption and intracellular structural damage, supporting ROS-mediated antimicrobial action. Aerosolized RWCD-P (6500 μg/mL) markedly prolonged the lag time and reduced the maximum population density of L. monocytogenes, while progressively inactivating S. typhimurium on enoki mushrooms at 10 °C. Contrasting responses in broth and mushrooms highlighted the influence of the food matrix on antimicrobial efficacy. These findings provide mechanistic, kinetic, and food-matrix evidence supporting photoactivated biomass-derived CQDs as a complementary antimicrobial hurdle, although food-quality effects, residual CQDs safety, and efficacy under realistic cold-chain conditions require further evaluation before their practical application in commercial food preservation systems.
