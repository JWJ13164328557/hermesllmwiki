# Alleviating chromium (Cr) toxicity in maize (Zea mays L.) using oak wood biochar and carbon nanoparticles: impacts on growth, physiology, and stress responses.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: International journal of phytoremediation (2026)
- **PMID**: [42968655](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42968655/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/15226514.2026.2751447](https://doi.org/10.1080/15226514.2026.2751447)
- **第一作者**: Amna Zulfqar
- **作者**: Amna Zulfqar, Xiaoping Xin, Zulfiqar Ahmad Saqib, Muhammad Hassan Bashir, Nabeel Khan Niazi, Zhenli He

## 摘要
The rising demand for food production necessitates innovative and sustainable agricultural practices, particularly in soils contaminated with heavy metals such as chromium (Cr). Oak wood biochar (OW-BC) and carbon nanoparticles (CNPs) have emerged as promising amendments for enhancing soil quality and plant health while mitigating Cr stress. This study explored the effect of OW-BC and CNPs on maize (Zea mays L.) growth, nutrient concentration, and soil enzymes under Cr stress (50 mg kg-1 Cr as total Cr) in a pot experiment. Results showed that OW-BC and CNP treatments increased shoot fresh weight by 30.2% and 28.0% without Cr stress, and by 34.1% and 28.4% under Cr stress, respectively, relative to the Cr treatment. Under Cr stress, OW-BC and CNPs improved shoot P concentration by 23.6% and 71.1%, respectively, relative to the Cr treatment, though shoot K concentration was not restored to control levels. Under Cr stress, both OW-BC and CNPs exhibited statistically non-significant, but numerically lower shoot Cr concentrations (by 8.1% and 10.0% compared with the Cr-alone treatment, respectively), as well as similar numerical increases in biomass (34.1% vs. 28.4%). The results in this study indicate that OW-BC and CNPs act as good soil conditioners and plant protectants to enhance plant tolerance to Cr toxicity and support sustainable crop production.

This study demonstrated that OW-BC and CNPs differentially modulated Cr accumulation and phytotoxicity in maize, reducing shoot Cr concentration by up to 8.1% and increasing shoot P concentration by up to 71.1% under Cr stress. These findings provide a mechanistic basis for recommending OW-BC and CNPs as affordable amendments for Cr-contaminated soils, with field-scale trials needed to confirm long-term efficacy.
