# Rejuvenation Potential of Developmental Genes Downregulated in Aging.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: International Journal of Stem Cells (2026)
- **DOI**: [10.15283/ijsc25144](https://doi.org/10.15283/ijsc25144)
- **作者**: Byungkuk Min, Juhyeon Nam, Minseo Choi, Junyoung Hong, Areum Baek
- **来源**: openalex
- **概念**: Rejuvenation, Biology, Gene, Genetics, Senescence

## 摘要
Partial reprogramming with Yamanaka factors effectively reverses cellular aging but poses potential oncogenic risks, limiting clinical translation. To identify safer rejuvenation induction factors (RIFs), we integrated five aging datasets and compared them with single-cell transcriptomics data from 811 mouse embryonic cells in preimplatation stages. This analysis identified genes exhibiting an inverse relationship between aging (downregulated) and development (upregulated). We further refined this selection by isolating genes common in the intermediate stage of Yamanaka factor-mediated reprogramming. Based on the molecular characteristics of reprogramming factors, we successfully isolated four RIFs associated with ribosome biogenesis, mitochondrial import, translational regulation, and serine metabolism. The expression of these RIFs in aged fibroblasts effectively reduced senescence markers and increased gene expression patterns inversely associated with aging-associated transcriptional change. Unlike the established Yamanaka factors, these identified RIFs modulate development-associated pathways that decline with aging, without activating pluripotency-associated programs, suggesting a potentially safer rejuvenation strategy.

### 核心发现
1. Our findings suggest that rejuvenation driven by aging-down/development-up genes might be more effec- tive to develop a safer path toward anti-aging interventions.
2. These results suggest that genes whose expression levels altered during the ag- ing and development could contribute in maintaining young and matured status of cells.
3. Collectively, these data indicate that embryonic devel- opmental transcriptional states are characterized by the activation of translational programs enriched in ADDU genes, together with suppression of pathways linked to ag- ing-associated transcriptional dysregulation and oncogenic susceptibility.
4. Collectively, these factors delineate a rejuvenation potential that enhances cellular function while maintaining cell identity.
5. Collectively, the ADDU strategy offers an effective approach for isolating safe rejuvenation-in- duction factors.
6. In conclusion, we suggest that the genes in the ADDU category could be considered as appropriate fac- tors for reverse-aging interventions that can replace the Yamanaka factors and circumvent safety concerns.
7. Interestingly, the signaling pathways and transcriptional programs that orchestrate em- bryonic growth often exhibit antagonistic pleiotropy, func- tioning as drivers of development while potentially con- tributing to dysfunction in later years (10).
8. Notably, epigenomic and phenotypic analyses re- vealed substantial overlap between aging-related genes and those essential for stemness and embryonic development.
