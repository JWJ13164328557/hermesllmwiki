### 核心发现
1. The location of the virus on the causal pathway determines what prevention can remove, what a diagnostic assay must demonstrate, and whether a viral target is likely to remain relevant in established disease.
2. This evolutionary perspective explains why viral oncogenes often target highly connected regulatory nodes and why the same host pathways are altered by somatic mutation in virus-negative tumours (Krump & You, 2018; Moore & Chang, 2010; White et al., 2014).
3. Thus, the relevant therapeutic target may be a viral protein, an infected-cell population, a paracrine circuit, or the immune deficit that permits the circuit to persist (Cesarman et al., 2019; Dittmer & Damania, 2016; Yarchoan & Uldrick, 2018).
4. The comparison shows that the most actionable viral targets are those both tumour restricted and persistently required, whereas inflammation-dominant cancers demand continued surveillance even after successful antiviral treatment.
5. Persistent high-risk infection is necessary for most cervical cancers, but infection alone is insufficient.
