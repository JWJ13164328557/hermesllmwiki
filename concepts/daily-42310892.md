Interploidy hybridization in plants is constrained by a postzygotic barrier. Triploid production via 2x× 4xinterploidy hybridization is an important strategy for obtaining seedless cultivar in some fruit tree crops including citrus. However, as the molecular mechanisms of postzygotic barrier in citrus remain unclear, triploid breeding efficiency is limited. Here, we compared seed development in citrus 2x× 4xand 2x× 2xcrosses. Embryo and endosperm developed normally in 2x× 2xcrosses, while endosperm cellularization completely failed in 2x× 4xcrosses, resulting in embryo arrest at the globular stage, demonstrating that impaired endosperm cellularization causes triploid embryo abortion. Transcriptomic analysis of laser microdissection (LMD)‐captured endosperm at pre‐ and post‐cellularization stages revealed extensive transcriptional misregulation in 2x× 4xcrosses, including persistent upregulation of type I MADS‐box genes (CrAGLs) and the potential paternally expressed geneCrbZIP. Consistently, functional analyses showed that overexpression ofCrAGL62,CrAGL80a, orCrbZIPdelayed endosperm cellularization in precocious Mini‐citrus (Fortunella hindsii), whilecragl62mutants exhibited severely impaired cellularization. Our findings showed CrAGL62 and CrbZIP as key regulators of endosperm cellularization in citrus, providing potential targets for modulating endosperm development to improve citrus triploid breeding efficiency. Keywords:Citrus, endosperm development, interploidy cross, postzygotic barrier, seedlessness A proposed working model for endosperm cellularization failure in citrus 2x× 4xcrosses. Seed development in flowering plants (angiosperms) starts with double fertilization: one sperm fuses with the egg to form the embryo, and the other with the central cell to form the endosperm (Huang & Sun,2025; Zhonget al.,2025). In most angiosperms, the endosperm is a triploid tissue with a 2 : 1 maternal‐to‐paternal genomic ratio, supports embryo development and is sensitive to parental genome dosage (Köhleret al.,2021; Doll & Ingram,2022). In most angiosperms, it begins as a coenocyte, where nuclei divide without cytokinesis (Olsen,1998), followed by cellularization via cell wall formation, eventually filling the embryo sac (Olsen,2004). This coenocyte‐to‐cellular transition occurs rapidly yet is essential for seed viability, and the timing of this transition impacts the final seed size. In rice interploidy crosses, maternal excess (4x× 2x) reduces endosperm proliferation and causes early cellularization, yielding smaller seeds, whereas paternal excess (2x× 4x) increases proliferation and delays cellularization, often leading to embryo abortion despite larger seed coats (Sekineet al.,2013).
