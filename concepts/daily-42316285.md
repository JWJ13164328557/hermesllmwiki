Carbon dots (CDs), as a class of carbon‐based nanomaterials have been confirmed to have great potential in promoting crop growth, but its molecular mechanism remains largely unknown. In this study, novel soluble humic acid‐ derived CDs (HA‐CDs) was developed and it could promote soybean growth and symbiotic nitrogen fixation (SNF) within a certain concentration range, which significantly lowered than the precursor. Further biochemical and molecular‐level analyses revealed that this growth‐ and SNF‐promoting effects of HA‐CDs was mainly due to improving the photosynthesis, regulating C/N metabolism and antioxidant enzyme activity of host, and enhancing rhizobial growth and its symbiotic ability with soybeans that depended on the genesrshandrpoZ. This study provided significant advance in our understanding of the molecular mechanism of HA‐CDs on legume growth and nitrogen fixation, as well as the potential application value of HA‐CDs in agriculture system. Keywords:carbon dots, photosynthesis, rhizobia, symbiotic nitrogen fixation HA‐CDs originating from water‐soluble humic acid enhance symbiotic nitrogen fixation in the soybean–rhizobium system through a dual mechanism: (i) ameliorating host soybean physiological metabolism and (ii) modulating rhizobial growth and colonisation viarpoZandrshgenes—ultimately promoting soybean growth and yield. Agricultural production underpins human society, however, rapid population growth, limited arable land, diminished soil fertility, and the increasing climate variability are expected to pose unprecedented challenges to global food security in the coming decades (Dönmez et al.2024; Van Esse et al.2020). Chemical fertilisers remain indispensable inputs in modern agriculture, yet prevailing environmentally intensive fertilisation practices is increasingly incompatible with long‐term agricultural sustainability. Consequently, there is an urgent need to develop innovative strategies that simultaneously enhance crop productivity and promote sustainable agricultural systems. Among emerging strategies to improve agricultural productivity, nanomaterials have attracted increasing attention due to their nanoscale dimensions, unique photoluminescent properties, high specific surface area and tunable surface chemistry (An et al.2022; Zhang et al.2024a; Tavakoli et al.2026). A wide range of metal‐based, polymer‐based, and carbon‐based nanomaterials have been synthesised for applications in crop production, demonstrating substantial potential for enhancing plant growth, development, and postharvest quality (Zhang et al.2024a; Guo et al.2022; Li et al.2023a,2023b; Khan et al.2025; Irshad et al.2025).
