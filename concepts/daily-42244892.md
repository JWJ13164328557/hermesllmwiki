This study investigated the effects of superfine grinding on the physicochemical and functional properties of PWB and its incorporation into bread at 10%, 20%, and 30%. Adequate dietary fiber intake is strongly associated with reduced risk of non-communicable diseases such as type 2 diabetes (T2D), colorectal cancer, and cardiovascular disorders (Reynolds et al., 2019). Meta-analyses indicate that consuming more than 25 g of fiber per day can lower T2D risk by 15–30%, with additional benefits observed beyond 30 g day−1(Reynolds et al., 2019). Yet, global intake still falls well below these levels, as most individuals consume less than 20 g day−1(Stephen et al., 2017). Cereal products provide significant daily fiber in many populations (van Rossum et al., 2011), enhancing the fiber content in staple foods such as bread therefore represents an effective strategy to address this deficit. To bridge this nutritional gap, purple wheat bran (PWB) presents an opportunity to simultaneously tackle widespread dietary fiber deficiencies and boost antioxidant intake.
