Green tea has a long history in China, which is one of the world's major green tea-producing countries and the largest producer of organic green tea (Zhen et al., 2023). China's annual organic green tea production is estimated at 220,000–250,000 tons, accounting for approximately 7.40% of the country's total tea output (Li et al., 2024). Owing to its favorable agroecological conditions and supportive government policies, Yunnan Province has become the largest organic green tea-producing region in China (Wang, Yue, et al., 2024). However, comparative information on the quality and flavor characteristics of organic and conventional teas from high-altitude regions remains limited. Most studies indicate that organic green tea contains higher levels of tea polyphenols and lower levels of free amino acids (Kazimierczak et al., 2015;Piyasena & Hettiarachchi, 2023). These differences are particularly evident in the composition of key catechins and phenolic compounds. Specifically, the contents of epigallocatechin (EGC), epicatechin (EC), epigallocatechin gallate (EGCG), flavonols and tannins are significantly higher in organic green tea than in conventionally grown green tea (Bagchi et al., 2015;Kim et al., 2015). However, comparative studies focusing on plateau regions remain limited. In this study, tea samples from the same cultivar cultivated under ORG and CON systems in the Yungui Plateau were comparatively analyzed in terms of physicochemical properties, heavy metal contents, and volatile profiles. Compared with CON tea, ORG green tea exhibited 71.9% and 171% higher fruity and woody aroma intensities, respectively, whereas the intensity of the grassy note decreased by 93.8%.
