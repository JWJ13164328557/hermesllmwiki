# The precursor of ethylene, 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid, induces resistance against the root-knot nematode Meloidogyne graminicola in rice

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [42413879](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42413879/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113315](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113315)
- **作者**: Karen De Kock, Da Cao, Xing Xu, Kristof Demeestere, Dominique Van Der Straeten, Sven Mangelinckx, Tina Kyndt, Karen De Kock, Da Cao, Xing Xu

## 摘要
The plant hormone ethylene (ET) plays a central role in numerous processes throughout the plant life cycle, including growth, fruit ripening, senescence, abscission, and response to both biotic and abiotic stress. In rice, ET has been identified as a positive regulator of defense against the root-knot nematode (RKN) Meloidogyne graminicola through synergistic interaction with jasmonic acid. Recent findings suggest that 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC), the direct precursor of ET, may exert an ET-independent function in plant development, yet its role in plant defense remains poorly understood. In this study, we investigated the contribution of ACC in rice defense and development by pharmacological inhibition of ACC synthase (ACS) and ACC oxidase (ACO), the two key enzymes in ET biosynthesis. Transcriptome analysis, in combination with phenotypic experiments and phytohormone analysis revealed that ACC accumulation, under ACO inhibition, induces resistance to the RKN M. graminicola. Contrarily, ACC depletion, by inhibition of ACS, hampered early rice seedling growth. Furthermore, mRNA-seq analysis of plants with elevated ACC levels but impaired ACC-to-ET conversion indicated that ACC accumulation activates defense mechanisms associated with the ET response. Moreover, genes involved in pathogenesis-related protein production, peroxidase activity, and abscisic acid biosynthesis and signaling were significantly upregulated in ACC-accumulating plants. Together, our findings uncover a novel role for ACC in defense against RKNs and plant growth in rice and highlight its potential as a defense-activating agent.
