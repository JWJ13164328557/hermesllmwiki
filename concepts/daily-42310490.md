Low bioavailability of inorganic phosphate (Pi) in soil severely constrains plant growth and crop productivity. Here, we demonstrate that Pideficiency induced localised lignification in rice roots. Overexpression ofOsDIR55, a beneficial gene we previously implicated in salt tolerance, significantly enhances plant performance under low Piconditions, characterised by increased root‐to‐shoot ratio and biomass accumulation, whereas loss‐of‐functiondir55mutants exhibit contrast phenotypes. Importantly, the transcription factor OsPHR2 physically binds to the P1BScis‐elements in theOsDIR55promoter, and Pideprivation upregulatesOsDIR55expression in an OsPHR2‐dependent manner. We further showed that OsDIR55 maintains cellular Pihomoeostasis under Pistarvation by reinforcing root apoplastic barrier functionality. Disruption ofOsDIR55leads to excessive Piaccumulation and aberrant physiological responses under Pi‐sufficient conditions with leaf tip necrosis, indicative of Pitoxicity. Our work uncovers an essential role of the OsPHR2‐OsDIR55 module in mediating root anatomical adaptations to Piscarcity, refines Pistress signalling pathways and highlights the significance of lignification in plant responses to environmental stresses. Keywords:nutrient homoeostasis, OsDIR55, phosphate deficiency, plant adaptation, root apoplastic barriers OsDIR55, a target gene of OsPHR2 is essential for Casparian strip formation and plant adaptation to phosphate scarcity, underlining the importance of cell wall modification in plant tolerance to environmental stresses. Phosphorus (P), primarily available to plants as inorganic phosphate (Pi), is an essential macronutrient for nucleic acid synthesis, Adenosine Triphosphate production and membrane lipid assembly. It directly regulates various cellular processes such as energy transfer, metabolic regulation and signal transduction, ultimately governing plant growth, development and reproduction (Kanno et al.2016; Yang et al.2024). It is estimated that more than 50% of global agricultural soils are Pi‐deficient, making Pilimitation a major constraint to crop productivity (Kirkby and Johnston2008; McDowell et al.2024). Plants have evolved intricate mechanisms to cope with Piscarcity, including enhanced Piacquisition, optimised resource allocation and structural modifications (Lu, Linm et al.2024; Gómez‐Felipe2025). However, the molecular mechanisms underlying Pihomoeostasis and adaptive responses in crops like rice remain incompletely understood. Core regulators of Pistress signalling include MYB transcription factors of the phosphate starvation response (PHR) family, such asArabidopsisAtPHR1 and its rice homologue OsPHR2 (Rubio et al.2001; Zhou et al.2008).
