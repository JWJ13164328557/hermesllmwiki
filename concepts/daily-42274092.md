Lodging decreases crop yields, but a strong stem improves lodging resistance in grains such as rice (Oryza sativa). In this study, we determined that the R2R3‐type MYB transcription factorRobust Root System 1(RRS1) negatively regulates culm mechanical strength in rice by affecting culm diameter and culm wall thickness. Specifically,RRS1reduces culm diameter by directly activatingSTRONG1expression, which in turn alters cellulose deposition and sclerenchyma cell wall development. Knocking outRRS1improved culm mechanical strength and increased yield while maintaining quality, highlighting its considerable value for rice breeding. The favourableRRS1allele (RRS1Hap5) has great potential as a target for improving lodging resistance in rice. Combined haplotype analysis showed that plants with group II haplotypes, comprising the two elite alleles ofRRS1andSTRONG1, exhibited the largest culm diameter in bothindicaandjaponicarice. Furthermore, thejaponicarice accessions harbouring the two elite alleles ofRRS1andSTRONG1exhibited the highest grain number per panicle. Thus, this study presents a valuable gene aggregation strategy for the simultaneous improvement of lodging resistance and grain yield injaponicarice. In rice (Oryza sativa) and other grain crops, lodging is a major cause of harvest difficulties, yield loss, and quality reduction (Islam et al.2007). Cereal crops generally exhibit three types of lodging, including root lodging, culm bending‐type lodging and culm breaking‐type lodging (Donald and Hamblin1976; Hirano et al.2017). Root lodging, resulting from insufficient anchorage by the root system, predominantly occurs in direct‐seeded crops. In transplanted rice, the development of a more robust root system renders it less susceptible to root lodging. Culm bending‐type lodging, characterised by a permanent deformation (without fracture) of the upper internodes under the weight of the panicle, is common in rice. Culm breaking‐type lodging occurs when excessive bending stress on the upper internodes, typically from strong wind or rainstorm, causes the lower internodes to fracture. In rice cultivation, culm breaking‐type lodging is a common occurrence and poses a significantly greater threat to yield than either root lodging or culm bending‐type lodging.
