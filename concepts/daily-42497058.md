Age-related macular degeneration (AMD) is the leading cause of vision impairment among the elderly. At present, transplantation therapy shows great promise as a treatment for AMD.1Several clinical trials have shown that subretinal transplantation of retinal pigment epithelium (RPE) cells derived from induced pluripotent stem cells (iPSCs) or embryonic stem cells (ESCs) can help maintain or restore vision in patients.2,3,4These findings establish a foundation for advancing cell-replacement therapy for AMD. While existing protocols for the differentiation, assessment, and purification of RPE cells are established,5,6,7,8further optimization is necessary to enhance safety and efficiency while reducing costs. A cost-effective, high-efficiency protocol will benefit both therapeutic applications and related research endeavors.
