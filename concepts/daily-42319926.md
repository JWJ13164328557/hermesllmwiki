Metabolic dysfunction–associated steatotic liver disease (MASLD) has emerged as the most prevalent chronic liver disorder globally, affecting ~30 to 40% adult population (1–3). Despite its escalating burden, MASLD remains a therapeutic enigma due to its heterogenous pathogenesis involving intricate interactions among lipotoxicity, insulin resistance, oxidative stress, immune-inflammatory pathway, and extrahepatic factors (4–8). Illuminating the mechanisms and disrupting lipid deposition at the early stage will be promising in preventing MASLD progression. Hepatic cells originating from periportal (PP) and pericentral (PC) zones exhibit distinct gene expression and metabolic profiles, termed liver zonation. Key regulators include oxygen gradients, Wnt/β-catenin signaling, R-spondin proteins, and E3 ligases ZNRF3/RNF43 (9). While liver regeneration, toxin exposure, and autoimmune hepatitis manifest zonated pathologies (10–13), the role of zonation in MASLD progression remains elusive. Clinical and preclinical evidence indicates PC-predominant lipid deposition (14,15) and MASLD-driven zonal metabolic reprogramming (16). Recent work by Heet al.(17) uncovered the spatial regulation of lipid metabolism by insulin, indicating the zonal-specific initiation of hepatic steatosis.
