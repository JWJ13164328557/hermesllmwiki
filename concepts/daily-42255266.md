Results showed that 1.5% SSPS significantly enhanced viscoelasticity and water retention, whereas 3% SSPS disrupted the gluten network, reducing stability time by 56.33%. Wheat has long served as a fundamental constituent of the human diet, underpinning the global production of fermented and baked foods. The tradition of creating wheat-based products, ranging from standard breads to specialized sourdoughs, is facilitated by the distinctive rheological properties inherent in the grain (Shewry & Belton, 2024). In food processing, the dominance of wheat as a primary ingredient attests to the singular composition of its storage proteins. Gliadins typically constitute about 30–35% of the total protein content in wheat dough, contributing to viscosity and extensibility, while glutenins make up approximately 40–50%, providing elasticity and strength (Li et al., 2021). The balance between these two protein fractions is crucial for forming a cohesive gluten network, ultimately influencing the gas retention, texture, and volume of the final product (Delcour et al., 2012). In addition, dough quality is significantly influenced by processing, including mixing intensity, temperature, hydration level, pH and other related factors (Zheng et al., 2025). Notably, the stability of this protein network is frequently compromised in acidic food systems, such as sourdough breads (Fraberger et al., 2020;Ma et al., 2021). ## 摘要
This study investigated the effects of soluble soybean polysaccharide (SSPS) on dough rheology and water mobility under acidic conditions. Results showed that 1.5% SSPS significantly enhanced viscoelasticity and water retention, whereas 3% SSPS disrupted the gluten network, reducing stability time by 56.33%. LF-NMR and MRI confirmed that SSPS promoted water immobilization. Mechanistically, 1.5% SSPS increased α-helix content and thermal stability, creating a dense, continuous gluten matrix through electrostatic and hydrophobic interactions. Conversely, excess SSPS (3%) exacerbated structural weakening. These findings suggest that moderate SSPS supplementation effectively offsets acid-induced damage, providing a strategic approach to enhance the structural integrity and processing performance of acidic wheat-based products.
