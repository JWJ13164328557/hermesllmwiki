However, the breadth of smoking-related DNAm changes remains unmapped, offering a space for employing novel technologies. To expand our understanding of smoking impacts on DNAm, we conducted an epigenome-wide association study (EWAS) comparing ever smokers to never smokers, using blood from a multiethnic U.S. study population (n=887).
