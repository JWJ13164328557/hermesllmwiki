P XANES can differentiate P species, but it has had limited use in Fe plaque characterization. P species in Fe plaque from soil‐grown plants were differentiated by P XANES using two preparation techniques. Fe plaque as a thin film deposit was superior to powdered and revealed dominance of organic P using K‐edge XANES. Plaque P concentration is too low for L‐edge P XANES to further characterize specific organic P species. Phosphorus (P) is an essential nutrient for rice, but much of it becomes trapped in iron (Fe) deposits that form on rice roots in flooded soils. We studied the chemical forms of P in these iron coatings, called Fe plaque, using advanced x‐ray techniques. Our goal was to find out whether this P is in forms that rice plants can use. We discovered that most of the P—about 40%–70%—was bound in organic forms, which are less available to plants than inorganic forms. This means that the P stored in root Fe plaque may not be as accessible as previously thought. Our results highlight the importance of considering organic P in rice nutrient management and could help improve fertilizer strategies to reduce waste and environmental impacts. Phosphorus (P) is an essential plant nutrient that often limits crop productivity; thus, understanding its cycling in soil‐plant systems is of critical importance. Despite the known complexity of P speciation in soil systems (Santoro et al.,2024), information about P speciation in paddy soils is more limited, particularly in the rice (Oryza sativaL.) rhizosphere. ## 摘要
Rice (Oryza sativa L.) plants accumulate phosphorus (P) on iron (Fe) plaque deposited on root surfaces. We aimed to investigate P speciation in rice root Fe plaque using x-ray absorption near edge structure (XANES) spectroscopy coupled to conventional techniques. We examined Fe plaque samples from rice experiments in different soils. Samples deposited on filters yielded sufficiently thin, concentrated samples with high-quality spectra despite low P concentrations. The low P concentrations in Fe plaque prevented us from using L-edge P XANES for organic P differentiation, but allowed K-edge P XANES. Fits of K-edge P XANES data showed that organic P comprised from 40% to 70% of root plaque P, while inorganic P represented only a minor fraction. These findings demonstrate the importance of organic P in the rice rhizosphere and may lead to targeted P fertilization with minimal environmental impact.
