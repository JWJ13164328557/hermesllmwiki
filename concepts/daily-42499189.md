Calcium (Ca2+) signaling is one of the earliest and most conserved hallmarks of plant immune activation (Wang et al., 2024). During pattern-triggered immunity, pathogen perception induces rapid and transient increases in cytosolic Ca2+, whereas effector-triggered immunity (ETI) is typically associated with stronger and more sustained Ca2+signatures that are tightly linked to hypersensitive cell death (Zhang et al., 2024). The recent discovery that activated nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat (NLR) resistosomes function as Ca2+-permeable channels at the plasma membrane (PM) has established a prevailing model in which extracellular Ca2+influx serves as a central driver of immune activation (Wang et al., 2019;Bi et al., 2021). However, this PM-centered framework leaves an important question unresolved: how are the unusually sustained and spatially complex Ca2+signatures associated with ETI generated and maintained? Plant cells contain extensive intracellular Ca2+stores, including the vacuole, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), chloroplasts, and mitochondria, all of which maintain Ca2+concentrations substantially higher than the Ca2+concentration in the cytosol (Wang et al., 2024). Previous studies have suggested that these organelles actively shape immune-associated Ca2+dynamics and downstream defense outputs (Wang et al., 2024). Yet, despite long-standing speculation about the contribution of intracellular Ca2+stores to plant immunity, direct mechanistic links between organellar Ca2+mobilization and NLR activation have remained largely elusive. A recent study now provides compelling evidence that helper NLR resistosomes can directly mobilize intracellular Ca2+pools (Ibrahim et al., 2026). Upon recognition of theXanthomonaseffector XopQ by theNicotiana benthamianaToll/interleukin-1 receptor (TIR)-type NLR (TIR-NLR) RECOGNITION OF XOPQ 1 (ROQ1), theN. benthamiana helper NLR N REQUIREMENT GENE 1 (NbNRG1) relocalizes to punctate structures associated with intracellular membranes, including the chloroplast envelope, in an ENHANCED DISEASE SUSCEPTIBILITY 1–SENESCENCE-ASSOCIATED GENE 101-dependent manner and mediates chloroplast Ca2+efflux into the cytosol (Ibrahim et al., 2026). These findings establish a direct mechanistic link between organellar Ca2+stores and resistosome signaling during ETI. More broadly, this work challenges the traditional view of resistosomes as immune pores that function exclusively at the PM, supporting instead an emerging model in which compartment-specialized resistosomes mobilize Ca2+from distinct intracellular stores to coordinate immune signaling (Figure 1).
