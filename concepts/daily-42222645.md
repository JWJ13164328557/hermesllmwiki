We thank Dr. Liu and Dr. Lu for their thoughtful commentary on our trial comparing continuous erector spinae plane block (ESPB) with thoracic epidural analgesia (TEA) after thoracotomy.1We appreciate the opportunity to address their methodological concerns and provide additional context for interpreting our findings. Dr. Liu and Lu note that we did not perform objective sensory block assessment and raise concerns about distinguishing interfascial block effects from systemic local anesthetic absorption, particularly given the higher ropivacaine exposure in the ESPB group. As acknowledged in our manuscript, sensory block levels were not assessed as a separate study measure. However, catheter placement was confirmed under imaging guidance with fluoroscopy for TEA and ultrasound for ESPB to ensure appropriate positioning. We recognize that the clinical effectiveness of ESPB remains controversial; volunteer studies have demonstrated limited anterior thoracic sensory changes,2and anatomical investigations have shown inconsistent ventral injectate spread following the block.3We agree that incorporating dermatomal mapping in future trials would help distinguish the relative contributions of interfascial spread and systemic absorption. Regarding local anesthetic dosing, the higher volume in the ESPB arm is a necessary consequence of the technique itself, not a design limitation.
