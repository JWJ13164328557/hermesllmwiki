# The Regulation of Flowering by FLOWERING LOCUS C: Epigenetic Memory of Winter Cold and the Role of Various Factors.

## 摘要
Flowering, a key stage in the transition of plants from vegetative to reproductive growth, is regulated by a variety of internal and environmental factors. As an important flowering repressor, FLOWERING LOCUS C (FLC) has been widely studied. FLC expression is mainly regulated by a variety of histone modifications, and the interplay between active and repressive markers determines the final expression levels of FLC. In this review, we provide a comprehensive overview of the roles of FLC in response to vernalization, the autonomous pathway, hormones, and diverse stresses. Furthermore, we summarize 153 regulatory factors of FLC across different flowering-related pathways, including 77 vernalization-related, 45 autonomous pathway-related, and 31 hormone- or stress-related regulators listed in appendices. We mainly focus on the positive regulation of FLC by the FRIGIDA (FRI) protein complex before vernalization, the establishment of mitotically stable H3K27me3-mediated repression during cold, which is maintained after vernalization and constitutes the 'memory of winter cold', the maintenance of this epigenetic memory in warm temperatures, and the resetting of FLC expression after fertilization. In addition, chromatin modifications, RNA-based post-transcriptional regulation, and post-translational modifications of FLC are reviewed in the autonomous pathway. Finally, we discuss how hormone pathways and stress signals converge on FLC to fine-tune flowering. The molecular mechanisms governing precise FLC expression provide a classic example of plant gene regulation and a useful framework for studying the function of other genes.
