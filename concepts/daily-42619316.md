## 摘要
The important cereal pathogen Fusarium graminearum is not only able to produce trichothecene toxins, like deoxynivalenol, but also the plant hormone auxin (indole-3-acetic acid, IAA). Highly elevated levels of IAA and auxin derivatives such as IAA-glucoside or IAA amino-acid conjugates were observed in the susceptible wheat cultivar Apogee infected with F. graminearum. We report here that the fungal pathogen can hydrolyse tryptamine-derived hydroxycinnamic acid amides produced in high amounts during plant defence and is able to convert the released tryptamine in high yield into IAA. We investigated the role of copper amine-oxidase candidate genes, which code for enzymes converting tryptamine into the IAA precursor indole-3-acetaldehyde.
