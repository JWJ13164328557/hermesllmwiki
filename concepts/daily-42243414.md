Arsenic (As) is a non-essential toxic metalloid whose elevated concentration in rice grains is a serious issue both for rice yield and quality, and for human health. The rice-As interactions, hence, have been studied extensively in past few decades. A deep understanding of factors influencing As uptake and transport from soil to grains can be helpful to tackle this issue so as to minimize grain As levels. As uptake at the root surface by rice plants depends on factors like iron plaque and radial oxygen loss. There is involvement of a number of transportersviz., phosphate transporters and aquaglyceroporins in the uptake and transport of different As species and in the movement to subcellular compartments. These processes are also affected by sulfur availability and consequently on the level of thiol (-SH)-containing As binding peptides viz., glutathione (GSH) and phytochelatins (PCs). Further, the role of phloem in As movement to the grains is also suggested. This review presents a detailed map of journey of As from soil to the grains. The implications for the utilization of available knowledge in minimizing As in rice grains are presented. ## 摘要
The production of Oryza sativa L. (rice) in polluted agroecosystems is severely affected by arsenic (As) and lead (Pb) toxicity. This study evaluates the efficacy of a novel halotolerant Bacillus inaquosorum SWA3TU (NCBI GenBank Accession No.: PQ097957), isolated from Sundarbans mangrove soil, as a plant growth-promoting bacterium (PGPR) for alleviating individual As and Pb stress in the IR-64 rice cultivar. SWA3TU demonstrated tolerance to NaCl (up to 10%) and heavy metals (As and Pb up to 150 µg/mL), along with plant growth-promoting traits including IAA production, phosphate and zinc solubilization, siderophore secretion, nitrogen fixation, exopolysaccharide synthesis, and antifungal activity. Under hydroponic conditions at 12 µg/mL (selected concentration) of As and Pb, SWA3TU inoculation significantly reduced toxicity. Compared to stressed controls, it increased biomass by 45%-62%, root and shoot length by 42%-57%, and chlorophyll content by 72%-78%. Antioxidant enzyme activities were elevated under stress and reduced by 31%-43% following bacterial inoculation, indicating mitigation of oxidative stress. Additionally, H2O2 levels and electrolyte leakage decreased by 36%-40% and 35%-38%, respectively. A 42%-46% reduction in As and Pb accumulation was observed, along with increased expression of Aux/IAA gene. Although the isolate is halotolerant, salinity stress was not included in plant experiments and is considered only as an environmental background factor relevant to the Sundarbans ecosystem. These findings highlight B. inaquosorum SWA3TU as a promising bioinoculant for mitigating arsenic and lead stress in rice.
