Leveraging single-cell transcriptomics and live-cell imaging ofArabidopsisroots, we reveal here that plant-derived phytocytokines elicit more potent immune responses than microbe-derived patterns across root cell types and zones. The differential expression of receptors and key signaling modules in distinct cell types and zones contributes to the response intensity to specific elicitors. Phytocytokines sustain growth-defense trade-offs by suppressing the expression of receptor-like kinase genes associated with root cell division and elongation. The intensity of immune responses in different root zones is associated with fungal and bacterial pathogen invasion sites. Furthermore, motif-informed network inference highlighted key transcriptional regulators driving cell identity-specific transcriptomic immune responses. Our study provides a comprehensive landscape of transcriptional responses in plant roots in response to diverse immune elicitors, highlighting how distinct phytocytokines orchestrate stage- and cell type-dependent transcriptional reprogramming. Xu, Ortiz-Morea, and Kim et al. combine single-cell transcriptomics and live-cell imaging to map immune responses across Arabidopsis root cell types and developmental zones to different elicitors. Their study reveals that plant-derived phytocytokines drive broad transcriptional reprogramming and uncover regulatory mechanisms linking root immunity, development, and pathogen susceptibility. Plants depend on innate immune responses to fend off a wide array of microorganisms. The plasma membrane (PM)-resident pattern recognition receptors (PRRs), encompassing receptor-like kinases (RLKs) and receptor-like proteins (RLPs), recognize microbe-associated molecular patterns (MAMPs) and activate pattern-triggered immunity (PTI)1–3, including activation of receptor-like cytoplasmic kinases (RLCKs), increase of cytosolic calcium, bursts of reactive oxygen species (ROS) and phosphatidic acid (PA), activation of mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK), transcriptional reprogramming, callose deposition, and stomatal closure4–7. Upon pathogen infection, plants produce and release damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs), including phytocytokines, sensed by PRRs to activate damage-triggered immunity (DTI)8–11.
