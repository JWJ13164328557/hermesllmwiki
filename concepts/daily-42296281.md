An 82‐year‐old woman with a history of radiofrequency (RF) catheter ablation for atrial fibrillation underwent a redo ablation procedure for symptomatic palpitations due to regular atrial tachycardia. The initial procedure included pulmonary vein isolation, left atrial posterior wall isolation, and superior vena cava isolation. The redo procedure involved left anterior linear ablation connecting the left superior pulmonary vein to the mitral annulus using a 9‐mm lattice‐tip catheter (Sphere‐9; Medtronic). After completion of the left atrial anterior linear ablation, cavotricuspid isthmus (CTI) ablation was performed using RF energy. The anterior RF setting was used, with a current limit of 80% and a target temperature of 73°C; automatic RF termination was set at 85°C. RF energy was delivered from the tricuspid annulus side. Following the initial application, a second application was performed at a site 6 mm proximal. At 4.0 s into RF delivery, a steam pop occurred at approximately 65°C, accompanied by a shower of microbubbles (Figure1, MovieS1) and tactile catheter feedback, without a distinct audible pop; the temperature subsequently rose to a maximum of 74.9°C (Figure2). The steam pop was recognized at approximately 4 s based on tactile feedback and intracardiac echocardiography (ICE) findings. However, by the time the operator attempted to stop RF delivery, the predefined 5‐s RF application had already completed automatically. No increase in pericardial effusion or signs of cardiac tamponade was observed, and the procedure was continued. CTI block was achieved after the fourth RF application. ## 摘要
We report a case of steam pop occurring during lattice-tip radiofrequency ablation. An 82-year-old woman underwent cavotricuspid isthmus ablation using a 9-mm lattice-tip catheter. Intracardiac echocardiography (ICE), electroanatomical mapping, and radiofrequency parameters were recorded simultaneously on a single system, allowing precise temporal correlation. A steam pop occurred at 4 s with a sudden microbubble shower on ICE at 65°C. The highest temperature was detected at the proximal thermocouple, suggesting nonuniform catheter-tissue contact. This case highlights limitations of temperature monitoring and the importance of spatial catheter orientation during lattice-tip ablation.
