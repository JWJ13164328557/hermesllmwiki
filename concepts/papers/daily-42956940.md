# SbMYB44 transcription factor from halophyte Salicornia brachiata confers salt and drought tolerance through physio-biochemical, oxidative and molecular modulation in transgenics.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2027)
- **PMID**: [42956940](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42956940/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113561](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113561)
- **第一作者**: Mohit Dangariya
- **作者**: Mohit Dangariya, Parinita Agarwal, Pradeep K Agarwal

## 摘要
Abiotic stresses significantly disrupt the morphological, physiological and biochemical functions of plants, ultimately limiting crop productivity. Salicornia brachiata is a succulent halophyte that can grow in extreme saline conditions by manoeuvring molecular and physio-biochemical adaptations. MYB family transcription factors are involved in various metabolic and stress-responsive pathways, however, their functional characterization from Salicornia remains limited. Therefore, the present study functionally validate the R2R3-type MYB TF SbMYB44 isolated from S. brachiata. Overexpression of SbMYB44 in tobacco plants, conferred improved tolerance to salt and drought stress by modulating a various morpho-physiological, biochemical and molecular responses. Transgenic plants exhibited improved membrane stability, higher levels of photosynthetic pigments and increased elemental content, alongside reduced electrolyte leakage and sodium accumulation compared to wild-type (WT) plants under both stress conditions. Biochemically, SbMYB44 transgenic lines demonstrated elevated levels of osmolytes, phenolics and antioxidative enzymes activity, and decreased accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). At the molecular level, SbMYB44 overexpression was associated with the upregulation of several stress-responsive genes under salt and drought stress. However, the detailed direct target-gene regulatory study by which SbMYB44 TF regulates these stress-responsive genes remains to be elucidated. Under stress conditions, SbMYB44 overexpression was associated with maintained pigments and ion homeostasis, enhanced accumulation of osmoprotectants and antioxidant enzyme activities, which may contribute to reduced oxidative damage and improved stress tolerance. Overall, present study shows that SbMYB44 overexpression strengthens the plant's ability towards abiotic stresses, suggesting potential applications in developing stress-resilient crops.
