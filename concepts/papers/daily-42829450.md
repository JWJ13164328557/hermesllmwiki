# Light wavelength-specific regulation of hypersensitive response dynamics in tobacco plants during non-host interaction with Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato.

- **PMID**: [42829450](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42829450/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/15592324.2026.2733228](https://doi.org/10.1080/15592324.2026.2733228)
- **第一作者**: Shamjetsabam Gangarani Devi
- **作者**: Shamjetsabam Gangarani Devi, Nguyen Van Khanh, Yong Hoon Lee

## 摘要
Light quality is a key environmental signal that integrates plant immune pathways, yet how specific wavelengths regulate the execution, timing, and efficiency of the hypersensitive response (HR) remains incompletely resolved. This study investigated how light spectral composition modulates HR dynamics and resistance outcomes during a non-host interaction. Tobacco leaves were inoculated with Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato DC3000 (PstDC3000) and incubated under red (RL), green (GL), blue (BL), white light (WL), or darkness. Monochromatic light treatments distinctly reshaped HR progression. BL and WL induced rapid HR characterized by early accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS), accelerated electrolyte leakage, and pronounced callose deposition. RL and GL delayed visible HR while effectively restricting bacterial proliferation by moderating ROS production, delaying membrane disruption, and sustaining cell wall reinforcement. In darkness, ROS accumulation was dysregulated, tissue collapse was uncontrolled, and pathogen growth was not effectively constrained. These results demonstrate that light wavelength fine-tunes HR kinetics and qualitatively distinct defense outcomes, with RL and GL favoring controlled immune responses over rapid cell death. By identifying GL as a key modulator of regulated immunity, this study provides a conceptual framework for managing light environments to optimize plant disease resistance.

### 核心发现
1. Genetic and physiological studies in photoreceptor mutants have demonstrated that light perception directly modulates defense signaling networks, including HR-associated programmed cell death.,, Using HR induced by the non-host pathogen PstDC3000 in tobacco, this study shows that light spectral composition acts as a higher-order regulatory layer that specifies how ETI is executed.
2. By integrating HR phenotyping with measurements of bacterial growth, ROS accumulation, membrane integrity, callose deposition, and defense-related gene expression, we show that spectral quality governs not only the speed but also the coordination and outcome of HR.
3. Overall, our results support a model in which light perception modulates HR by differentially regulating ROS dynamics, membrane integrity, structural defenses, and defense gene expression.
4. By identifying GL as a key modulator of regulated immunity, this study provides a conceptual framework for managing light environments to optimize plant disease resistance.
5. These results suggest that light-dependent cell wall reinforcement contributes to pathogen restriction even when HR execution is temporally delayed.
6. Collectively, these results indicate that different light wavelengths coordinate defense activation with redox homeostasis, resulting in distinct transcriptional programs associated with HR progression and outcome.
7. Importantly, light not only amplifies ROS production but also governs its spatial and temporal dynamics.
8. Importantly, despite delayed HR onset, GL-treated leaves exhibited substantial callose deposition at later stages.
