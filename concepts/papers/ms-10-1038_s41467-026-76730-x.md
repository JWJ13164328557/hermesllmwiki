1. We report a similar null ﬁnding when using BMS- 986187 to determine a DADLE-bound δOR-Gi complex by cryo-EM.
2. The δ-opioid receptor (δOR) represents a promising ther- apeutic target for developing safer opioid analgesics with reduced adverse effects compared to conventional μ-opioid receptor-targeting drugs.
3. Despite this promise, ﬁrst- generation δOR agonists targeting the orthosteric binding site encountered development challenges due to on-target side effects, including seizures and tolerance development11–13.
4. While next- generation δOR agonists have successfully mitigated seizure risk, they still face signiﬁcant hurdles, including the development of toler- ance, insufﬁcient clinical efﬁcacy and off-target effects10,14.
5. δOR agonists also represent promising therapeutic targets for gastrointestinal (GI) disorders.
6. 7. Rather than directly activating the receptor, PAMs bind to distinct allosteric sites and selectively enhance the activity of endogenous opioid peptides, thereby preserving natural spatiotemporal signalling patterns while potentially circumventing some limitations inherent to orthosteric agonists30.
8. Collectively, our ﬁndings identify an allosteric binding site and deﬁne the mechanism by which these PAMs potentiate δOR agonist binding, providing infor- mation relevant to their rational design.
