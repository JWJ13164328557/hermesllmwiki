# Revisiting the Pea Chloroplast Envelope Translocons: Evidence for a Physically Distinct and Functional Translocon at the Outer Membrane (TOC).

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: The Journal of biological chemistry (2026)
- **PMID**: [43025595](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025595/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113751](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jbc.2026.113751)
- **第一作者**: Gent Ballabani
- **作者**: Gent Ballabani, Thomas Badet, Giacomo Silvestri, Véronique Douet, Venkatasalam Shanmugabalaji, Felix Kessler

## 摘要
Chloroplast protein import components have been well documented in Arabidopsis and Chlamydomonas, but remain largely unexplored in other species. This is also true of the pea (Pisum sativum), a classic model system in which many of the early discoveries were made. This study provides an inventory of the components involved in the import of chloroplast proteins,and characterises the heterotrimeric translocon at the outer membrane of pea chloroplasts (TOC). We have identified a gateway in chloroplasts that is composed of TOC159, TOC75 and TOC34. Genome analysis revealed a single gene for each of TOC75 and TOC34, but multiple homologues of TOC159. This suggests that these genes are the primary determinants of the specificity of preproteins. Quantitative analysis of the chloroplast proteome demonstrates that TOC159-1 is dominant, implying a role in the photosynthesis-associated TOC complex (TOC-P). The TOC-P components are approximately three times more abundant than those of the 1MDa TIC complex. Quantitative mass spectrometry of isolated complexes also shows an excess of TOC-P over the 1MDa TICs, indicating separation of TOC and TIC. In a functional protein import assay, preRbcS co-isolated together with the TOC complex, demonstrating its preprotein receptor activity. AlphaFold 3 modelling suggests that dimers of the GTP-binding domains of TOC34 and TOC159-1 control access to a hybrid β-barrel assembly formed by the TOC75 and TOC159-1 β-barrel domains. These models also predict the presence of a transit peptide-binding groove within the TOC159 GTP-binding domain. Together, these findings contribute to a foundational model for TOC function in land plants.
