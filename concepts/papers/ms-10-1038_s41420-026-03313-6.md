### 核心发现
1. Strikingly, we show that GCS inhibition resulted in stalled autophagy marked by an increase in LC3-II-positive autolysosomes and an accumulation of autophagolysosome-localized cellular products including ER-associated membranes, p62-positive cargo, and cholesterol.
2. Surprisingly, we found that myriocin or FB1 cotreatment sensitized cells to eliglustat rather than rescuing them (Figure 1B and Figure S1A).
3. More importantly, we found that in the cotreated cells, whose growth is still significantly inhibited, eliglustat-induced increase in ceramide was prevented by myriocin (Figure 1F).
4. We found that inhibition of GCS by eliglustat, an FDA-approved GCS inhibitor, or other pharmacological inhibitors (Genz123346 and miglustat), or genetic silencing of GCS induced cell death in several cancer cell lines which was not rescued by prevention of ceramide generation, suggesting that the cell death may be independent of ceramide accumulation.
5. We found that GCS inhibition, or silencing, in several cancer cell lines results in cell death due to dysregulation of autophagy and lysosome turnover, which were not rescued by inhibition of ceramide accumulation.
6. Further, we found a significant decrease in ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS mitochondrial membrane potential following eliglustat treatment which was, again, not prevented by myriocin cotreatment (Figure S1C).
7. We found that all three cell lines were sensitive to eliglustat, and myriocin did not prevent cell death (Figure 2C and 2D, Figure S2C).
8. We found that eliglustat induced LC3B lipidation in total cell, soluble, and insoluble fractions, as well as p62 in the insoluble fractions, suggesting incomplete autophagy (Figure 3A-B, Figure S3A).
