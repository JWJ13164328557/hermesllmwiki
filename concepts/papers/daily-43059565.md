# Optimization of a Lab-screening Procedure to Evaluate Seed Treatment Efficacy: Detection of Ustilago nuda in Hordeum vulgare Seedlings.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43059565](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43059565/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-05-26-0912-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-05-26-0912-RE)
- **第一作者**: Cecilia Panzetti
- **作者**: Cecilia Panzetti, Eveline Jenny, Daniel Croll, Karen E Sullam

## 摘要
To prevent plant disease outbreaks and economic losses, rapid and reliable tools to evaluate newly developed, low-risk treatments are needed. Visual inspections of grown plants are used to assess treatment efficacy a labor-intensive and costly process, especially for seedborne pathogens that are asymptomatic until flowering. For one such pathogen, Ustilago nuda that causes loose smut of barley (Hordeum vulgare), we developed a lab-screening procedure combining optimized growth procedures and qPCR to quantify its DNA in seedlings, thereby accelerating seed treatment evaluations. We cultivated seedlings for 5, 10 and 14 days with two techniques: growth on filter paper in dark and in potting soil under light-dark conditions. Seedlings were grown from two naturally infected seed lots, which were either not treated or treated with a synthetic fungicide or warm water. During two seasons, these non-treated and treated seeds were also grown in the field. Infection levels in the seedlings were based on qPCR-measured U. nuda DNA normalized to H. vulgare DNA. Treatment efficacies were calculated using laboratory-measured infection levels and field-observed disease incidence, and a lab-to-field predictability factor was used to compare seed treatment evaluations among the seedling growth procedures. Infection levels assessed using qPCR on seedlings grown for 10 days on filter paper and 14 days in potting soil most accurately represented field results and showed clear treatment effects. Our lab-screening method could provide an early and efficient evaluation of seed treatment efficacy, aiding in the development of new, low-risk control strategies to reduce synthetic plant protection product applications.
