# FLOWERING LOCUS C: Deciphering Epigenetic Memory to Floral Awakening in Brassicaceae Members.

- **PMID**: [43043682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43043682/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/pce.71056](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.71056)

- **作者**: Sudheer Bishnoi, Nikhil Ram Kumar, Damini Singh, Nimisha Sharma, Joshitha Vijayan, Ramcharan Bhattacharya

## 摘要
Seasonal flowering depends on how plants measure winter and convert that information into a stable developmental response. In Arabidopsis and related Brassicaceae, FLOWERING LOCUS C provides one of the clearest examples of how environmental information is translated into chromatin-based memory through a mechanistically defined gene regulatory system. This review focuses on recent advances that have reshaped the field, including distributed thermosensing that distinguishes prolonged winter from brief cold spells, cold-induced nucleation of Polycomb silencing, the distinct phases of H3K27me3 spreading and maintenance, the roles of COOLAIR, COLDAIR, and COLDWRAP, single-cell evidence for stochastic allele switching, and embryonic resetting of the vernalized state. It also examines how natural variation at FLC and its regulators tunes vernalization responses across Arabidopsis accessions and Brassica crops, linking molecular mechanism to adaptation under different climates. A central theme is that FLC is not only a floral repressor but also a model for understanding how plants encode fluctuating temperature histories into robust developmental outputs. Open questions include how chromatin topology and lncRNA activity integrate under the full complexity of natural field winters, how vernalization requirements shift as regional climates warm, and how paralogous FLC networks in Brassica can be translated into predictive, climate-resilient breeding.
