# Strigolactones constrain rice drought acclimation by suppressing ROS scavenging through the D53-OsWRKY31-ZFP36 module.

## 摘要
Strigolactones (SLs) are a class of plant hormones essential for tiller development and yield under diverse environmental conditions. Drought is a major limiting factor for rice yields. Although SLs contribute to drought resistance, the mechanisms and practical applications of the SL pathway in rice drought acclimation remain poorly understood. In this study, we show that short-term dehydration represses SL biosynthesis in rice roots. Genetic assays indicate that disrupting SL biosynthesis or signaling elevates rice drought resistance, whereas activating SL signaling or supplementing with the SL analog GR244DO impairs drought resistance. We found that SLs negatively regulate drought acclimation by promoting degradation of the repressor protein DWARF53 (D53). Biochemical assays unveil that D53 interacts with the transcription factor OsWRKY31 via its N-terminal domain and suppresses the protein level of OsWRKY31, which could bind the ZFP36 promoter to repress its transcription. ZFP36 encodes a zinc-finger transcription factor that promotes H2O2 scavenging to sustain reactive oxygen species homeostasis during drought stress. Notably, the drought-resistant upland rice variety IRAT109 exhibits lower SL levels in root exudates than the lowland rice variety Nipponbare (NP). Genome editing of key components in the SL pathway enhances drought resistance in NP, Huazhan, and IRAT109. The agronomic potential of tuning SL biosynthesis is further supported by the elite D17/HTD1 allele, which weakens SL biosynthesis and improves drought resistance and grain yield in Nekken 2 under field conditions. Taken together, these findings uncover a key mechanism underlying SL-repressed drought acclimation in rice and provide an effective strategy to improve drought resistance in diverse rice varieties amid ongoing climate change.
