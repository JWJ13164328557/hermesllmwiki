1. Importantly, we found that disruption of the maternal microbiome in C57BL/6 dams decreased the number of germ cells per ovary at P3 and reduced the PmF stage follicles in adult offspring of ABX dams (Fig.
2. Taken together, these results suggest that maternal microbiome disruption increases DNA damage in fetal germ cells, and this accumulated DNA damage may persist into adulthood in offspring, leading to elevated apoptosis and ultimately compromising oocyte maturation and fertilization capacity.
3. Additionally, through in vitro fertilization assay, we found that maternal microbiome disruption impaired oocyte quality in offspring, while FMT and supplementation with bile acid metabo- lites alleviated this effect.
4. In this study, by applying an antibiotic-induced microbiome disruption, we show that maternal antibiotic-induced microbiome disruption leads to DOR in offspring.
5. Impor- tantly, through FMT and selected metabolite supplementation, we show that the maternal microbiome may regulate in-utero fetal ovarian reserve establishment through microbially mediated bile acid metabolism in the maternal serum.
