# Naked barley and the Nud mutation: post-domestication origin, genetic basis and breeding prospects.

## 摘要
Naked barley arose after domestication through loss of Nud, an ERF gene; introgressing Nudinto, or knocking out Nudin, elite covered cultivars is the most efficient breeding route. Hull adherence determines how barley is processed and used. In most cultivated barley, the lemma and palea are cemented to the caryopsis, whereas naked (hulless) barley threshes free of the hull, which provides nutrition and market value. The naked caryopsis results from loss of function of Nud, an ethylene response factor (ERF) gene on chromosome arm 7HL that is required for the cuticular cementing layer; almost all naked barleys carry the same 17-kb deletion encompassing this gene. Because the hull accounts for about 10-13% of the grain dry weight, naked grain has higher concentrations of protein, β-glucan and digestible energy, although grain β-glucan content is controlled mainly by loci other than Nud. Outside the Himalayas and East Asia, naked barley remains a minor crop, constrained by lower whole-grain yield, mechanical damage to the exposed embryo, poorer stand establishment, and malting difficulties. This article examines the origin of the Nudmutation in domesticated barley, the mechanism of hull-caryopsis adhesion, the inheritance of threshability, and breeding options for naked barley. We propose that, instead of breeding from the narrow naked gene pool alone, breeders should introgress the recessive Nudallele into elite covered cultivars by marker-assisted backcrossing, or create it directly by CRISPR/Cas9-mediated knockout of Nud, while addressing the agronomic and end-use limitations of naked grain in parallel. By integrating genetic, mechanistic, and agronomic evidence, the review identifies the research priorities needed to make naked barley competitive with covered barley.
