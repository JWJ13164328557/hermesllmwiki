# Cultivation temperature shapes the rice seedling-protective activity of Bacillus subtilis LSG20, with TasA as a candidate extracellular factor.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of applied microbiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033001](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033001/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/jambio/lxag280](https://doi.org/10.1093/jambio/lxag280)
- **第一作者**: Xiangrui Zeng
- **作者**: Xiangrui Zeng, Xuejie Zhang, Beibei Liu, Yukun Zou, Jing Zhang

## 摘要
AIMS: Extreme heat events driven by climate change increasingly threaten rice production. Microbial approaches offer sustainable and low-input strategies to improve crop thermotolerance, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. This study investigated the thermophilic Bacillus subtilis LSG20 to assess its capacity to enhance rice thermotolerance and to identify its intrinsic heat-adaptation strategies and factors associated with its plant-protective activity.

METHODS AND RESULTS: Combined genomic, transcriptomic and physiological analyses indicated that LSG20 withstands high temperature by maintaining protein homeostasis, cellular integrity, and enhancing antioxidant defense and DNA repair systems. Notably, the plant-protective effect of LSG20 was not directly coupled with its heat-adaptation state. Instead, culture filtrate produced at 28 °C, rather than at 45 °C or 60 °C, showed the strongest ability to improve rice thermotolerance. Transcriptomic analysis identified the amyloid protein TasA as a candidate factor associated with this low-temperature-induced activity. Under equal total-protein loading, the relative TasA abundance in culture filtrates was significantly higher at 28 °C than at 45 °C and 60 °C. Consistently, recombinant TasA improved rice seedling performance under both heat and PEG-induced osmotic stress.

CONCLUSIONS: These results demonstrate that the plant-protective activity of LSG20 did not parallel its intrinsic heat-adaptation state, and TasA may be a candidate extracellular contributor to this protective effect. These findings highlight the potential of thermophilic microbial resources and their bioactive products for climate-resilient agriculture.
