# Evaluating tropospheric ozone effects on wheat productivity using the CERES-O3 model.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Environmental pollution (Barking, Essex : 1987) (2027)
- **PMID**: [42901466](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42901466/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129382](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envpol.2026.129382)
- **第一作者**: Manuela Njiki
- **作者**: Manuela Njiki, Benoît Gabrielle, Ronny Lauerwald, Jean-François Castell, Patrick Stella, Raia-Silvia Massad

## 摘要
Tropospheric ozone (O3) is a major air pollutant that reduces crop productivity by altering photosynthesis, stomatal conductance, and carbon allocation. Process-based crop models are useful for assessing these impacts under changing environmental conditions. In this study, we developed and evaluated the CERES-O3 model to simulate and quantify O3 impacts on wheat physiology and biomass production, using a flux-based approach. The model incorporates O3 effects on carboxylation and electron transport rates, as a function of the phytotoxic ozone dose (POD6). CERES-O3 was calibrated using data from a free-air fumigation experiment in Grignon, France, in 2009, with four O3 treatments, and evaluated using independent field experiments from 2003 to 2004 and FR-Gri ICOS ecosystem data from 2006, 2009, and 2012. CERES-O3 reproduced the O3-induced decline in photosynthesis and stomatal conductance. Agreement with ICOS GPP was good, with annual regression slopes ranging from 0.84 to 1.05 and R2 values from 0.79 to 0.85. Simulated canopy stomatal O3 deposition accounted for 33-60% of measured total ecosystem deposition during cropping periods. Grain yield and above-ground biomass showed variable agreement, with particularly large yield underestimation in 2006 associated with strong simulated water stress. The fitted POD6-relative-yield relationship (RY = 1.003 - 0.041 POD6, R2 = 0.93) was consistent with established wheat flux-response relationships. Across ICOS years, the derived relationship indicated an estimated mean O3 -associated yield reduction of 6.5%. Overall, CERES-O3 provides an efficient framework for representing O3 impacts on wheat productivity, while highlighting remaining uncertainties in canopy scaling and the representation of crop responses to water limitation.
