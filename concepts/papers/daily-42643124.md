## 摘要
Global agriculture is increasingly challenged by climate instability, genetic erosion, emerging pathogens and rising food demands, exposing the limitations of conventional breeding and traditional domestication strategies. Recent advances in CRISPR-based genome editing, pangenomic, synthetic biology, artificial intelligence (AI)-assisted breeding and predictive phenomics are transforming de novo domestication from a slow evolutionary process into a programmable framework for rational crop redesign. This review synthesises recent advances in programmable de novo domestication and highlights how crop wild relatives and underutilised germplasm can be harnessed to develop resilient, climate-adaptive and sustainable crop systems. The integration of multiplex genome editing, pan-genomic variation discovery, AI-driven genomic prediction and predictive breeding enables precise engineering of key domestication traits governing plant architecture, yield potential, stress resilience and nutritional quality. Furthermore, we propose a trajectory-based framework for programmable domestication comprising Adaptive Rescue, Agronomic Refinement and Novel Chassis Engineering, which illustrates distinct evolutionary pathways, engineering complexity and crop redesign objectives. We also examine the major system level challenges that constrain programmable domestication, including cryptic genetic variation, epistasis, gene regulatory network complexity, genotype phenotype predictability, biodiversity conservation and regulatory considerations. Collectively, programmable domestication represents a transformative shift from conventional crop improvement towards system-level engineering of next-generation crops, providing a strategic foundation for enhancing global food security, agricultural sustainability and environmental resilience in the face of accelerating climate change.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, programmable domestication represents a transformative shift from conventional crop improvement towards system‐level engineering of next‐generation crops, providing a strategic foundation for enhancing global food security, agricultural sustainability and environmental resilience in the face of accelerating climate change.
2. Rather than relying exclusively on naturally occurring variation and prolonged breeding cycles, programmable domestication seeks to identify adaptive genetic variation, establish robust genotype–phenotype relationships, precisely engineer target loci and accelerate the development of improved crop ideotypes.
3. Collectively, these advances have transformed de novo domestication from isolated proof‐of‐concept studies into a promising systems‐oriented strategy for developing climate‐resilient and sustainable crops.
4. Pangenomic analyses expand the accessible target space, multi‐omic approaches prioritise functional variants and genome editing enables their site‐specific modification.
5. Translating candidate SVs and haplotypes into actionable engineering targets therefore requires moving beyond statistical association towards functional evaluation.
6. Accordingly, candidate loci prioritised by pan‐genomes must be integrated with multi‐omic and experimental approaches to establish biological function and refine engineering targets, providing the essential link between genomic discovery and precision target selection in the subsequent phase of the domestication design cycle.
7. Consequently, programmable domestication depends on converting candidate genomic signals into validated engineering targets through an integrative functional validation pipeline that unifies genetic mapping, multi‐layer omics and direct experimental validation.
8. Thus, genetic mapping establishes the candidate genomic space, whereas multi‐omic integration provides the biological resolution required for precise target prioritisation.
