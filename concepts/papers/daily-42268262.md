# Comparative efficacy of foliar-applied biogenic copper and iron nanoparticles for mitigating salinity stress in chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.).

## 摘要
Plant synthesized iron oxide nanoparticles (FeO NPs) and copper nanoparticles (Cu NPs) were evaluated to promote chickpeas growth under salt stress. The characterization of the FeO NPs and Cu NPs was carried out by X-ray Diffraction (XRD), Fourier Transform Infrared Spectroscopy (FTIR) and Scanning Electron Microscopy (SEM) analysis. Foliar applications of FeO (100 mg/L) and Cu NPs (75 mg/L) significantly improved chlorophyll (47% & 40%), carotenoids (59% & 56%), and total soluble sugars (36% & 30%) compared to the control. Growth parameters increased 31% to 53% by 100 mg/L FeO and 27% to 53% by 75 mg/L Cu NPs compared to the control. Similarly, the antioxidant's superoxide dismutase, peroxidase, and ascorbate peroxidase were significantly increased, while malondialdehyde, hydrogen peroxide, and superoxide radical (O2-) were significantly decreased. Both NPs significantly reduced sodium (Na) concentration in roots (145% & 48%) and shoots (234% & 78%), while increasing potassium (70% & 51%) and calcium (71% & 47%) concentrations. Additionally, zinc and manganese uptake improved by 63% and 82% with FeO, and 56% and 76% with Cu NPs, respectively. The findings suggest that foliar application of biogenic FeO and Cu nanoparticles is dose-dependent with potential to reduce salinity stress in chickpea plants.

This study presents a novel comparative evaluation of biogenically synthesized copper and iron nanoparticles as foliar-applied nano-nutrient formulations for mitigating salinity stress in chickpea (Cicer arietinum L.), a crop highly sensitive to soil salinity. Unlike previous studies that primarily focus on chemically synthesized nanoparticles or single-nanoparticle treatments, this research integrates green synthesis using Conocarpus leaf extracts with a direct comparison of two functionally distinct micronutrient nanoparticles under identical saline conditions. The work uniquely demonstrates dose-dependent and differential physiological, biochemical, and ionic regulation responses induced by FeO and Cu nanoparticles, highlighting their distinct mechanisms in improving antioxidant defense, osmolyte accumulation, and ion homeostasis (Na+ exclusion and K+/Ca2+ enrichment). Furthermore, the study advances current knowledge by establishing foliar nano-application as an efficient delivery pathway for enhancing nutrient uptake and stress resilience while minimizing soil interactions.
