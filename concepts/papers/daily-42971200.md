# Reduced dosage of phytochrome F engages SELF-PRUNING6A to confer heat-resilient tuberization in potato.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [42971200](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42971200/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.cub.2026.10.012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2026.10.012)
- **第一作者**: Shenglin Jing
- **作者**: Shenglin Jing, Xiaoran Li, Peng Jiang, Huan Chen, Changyi Li, Jun Qin, Bo Zhu, Zixian Zeng

## 摘要
Natural variation at the earliness locus, or potato CDF1, enabled cultivated potato to overcome strict photoperiodic control of wild andigena landraces and tuberize under long days. Yet long-day conditions (LDs) are often associated with warm temperatures, which strongly suppress tuberization at night. With the rising ambient temperatures due to global warming, uncovering genetic factors that enable tuber formation under elevated temperatures has become a priority for breeding new cultivars with improved heat tolerance. Here we report that heat-resilient tuberization in potato can be unlocked by inactivating the photoreceptor phytochrome F (phyF) and optimized by exploiting its haploinsufficiency. Knockdown of phyF activates the SELF-PRUNING 6A (SP6A) tuberization-inducing signal and facilitates tuber formation under heat-stress conditions. Unexpectedly, loss of function of both SP6A and phyF still leads to early tuberization and heat escape, although it reduces the resilience of phyF mutants. Intriguingly, we show that haplotype-specific phyF editing in the 5' UTR or exonic regions leads to haploinsufficiency phenotypes enabling tuberization under heat-stress conditions, without other phytochrome-related penalties. Our results suggest that derepressing SP6A by a reduction of phyF dosage provides a key to unlock heat-resilient tuberization and highlight haploinsufficiency as a strategy for generating dominant alleles to enable rapid improvement of crops.
