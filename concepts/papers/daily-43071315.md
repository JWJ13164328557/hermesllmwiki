# Global m6A profiling reveals epitranscriptomic regulation of gene expression in rice roots under alkaline stress.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Stress biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43071315](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43071315/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s44154-026-00349-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44154-026-00349-7)
- **第一作者**: Zijing Fu
- **作者**: Zijing Fu, Kaixin Xue, Zhikai Zhu, Hongli Zhou, Jing Zhou, Tao Yan, Liuhui Kuang, Lin Li

## 摘要
Rice (Oryza sativa) is a crucial staple crop globally, but its productivity is often hindered by alkaline stress. N6-methyladenosine (m6A) modifications play a crucial regulatory role in plant adaptation to various abiotic stresses. However, the role of these post-transcriptional RNA modifications in rice's response to alkaline stress remains largely unexplored. In this study, m6A-seq and RNA-seq analyses were conducted to explore the molecular mechanisms underlying the alkaline stress response and adaptation. In this study, 1,118 newly gained m6A peaks and 1,328 differential m6A peaks (DPs) were identified in rice roots after alkaline treatment, and the associated genes were significantly enriched in membrane-related and oxidative stress response pathways. Meanwhile, 1,610 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified and showed similar enrichment patterns, suggesting that m6A methylation may facilitate alkaline stress adaptation by modulating these processes. Serval potassium transporters form the HAK family have been identified as DEGs or DPs, indicating that m6A modification may coordinate ion transporter expression to maintain homeostasis under alkaline conditions. Transcription factor analysis revealed 85 differentially expressed TFs, with six of them also exhibiting differential m6A modifications and eight showing newly gained m6A peaks, pointing to a potential epitranscriptional regulatory layer that modulates TF activity and downstream stress responses. Collectively, our findings provide novel insights into the role of m6A methylation in reprogramming gene expression and transcription factor regulation in rice roots under alkaline stress.
