# Acetophenone is a key receiver-active component of a Green leaf volatile-free, herbivory-induced volatile blend that primes Arabidopsis defense against Plutella xylostella.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant signaling & behavior (2026)
- **PMID**: [43056147](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056147/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/15592324.2026.2765989](https://doi.org/10.1080/15592324.2026.2765989)
- **第一作者**: Imran Haider
- **作者**: Imran Haider, Azizullah Khalili, Muhammad Usman Aslam, Salman Ahmed, Salman Haidar Abbasi, Muhammad Yousaf Nadeem, Muhammad Mubushar, Muhammad Usman Tahir

## 摘要
Herbivore-induced volatile blends can activate calcium signaling in neighboring plants without green leaf volatiles (GLVs), but the contributions of individual non-GLV compounds remain unresolved. We tested whether acetophenone contributes to receiver activation and subsequent resistance to Plutella xylostella in Arabidopsis thaliana. Six candidate compounds were quantified by gas chromatography-mass spectrometry in air reaching receivers from herbivore-damaged Columbia-0 plant emitters. Synthetic emitters then delivered individual compounds and reconstructed blends at matched concentrations, while receiver cytosolic Ca2⁺ was imaged with GCaMP3. Acetophenone and octanal elicited Ca2⁺ responses individually, whereas the four tested hydrocarbons did not. Acetophenone alone increased receiver Ca2⁺ area under the curve by 70.23 ΔF/F₀ · s relative to clean air. Removing acetophenone from the six-component blend reduced this response by 62.70 ΔF/F₀ · s, and reintroducing it restored activity to a level equivalent to the full blend within the prespecified margin. Following a 4 h VOC-free washout, full-blend pre-exposure reduced leaf consumption during a subsequent herbivore challenge by approximately 36% and also reduced larval mass gain. Acetophenone omission weakened these effects, and add-back restored them. Acetophenone is therefore a major receiver-active component of this GLV-free blend and contributes to a persistent receiver state associated with reduced herbivore performance. The activity of octanal and the blend lacking acetophenone indicates that additional components also contribute to receiver signaling.
