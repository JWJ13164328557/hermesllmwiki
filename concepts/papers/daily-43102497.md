# Pathogen‑Induced Accumulation of Cuticular Wax Mediates Resistance Against Curvularia Leaf Spot in Maize.

## 摘要
Curvularia leaf spot (CLS) is a widespread fungal disease of maize, however, the genetic and molecular mechanisms underlying CLS resistance remain unclear. In this study, through a CLS resistance evaluation of 334 maize inbred lines, we identified CIMBL77 and B73 as highly resistant and susceptible lines, respectively. Cytological and transcriptomic analyses revealed that in CIMBL77, fungal hyphae of Curvularia lunata were restricted to the epidermal cell layer and immune responses were activated at an earlier stage than in B73. Notably, the cutin, suberine, and wax biosynthesis pathway was significantly enriched during early fungal infection in CIMBL77 but not in B73. Scanning electron microscopy showed that Curvularia lunata inoculation triggered significantly greater numbers of larger epicuticular wax granules on CIMBL77 leaves relative to B73, and artificial wax removal markedly reduced resistance to CLS. Among the early-responsive cuticular wax biosynthetic genes, ZmWAX2 was functionally characterized. The loss-of-function Zmwax2 mutant exhibited enhanced susceptibility to CLS. Collectively, this study demonstrated that cuticular wax acts not only as a preformed physical barrier but also as an inducible defense component, and that pathogen-inducible wax biosynthetic genes are promising targets for improving CLS resistance.
