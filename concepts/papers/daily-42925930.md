# Acclimation of the photosynthetic apparatus of tomato hp mutants to light with different red to far-red light ratios.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2027)
- **PMID**: [42925930](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42925930/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113534](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113534)
- **第一作者**: Pavel Pashkovskiy
- **作者**: Pavel Pashkovskiy, Mikhail Vereshchagin, Anna Abramova, Aleksandr Ashikhmin, Maxim Bolshakov, Leonid Kulkov, Alexandra Khudyakova, Alexander Voronkov

## 摘要
The red/far-red light (RL/FRL) ratio influences phytochrome signalling and the balance of excitation between photosystems. This study compared photosynthetic acclimation in tomato hp-2 and hp-1 lines carrying mutations affecting DET1- and DDB1/CUL4-dependent light signalling, respectively, and examined whether elevated pigment content coincided with greater CO₂ assimilation under contrasting light regimes. The wild-type comparator LA2706, the hp-2 lines LA3005 and LA3006, and the hp-1 line LA3538 were grown at RL/FRL ratios of 2/1, 1/1, and 1/2 and under white fluorescent light. Net CO₂ assimilation rate (Pn), stomatal conductance (gs), PSII photochemical activity, pigment content, relative transcript levels, and chloroplast ultrastructure were evaluated. At RL/FRL 1/1, Pn in both hp-2 lines was nearly twice that in the wild type, although their molecular and chloroplast profiles differed. At RL/FRL 2/1, the two hp-2 lines showed their largest observed difference in gas exchange despite similarly high Y(II). At the shade-associated RL/FRL 1/2 ratio, the hp-1 line combined elevated pigment content and high PSII efficiency with low Pn. Relative transcript levels of PHYB1, PSY, psbD, CAB1, CAB6A, GLK2, and PSBS showed gene- and line-specific patterns within the light treatments. Elevated pigment content and high Y(II) did not consistently coincide with higher CO₂ assimilation. The non-isogenic comparison identifies line-specific acclimation patterns without separating mutation effects from cultivar-background effects.
