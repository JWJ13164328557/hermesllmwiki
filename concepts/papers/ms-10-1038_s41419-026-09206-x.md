### 核心发现
1. This study reveals a novel predictive biomarker and feasible target for preventing PAAs.
2. Overall, we demonstrated that targeting HCK in M1 macrophages alleviates postoperative abdominal adhesions by suppressing NF-κB/IL-6 axis-driven MMT, providing a new target and available strategy for the prevention of PAAs.

4. 2.3 HCK deficiency or inactivation suppressed M1 macrophage polarization in vitro To assess whether the upregulation and activation of HCK is required for the induction and maintenance of M1 polarization, BT424 and A419259 were used to inhibit HCK signaling in the RAW 264.7 cell line.
5. Notably, the outcomes of BT424 and A419259 treatment—which suppressed HCK and pHCK expression and attenuated M1 polarization—closely mirrored those achieved with HCK knockout.
6. To definitively determine whether HCK kinase activity is required for M1 polarization, we reconstituted HCK‑knockout macrophages with either wild-type HCK (HCK-WT) or kinase-dead HCK (HCK-K288E).
7. 2.6 Macrophage depletion and adoptive transfer confirmed the causal role of M1 macrophages in PAAs To directly determine whether M1 macrophages are required for PAA development, we performed abdominal macrophage depletion and reconstitution experiments in mice using clodronate liposomes.
8. These results further validate that HCK activity is required for M1‑CM‑driven MMT.
