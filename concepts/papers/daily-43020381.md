# Role of nitrogen utilization in photosynthetic compensation of maize under horizontal heterogeneous light.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43020381](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43020381/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113591](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113591)
- **第一作者**: Jie Liu
- **作者**: Jie Liu, Cuie Ma, Chang Liu, Xiangru Ma, Hui Yang, Junbo Du, Xin Sun

## 摘要
When plants are exposed to heterogeneous light conditions, photosynthesis in unshaded leaves is often stimulated to compensate for the decline in photosynthesis in shaded leaves, a phenomenon known as photosynthetic compensation. However, the underlying mechanism remains unclear. In this study, a maize cultivar with obvious photosynthetic compensation was selected and planted in the field in a wide-narrow row intercropping system. The photosynthetic performance and nitrogen (N) utilization were investigated to elucidate the physiological mechanism of photosynthetic compensation. The results indicated that compared with the equal-row monoculture, the nitrogen content and nitrate reductase (NR) activity in the leaves of the wide rows significantly increased, while those in the narrow rows significantly decreased. Further investigation showed that the investment of leaf N into photosynthetic apparatus (NP) increased significantly in wide rows, especially for the components of carbon assimilation (NC) and bioenergetics (NB). In contrast, the NP decreased significantly in narrow rows, although the components of light-harvesting (NL) increased. These results suggest that the photosynthetic compensation of wide-narrow-row planted maize is supported by a preferential N distribution, as well as stronger N assimilation, to leaves on wide-row side. In these leaves, N allocation for photosynthetic energy conversion and CO2 assimilation is enhanced, further facilitating an improved photosynthesis to compensate for the impaired photosynthesis of leaves on narrow-row side, which is caused by reduced photosynthetic N.
