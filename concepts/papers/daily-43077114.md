# Grafting monoclonal antibody expressing transgenic tobacco onto tomato rootstocks reduces alkaloid load and benefits downstream protein A affinity chromatography.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Planta (2026)
- **PMID**: [43077114](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077114/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00425-026-05226-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-026-05226-1)
- **第一作者**: Mathew Paul
- **作者**: Mathew Paul, Tuulikki Seppänen-Laakso, Anneli Ritala, Julian K-C Ma

## 摘要
Endogenous secondary metabolite levels in tobacco plants can be significantly reduced by grafting onto non-tobacco rootstock. This is a convenient way to simplify downstream processing for plant molecular farming applications. Plants of the Nicotiana genus are commonly used for the production of recombinant products, including proteins and small molecules. The presence of potentially toxic natural alkaloids, the most important being nicotine, presents a challenge however, for downstream processing and manufacturability. Here, we combine current "molecular farming" technology for monoclonal antibody expression with the traditional practice of grafting and demonstrate that transgenic N. tabacum plants can be grown on Solanum lycopersicum root stocks, to significantly reduce alkaloid levels in leaf tissues. Nicotine levels in extracts from leaves from grafted plants were 3-43 mg/kg compared to 396-483 mg/kg in non-grafted plants. Similar results were observed for nornicotine, anatabine, anabasine, and pseudoxynicotine. The grafting process did not affect the quality or quantity of the expressed recombinant monoclonal antibody. In a model system for Protein A affinity purification of the monoclonal antibody, the binding capacity of Protein A was shown to decline to 70% under repeated cycles of use and regeneration using extracts from plants with normal alkaloid content, but the binding capacity was unchanged using extracts from grafted plants with reduced nicotinoids, under the same conditions.
