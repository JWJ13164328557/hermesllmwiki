# A Pair of Copper Radical Oxidase and Peroxidase Secreted at the Appressorium Pore Coordinates Timely Plant Penetration by the Rice Blast Fungus.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Molecular plant-microbe interactions : MPMI (2026)
- **PMID**: [43049674](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049674/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/MPMI-10-26-0113-R](https://doi.org/10.1094/MPMI-10-26-0113-R)
- **第一作者**: Sayo Kodama
- **作者**: Sayo Kodama, Takumi Nishiuchi, Bastien Bissaro, Jean-Guy Berrin, Yasuyuki Kubo

## 摘要
Appressorium-mediated host invasion is a key step in infections caused by plant pathogenic fungi that form melanized appressoria. We previously identified a conserved oxidase pair composed of a copper radical alcohol oxidase (AlcOx) and a peroxidase (Perox). In the cucumber anthracnose fungus Colletotrichum orbiculare, the Perox-AlcOx pair functions specifically at the onset of penetration peg formation and localizes to the pore formed at the fungus-plant interface. Oxidation of long-chain primary alcohols in the plant cuticle by the Perox-AlcOx pair generates aldehydes, which were proposed to trigger a cascade leading to appressorium-mediated penetration. Beyond its conservation in most Colletotrichum species, genes encoding the Perox-AlcOx pair are also present in Magnaporthe species. Here, using the rice blast fungus Magnaporthe oryzae (syn. Pyricularia oryzae) as a representative model, we analyzed the contribution of the Perox-AlcOx pair to appressorium-mediated penetration and lesion development. Gene disruption analyses showed that both enzymes are required for efficient plant infection, as mutants showed delayed host penetration despite forming normal appressoria. Fluorescence microscopy revealed that the Perox-AlcOx pair accumulates at the appressorial penetration pore, a highly specialized pathogen-host interface. Transcriptome analyses indicated that Perox-AlcOx-dependent oxidative reactions provide chemical signals required for host invasion by inducing gene expression changes associated with penetration peg formation. Together, these findings suggest that the conserved tandem oxidase pair contributes to the spatial and biochemical control of the appressorial invasion site and constitutes a lineage-specific module supporting efficient host penetration by high-turgor appressorium-forming fungi.
