# Translating pathogen biology into diagnostic and management strategies for gummy stem blight in cucumber production.

## 摘要
Gummy stem blight (GSB) is a major disease of cucurbits caused by Stagonosporopsis spp. and is increasingly problematic in intensive cucumber production systems, particularly where warm, humid environments favour infection, sporulation and rapid disease spread. GSB can infect vegetative and reproductive tissues and remain latent in fruit for several days before visible symptoms develop, creating a distinctive commercial challenge: disease may be difficult to detect before harvest yet cause significant post-harvest losses, reducing grower and retailer confidence. Compounding these problems, current diagnostic tools for detecting pre-symptomatic infection are often insufficiently validated for species specificity, sensitivity, turnaround time, cost, crude-sample compatibility and practical use by production staff. Many of these limitations reflect the relative lack of genomic resources for Stagonosporopsis. Therefore, this review integrates evidence from other pathosystems with current knowledge of Stagonosporopsis taxonomy, infection biology, epidemiology, diagnostics, fungicide resistance, effector biology and host resistance to identify pathways towards grower-ready tools. Specifically, we focus on how recent advances in genomics, paired with previously designed detection systems such as loop-mediated isothermal amplification (LAMP) and clustered regularly interspaced short palindromic repeats/CRISPR-associated proteins (CRISPR/Cas), could be adapted for species-specific detection of the causal pathogen and, simultaneously, provide a regional profile of fungicide resistance. In parallel, we explore how a deeper functional genomic understanding of the disease, particularly of effector proteins expressed during the early stages of infection, could provide valuable insights into the dynamics of this host-pathogen interaction and support strategies to disrupt these interactions through novel fungistatic compounds and the development of disease-resistant cultivars. Together, these approaches could support a staged technology-development and adoption roadmap for more precise, rapid and sustainable GSB management in cucumber production.
