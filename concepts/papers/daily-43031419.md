# Morphological, Molecular and Pathogenicity Evidence Identifies Verticillium dahliae as the Primary Causal Agent of Potato Early Dying in Colorado.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43031419](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43031419/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-08-26-1592-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-08-26-1592-RE)
- **第一作者**: Hafiz Muhammad Usman Aslam
- **作者**: Hafiz Muhammad Usman Aslam, Jeremy Daniel, Mohamad Chikh-Ali

## 摘要
Potato early dying (PED) is a soilborne disease complex that causes premature vine senescence and substantial yield losses in potato production worldwide. Although PED has long been associated with pathogen complexes, the relative contribution of individual pathogens and pests remains unclear in many regions and may vary across production systems. PED is a major disease affecting potato production in the San Luis Valley (SLV) of Colorado however, limited information is available on its causal agents, hindering effective disease management. This study investigated the causal agents of PED in the SLV using a combination of field diagnostics, morphological and molecular identification and greenhouse pathogenicity assays. Surveys of 18 potato fields over three growing seasons consistently identified Verticillium dahliae as the major pathogen, detected in 100% of PED-affected samples. Colletotrichum coccodes, the causal agent of black dot, was also frequently detected in symptomatic samples. Greenhouse bioassay demonstrated that V. dahliae alone was sufficient to reproduce the full PED syndrome, causing rapid disease onset, higher disease incidence and severity. In contrast, C. coccodes caused minimal disease when inoculated alone and did not significantly enhance disease development in the presence of V. dahliae. These findings establish V. dahliae as the primary causal agent of the PED in the SLV, Colorado, and emphasize the importance of controlling V. dahliae to effectively manage PED.
