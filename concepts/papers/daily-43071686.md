# Fungal secreted leucine-rich repeat proteins suppress plant immunity to enhance colonisation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: PLoS pathogens (2026)
- **PMID**: [43071686](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43071686/)
- **DOI**: [10.1371/journal.ppat.1014823](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.ppat.1014823)
- **第一作者**: Haider Ali
- **作者**: Haider Ali, Elisha Thynne, Toby Ellis, Catherine Booth, Ruth Bates, Matthew S Hope, Emma J Wallington, Emma E Crean

## 摘要
Plant pathogens secrete proteins (effectors) to alter the molecular environment of their host, manipulating general physiology and defence processes to enhance virulence. Here we describe a previously uncharacterised group of plant-like, secreted, leucine-rich repeat (sLRR) proteins produced by fungal pathogens, which contribute to this process. Zymoseptoria tritici is the fungus responsible for the wheat disease, septoria tritici blotch (STB). Using a sLRR protein from Z. tritici (ZtLRR) as an exemplar, we transiently expressed this effector in Nicotiana benthamiana and found that it was a potent inhibitor of plant immune responses. ZtLRR inhibited reactive oxygen species (ROS) burst responses after treatment with the pathogen-derived immune elicitors flg22, laminarin, and chitin, and also suppressed cell death triggered by the receptor-recognised Z. tritici effector, Zt12. An in silico structural analysis of ZtLRR, whose structure was predicted with strong statistical support, suggested high levels of similarity to plant LRRs contained within cell-surface immune receptors. To test ZtLRR function in wheat, we generated ZtLRR-expressing transgenic lines and found that these plants had compromised immune responses and were hyper-susceptible to a compatible Z. tritici isolate. Additional plant-like and bacterial-like fungal sLRRs were identified in a diverse range of fungal genomes, which all shared high levels of conserved structural similarity despite low primary sequence similarity. A panel of these fungal sLRRs was screened for immune-suppressive activity, and the majority of the plant-like, fungal sLRR suppressed plant immune responses in a manner similar to ZtLRR. This work indicates that deployment of sLRR effectors may be a shared strategy by diverse fungi to suppress or modify plant immune signalling.
