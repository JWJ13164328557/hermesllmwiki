# Optimisation of the microwave-assisted extraction process and comparative phytochemical, nutritional and antimicrobial profiling of Allium stracheyi (Baker) across an altitudinal gradient.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: RSC advances (2026)
- **PMID**: [43022178](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43022178/)
- **DOI**: [10.1039/d6ra08139h](https://doi.org/10.1039/d6ra08139h)
- **第一作者**: Pooja Singh
- **作者**: Pooja Singh, Prabhakar Semwal, Prateek Gururani, Pooja Bhatnagar, Shabaaz Begum Jameel Pasha, Ashish Thapliyal

## 摘要
Allium stracheyi is an important ethnomedicinal plant species in the genus Allium, commonly used as a vegetable and spice in the Himalayan region. This study optimised microwave-assisted extraction (MAE) of A. stracheyi leaves using a Box-Behnken Design (BBD) based on Response Surface Methodology (RSM). The extraction conditions were optimised for aqueous and ethanolic solvents to maximise yield, Total Phenolic Content (TPC), and Total Flavonoid Content (TFC). A comparative analysis of phytochemical activities, antimicrobial activities, and nutritional profiling (elements, amino acids, and phenolic acids) was conducted on optimised A. stracheyi samples (collected from four different altitudes). We conducted elemental analysis using AAS, tentatively profiled bioactive compounds by GC-MS, and identified amino acids and phenolic compounds using U-HPLC. The optimised MAE yielded the following conditions for aqueous (power: 480 W; time: 4.05; solid-to-solvent ratio: 18.43) and ethanolic (power: 480 W; time: 4.08; solid-to-solvent ratio: 15.51) solvents. Comparative analysis showed that samples from high-altitude sites had higher biological and phytochemical activities than those from low-altitude sites. High-altitude samples showed higher levels of minerals, amino acids, and phenolic acids than low-altitude samples. Results revealed a strong correlation between altitude and the accumulation of nutritional and bioactive compounds, driven by climatic factors at higher altitudes, which explains the higher biological activity of high-altitude sites than low-altitude sites.
