### 核心发现
1. Taken together, these findings suggest that while healthcare access appears related to the magnitude of the insurance effect, access alone is unlikely to be the sole driver of insur- ance-related differences in epigenetic age sensitivity to lead exposure.
2. Taken together, these results suggest that socioeconomic factors and pat- terns of healthcare engagement may influence expo- sure profiles across different chemical classes.
3. In earlier work, we found that having health insurance was associated with weaker relationships between blood lead levels and greater epigenetic aging in two DNA methyla- tion–based aging biomarkers, GrimAge2 and Duned- inPoAm [13].
4. These findings suggest that healthcare access may partially contribute to the attenuation of lead-related epigenetic aging observed among insured adults and a need for further research to better under- stand these complex relationships.
5. Taken together, these biomarker findings sup- ported the possibility that health insurance may offer some buffering against the biological impacts of lead exposure.
6. Rather, we believe that these results suggest that healthcare access may partially contribute to the previously observed buffer- ing effect of insurance on lead-associated epigenetic aging.
7. These relationships may be useful to future initiatives that consider leveraging health systems to target specific environmental exposures.
8. In summary, our findings indicate that healthcare access may contribute to but does not fully explain the attenuated associations between lead exposure and epigenetic aging observed among insured partici- pants.
