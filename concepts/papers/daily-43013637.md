# The ZmJAZ14-ZmMYC2 module regulates anthesis-silking synchrony in maize.

## 摘要
The evolution of traits can increase yield potential but may create physiological constraints. Jasmonic acid (JA) signaling regulates sex determination in male (tassel) and female (ear) inflorescences in maize. Although the formation of ears laid the foundation for high yields, the asynchronous development of the tassel and the ear prolongs the anthesis-silking interval (ASI), threatening reproductive success and reducing yield. Whether JA signaling coordinates ear and tassel development remains unclear. Here, we show that the core JA signaling module, ZmJAZ14-ZmMYC2a/b, orchestrates the expression of SPL29 for reproductive transition, BA1 for female inflorescence initiation, and EXPA5 for silk elongation. Coordination of these physiological processes underpins synchronous growth of the tassel and ears, whereas disruption of ZmMYC2a/b or accumulation of the repressor ZmJAZ14 prolongs ASI. Our work suggests that the repurposing of JA signaling to coordinate ear and tassel development may represent a lineage-specific evolutionary burden in maize. Deciphering the link between gains and unforeseen costs of evolution could further improve yields.
