# Codon Optimization Enables Recombinant Expression of Soybean GmPRR7-1 in Escherichia coli: A Rosetta(DE3) Fed-Batch Production and Purification Workflow.

- **PMID**: [43025820](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025820/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.pep.2026.107035](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pep.2026.107035)

- **作者**: Daewoong Lee, Eunjin Seo, Hyeok-Won Lee, Tae-Hwan Jun, Ju Seok Lee

## 摘要
Pseudo-Response Regulators (PRRs) are circadian clock components that regulate plant development, flowering and abiotic stress responses. Functional studies of plant PRRs are slowed by the time required for transformation and phenotypic evaluation, so purified recombinant protein offers a practical route to in vitro interaction analyses. Heterologous production of plant PRRs in Escherichia coli is nevertheless challenging because of codon-usage differences and their aggregation-prone multidomain architecture. Here we established an E. coli expression and purification workflow for functional soybean GmPRR7-1. The native GmPRR7-1 coding sequence in pET-28a(+) and a codon-optimized construct in pBT7-C-HIS were screened in BL21(DE3) and Rosetta(DE3) at 16, 25 and 37°C. The native construct was not detectably expressed in either host under any condition, whereas the codon-optimized construct yielded a target-sized band, identifying codon optimization as a prerequisite for expression of this protein. The target band remained detectable in Rosetta(DE3) at 25°C; because these whole-cell lysates were not fractionated, this screening identifies a productive construct, host and induction combination rather than ranking the two hosts. Rosetta(DE3) carrying the optimized sequence was cultivated in a 5-L fed-batch bioreactor, and soluble GmPRR7-1 was recovered by single-step Ni-NTA affinity chromatography. Eluted protein corresponded to 0.83% of the soluble-fraction protein, comparable to small-scale cultures induced at 25°C (0.77%); these values describe total eluted protein rather than target-specific yield. Anti-His immunoblotting confirmed the target and detected minor His-reactive degradation products. Purified GmPRR7-1 retained specific interactions with MBP-tagged GmFDL19 and three GmLUX homologs, demonstrating a workflow that reproducibly delivers interaction-competent GmPRR7-1.
