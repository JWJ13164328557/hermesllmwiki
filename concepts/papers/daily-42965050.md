# The WRKY transcription factor IbWRKY24 regulates flavonoid biosynthesis in sweetpotato (Ipomoea batatas [L.] Lam.) blades.

- **作者**: Jilong Gao, Ruihua Qin, Qina Lv, Peitao Chen, Chunli Wu, Rong Huang, Yulong Liu, Lina Jiang

## 摘要
Sweetpotato (Ipomoea batatas [L.] Lam.) vines exhibit strong regenerative capacity, and their blades are rich in flavonoids, making them a promising target for nutritional biofortification. Here, we quantitatively assessed flavonoid contents and antioxidant capacities in blades from 90 genetically diverse sweetpotato genotypes. Integrated metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses identified IbWRKY24 as a key transcriptional regulator of flavonoid biosynthesis in sweetpotato blades. Overexpression of IbWRKY24 in stable transgenic sweetpotato lines significantly increased blade flavonoid accumulation (1.22-2.34-fold) and antioxidant activity (1.29-2.16-fold), whereas gene silencing caused marked reductions in both traits. Mechanistically, yeast one-hybrid, dual-luciferase, and electrophoretic mobility shift assays demonstrated that IbWRKY24 directly binds specific W-box elements in the promoters of core flavonoid biosynthetic genes, including IbCHS, IbCHI, and IbFLS, thereby activating their transcription. Moreover, developmental profiling revealed a strong concordance between IbWRKY24 transcript abundance and flavonoid accumulation in blades (r = 0.806，P < 0.0001). Together, these results establish IbWRKY24 as a positive regulator of flavonoid biosynthesis and provide insights into transcriptional control of specialized metabolism in sweetpotato. This work offers a molecular target for flavonoid biofortification and high-value utilization of sweetpotato blade resources.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, these findings suggest that IbWRKY24 is a promising molecular target for improving flavonoid accumulation in sweetpotato blades, although its regulatory contribution may vary across different genetic backgrounds.
2. By integrating large-scale germplasm evaluation, multi-omics analyses, and functional validation, we show that blades are a major site of flavonoid accumulation and identify a nuclear-localized transcription factor, IbWRKY24, as a key regulator of this process.
3. Overexpression of IbWRKY24 in stable transgenic sweetpotato lines significantly increased blade flavonoid accumulation (1.22–2.34-fold) and antioxidant activity (1.29–2.16-fold), whereas gene silencing caused marked reductions in both traits.
4. This work offers a molecular target for flavonoid biofortification and high-value utilization of sweetpotato blade resources.
5. Collectively, these observations highlight the potential of sweetpotato vine tissues as a sustainable source of natural antioxidants and as novel functional ingredients for nutritional enhancement.
6. Together, these findings reveal an important role for WRKY transcription factors in controlling flavonoid biosynthesis in sweetpotato blades and provide a molecular basis for developing high-flavonoid sweetpotato cultivars through biofortification-oriented molecular breeding.
7. These results suggest that the 236 overlapping genes are closely associated with flavonoid accumulation.
8. Collectively, gain- and loss-of-function analyses establish IbWRKY24 as a key regulator of flavonoid biosynthesis, contributing to differential flavonoid accumulation in sweetpotato blades.
