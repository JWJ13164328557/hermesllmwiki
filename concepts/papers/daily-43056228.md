# Cool nights attenuate morning OsRLCK309 expression and compromise blast resistance in rice.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: The Plant cell (2026)
- **PMID**: [43056228](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056228/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plcell/koag359](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koag359)
- **第一作者**: Di Fang
- **作者**: Di Fang, Yisong Chen, Gang Li, Ao Zhang, Siyu Chen, Muhammad Fahad, Yufeng Zhang, Yongxiang Chen

## 摘要
Nighttime cooling during day-night thermocycles has long been associated with increased rice blast severity, yet the host mechanisms remain unclear. Here, we show that cool-night pre-exposure weakens subsequent blast resistance in rice under a common post-inoculation regime and is associated with reduced morning expression of a receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase (RLCK)-enriched gene set. We identify OsRLCK309, a cool-night-responsive receptor-like cytoplasmic kinase, as a positive regulator of blast resistance and pattern-triggered immunity (PTI), including chitin-induced reactive oxygen species production, mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) activation, and Ca2+ influx. OsRLCK309 associates with the chitin co-receptor kinase CHITIN ELICITOR RECEPTOR KINASE 1 (OsCERK1), and OsCERK1-mediated phosphorylation of OsRLCK309 at Ser245 and Thr246 contributes to full activation of defense against rice blast. Under warm nights, CIRCADIAN CLOCK ASSOCIATED 1 (OsCCA1) promotes a morning-phased expression peak of OsRLCK309. Cool nights attenuate this OsCCA1-associated morning peak and induce dehydration-responsive element-binding protein 1A (OsDREB1A), which activates the OsRLCK309 promoter and contributes to its nighttime induction under cool-night conditions. In transgenic complementation lines, mutation of the two OsDREB1A-binding sites in the OsRLCK309 promoter attenuated cool-night-responsive transcriptional redistribution and was associated with reduced disease severity after cool-night pre-exposure, with minimal detectable agronomic trade-offs under our conditions. These results support a thermocycle-sensitive model linking night-temperature history to transcriptional timing, receptor-proximal immune signaling, and blast disease in rice, and suggest that modification of the DREB-binding motifs in the OsRLCK309 promoter may help maintain blast resistance under variable night temperatures.
