# Single-nucleus RNA and ATAC sequencing reveals the impact of chromatin accessibility on gene expression in Arabidopsis roots

---

## 1. Scientific Context

### Existing Consensus (Before This Paper)

By early 2021, plant single-cell transcriptomics had advanced rapidly from the landmark 2019 studies. The Arabidopsis root remained the premier model for single-cell atlases, but all published studies shared a critical methodological limitation — **protoplasting**:

- **Zhang et al. 2019** (Mol Plant) — 10x scRNA-seq, 7,695 cells, 24 clusters, ion assimilation + hormone atlas
- **Denyer et al. 2019** (Dev Cell) — 10x scRNA-seq, trajectory-focused, transcription factor networks
- **Shulse et al. 2019** (Cell Rep) — Drop-seq, >12,000 cells, sucrose perturbation, endodermis trajectory
- **Ryu et al. 2019** (Plant Physiol) — Drop-seq, root-hair focused
- **Jean-Baptiste et al. 2019** (BioRxiv) — 10x scRNA-seq, Monocle 3 trajectories, heat stress

All of these studies required enzymatic digestion of the plant cell wall (cellulase, pectolyase, macerozyme) to release single protoplasts — a process known to cause significant problems:

- **Transcriptional artifacts**: Protoplasting induces ~1,500+ stress-responsive genes within 30 minutes, confounding cell-type identity with wound response
- **Cell-type bias**: Some cell types (e.g., mature vasculature, stele cells with thick secondary walls) are resistant to protoplasting and systematically underrepresented or absent
- **Tissue disruption**: Cell wall removal destroys spatial context and cell-cell communication cues
- **Enzymatic variability**: Batch effects introduced by variation in digestion efficiency across samples and labs

Meanwhile, in mammalian genomics, **single-nucleus approaches** (snRNA-seq, snATAC-seq) had become well-established alternatives that avoid enzymatic dissociation artifacts entirely — using nuclei isolated by mechanical homogenization rather than live-cell protoplasting. No one had yet applied single-nucleus methods to plants, nor combined snRNA-seq with snATAC-seq in any plant system.

### Existing Models

- **Protoplast-dependent scRNA-seq paradigm**: All plant single-cell transcriptomic atlases to date relied on protoplast isolation, implicitly assuming this captured a representative sample of cell types
- **Transcriptome-chromatin disconnect**: Plant scRNA-seq atlases described gene expression landscapes but had no paired chromatin accessibility data to explain the regulatory basis of cell-type-specific expression
- **Single-modality assumption**: No plant study had generated paired single-cell (or single-nucleus) multi-omics data, leaving the relationship between chromatin state and transcription unexplored at individual-cell resolution

### Knowledge Gaps

- No single-nucleus RNA-seq protocol had been demonstrated in plants — was nuclear transcriptome representative of cellular transcriptome?
- No plant snATAC-seq data existed; chromatin accessibility landscapes at cell-type resolution were completely unknown
- Unknown whether protoplasting systematically misses cell types that nuclei-based methods could capture
- The regulatory logic linking cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility to gene expression was entirely uncharacterized in plants
- No methodology existed for multi-omics integration at the single-cell level in plant systems

### Why This Paper Matters

This was the **first plant study to apply both snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq**, and the **first to combine them into an integrated multi-omics atlas**. By using nuclei instead of protoplasts, it circumvented the protoplasting stress artifact entirely and provided evidence that nuclei-based approaches capture cell types missed by protoplast methods. The paired RNA + ATAC design enabled, for the first time in plants, a direct demonstration that cell-type-specific marker gene expression correlates with cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility — establishing a mechanistic framework for gene regulation at the single-cell level in Arabidopsis roots. This paper opened the door for plant single-cell multi-omics and set a new methodological standard that avoids the confounds of enzymatic cell wall digestion.

## 2. Research Questions

### Primary Question

Can single-nucleus RNA-seq (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus ATAC-seq (snATAC-seq) be combined to generate an integrated multi-omics atlas of the Arabidopsis root, and does chromatin accessibility at the single-nucleus level explain cell-type-specific gene expression?

### Secondary Questions

1. Can snRNA-seq on nuclei recapitulate and validate protoplast-based scRNA-seq cell-type transcriptomes?
2. Does snRNA-seq uncover additional cell subtypes not identified by protoplast-based scRNA-seq?
3. Can snATAC-seq identify cell-type-specific chromatin accessibility landscapes in Arabidopsis roots?
4. Does the integration of snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data demonstrate a mechanistic link between chromatin accessibility and marker gene expression at the cell-type level?
5. Is differential chromatin accessibility a critical regulatory mechanism for cell-type-specific gene activity in plants?

### Explicit Hypotheses

- Nuclei isolated from Arabidopsis root tissue by mechanical homogenization will yield transcriptomes comparable to those from protoplast-based methods, validating nuclei as a biological proxy for plant single-cell transcriptomics
- Nuclei-based methods will capture cell types and subtypes that are systematically underrepresented or absent in protoplast-based scRNA-seq datasets
- snATAC-seq will reveal cell-type-specific patterns of chromatin accessibility that distinguish Arabidopsis root cell types
- Integration of snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq will show that marker gene expression is associated with open chromatin at cognate genomic loci in a cell-type-specific manner
- Differential chromatin accessibility is a fundamental mechanism regulating cell-type-level gene activity

### Implicit Hypotheses

- Protoplasting-induced transcriptional artifacts contaminate existing scRNA-seq reference atlases; nuclei approaches provide a less biased view
- Chromatin accessibility landscapes are as cell-type-specific as transcriptomic landscapes
- Multi-omics integration at single-cell resolution can reveal regulatory logic (enhancer-gene pairs) previously invisible in bulk assays
- The Arabidopsis root cell-type transcriptome is substantially shaped by chromatin state, not solely by transcription factor binding on constitutively accessible chromatin

### 核心发现
1. As a necessary step in gaining a deeper understanding of the regulatory mechanisms controlling plant gene expression, we report the use of single-nucleus RNA sequencing (sNucRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase accessible chromatin sequencing (sNucATAC- seq) technologies on Arabidopsis roots.
2. Our data suggest that the differential chromatin accessibility is a critical mechanism to regulate gene activity at the cell-type level.
3. Our data reveal high similarities between the nuclear and the protoplast transcriptomes and the discovery of additional root cell-type transcriptomes that support the use of isolated nuclei as valuable biological entities to access single-cell gene expression.
4. Supporting the signiﬁcance of our sNucATAC-seq data, we found a high correlation between bulk ATAC-seq from intact whole Ara- bidopsis root tips (Maher et al., 2018) and our pseudo-bulked sNucATAC-seq dataset (i.e., SRCC = 0.95).
5. These results suggest that the sNucRNA-seq transcriptome of the Arabidopsis root is as highly correlated to a whole root transcriptome as protoplast-based transcriptomes.
6. Taken together, these results revealed that 73.7% of the expressed Arabidopsis protein-coding genes were detected by combining sNucRNA-seq and scRNA-seq technologies (Supplemental Figure 4).
7. These results show that the cellular and nuclear transcriptomes provide similar transcriptomic information and suggest that isolated plant nuclei can be utilized to establish meaningful transcriptomic information at the single-cell-type level.
8. These results suggest that the nuclear transcriptome is sufﬁcient to reveal the tissue heterogeneity of the Arabidopsis root.
