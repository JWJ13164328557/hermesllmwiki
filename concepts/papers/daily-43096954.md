# Brillouin microscopy reveals expansin-dependent changes in cell wall biomechanics in Physcomitrium patens.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of microscopy (2026)
- **PMID**: [43096954](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43096954/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/jmi.70213](https://doi.org/10.1111/jmi.70213)
- **第一作者**: Vit Rezac
- **作者**: Vit Rezac, Simon Vrana, Si Thanh Nguyen, Ooi-Kock Teh, Marketa Samalova

## 摘要
Expansins are cell wall (CW)-localised proteins that facilitate CW remodelling and play important roles in plant growth, yet their contribution to CW biomechanics in early-diverging land plants remains poorly understood. Here, we applied Brillouin light scattering (BLS) microscopy to investigate expansin-dependent changes in protonemal CWs of the moss Physcomitrium patens. BLS microscopy was optimised for spatially resolved measurements of living protonemal cells, with transverse CWs between apical and first subapical cells providing a reproducible region for quantitative analysis. We examined the effects of altered expansin abundance using an expa1,6 double-knockout and a β-estradiol-inducible EXPA6 overexpression line. Simultaneous disruption of EXPA1 and EXPA6 significantly reduced protonemal colony growth and CW thickness and resulted in lower Brillouin frequency shift and linewidth, together with an increased Brillouin loss tangent in transverse CWs. Surprisingly, inducible EXPA6 overexpression produced a similar growth phenotype and Brillouin response, although CW thickness remained unchanged. Thus, perturbation of expansin abundance in either direction affects protonemal growth and alters the biomechanical properties of the CW, suggesting that normal growth requires tightly regulated expansin activity rather than a simple linear relationship between expansin abundance and cell expansion. Our results demonstrate that BLS microscopy provides a sensitive, non-contact approach for detecting expansin-associated changes in living moss CWs and establish P. patens as a useful system for combining genetic manipulation with Brillouin microscopy to investigate plant CW biomechanics. Lay Description Plant cells are surrounded by a strong but flexible cell wall that provides structural support while still allowing cells to grow. Proteins called expansins help modify the cell wall during growth, but little is known about how they influence the physical properties of cell walls in mosses. In this study, we investigated two expansins in the moss Physcomitrium patens using Brillouin microscopy, a non-invasive technique that uses light to detect changes in the mechanical properties of biological materials. We first adapted the technique to examine the very small cell walls of living moss filaments. We then compared plants in which two expansin genes were deleted, with plants in which the production of one expansin was experimentally increased. Surprisingly, both genetic changes reduced moss growth and produced similar changes in the Brillouin signal from the cell walls. These findings suggest that successful growth requires a carefully controlled level of expansin activity: both too little and too much may disturb normal cell wall function. Our study also demonstrates that Brillouin microscopy can be used to investigate changes in the cell walls of living moss cells and provides a new approach for studying how cell wall proteins contribute to plant growth.
