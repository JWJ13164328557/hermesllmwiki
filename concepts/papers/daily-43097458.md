# TOC1 and ELF3 are associated with distinct transcriptional responses to ER stress in Arabidopsis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant signaling & behavior (2026)
- **PMID**: [43097458](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097458/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/15592324.2026.2769642](https://doi.org/10.1080/15592324.2026.2769642)
- **第一作者**: Song Yi Jeong
- **作者**: Song Yi Jeong, Woe-Yeon Kim, Gyeongik Ahn

## 摘要
The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) stress response is essential for maintaining protein homeostasis and cellular viability under adverse environmental conditions. Although the circadian clock coordinates physiological responses to environmental cues, the contributions of individual clock components to ER stress responses remain poorly understood. Here, we investigated the involvement of circadian clock genes in tunicamycin (Tm)-induced ER stress responses. Under chronic ER stress, toc1-101 exhibited increased sensitivity, whereas elf3-1 showed relative tolerance. To further characterize their early transcriptional responses, we examined gene expression following acute Tm treatment ZT16, corresponding to the end of the light period. Compared with toc1-101, elf3-1 exhibited broader induction of ER chaperone genes, with increased expression of BIP3, ERdj3b, CNX1, and PDIL, whereas toc1-101 showed induction primarily of BIP3 and ERdj3b. The mutants also differed in the expression of unfolded protein response (UPR)-associated genes: toc1-101 exhibited increased expression of BZIP60S, BZIP60U, and BZIP28, while elf3-1 showed reduced BZIP60S induction without significant changes in the other UPR-associated genes examined. Furthermore, toc1-101 showed increased expression of cell death-associated genes, including NRP1, MC8, γVPE1, and OXI1. In contrast, elf3-1 reduced expression of these genes. Together, these findings reveal that TOC1 and ELF3 contribute to ER stress outcomes by modulating the balance between adaptive proteostasis and cell death-associated responses.
