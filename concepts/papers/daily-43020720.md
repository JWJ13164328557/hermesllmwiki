# Resistance fuels biological invasions while offering new avenues for RNAi-based pest management.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Trends in biotechnology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43020720](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43020720/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.tibtech.2026.10.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tibtech.2026.10.014)
- **第一作者**: Chao He
- **作者**: Chao He, Jinjin Liang, Rong Zhang, Pei Cao, Huixin Zheng, Jing Yang, Yao Ji, Chengjia Zhang

## 摘要
Global trade and climate change have accelerated the spread of invasive agricultural pests while intensifying insecticide resistance. In the sweet potato whitefly, Bemisia tabaci, 2 decades of nationwide monitoring efforts revealed rapid displacement of one population, B/MEAM1, by another, Q/MED, illustrating an 'invasion treadmill' driven by competitive asymmetry between invasive lineages. In this research article, we identify the cytochrome P450 CYP6CX3 as a key contributor to imidacloprid resistance and exploit it as a molecular target for mechanism-informed spray-induced gene silencing. Functional analyses demonstrated that CYP6CX3 overexpression and allelic variation contribute to resistance, while semi-enclosed greenhouse trials showed that topical double-stranded RNA delivery enhanced insecticide sensitivity and mitigated host damage from both herbivory and sooty mold. These findings connect invasion dynamics with resistance evolution and establish detoxification genes as practical RNAi targets for sustainable pest management and integrated pest management programs by reducing chemical inputs while maintaining or restoring the efficacy of conventional insecticides.
