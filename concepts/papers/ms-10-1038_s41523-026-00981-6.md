### 核心发现
1. Notably, we found that apocrine carcinoma in ER-positive breast cancer was associated with worse clinical outcomes compared with non-apocrine tumors.
2. Our results show that apocrine carcinoma is associated with better survival in ER-negative, whereas with worse survival in ER-positive breast cancer.
3. Although ER-negativity was long considered one of the diagnostic criteria for carcinoma with apocrine differentiation, we found that clinical and biological relevance of apocrine morphology are opposite by the ER status.
4. In conclusion, apocrine morphology represents an ER-dependent biological state with distinct prognostic and molecular implications.
5. Collectively, these findings indicate that apocrine morphology is associated with distinct, ER- dependent mutational patterns—characterized by increased genomic instability in ER-positive disease and a context-dependent relationship with PIK3CA mutations in ER-negative versus ER-positive breast cancer.
6. Discussion ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS This study identifies apocrine morphology as a unique aspect of breast cancer biology that is not fully captured by the AR expression-based molecular apocrine subtype.
7. Together, these findings suggest that apocrine morphology may serve as an ER-dependent indicator with potential utility for prognostic stratification and therapeutic decision-making in breast cancer.
8. Collectively, these findings indicate that although apocrine morphology is biologically linked to androgen-related programs, molecular AR- associated features cannot fully substitute for morphologic classification.
