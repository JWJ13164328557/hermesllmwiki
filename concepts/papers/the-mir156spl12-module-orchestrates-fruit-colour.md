### Introduction
Colour change is a significant and easily observable event that occurs during plant development, particularly during fruit ripening and leaf senescence. This process can greatly impact the nutritional and quality value of crops, particularly those that bear fleshy fruit. Colour change is typically determined by the coordination of chlorophyll breakdown and pigment accumulation, with anthocyanins often being the primary pigments responsible for red, blue, and purple hues (Karlova et al ., 2014 ). Although the biosynthetic genes and regulatory factors of anthocyanins have been extensively studied during fruit colour change (Jaakola, 2013 ; Karppinen et al ., 2021 ; Ma et al ., 2021 ; Ren et al ., 2021 ; Sun et al ., 2023 ), our understanding of the regulation of chlorophyll metabolism in fruit is still limited, with only a few well‐documented transcriptional regulators (Ma et al ., 2021 ; Wang et al ., 2022a ). The orchestration of chlorophyll breakdown and pigment accumulation to achieve colour change during fruit ripening remains largely unknown.
MicroRNA156s (miR156s) are a highly conserved family of small RNAs in plants that post‐transcriptionally regulate the expression of SQUAMOSA PROMOTER BINDING PROTEINLIKE ( SPL ) genes, guiding transcript cleavage and/or translational repression (Chen et al ., 2010 ). As such, miR156/ SPL s are considered regulatory modules that govern various processes of plant growth and development (Wang and Wang, 2015 ). Recently, increasing evidence has indicated that miR156/ SPL modules play important roles in regulating anthocyanin accumulation in plants. For instance, in Arabidopsis, miR156‐targeted SPL9 negatively regulates anthocyanin biosynthesis by inhibiting the formation of the MYB‐bHLH‐WD40 complex (Gou et al ., 2011 ) and also directly suppresses the expression of DFR to control anthocyanin biosynthesis (Cui et al ., 2014 ). VcMIR156a/VcSPL12 affects the accumulation of chlorophylls and anthocyanins in blueberry
To further investigate the functional roles of VcMIR156a in blueberry, we generated transgenic blueberry lines overexpressing VcMIR156a , and three independent lines ( OX‐1 , OX‐3 , and OX‐6 ) were selected for further study (Figure S1a,b ). The transgenic lines showed an obvious red shade with light green on seedlings clonally propagated by tissue culture (referred to as tissue culture seedlings hereafter), especially tender shoots (Figure 1a and Figure S1a ). Subsequently, we measured the contents of chlorophylls and anthocyanins using spectrophotometry. Our results showed that the chlorophyll contents in transgenic lines were decreased to 61%–75% of WT, whereas VcMIR156a overexpression significantly elevated the anthocyanin levels, especially in OX‐1 and OX‐3 with more than a 2‐fold change compared to WT (Figure 1b,c ). These results indicated that VcMIR156a is involved in regulating the accumulation of anthocyanins and chlorophylls in blueberry.

### The VcMIR156a/VcSPL12 module regulates colour change via affecting ethylene production pathway
Our transcriptomic analysis revealed a substantial number of differentially expressed genes involved in plant hormone biosynthesis and signalling pathways, with ethylene‐associated genes representing the largest proportion (almost 30%) (Figure S3a and Dataset S1 ). Further observation demonstrated that all ethylene biosynthetic genes, including seven VcSAMS s, four VcACSs , and four VcACOs , were upregulated by VcMIR156a overexpression, with the exception of G30255 .t1 ( VcSAMS ) and CUFF.8159 ( VcACO6 ) (Figure 3a and Figure S3b,c ), indicating that VcMIR156a has positive effects on the expressions of ethylene biosynthetic genes. In contrast, ethylene receptors and their downstream genes, such as VcETR s, VcCTR s, VcEIN2 s and VcEIN3 s, remained unchanged or showed a slight transcriptional downregulation in the VcMIR156OX line (Figure 3a ). Subsequently, seven genes were selected for qRT‐PCR analysis. As indicated in Figure 3b , most of them showed a similar expression pattern to the transcriptomic data. These results suggested that VcMIR156a may primarily affect ethylene production rather than its signalling. Moreover, our metabolomic data indicated that the contents of all three ethylene precursors, L‐methionine, S‐adenosylmethionine (SAM), and 1‐aminocyclopropane‐1‐carboxylic acid (ACC), were significantly increased in the VcMIR156OX line (Figure 3c ). These results suggested that VcMIR156a may be involved in the regulation of ethylene production by activating ethylene biosynthetic genes.

### Discussion
Ethylene plays imperative roles in diverse developmental programmes from modulating organ growth to initiating fruit ripening (Dubois et al ., 2018 ; Fenn and Giovannoni, 2021 ). Meanwhile, miR156/ SPL s have been recognized as key regulatory hubs to control a wide variety of developmental processes (Wang and Wang, 2015 ). However, the connections between the ethylene pathway and miR156/ SPL ‐mediated regulation are very poorly understood. To date, only a few studies have reported that SPL s seemingly regulate developmental programmes through the ethylene pathway, including somatic embryogenesis in cotton (Wang et al ., 2018a ), spike development in wheat (Cao et al ., 2021 ), and fruit ripening in tomato (Adaskaveg et al ., 2021 ; Manning et al ., 2006 ; Wang et al ., 2020 ). In the present study, we documented that VcMIR156a/VcSPL12 can regulate ethylene production to control colour change in blueberry. Our transcriptomic and metabolomic data showed that VcMIR156a overexpression in tissue culture seedlings of blueberry not only elevated the expressions of the ethylene biosynthetic genes ( VcSAMS s, VcACS s, and VcACO s) but also enhanced the levels of the ethylene intermediates SAMS and ACC (Figure 3a–c ). Conversely, VcSPL12 decreased the expressions of the ethylene biosynthetic genes VcSAMS1 and VcACS1 (Figure 3d ). ### Plant materials and growth conditions
Eight‐year‐old blueberry trees ( Vaccinium corymbosum , cv. ‘Northland’ and ‘Bluetta’) were grown at the experimental station at Jilin University (Changchun, China). Fruits at six different developmental stages were randomly harvested from at least six different blueberry trees as previously described (Zifkin et al ., 2012 ), including green pad (F1S), green cup I (F2S), green cup II (F3S), light green (FWS), pink (FPS) and blue (FBS) fruits. Tissue culture seedlings of transgenic blueberry lines and untransformed control (WT) were grown in WPM medium (PhytoTech) with 2% sucrose, 0.7% agar, 1 mL/L vitamins, 2 mg/L Zeatin and pH 5.2 or substrate (moss peat:vermiculite = 3:1) under long‐day conditions (16 h light, 23 °C/8 h dark, 20 °C).

### Generation of transgenic blueberry and Arabidopsis
The genomic fragment of VcMIR156a (447 bp) and the full‐length coding sequence (CDS) of VcSPL12 (1377 bp) were amplified using gene‐specific primers (Table S1 ) and cloned into pBI121 vector. The full‐length CDSs of VcACS1 (1467 bp) and VcACO6 (960 bp) were also cloned and introduced into the pDONR207 vector, which were subsequently recombined into the destination vector pEarlyGate101. All binary vectors were transformed into Agrobacterium tumefaciens (GV3101) for plant transformation.

### Transcriptome and metabolome analysis
The tender stems of the VcMIR156a ‐overexpressing transgenic blueberry line and untransformed control were collected, frozen in liquid nitrogen, and used for transcriptome sequencing and wide‐targeted metabolome analysis.
RNA‐Seq libraries were prepared using the NEBNext UltraTM RNA Library Prep Kit for Illumina (NEB) following the manufacturer's instructions and sequenced on an Illumina high‐throughput platform. Paired‐end reads were generated and mapped to the blueberry reference genome (Gupta et al ., 2015 ; Kim et al ., 2019 ) using HISAT2 (Kim et al ., 2019 ). The threshold for significant differential expression was set at FDR &lt; 0.01 and |log 2 (Foldchange)| ≥ 1. The data used for heatmap generation is shown in Dataset S1 . The data that support the findings of this study are openly available in NCBI, reference number PRJNA956817.

### Yeast one‐hybrid (Y1H) assay
The full‐length coding sequence of VcSPL12 (1380 bp) was cloned and inserted into the pB42AD vector (pB42AD‐ VcSPL12 ), while promoter fragments of various blueberry genes, including VcSAMS (365 bp), VcACS1 (366 bp), VcACS6 (377 bp), VcACO1 (383 bp), VcACO4 (275 bp), VcACO6 (368 bp), VcERF19 (two fragments: VcERF19‐1 , 261 bp; VcERF19‐2 , 265 bp), VcWRI3 (252 bp), VcHEMH (448 bp), VcDVR1 (two fragments: VcDVR‐1 , 261 bp; VcDVR‐2 , 369 bp) and VcPORA (432 bp), were cloned from blueberry genomic DNA as template and constructed into the pLacZi vector. Three predicted SPL binding sites were identified in the promoter fragment of VcACS1 , while one was present in VcACO6 . Site‐mutated PCR was used to generate deletion of binding sites, and the resulting promoter fragments with different deletions of binding sites were also constructed into the pLacZi vector.

### Virus‐mediated gene silencing (VIGS) and transient expression in fruit
Virus‐mediated gene silencing was performed as described by Liu et al . ( 2002 ). Two specific fragments of VcSPL12 mRNA were selected and named SPL12 ‐ 1 (400 bp) and SPL12 ‐ 2 (342 bp). The two fragments were cloned and introduced into the pTRV2 vector, and then transformed into GV3101. Agrobacterium containing the pTRV1 vector acted as the assistant, which was mixed with Agrobacterium harbouring pTRV2, pTRV2‐ SPL12 ‐ 1 , or pTRV2‐ SPL12 ‐ 2 , respectively. Mixtures were infiltrated into one side of on‐tree fruit at the green cup I stage (F2S) as described previously (Karppinen et al ., 2021 ). Meanwhile, VcSPL12 was introduced into pBI121 vector and transformed into GV3101 for transient expression in fruit, which was also infiltrated into on‐tree fruits at the F2S stage. Infiltrated fruits were grown for another 2 weeks under natural conditions, and peels around infiltration site were collected for further study.

### Measurement of chlorophyll and anthocyanin contents
The contents of anthocyanins and chlorophylls were measured using spectrophotometry in tissue culture seedlings of untransformed control and transgenic blueberry lines overexpressing VcMIR156a and VcSPL12 . The peels around the infiltrated site were collected from transiently VcSPL12 ‐overexpressing and silencing fruits to investigate the change of pigments.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, our results show that VcMIR156a/VcSPL12 alters ethylene production through targeting VcACS1 and VcACO6, therefore governing fruit colour change.
2. VcSPL12 negatively regulates ethylene production through directly targeting VcACO6 and VcACS1 To investigate whether VcSPL12 directly regulates ethylene biosynthesis, the yeast one-hybrid (Y1H) assay was used to determine its ability to bind to the promoters of eight ethylene- related genes.
3. These results suggest that VcSPL12 may speciﬁcally bind to the promoters of VcACO6 and VcACS1.
4. Protein- DNA interaction assays indicated that VcSPL12 could speciﬁcally bind to the promoters and inhibit the activities of the ethylene biosynthetic genes VcACS1 and VcACO6.
5. Additionally, VcSPL12 may directly interact with the promoter region of the chlorophyll biosynthetic gene VcDVR, thereby activating its expression.
6. For instance, in Arabidopsis, miR156-targeted SPL9 negatively regulates anthocyanin biosynthesis by inhibiting the formation of the MYB-bHLH-WD40 complex (Gou et al., 2011) and also directly suppresses the expression of DFR to control anthocyanin biosynthesis (Cui et al., 2014).
7. Ethylene plays a critical role in fruit colour change, and its biosynthesis involves several enzyme steps, including SAM SYNTHETASE (SAMS), ACC SYNTHETASE (ACS), and ACC OXYGENASE (ACO) (Pattyn et al., 2021).
8. In a previous study, we demonstrated that VcMIR156a can inﬂuence anthocyanin and chlorophyll accumu- lation through its direct target VcSPL12 in tomato and Arabidopsis (Li et al., 2020b).
