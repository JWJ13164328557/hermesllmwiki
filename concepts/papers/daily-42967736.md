# Season-associated flavor variation in commercial Yingde black teas: Integrated sensory and metabolite profiling.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Food research international (Ottawa, Ont.) (2026)
- **PMID**: [42967736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42967736/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.foodres.2026.120927](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodres.2026.120927)
- **第一作者**: Yang Liu
- **作者**: Yang Liu, He Xie, Qin An, Huimin An, Fengji Zhang, Zhenlong Zhang, Xiaoyan Qiao, Chao Wang

## 摘要
Commercial Yingde black teas produced in spring, summer, and autumn differ in sensory quality, but their paired volatile and non-volatile profiles remain incompletely characterized. Here, 18 finished tea products from 11 manufacturers were evaluated using sensory analysis, targeted chemical quantification, GC × GC-Q-TOF-MS volatile profiling, and UHPLC-Q-Exactive Orbitrap MS/MS non-volatile profiling. The surveyed spring products were characterized by sweet-fruity aromas associated with higher relative abundances of esters and short-chain alcohols, together with a mellower taste, whereas the surveyed summer and autumn products showed floral and nutty aromas together with stronger bitterness and astringency. Correlation analysis identified several metabolites strongly associated with bitterness and astringency, including catechins, flavonols, and salicylic acid. In the two manufacturers represented across all three seasons, targeted salicylic acid concentrations were lower in the spring products and higher in the summer and autumn products, whereas the relative abundance of methyl salicylate showed the opposite pattern. These contrasting patterns may reflect differences in salicylate metabolism among the product groups. Overall, this study provides a chemical basis for the season-associated flavor variation of commercial Yingde black teas.
