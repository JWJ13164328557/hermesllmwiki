# Identification and preliminary functional characterization of MsPAL2, a key salt-tolerance gene during seed germination in Medicago sativa.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: BMC plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [42665810](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42665810/)
- **DOI**: [10.1186/s12870-026-09836-5](https://doi.org/10.1186/s12870-026-09836-5)
- **第一作者**: Donghan Cao
- **作者**: Donghan Cao, Xinran Cheng, Peisheng Mao, Liru Dou

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Alfalfa (Medicago sativa) is an important legume forage crop. Salt stress severely impairs seed germination, compromising field establishment and subsequent seedling growth. However, the key genes underlying alfalfa seed responses to salt stress during germination remain largely unidentified, hindering targeted genetic improvement of salt tolerance within the genus Medicago.

RESULTS: In this study, transcriptomic sequencing was performed on germinated and ungerminated seeds of alfalfa treated with 150 mmol/L NaCl, using the 'Zhongmu No. 4' reference genome. Through integrated KEGG pathway enrichment, gene expression fold-change analysis, and Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA), we identified the gene MsPAL2 (Msa1079410), which encodes the key rate-limiting enzyme in phenylpropanoid metabolism, as a critical salt-tolerance gene. Functional characterization via virus-induced gene silencing (VIGS) revealed that silencing MsPAL2 did not affect seed germination under normal conditions but significantly reduced the germination rate under salt stress. These findings suggest that MsPAL2 acts as a positive regulator of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress. Promoter analysis and AlphaFold3 prediction collectively revealed MYB-binding cis-elements and identified MYB transcription factors as candidate upstream regulators of MsPAL2, laying the groundwork for elucidating its regulatory network.

CONCLUSIONS: Phenylpropanoid metabolism constitutes a key mechanism underlying salt tolerance in alfalfa. Through transcriptome analysis, we identified MsPAL2, a phenylpropanoid pathway gene, as a critical regulator of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress, providing valuable genetic resources for molecular breeding of salt-tolerant alfalfa.

### 核心发现
1. Our findings reveal that the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway, particularly its rate-limiting enzyme gene MsPAL2, plays a pivotal role in salt tolerance during germination.
2. Collectively, these findings reveal the pivotal role of phenylpropanoid metabolism in salt tolerance during alfalfa germination and establish MsPAL2 as a promising candidate gene for molecular breeding of salt-tolerant varieties.
3. Notably, the two pathways directly related to phenylpropanoid metabolism—“flavonoid biosynthesis” and “phenylpropanoid biosynthesis”—both ranked among the most significantly enriched pathways, exhibiting the lowest q-values (indicated in red) and a substantial number of enriched genes, suggesting that phenylpropanoid metabolism plays a central role in the response to salt stress.
4. These findings suggest that MsPAL2 acts as a positive regulator of alfalfa seed germination under salt stress.
5. These results suggest that successful germination under salt stress is closely associated with extensive transcriptional reprogramming.
6. Together with “response to oxidative stress” and “hydrogen peroxide catabolic process”, these findings suggest that transcriptional reprogramming in germinating seeds under salt stress is concentrated on antioxidant defense and phenylpropanoid secondary metabolism, providing a basis for subsequent targeting of the phenylpropanoid metabolic pathway.
7. In summary, using two independent methods—KEGG enrichment and GSEA—we identified the phenylpropanoid biosynthesis pathway as one of the most critical salt stress response pathways during the seed germination stage of alfalfa and identified MsPAL2 (Msa1079410) as a candidate gene within this pathway.
8. These results confirm that the VIGS silencing system is specific and effective, and that vector infection itself does not affect MsPAL2 expression.
