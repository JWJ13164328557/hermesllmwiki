# Multi-omics deciphers the Zn-Mg code: Concurrent activation of carbon fixation and auxin signaling for synergistic growth promotion in tobacco.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology and biochemistry : PPB (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033527](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033527/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111963](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111963)
- **第一作者**: Yaoyang Chen
- **作者**: Yaoyang Chen, Longhui Ren, Qingjiao Zhang, Wenping Zhang, Gaokun Zhao, Yuping Wu, Jianlei Zi, Ruting Wang

## 摘要
Improving photosynthetic carbon assimilation and vegetative growth is essential for high-quality and efficient tobacco production. However, the synergistic mechanisms by which zinc (Zn) and magnesium (Mg) co-application regulate photosynthesis, carbon metabolism, and hormone signaling remain poorly understood. Here, we integrated agronomic, physiological, transcriptomic, and metabolomic analyses to investigate the regulatory effects of Zn and Mg co-application on tobacco growth. The results showed that Zn-Mg co-application significantly enhanced plant height, stem circumference, leaf size, photosynthetic pigment contents, and antioxidant enzyme activities, while reducing malondialdehyde (MDA) content. Integrated multi-omics analysis revealed that Zn-Mg co-application promoted photosynthetic pigment accumulation by modulating porphyrin metabolism, enhanced photosystem activity and photosynthetic electron transport, and upregulated key genes and metabolites involved in carbon assimilation and carbon metabolism. Concurrently, it activated tryptophan metabolism, thereby promoting auxin biosynthesis and signal transduction to drive vegetative growth. Collectively, these findings demonstrated that Zn-Mg co-application synergistically enhances photosynthetic carbon acquisition and growth regulation in tobacco through two coordinated pathways: "porphyrin metabolism-photosynthetic electron transport-carbon metabolism" and "tryptophan metabolism-auxin signaling." This study provided a theoretical basis for precise Zn-Mg fertilization in tobacco production and offered multi-omics evidence for understanding how mineral nutrient interactions regulate crop growth and photosynthetic efficiency.
