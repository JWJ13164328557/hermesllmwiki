# Bran stir-frying remodels Fructus Aurantii through flavonoid deglycosylation and limonoid reduction.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [42927300](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42927300/)
- **DOI**: [10.3389/fphar.2023.1114010](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphar.2023.1114010)
- **第一作者**: Yuhao Zhang
- **作者**: Yuhao Zhang, Wanshu Li, Qingqing Li, Lixia Chen, Bo Zhao, Qi Chen, Xinyan Zhu, Hangjuan Lin

## 摘要
Bran stir-frying (BS) is traditionally used to improve the properties of Fructus Aurantii (FA), but previous studies mainly focused on chemical changes or pharmacological differences, leaving global transformation pathways and functional consequences unclear. This study integrated in-depth analysis of molecular networking (IDAMN), metabolomics, mechanism validation, and functional validation to elucidate BS-FA processing mechanisms. Compared with conventional molecular networking, IDAMN can identify and remove 63.2% redundant nodes derived from other adduct, isotope, and in-source fragments. BS processing increased flavonoid aglycones while reducing flavonoid glycosides, limonoids, and phosphoglycerides. Mechanism validation indicated heat-assisted glycoside cleavage promoted aglycone formation, whereas limonoid reduction was associated with wheat bran adsorption. Functional validation demonstrated enhanced gastrointestinal motility of BS-FA compared with Raw-FA (47.1% vs 24.5% emptying rate at 200 μg/mL). Naringenin showed stronger activity than naringin, while limonin exhibited reduced effects at higher concentrations. Collectively, BS processing may improve FA quality by enriching efficacy-related flavonoid aglycones while reducing bitterness/irritation-related limonoids.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, these findings suggest that BS processing involves multiple physicochemical processes, including flavonoid deglycosylation, partial transfer of limonin to WB, and possible degradation of membrane lipids.
2. In the above IDAMN analysis above, we found that flavonoid glycosides and flavonoid aglycones with similar substructures were not co-clustered, likely because sugar chains in flavonoid glycosides interfere with the flavonoid aglycone signal.
3. Collectively, BS processing may improve FA quality by enriching efficacy-related flavonoid aglycones while reducing bitterness/irritation-related limonoids.
4. These findings suggest that the chemical transformations may constitute the chemical basis for the reduced irritation and enhanced efficacy associated with BS processing of FA.
5. Collectively, these results demonstrate that BS processing induces distinct chemical changes through different mechanisms: heat-assisted cleavage of glycosidic bonds drives the conversion of flavonoid glycosides into their corresponding aglycones, whereas the decrease in limonoids is partly explained by their transfer to and retention in WB.
6. Collectively, these results demonstrate that BS processing enhances the anti-inflammatory activity of FA while improving its biological tolerance, which may be associated with BS-induced remodeling of bioactive constituents.
7. Collectively, the zebrafish and IEC-6 experiments provided complementary evidence for the functional effects associated with BS processing.
8. These findings suggest that transfer to WB contributes to limonin reduction, while other processes underlying the remaining loss require further investigation.
