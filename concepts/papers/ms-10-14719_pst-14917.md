### 核心发现
1. The microbial community that exists in the soil and interacts with plant roots either nearby (rhizospheric microbiome) or far away (non-rhizospheric microbiome) is referred to as the “belowground microbiome.” The phyllosphere, endosphere and spermosphere microbiomes that circulate above the soil in plant components and interact with plant functions are included in the “aboveground microbiome” (10)
2. In contrast, pathogen infection causes SAR, which is marked by the buildup of salicylic acid and long-range mobile signals that frequently interact with the microbiome structure in leaves and roots.
3. The crop microbiome plays a key role in the production of GHGs and carbon sequestration and represents an opportunity for microbiome manipulation to tackle climate change (90).
4. The phyllosphere, where wide variety of microbial communities living includes epiphytes (on the leaf surface) and endophytes (colonise inside leaves), which component is essential for plant growth, development and resistance to both abiotic and biotic stresses.
5. These tripartite interactions between microbes, plants and microbes in a more general sense show how interactions between microbes and their hosts are essential to the development of individual plants but also the diversity of plant species and health of ecosystems of natural environments.
