# Two GmHAL3 members interact to enhance salt tolerance in soybean by modulating coenzyme A biosynthesis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43084621](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43084621/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag869](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag869)
- **第一作者**: Han Gou
- **作者**: Han Gou, Jilei Gan, Qin Liu, Juan Liu, Sushuang Deng, Ling Gan, Lu Li, Xiaowan Fang

## 摘要
Salt stress severely impairs soybean growth, yield, and quality. Although the halotolerance protein HAL3 mediates salt tolerance in yeast, its role in soybean requires further characterization. In this study, we demonstrate that overexpression of GmHAL3b enhances salt tolerance in soybean, enabling plants to maintain a favorable Na+/K+ ratio, retain chlorophyll, and preserve shoot biomass under saline conditions. Conversely, CRISPR-Cas9-generated GmHAL3b mutants exhibit increased sensitivity to salt stress. We further identified GmMYB68 as a direct transcriptional activator of GmHAL3b. Notably, GmHAL3b physically interacts with its homolog, GmHAL3a. Overexpression of GmHAL3a also improved salt tolerance, whereas single and double mutants of GmHAL3a and/or GmHAL3b were salt-sensitive. In transgenic hairy roots, overexpression of GmHAL3b improved salt tolerance in the wild-type background but not in the GmHAL3a knockout background, indicating that the two homologs are not functionally redundant and that both are required. Transcriptome analysis revealed changes in the expression of salt-responsive genes. Consistent with this finding, acetyl-CoA levels were higher in GmHAL3-overexpressing lines than in the wild type and lower in the double mutant. Exogenous application of pantothenic acid (PA), a precursor of CoA, largely restored growth, chlorophyll content and shoot biomass in the GmHAL3 mutants under salt stress, an effect that was substantially greater than that observed in the wild type. Collectively, our work elucidates a GmHAL3-mediated pathway that confers salt tolerance in soybean, likely through modulating coenzyme A biosynthesis.
