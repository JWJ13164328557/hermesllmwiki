## 摘要
Chrysanthemum morifolium cv. Chuju is a high-value edible flower with unique aroma characteristics but is susceptible to quality deterioration during drying. This study systematically evaluated the effects of four drying methods, including natural drying (ND), hot-air drying (HAD), microwave drying (MD), and vacuum freeze-drying (VFD), on the aroma profiles and metabolic characteristics of Chuju using sensory quantitative descriptive analysis (QDA), electronic nose (E-nose), gas chromatography-ion mobility spectrometry (GC-IMS), and untargeted metabolomics. A total of 90 volatile compounds and 2605 metabolites were identified. Drying treatments significantly altered volatile fingerprints and metabolic profiles. HAD exhibited higher signal intensities of several volatile compounds potentially associated with heat-induced transformations, whereas VFD showed relatively higher levels of aroma-related volatile compounds and thermolabile metabolites. Metabolite-volatile correlation analysis indicated that aroma differentiation among drying treatments was associated with amino acid metabolism, lipid oxidation-related pathways, phenylpropanoid metabolism, and flavonoid-related metabolism. These findings provide insights into the metabolic basis underlying drying-induced aroma variation and contribute to the optimization of Chuju drying strategies for quality preservation.

### 核心发现
1. This study provides comparative profiling rather than absolute quantitative determination.
2. Taken together, the PCA and QC analyses supported the consistency of the metabolomic dataset and provided an overview of the metabolic differences associated with the four drying treatments.
3. These results suggest that low-temperature drying may favor the preservation of some thermolabile metabolites compared with thermal drying.
4. Collectively, these results indicate that drying treatments induced broad changes in both primary and secondary metabolism, with phenylpropanoid/flavonoid, lipid, and terpenoid metabolism representing prominent metabolic features distinguishing the drying treatments.
5. Overall, these results suggest that different drying strategies influenced Chuju aroma through distinct metabolic responses rather than a single regulatory pathway.
6. Therefore, the identified volatile compounds were interpreted as tentative annotations, and future studies using authentic standards and quantitative calibration are required for absolute confirmation.
7. Further validation using authentic standards and targeted quantitative approaches is required for absolute confirmation of individual metabolites.
8. The integration of metabolomics and volatile profiling provides potential mechanistic explanations for aroma differentiation; however, further enzyme activity and targeted metabolite analyses are required to confirm the specific biochemical mechanisms involved.
