### 核心发现
1. The primary outcome of interest derived from the follow-up assessments (i.e., the binary target variable for the model predictions) was the one-year LBP incidence, indicating whether a participant developed LBP during the one-year follow-up period.
2. The binary target variable was the one- year LBP incidence, with 76 participants (56.3%) classified as not having developed LBP (“No LBP”, class 0) and 59 participants (43.7%) classified as having developed LBP (“LBP”, class 1).
3. Notably, a history of LBP has consistently been identified as a robust risk factor for future LBP17–23.
4. Moreover, recent evidence has emphasized the lack of longitudinal studies on lumbar spine kinematics and LBP49, which are crucial for exploring potential causal relationships between psychological factors, lifting biomechanics, and LBP.
5. No imputation was required for categorical features, as they contained no missing values.
