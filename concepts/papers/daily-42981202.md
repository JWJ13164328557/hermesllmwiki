# Gamma irradiation concerning genetics of Ralstonia solanacearum and export quality in potatoes.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: International journal of radiation biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [42981202](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42981202/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/09553002.2026.2747697](https://doi.org/10.1080/09553002.2026.2747697)
- **第一作者**: M M Haque
- **作者**: M M Haque, M Haque, M K Hasna, M I Khalil, M F Mortuza, M A Hossain, N R Paul, S Akter

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Potato brown rot, caused by Ralstonia solanacearum, is an important quarantine disease that can seriously affect tuber quality and limit the movement and marketability of potatoes. Gamma irradiation may help control the pathogen after harvest, but the dose used can also affect the nutritional value and quality of potato tubers.

PURPOSE: We aimed to study the genetic diversity of the potato brown rot pathogen, Ralstonia solanacearum, using molecular markers. We also evaluated how irradiation affects the physicochemical properties of treated and untreated potato tubers.

MATERIALS AND METHODS: Commercial potato varieties were collected from major potato-growing areas of Bangladesh for pathogen isolation and irradiation studies. Forty-two bacterial isolates were characterized on TTC medium and examined using PS-1/PS-2 PCR, 16S rRNA sequencing, phylotype-specific PCR, and mutS amplification. After irradiation, the tubers were assessed for weight loss, ascorbic acid, anthocyanin, carotenoid, total soluble solids (TSS), and mineral content.

RESULTS: Virulent isolates produced the expected 553-bp PS-1/PS-2 amplicon. Sequence analysis placed a representative isolate close to Asian Ralstonia reference strains, while phylotype-specific PCR generated a 213-bp product corresponding to phylotype IV. The pathogen-specific 553-bp amplicon remained detectable up to 5.5 kGy but was not detected at 6 kGy under the conditions used in this study. A reduction in mutS band intensity was also observed following irradiation. The effects of irradiation on tuber quality varied with cultivar and dose. Ascorbic acid and carotenoid contents generally decreased with increasing irradiation dose, whereas anthocyanin and TSS remained comparatively stable. Mineral concentrations were generally higher in irradiated tubers than in untreated samples.

CONCLUSION: Gamma irradiation reduced the detectability of the pathogen-specific PCR target, although this does not confirm complete pathogen inactivation. Irradiation conditions require optimization to balance pathogen suppression and nutritional quality. Further viability testing and genomic characterization are recommended.
