## 摘要
The development of multicellular organisms relies on the precise regulation of cellular differentiation. As such, there has been significant effort invested to understand the process through which an immature cell undergoes differentiation. In this review, we highlight key discoveries and advances that have contributed to our understanding of the transcriptional networks underlying Arabidopsis root endodermal differentiation. To conclude, we propose perspectives on how advances in molecular biology, microscopy, and nucleotide sequencing will provide the tools to test the biological significance of these gene regulatory networks (GRN).

Keywords:cell differentiation, Arabidopsis root, endodermis Research across developmental systems has demonstrated that the molecular path from stem cell to differentiation is vastly complex [1]. A way to manage molecular complexity is to delineate the molecular inputs feeding into a process (e.g. differentiation), known as a gene regulatory network (GRN). The delineation of GRN architecture will generate hypotheses to address specific mechanisms of differentiation. Here, we focus on how GRNs have been used to provide insight into differentiation of theArabidopsisroot endodermis, one cell type of the root ground tissue that has been the subject of intensive study. The cell types of the root are organized in radially symmetric cylinders around the inner, bilaterally arranged root vasculature (referred to as the stele in this review). The endodermis surrounds the stele and is often likened to a mammalian epithelium - it is polarized and plays the physiological role of a barrier to the external environment [2], reviewed in [3]. Since plant cells are immobile, entire cell lineages and developmental stages are easily traced along the longitudinal axis of the root. ### 核心发现
1. SHR activates expression of SCR, then forms a complex with the SCR protein to regulate a common set of downstream target genes [9–11].
2. Yeast two-hybrid experiments have shown that SHR and SCR can bind to JACKDAW, as well as to its closest homologs BALD IBIS and MAGPIE [18,20].
3. These results indicate that SHR-SCR cannot bind to DNA alone, but requires formation of a complex with an IDD TF.
4. Although SEU has been shown to be a negative co-regulator in ﬂower development, it can directly bind to upstream regions of SHR, SCR, and SCL3 during middle cortex formation [28– 30].
5. This study provides one explanation as to how expression can be established and maintained in the absence of a priori speciﬁcity.
6. While it has been shown that SHR-SCR can bind to JACKDAW and MAGPIE (and may be required to do so for SHR-SCR DNA-binding function), the expression domains of JACKDAW and MAGPIE are restricted to immature meristem tissue, while SHR and SCR are expressed throughout the root endodermis [38,39].
7. Moreover, MYB36 can directly bind to the upstream regulatory regions of CASPs and other CS-related genes [43], although this interaction likely requires a co-factor, which is also downstream of MYB36 [44].
8. Given its expression pattern and role in CS formation, it is unsurprising that MYB36 was identiﬁed as a target of SHR- SCR in time-course induction experiments [15,44].
