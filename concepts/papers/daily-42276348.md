# Plasma ball milling-assisted synthesis of silico-manganese heterojunction adsorbent from manganese slag and rice husk biochar for efficient removal of Cu(II) and Ni(II) from aqueous solutions.

## 摘要
Heavy metal pollution from industrial activities poses severe threats to aquatic ecosystems and human health, urging the development of low-cost, high-efficiency adsorbents via resource reuse. Herein, a silico-manganese heterojunction adsorbent (RHB&MS) was fabricated by plasma ball milling (PBM) using manganese slag (MS) and rice husk biochar (RHB) as raw materials. The synthesis mechanism, structural characteristics, and adsorption performance of RHB&MS for Cu(II) and Ni(II) were systematically investigated via multiple characterizations and batch adsorption experiments. The results indicated that PBM induced the formation of stable Si-O-Mn covalent bonds at the interface, constructing a "crystalline silica framework-amorphous carbon matrix-nano manganese oxide active site" heterojunction structure. The specific surface area of RHB&MS reached 108.30 m2/g, which was 7 times higher than that of pristine RHB. Under optimal conditions, the maximum adsorption capacities of RHB&MS for Cu(II) and Ni(II) calculated by Langmuir model were 235.51 mg/g and 170.68 mg/g, respectively, outperforming most reported solid waste-based adsorbents. The adsorption process followed the pseudo-second-order kinetic model and Langmuir isotherm model, indicating that chemical adsorption dominated the monolayer adsorption. The adsorption mechanism involved synergistic effects of coordination complexation, electron transfer mediated by Mn valence evolution, and electrostatic attraction. Moreover, RHB&MS exhibited excellent cyclic stability (adsorption capacity retention rate >87% after 10 cycles) and environmental safety (leaching toxicity met GB5085.3-2007 standard). This study provides a novel strategy for the high-value utilization of industrial and agricultural wastes, and offers a promising adsorbent for heavy metal-contaminated wastewater treatment.
