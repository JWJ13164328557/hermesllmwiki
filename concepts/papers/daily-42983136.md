# Genome-wide identification and integrative analysis of the cytochrome P450 superfamily in pea (Pisum sativum L.) reveals candidate genes associated with Aphanomyces root rot response.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [42983136](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42983136/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s10142-026-02095-5](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10142-026-02095-5)
- **第一作者**: Muhammad Sufyan Tahir
- **作者**: Muhammad Sufyan Tahir, Sangeeta Dhaubhadel

## 摘要
Cytochrome P450 monooxygenases (P450) constitute one of the largest and most functionally diverse enzyme superfamilies in plants, underpinning the biosynthesis of a plethora of primary and specialized metabolites involved in growth and development as well as stress response. Here, we present the first integrated genome-wide analysis of the pea P450 superfamily (CYPome), combining phylogenetic, structural, evolutionary, transcriptional, and genetic approaches to identify defense-associated candidates. We identified 210 P450 genes encoding 241 proteins in pea genome, classified into 45 CYP families across 10 clans. Comparative analyses revealed both highly conserved legume P450 lineages and a substantial fraction of pea-specific expansions, reflecting dynamic CYPome evolution. Gene structure and motif analyses confirmed conservation of core catalytic features, alongside family-specific variation consistent with functional diversification. A conserved core of many PsP450 genes induced across multiple pea cultivars at a wide range of time points post Aphanomyces euteiches infection together with their expression in root tissue was discovered. Integration of aphanomyces root rot resistance-associated quantitative trait loci further refined candidate prioritization, identifying PsP450s that co-localized with resistance loci, exhibited strong co-expression with PsIFS genes, and were rapidly induced upon infection. Collectively, this integrative analysis defines the pea CYPome and identifies a focused set of high-confidence P450 candidates likely involved in defense-associated metabolic reprogramming during A. euteiches infection. These findings provide a foundational resource for functional genomics and offer promising targets for improving disease resistance in pea and related legume crops.
