### 核心发现
1. These findings suggest that squalene may serve as a promising adjunctive nutritional strategy to support hemodynamic regulation, mitochondrial bioenergetics, and cardiovascular health.
2. These findings suggest that squalene may exert a vasculo-protective effect, potentially through enhanced endothelial nitric oxide bioavailability and reduced oxidative vascular stress, mechanisms that have been described for antioxidant interventions in cardiovascular populations [23,29].
3. The improvement in CoQ10 levels is particularly important because CoQ10 plays a key role in mitochondrial electron transport and also functions as a lipid-soluble antioxidant [12,27,31].
4. In conclusion, squalene supplementation showed potential benefits in patients with stable IHD by improving mitochondrial bioenergetic markers, supporting antioxidant defense, and contributing to better cardiovascular regulation.
5. Additional recent functional food studies have also emphasized oxidative stress, metabolic inflammation, immune regulation, and inflammation- related cardiometabolic biomarkers as important targets for bioactive compounds [43–45].
6. Bridging squalene research to the FFC 17-step functional food development model: The present study supports the early stages of the Functional Food Center (FFC) 17- step functional food development model by identifying stable ischemic heart disease as the target condition and squalene as the key bioactive compound with cardiovascular and mitochondrial bioenergetic relevance (Steps 1–2) [46].
7. Therefore, this study provides a clinical foundation for future development of squalene as a functional food or nutraceutical ingredient for cardiovascular support.
8. Mitochondrial redox status as a target for cardiovascular disease.
