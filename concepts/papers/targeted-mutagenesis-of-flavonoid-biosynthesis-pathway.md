## 摘要
SummaryYellow‐seed is widely accepted as a good‐quality trait in Brassica crops. Previous studies have shown that the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway is essential for the development of seed colour, but its function in Brassica napus, an important oil crop, is poorly understood. To systematically explore the gene functions of the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway in rapeseed, several representative TRANSPARENT TESTA (TT) genes, including three structural genes (BnaTT7, BnaTT18, BnaTT10), two regulatory genes (BnaTT1, BnaTT2) and a transporter (BnaTT12), were selected for targeted mutation by CRISPR/Cas9 in the present study. Seed coat colour, lignin content, seed quality and yield‐related traits were investigated in these Bnatt mutants together with Bnatt8 generated previously. These Bnatt mutants produced seeds with an elevated seed oil content and decreased pigment and lignin accumulation in the seed coat without any serious defects in the yield‐related traits. In addition, the fatty acid (FA) composition was also altered to different degrees, i.e., decreased oleic acid and increased linoleic acid and α‐linolenic acid, in all Bnatt mutants except Bnatt18. Furthermore, gene expression analysis revealed that most of BnaTT mutations resulted in the down‐regulation of key genes related to flavonoid and lignin synthesis, and the up‐regulation of key genes related to lipid synthesis and oil body formation, which may contribute to the phenotype. Collectively, our study generated valuable resources for breeding programs, and more importantly demonstrated the functional divergence and overlap of flavonoid biosynthesis pathway genes in seed coat colour, oil content and FA composition of rapeseed.

### Introduction
Brassica napus L. (AACC, 2n = 38) is the largest oil seed crop in China, providing approximately 55% of domestic vegetable oil and contributing significantly to animal feed and biofuel. Therefore, increasing rapeseed yield and oil content while improving oil quality have been the main objectives for rapeseed breeding (Abbadi and Leckband, 2011 ). Yellow‐seeded B. napus has fewer proanthocyanidins (PAs), phenolic compounds and tannins than the black‐seeded type (Chao et al ., 2022 ; Ding et al ., 2021 ; Xie et al ., 2020 ; Zhai et al ., 2020 ), which helps simplify oil processing with less nutrient loss (Akhov et al ., 2009 ; Wang et al ., 2017 ). In addition, yellow seeds are generally associated with a low‐lignin content and high‐oil content compared to those of black‐seeded B. napus (Chao et al ., 2022 ; Ding et al ., 2021 ; Zhang et al ., 2022 ).
The seed coat develops from the inner and outer integuments of the ovule, and pigment mainly exists in the inner integument and palisade cell layer of the seed coat (Moïse et al ., 2005 ; Wan et al ., 2002 ; Zhang et al ., 2022 ). Many studies have shown that the flavonoid pathway is involved in seed coat colour formation in Brassica species (Akhov et al ., 2009 ; Auger et al ., 2009 ; Li et al ., 2010 ; Lian et al ., 2017 ; Xie et al ., 2020 ; Zhai et al ., 2020 ). The metabolites of this pathway, flavonols and PAs, are the main pigments for seed coat colouring and are oxidized by polyphenol oxidase to produce brown substances during seed ripening, causing the seed coat to turn black (Lepiniec et al ., 2006 ; Pourcel et al ., 2005 ; Routaboul et al ., 2012 ; Zhang et al ., 2013 ).

### Results
BnaTT genes display distinct functional domains with different expression patterns
To systematically investigate the gene functions of the flavonoid biosynthesis pathway in relation to seed colour formation, we selected seven representative BnaTT genes and isolated the genomic DNA as well as the coding sequences from RNA from the B . napus pure line J9707. There are two homologous copies of BnaTT1 , BnaTT2 , BnaTT7 and BnaTT12 , three homologous copies of BnaTT10 and four homologous copies of BnaTT18 (Table S1 ; Figure S1 ). These homologous copies of each gene have similar gene structures (Figure S1 ).
Amino acid sequence analysis showed that all homologous copies of these BnaTT genes except BnaTT10 share the same conserved domain as the Arabidopsis homologues (Figure 1a ; Figures S2–S8 ). BnaTT7 contains a cytochrome P450 domain, encoding structural protein F3′H and flavonol synthase (Schoenbohm et al ., 2000 ). BnaTT18 contains the N‐terminal DIOX_N and the C‐terminal 2OG‐FeII_Oxy domains. It encodes the structural protein LDOX, which synthesizes anthocyanins and PAs (Abrahams et al ., 2003 ). BnaTT12 contains two conserved MatE domains and transports PAs into the vacuole (Zhao et al ., 2010 ). Two homologous copies of BnaTT10, BnaTT10.A06/BnaTT10.C02, contain three conserved copper‐binding domains similar to those of Arabidopsis TT10 (Figure 1a ; Figure S6 ); but the predicted BnaTT10.A02 lacked the copper‐binding domains, which are essential for TT10 function (Figure S6 ). BnaTT1 , BnaTT2 and BnaTT8 are transcription factors that regulate the transcription of PA pathway‐specific genes (Yu, 2013 ). BnaTT1 contains a SUF4‐like domain, which contains a zinc finger domain; BnaTT2 contains two typical Myb domains; and the functional copies of BnaTT8 contain the N‐terminal MYB interaction region (bHLH‐MYC_N) and the HLH domain in the C‐terminal region (Figure 1a ; Figures S6–S8 ).

### Creation of CRISPR/Cas9‐targeted mutations in BnaTT genes
To generate Cas9‐induced knockout mutations in all of the functional copies of each selected BnaTT gene, a binary construct carrying two to four sgRNAs with Cas9 driven by the 2 × 35S promoter was generated as previously described (Figures S1 and S9 ; Ma et al ., 2015 ). All of these sgRNAs were designed to target the first exon or conserved domain of each BnaTT gene and ensure the success rate of gene disruptions altering the reading frame (Figure 1a ; Figures S1 and S9 ).
The resulting six constructs (named PSH36‐PSH39 and PSH41‐PSH42) were independently transformed into J9707 using Agrobacterium ‐mediated transformation and generated 60 to 233 independent plants for each construct, of which 50.00% to 91.42% were positive transformants (Table S2 ). The targeted mutations of each BnaTT were identified by Hi‐TOM sequencing analysis of the target sites, and the editing efficiency ranged from 22.24% to 62.41% in the T 0 generation (Table S2 ).
To obtain stable lines with targeted mutations, these T 0 edited plants were self‐pollinated for several generations. The targeted mutations of progenies from T 0 plants were verified by Hi‐TOM sequencing analysis of the target sites. Variable numbers and types of homozygous mutants with different allelic combinations in each selected BnaTT gene were detected (Table S3 ). The homozygous mutant lines for each BnaTT gene with different frameshift mutations in all functional copies, which were predicted to result in nonfunctional proteins, were chosen for subsequent phenotypic characterization and gene expression analysis (Figure S10 ).
To ascertain whether off‐targeting occurred in the present study, we searched the B. napus genome for putative off‐target sites with high homology to these target sites according to the CRISPR‐P2.0 program ( http://crispr.hzau.edu.cn/CRISPR2/ ). A total of 96 putative off‐target sites were identified for 15 sgRNAs, and no off‐target editing was detected in T 0 edited plants by gene‐specific primers 

### Discussion
Yellow seeds have always been a major goal of the global breeding goal of Brassica oilseed crops (Abbadi and Leckband, 2011 ). As rapeseed naturally lacks yellow‐seeded mutants, it has been exclusively developed through interspecific hybridization (Zhang et al ., 2011 ). However, this strategy is time‐consuming and inefficient (Chen et al ., 2019 ). In this study, an effective approach was successfully used to produce targeted mutations in six key genes in the flavonoid biosynthetic pathway in B. napus . Among them, the targeted mutants of five genes ( BnaTT1 , BnaTT7 , BnaTT10 , BnaTT12 and BnaTT18 ) were produced for the first time in rapeseed. The homozygous knockout mutants together with the Bnatt8 mutant generated previously could produce seeds with a visibly lighter seed colour to different degrees, as well as an elevated seed oil content, a decreased lignin content and different alterations in FA composition without any serious defects in yield‐related traits, which revealed functional divergence and overlap of BnaTTs in rapeseed.
BnaTT genes exhibit functional differentiation in pigment accumulation in the seed coat
In a previous study, we showed that disruption of the BnaTT8 gene completely blocked the PA‐specific deposition in the seed coat and resulted in seeds with a visible yellow seed coat phenotype (Zhai et al ., 2020 ). In the present study, targeted mutants of the other six representative genes in the flavonoid pathway, including three structural genes ( BnaTT7 , BnaTT18 , BnaTT10 ), three regulatory genes ( BnaTT1 and BnaTT2 ) and a transporter ( BnaTT12 ) were successfully generated. The homozygous knockout mutants of these genes could produce seeds with extensive variations in seed coat colour, in which the yellow‐seed phenotype of the Bnatt10 mutant was more obvious than that of the Bnatt8 mutant. The yellow seed colour of the Bnatt2 mutants was very close to that of the Bnatt8 mutant but more obvious than that of the Bnatt18 , Bnatt7 and Bnatt1

### Identification of CRISPR‐edited plants and off‐target mutation analysis
Firstly, PB‐L/PB‐R primers were used to identify the T‐DNA (Table S8 ), to confirm that the regenerated seedlings successfully gained the CRISPR system. To further explore whether the sequences of targeted genes were modified, genomic fragments containing the targeted sites of each BnaTT was confirmed by the high‐throughput tracking of mutations (Hi‐TOM) platform (Liu et al ., 2019 ). The sequencing data was then decoded using a corresponding online tool to track the mutations of the target sites ( http://www.hi‐tom.net/hi‐tom/ ).

### Field experiments and phenotyping
The T 0 and its generation mutants were all grown in transgenic field of Huazhong Agriculture University, Wuhan, China (30.48 N, 114.28 E). The field experiment followed a randomized complete block design with three replications. Each replicates had three rows with 11–12 plants per row, with a distance of 21 cm between plants within each row and 30 cm between rows. The field management was performed in line with standard breeding practice.
The developing seeds were used for seed coat staining, seed coat sectioning, and RNA sampling. Mature plants were used for quality traits, including oil content, protein content and FA composition. The FA profiles were determined using gas chromatography (GC) system with a Model 6890 GC analyser (Agilent Technologies, Inc., Wilmington, DE) according to the method previously described by Yang et al . ( 2012 ). The protein and oil contents were examined using a Foss NIRSystems 5000 near‐infrared reflectance spectroscopy (NIRS) using the parameters described by Gan et al . ( 2003 ), and the content was expressed as a percentage of the total seed dry weight.
