# Late-season cooling and local sensitivity shape blue ring formation in northern Fennoscandian Scots pine.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Tree physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033104](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033104/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/treephys/tpag171](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/tpag171)
- **第一作者**: Liliana Siekacz
- **作者**: Liliana Siekacz, Paweł Matulewski, Ylva Sjöberg, Alma Piermattei, Alan Crivellaro, Mikko Sipilä, Janne Mukkala, Agata Buchwal

## 摘要
Blue rings (BRs) are wood anatomical anomalies formed by incomplete lignification of latewood tracheids and provide a direct record of disruptions during the final stages of wood formation. Because lignification is temperature sensitive, BRs offer a unique opportunity to investigate how short-lived cooling events affect xylem development. However, the timing of cooling-induced lignification failure and how local site conditions influence BR formation remain poorly understood, particularly at high-latitude treelines where wood formation operates close to thermal limits. Using paired 300-year Scots pine (Pinus sylvestris L.) chronologies from two northern Fennoscandian treeline sites approximately 250 km apart, we present the first direct comparison of BR formation between neighbouring populations exposed to broadly similar regional climatic forcing. BR frequency was consistently higher at Värriö (Finland, 67°N) than at Iškoras (Norway, 69°N), despite strong synchrony in major BR years and a coherent long-term decline in BR occurrence at both sites. BRs largely disappeared from Iškoras after the early twentieth century, whereas they persisted at Värriö. Daily temperature analyses suggest an association between BR formation and short-lived late-season cooling, with the largest observed differences between BR and non-BR years occurring around mid-September. However, differences in BR frequency between sites were not fully explained by the available measures of cold exposure. Instead, our findings are consistent with the hypothesis that BR formation depends on the temporal alignment between cooling events and a vulnerable phase of latewood lignification, potentially contributing to site-specific differences in sensitivity to cooling. Consequently, similar cooling events may trigger lignification failure in one population but not another, leading to different anatomical responses under comparable regional climate forcing. Our findings suggest that local controls on wood formation dynamics may mediate how climatic forcing is translated into anatomical responses, highlighting developmental timing as a potential control on tree sensitivity to short-term temperature variability at the northern climatic limit of Scots pine.
