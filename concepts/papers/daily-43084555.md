# Pyrethroid insecticide exposure enhances the ability of Helicoverpa armigera to counter host plant defenses through symbiotic bacteria.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2026)
- **PMID**: [43084555](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43084555/)
- **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2634296123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2634296123)
- **第一作者**: Meng Gu
- **作者**: Meng Gu, Qiong Yang, Amosi Leonard Mbuji, Jing Yu, Rujing Kang, Lulu Zhang, Yuhao Cai, Laixin Luo

## 摘要
Like plant toxins, synthetic insecticides can exert strong selective pressures on pest populations. While responses to both plant toxins and synthetic insecticides can depend on adaptive changes in the nuclear genotype of the pest species, increasing evidence suggests that symbiotic microbes, particularly gut microbes, can also influence these responses. In the cotton bollworm, Helicoverpa armigera, concentrations of the symbiont Corynebacterium glyciniphilum increased in moth strains that were treated with the pyrethroid pesticide lambda-cyhalothrin (LCH), and they were also high in LCH-resistant strains and in populations exposed to 2-Tridecanone (2-TD), a secondary defense compound that is present in the Solanaceae. This increase enhanced the ability of H. armigera to overcome not only the pesticide but also a key defense mechanism in the solanaceous plants. In field populations of H. armigera, the abundance of C. glyciniphilum was lowest in populations feeding on nonsolanaceous plants that were sensitive to LCH. Based on gene knockouts, the underlying reason for the enhanced defensive capability provided by C. glyciniphilum was attributed to two detoxification enzyme genes (a P450 cytochrome and an esterase) which can metabolize both LCH and 2-TD into nontoxic degradation products. These findings suggest that a common mechanism mediated by a symbiont contributes to the insect's capacity to develop resistance to both a pesticide and a host plant toxin.
