### 核心发现
1. These results suggest that part of the chemical nitrogen fertilizer can be eﬀectively replaced by organic fertilization without reducing crop productivity.
2. These results suggest that seaweed extract may enhance nutrient uptake eﬃciency and utilization, allowing partial substitution of chemical nitrogen fertilizers without compromising productivity.
3. These ﬁndings conﬁrm that organic fertilization plays a key role in improving both tomato yield and fruit nutritional quality (Figure 2; Table 7), which is consistent with previous studies conducted on tomato crops (Santaniello et al., 2017; Tao et al., 2022; Leﬁet al., 2023).
4. Although nitrogen is essential for tomato growth and fruit development, its excessive use has resulted in serious environmental and agronomic problems, including nitrate leaching, soil degradation, and reduced microbial diversity (Odegard and Van der Voet, 2014; Suchithra et al., 2022; Agaba et al., 2023).
5. Understanding the relationships among growth, yield, and fruit quality traits is essential for optimizing nutrient management strategies in tomato production systems.
6. 4.7 Study novelty and practical implications The present study provides new evidence on the eﬀective of integrating reduced mineral nitrogen fertilization with organic amendments and Ascophyllum nodosum based bio- stimulants in tomato production systems.
7. Importantly, the results indicate that mineral nitrogen inputs can be reduced by approximately 25% without compromising yield productivity, while simultaneously improving nutrient use eﬃciency and fruit nutrient composition.
