# Halophyte-derived rhizosphere microbiome transplantation improves physiological homeostasis and salt tolerance in alfalfa.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Microbiological research (2027)
- **PMID**: [42981905](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42981905/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.micres.2026.128801](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micres.2026.128801)
- **第一作者**: Jinpeng Hu
- **作者**: Jinpeng Hu, Yuanyuan He, Wenjing Cui, Li Wei, Li Li, Li Wang, Hend Alwathnani, Christopher Rensing

## 摘要
Halophyte-associated microbiomes are promising reservoirs of stress-adapted microorganisms, yet the consequences of transferring whole rhizosphere communities to phylogenetically distinct crops have remained unclear. Here, we evaluated rhizosphere microbiome transplantation (RMT) from Suaeda salsa to alfalfa (Medicago sativa) in a 2 × 2 factorial experiment comprising control and RMT treatments at 0 and 300 mM NaCl. Plant physiological responses were integrated with metagenomic profiling, community assembly analysis, microbial association networks, and functional gene analyses. Under salinity, RMT increased plant height, leaf area, and both shoot and root biomass, reduced shoot Na+ accumulation, and increased the shoot K+/Na+ ratio. RMT also reduced H2O2, malondialdehyde, and relative electrical conductivity while increasing antioxidant enzyme activities and osmolyte concentrations, whereas gas-exchange parameters showed no significant recovery. RMT altered rhizosphere community composition and assembly and was associated with greater network connectivity and simulated robustness. Metagenomic KO profiles showed higher relative representation of functions correlated to osmoprotection, ion transport, redox protection, and cell-envelope processes. These microbial functional shifts coincided with the physiological domains most strongly improved by RMT but did not establish direct causal microbial mechanisms. Overall, halophyte-derived RMT improved alfalfa performance under salinity, with coordinated physiological recovery accompanied by substantial reorganization of the recipient rhizosphere, supporting further evaluation of whole-community microbiome transplantation for crop production in saline soils.
