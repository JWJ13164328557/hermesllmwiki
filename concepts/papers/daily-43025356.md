# Glycine supply regulates the nitrogen metabolism, photosynthesis, and growth of apple rootstock under low temperature.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology and biochemistry : PPB (2026)
- **PMID**: [43025356](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025356/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111954](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111954)
- **第一作者**: Yang Liu
- **作者**: Yang Liu, Hui Yong, Sijun Qin, Deguo Lyu, Lijie Li, Chenghui Li

## 摘要
Low temperature impairs nitrogen uptake and transport in roots, thereby limiting apple growth and biomass accumulation. Glycine (Gly), an organic nitrogen source, has been reported to enhance plant stress tolerance; however, its role in regulating nitrogen metabolism in apple under low-temperature conditions remains unclear. In this study, Malus baccata Borkh., a widely used apple rootstock in northern China, was used to investigate the effects of low temperature and exogenous Gly application on photosynthesis, plant growth, tissue nitrogen accumulation, nitrogen metabolism enzyme activities, and the expression of nitrogen-related genes. Our results showed that low temperature inhibited root nitrogen uptake and assimilation, decreased root activity and leaf photosynthetic capacity, and consequently restricted root development and biomass accumulation. Exogenous Gly partially alleviated these adverse effects by enhancing the activities of NR, GS, GOGAT, GDH, GOT, and GPT and upregulating genes involved in nitrogen transport and assimilation, including NRT1.1, NRT2.4, NRT2.5, NRT2.7, AMT1.2, AMT2.1, NR, NiR, and GOGAT. These changes promoted nitrate-N and total nitrogen accumulation, reduced excessive ammonium-N accumulation, and increased the contents of glutamate, glutamine, and aspartate, thereby mitigating the suppression of nitrogen metabolism caused by low temperature stress. As a result, exogenous Gly application improved root activity, photosynthetic performance, and plant growth under low temperature, although a specific role for Gly beyond its nitrogen supply cannot be excluded without an isonitrogenous control. These findings provide a theoretical basis for efficient nutrient management of apple cultivation in cool regions.
