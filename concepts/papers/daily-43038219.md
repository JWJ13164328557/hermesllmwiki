# The complete chloroplast genome of Chrysanthemum morifolium cv. 'Chuju' and comparative chloroplast genomes of the members of the subtribe artemisiinae.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: PloS one (2026)
- **PMID**: [43038219](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43038219/)
- **DOI**: [10.1371/journal.pone.0362406](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0362406)
- **第一作者**: Feng Wen
- **作者**: Feng Wen, Nan Zhang, Xiaozhu Wu, Lisi He, Suqing Zhu, Ruihua Fei, Wenjing Jiang, Huijing Jiang

## 摘要
Chrysanthemum morifolium cv. 'Chuju' ('Chuju') is a perennial herbaceous plant of the Asteraceae family, renowned for its medicinal value and recognized as a prominent "Daodi" (geo-authentic) herbal medicines in Anhui province, China. Despite its significant economic and pharmacological importance, the genomic information of 'Chuju' remains limited, hindering in-depth research on its evolution, species identification, and molecular breeding. In this study, we successfully assembled and characterized the complete chloroplast (cp) genome of 'Chuju', spanning 151,025 bp without gaps and containing 129 genes. Comparative genomic analyses across 20 species within the subtribe Artemisiinae revealed a highly conserved quadripartite structure, alongside distinct structural dynamics such as the premature truncation of the rps19 gene due to IR/LSC boundary contraction. Furthermore, through sliding window analysis, we identified and validated six highly variable loci (matK, petD, infA, trnN-GUU, rpl32, and ycf1) as robust candidate DNA barcodes. Pairwise Ka/Ks analysis detected significant positive selection and rapid evolution in specific functional genes (including accD, matK, atpF, rps12, and ycf2), which may underpin the adaptive evolution and unique secondary metabolite accumulation of 'Chuju' in its native habitat. Crucially, phylogenomic reconstructions incorporating 52 species demonstrated that complete plastome sequences possess superior resolving power over concatenated CDS matrices for deciphering shallow evolutionary nodes. The whole-plastome phylogeny illuminated a deeply intermingled, reticulate evolutionary history within the Chrysanthemum-Ajania complex, providing compelling genomic evidence that traditional generic boundaries based solely on morphology require critical re-evaluation. Ultimately, this study provides robust genomic resources and evolutionary insights, laying a solid foundation for the germplasm identification, conservation, and molecular breeding of medicinal chrysanthemums and the broader subtribe Artemisiinae.
