# A herbivore-induced plant volatile facilitates group foraging in locusts.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2026)
- **PMID**: [43077492](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077492/)
- **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2616731123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2616731123)
- **第一作者**: Lingzhi Shi
- **作者**: Lingzhi Shi, Jinmeng Guo, Jinxi Yuan, Yan Yan, Jiancai Li, Guirong Wang, Bill S Hansson, Hetan Chang

## 摘要
Plants have evolved sophisticated defense strategies against herbivory, often releasing volatile compounds upon attack to deter further feeding. Here, we demonstrate the induced volatile S-linalool promotes phase- and stage-specific group foraging in the migratory locust (Locusta migratoria). We show that S-linalool, a volatile whose emission increases more than 10-fold following damage by gregarious nymphs, acts as a potent attractant that drives other nymphs to aggregate and feed on previously attacked rice plants. Mechanistically, we identified the olfactory receptor LmOR49 that detects S-linalool and used genome editing to make this receptor nonfunctional, thereby abolishing both electrophysiological responses and behavioral attraction. We also inactivated rice genes required for S-linalool biosynthesis, abolishing its emission, rendering plants unattractive, and disrupting locust feeding preference. Although feeding on previously attacked plants provides no detectable direct fitness benefit under our experimental conditions, prior locust feeding suppresses the accumulation of deterrent metabolites, potentially increasing plant palatability for subsequent feeding. S-linalool thus serves as an olfactory cue that guides gregarious nymphs toward previously attacked, more palatable plants. Collectively, these findings uncover a comprehensive mechanism whereby a plant defense volatile is exploited by an insect herbivore to promote group foraging, broadening our understanding of plant-insect interactions and suggesting molecular targets for locust control.
