### 核心发现
1. Collectively, these findings suggest altered transcriptional regulation of HDL- associated cholesterol trafficking in small intestinal enterocytes in formula-fed piglets.
2. Here, through RNA-seq-based transcriptome analysis of the liver and small intestine, we found a significant higher expression of cholesterol biosynthesis genes in formula-fed piglets compared to their sow-fed counterparts (Figure 1).
3. Together, these findings suggest that formula feeding alters transcriptional regulation of cholesterol esterification and chylomicron-associated lipid transport pathways.
4. In contrast, MYLIP (myosin regulatory light chain interacting protein; IDOL), an E3 ubiquitin ligase that targets LDLR for degradation, was lower in formula-fed piglets in the pooled small-intestine analysis.
5. Together, these findings suggest altered transcriptional regulation of LDLR-associated cholesterol uptake pathways in the small intestine with formula feeding.
6. Hepatic LDLR, a transcriptional target of SREBP228 that mediates uptake of LDL particles and apoE-rich chylomicron remnants, was significantly higher in formula-fed piglets than in sow-fed controls, consistent with the observed increase in hepatic SREBF2 expression.
7. Collectively, these transcriptional changes may suggest a reduced biliary cholesterol export and increased biliary cholesterol reuptake in formula-fed piglets.
8. Collectively, these changes reflect an adaptive response linking cholesterol availability and synthesis to enhanced conservation of bile acids through increased intestinal recycling.
