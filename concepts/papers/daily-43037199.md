# Physiological analyses and transcriptomic provide insight into the embryogenic callus proliferation mechanism in Pinus kesiya var. langbianensis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Tree physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43037199](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037199/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/treephys/tpag173](https://doi.org/10.1093/treephys/tpag173)
- **第一作者**: Yaqi Qin
- **作者**: Yaqi Qin, Jiasong Yang, Xia Zhao, Guihua Huang, Jianmin Xu, Qiang Han

## 摘要
Somatic embryogenesis is an ideal means for plant propagation and genetic improvement in conifers, yet the low proliferation rate of embryogenic callus remains a major bottleneck, particularly in Pinus kesiya var. langbianensis. In this study, we established an embryogenic callus induction system for P. kesiya var. langbianensis and selected two cell lines (CL16 and CL26) exhibiting contrasting proliferation efficiencies. We investigated the dynamic changes in endogenous soluble sugar contents and sugar metabolism-related enzyme activities during callus proliferation, and performed comparative transcriptomic analysis to elucidate the underlying regulatory mechanisms. The results revealed that embryogenic callus with higher proliferation efficiency exhibited lower sucrose content but higher glucose and fructose levels, suggesting extensive sucrose hydrolysis to fuel cell division. A total of 12,111 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) were identified between the two cell lines, with KEGG enrichment analysis highlighting "starch and sucrose metabolism" and "plant hormone signal transduction" as key pathways. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) identified a proliferation ability- associated module of 143 genes, which was enriched in sugar metabolism pathways. Among the hub genes, members of the B3-ARF transcription factor family, including ARF19, ARF16, ARF3, and ARF15 were significantly upregulated in the high-proliferation line during early callus proliferation, along with SERK2, AP2, and PK2. These findings suggest that the coordinated regulation of sugar metabolism and auxin signaling drives embryogenic callus proliferation, providing a theoretical basis for clonal propagation and germplasm innovation of pines through somatic embryogenesis.
