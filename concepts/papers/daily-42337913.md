## 摘要
Singlet oxygen (1O2) is a highly reactive, chloroplast-derived reactive oxygen species that functions not only as a damaging oxidant but also as a signaling molecule regulating gene expression, stress responses, and development. The EXECUTER (EX) family, comprising EX1 and its paralog EX2, is central to 1O2-triggered retrograde signaling in Arabidopsis thaliana. Recent mechanistic advances have revealed that EX1 undergoes post-translational oxidation and proteolytic turnover and that EX1-derived small peptides interact with TOC/TIC translocons to activate signaling cascades. EX1 also has unexpected functions in darkness, during which it modulates auxin-responsive gene networks. Comparative genomic analyses further show that EX-like proteins containing the conserved singlet oxygen sensor domain are present in green algae. By contrast, the complete EX architecture, including both the singlet oxygen sensor and UvrBC-like domains, emerged in charophytes and subsequently diversified across major plant lineages. In this review, we present classical and emerging insights into EX-mediated signaling, highlight evolutionary patterns of EX proteins across the plant kingdom, and discuss how understanding EX1/EX2 dynamics may inform strategies to engineer reactive oxygen species perception, stress tolerance, and crop growth. We propose a unified model of EX-mediated 1O2 sensing and outline key unresolved questions to guide future research.

### 核心发现
1. Recent mechanistic advances have revealed that EX1 undergoes post-translational oxidation and proteolytic turnover and that EX1-derived small peptides interact with TOC/TIC translocons to activate signaling cascades.
2. Collectively, these findings indicate that distinct suborganellar sources of 1 O 2 engage mechanistically separable signaling pathways.
3. In vivo substrate-trapping experiments have provided evidence that ClpC1 interacts with EX1 and its paralog EX2: ClpC1-TRAP, a mutant ClpC1 variant that binds but cannot release its substrates because of defective ATP hydrolysis, captured both EX1 and EX2, either directly or as part of associated complexes (; ).
4. Consistent with these findings, previous co-immunoprecipitation analyses showed that EX1 interacts with both the FtsH protease and ClpC1/2 chaperones ( ).
5. Collectively, the dynamic and context-specific interplay between EX1 and EX2 enables plants to fine-tune their stress signaling responses.
6. A recent study revealed that the UvrBC domain of EX1 interacts with the plastid translocon components TOC33 and TIC110 ( ).
7. Cell fractionation and co-immunoprecipitation analyses further revealed that the UvrBC domain localizes to the nucleus and interacts with the transcription factors WRKY18 and WRKY40 (hereafter referred to as WRKY18/40) ( ).
8. Taken together, the proteolytic degradation of EX1 not only removes oxidized EX1 but is also proposed to generate a chloroplast-derived danger signal, most likely in the form of one or more small peptides that are transmitted to the nucleus via the TOC/TIC translocon machinery.
