# ABA-Ethylene Antagonism Shapes Endodermal Barriers and Potassium Fluxes in Tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum L.) Roots.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant, cell & environment (2026)
- **PMID**: [43042639](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43042639/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/pce.71028](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.71028)
- **第一作者**: Yunxiang Xu
- **作者**: Yunxiang Xu, Madan Lal Kolhi, Yingying Zhu, Liwen Zhang, Yingchen Gu, Hao Zong, Jiang Liu, Mingfeng Yang

## 摘要
Potassium (K+) is an essential macronutrient for plant growth, development and stress tolerance. However, limited K+ retention in roots remains a major constraint on nutrient-use efficiency. The endodermis functions as a critical regulatory interface for radial ion transport by establishing a suberin lamellae (SL)-based secondary diffusion barrier, yet the hormonal mechanisms governing endodermal plasticity and K+ homeostasis remain unexplored. Here we demonstrated that abscisic acid (ABA) and ethylene 1-aminocyclopropane-1-carboxylic acid (ACC) antagonistically modulate endodermal suberization and spatial pattern of K+ efflux in tobacco roots under K+-limiting conditions. We found that ABA enhanced suberization, extending the suberized zone close to the root apex, reduced apoplastic leakage, and increased translocation to the shoot. In contrast, ACC suppressed suberin deposition, maintaining a more permeable endodermis and increasing K+ efflux. Integrated suberin imaging and non-invasive self-referencing microsensor technique (SRMT) showed that K+ efflux patterns were tightly coupled to endodermal status, with ABA-induced suberization generating a localized restriction of K+ flux near the site of barrier formation. These findings establish hormonal regulation of endodermal plasticity as a key mechanism integrating root anatomical remodelling with nutrient acquisition and retention and provide a framework for improving K+-use efficiency and crop resilience under fluctuating soil nutrient availability.
