# An efficient gene delivery method utilizing the regenerative capacity of alfalfa (Medicago sativa).

## 摘要
Alfalfa (Medicago sativa), an autotetraploid species, is a globally important forage crop. Traditional genetic transformation methods predominantly rely on leaf-derived callus, a process that is complex, inefficient, and particularly challenging to generate stable transgenic lines in polyploid plants. To address these limitations, we developed an efficient transformation system that bypasses callus induction by directly exploiting stem regeneration. Our protocol enables the generation of stable gene-edited alfalfa plants from shoot tips. It accommodates stem explants from various developmental stages and significantly reduces the time required. Moreover, this approach was effective across multiple alfalfa cultivars, and preliminary proof-of-concept experiments in Nicotiana tabacum and Solanum lycopersicum suggested potential transferability beyond alfalfa. This study represents a substantial advancement in perennial plant transformation and provides a robust and practical platform for optimizing stem regeneration-based genetic modification systems.

### 核心发现
1. These results show that the shoot‐tip transformation route supports visual identification and recovery of stable transgenic alfalfa plants within approximately 2 months.
2. Sequence analysis of representative line #Mspds‐5 identified substitutions, frameshift deletions, and an in‐frame deletion at the target region (Figure; Figure ).
3. Sequence analysis of representative lines #Mssig6‐10, #Msdg1‐13, and #Msbri1‐32 confirmed multiple deletions and frameshift mutations at the target regions (Figure ).
4. Regeneration and editing efficiencies varied by target and cultivar (Figure; Table ).
5. Representative line # Msdg1‐1 showed a dwarf phenotype and carried multiple edited alleles at the MsDG1 target site (Figure ).
6. These findings show that mature stem segments provide a complementary route for both transgene expression and genome editing.
7. The variation in editing efficiency among MsSIG6, MsDG1, and MsBRI1 is consistent with the target dependence of CRISPR/Cas9 activity.
8. Allelic sequence variation adds another layer of target dependence in autotetraploid alfalfa, where complete mutants require editing of multiple homologous alleles.
