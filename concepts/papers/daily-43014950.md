# Bottom-up design of a synthetic microbial community (SynCom) for cadmium remediation and growth promotion in rice: From in vitro construction to soil-pot validation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of environmental sciences (China) (2026)
- **PMID**: [43014950](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014950/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jes.2026.02.029](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jes.2026.02.029)
- **第一作者**: Chaoqun Yan
- **作者**: Chaoqun Yan, Huakang Liu, Jiaxin Liu, Shiyu Ma, Shulin Zhang, Xianghan Chen, Zaijin Sun, Heng Xu

## 摘要
Despite significant progress having been made in understanding the metal tolerance of plants, the specific mechanism of the role of the rhizosphere microbial community in regulating the resistance of rice to cadmium (Cd) remains unclear. To address this, we adopted a functional bottom-up approach. First, we isolated 34 culturable bacterial strains from Cd-contaminated rice rhizosphere soil. Through a targeted functional screen, we selected strains based on complementary traits: Cd tolerance, indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) production, phosphorus solubilization and siderophore production. This led to the construction of an initial synthetic community (SynCom1, SC1) featuring Bacillus and six other synergistic members. Subsequent in vitro co-culture and metabolic interaction analysis refined SC1 into a simplified and more effective 4 strains (SC). Hydroponic experiments confirmed that SC significantly enhanced rice growth and stress tolerance under Cd exposure. Soil pot trials further demonstrated that SC inoculation altered soil enzyme activities and was associated with distinct changes in the indigenous bacterial community composition. These changes coincided with improved rice physiological responses and a marked increase in Cd remediation efficiency. This study validates a strategy for constructing functional SynCom based on screening and combining indigenous, trait-specific bacteria, offering a promising, sustainable solution for mitigating agricultural heavy metal pollution.
