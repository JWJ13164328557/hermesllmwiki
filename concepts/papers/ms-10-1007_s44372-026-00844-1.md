Collectively, these examples span editing targets from guard cells to vascular tissue to root meristems, illustrating the value of a multi-target precision editing strategy though, as the OsNAC2 case shows, each target’s evidence base should be evaluated on its own terms rather than assumed to be uniformly strong.
2. Training populations that capture the full range of adaptive water-use variation are essential for building accurate prediction models; including indica, japonica, and rain­ fed landrace accessions in these panels broadens the allelic diversity represented and improves applicability across target environments [17, 43].
3. Alongside marker-assisted selection, base editing and prime editing enable targeted allelic refinement with minimal off-target effects.
4. Genomic selection (GS) models that incorporate environmental covariates have been shown to improve prediction accuracy across diverse rainfed environments, and precision genome editing allows targeted allelic refinement with minimal off-target effects [20].
5. Such panels improve prediction accuracy and selection efficiency when used to train genomic selection mod­ els [43–45], support the identification of targets for functional validation and genome editing [46–48], and reduce ascertainment bias in pangenome-based structural variant detection [40].
6. Taken together, this convergent genomic and functional evidence sup­ ports OsNAC14 as a high-confidence candidate for marker-assisted selection or preci­ sion genome editing.
