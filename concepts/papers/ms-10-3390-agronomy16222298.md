# Soil Alkalinity Reduces Soybean Yield by Inhibiting Vegetative Growth and Yield Component Formation in Saline-Sodic Soils

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Agronomy ()
- **DOI**: [10.3390/agronomy16222298](https://doi.org/10.3390/agronomy16222298)
- **作者**: Yuanhao Li, Lei Tian, Shaoqi Huangfu, Fengyi Zhang, Baishun Liu
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要
Saline-sodic soils are potential cropland resources for soybean production, but soil salinity and alkalinity limit soybean yield improvement. Previous studies have shown that saline-sodic conditions affect soybean growth and yield formation. However, further quantitative analysis is needed to clarify the relative independent contributions of different yield components and soil factors to soybean yield variation, as well as the pathways linking soil factors, vegetative growth, yield components, and yield. We surveyed soybean growth, yield components, yield, and soil salinity–alkalinity and nutrients at field sites in Tongliao (A) and Hinggan League (B), China. Hierarchical partitioning (HP) and partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM) quantified relative contributions and assessed pathways linking soil factors, vegetative growth, yield components, and yield. Pod number per unit area was the most important yield component, with relative independent contributions of 48.7% in A and 51.2% in B. Soil pH, CO32− + HCO3−, and Na+ were the main soil constraints, with pH contributing most in A and Na+ in B. PLS-SEM indicated that CO32− + HCO3− increased soil pH, inhibited vegetative growth, and reduced pod number per unit area and 100-seed weight, ultimately decreasing yield. Therefore, future research on improving soybean yield in saline-sodic soils should focus on the degree of soil alkalization and pod number per unit area.
