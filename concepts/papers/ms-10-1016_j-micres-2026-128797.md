# Genome-guided development of a halotolerant endophytic consortium-based biofertilizer enhances rice plant growth and salinity tolerance with reduced chemical fertilizer inputs.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Microbiological Research (2027)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.micres.2026.128797](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micres.2026.128797)
- **作者**: Gargi Das, Saikat Jana, Paltu Kumar Dhal
- **来源**: crossref


## 摘要
Soil salinity is a major abiotic stress limiting rice productivity worldwide and necessitates sustainable alternatives to intensive chemical fertilizer use. We developed a genome-guided halotolerant endophytic bacterial consortium to enhance rice growth and resilience in salt-affected agroecosystems. A total of 101 endophytic bacteria were screened using a multi-phase strategy based on salt tolerance, plant growth-promoting (PGP) traits, retention of PGP functions under saline stress, and enhancement of rice seed germination under increasing salinity. Seven compatible strains were selected and identified as Phytobacter diazotrophicus, Mangrovibacter phragmitis (two strains), Brachybacterium paraconglomeratum, Priestia flexa, Micrococcus luteus, and Staphylococcus haemolyticus, for formulation into a talc-based biofertilizer. Whole-genome sequencing revealed genetic determinants linked to plant growth promotion and salinity adaptation, including genes associated with indole-3-acetic acid biosynthesis, phosphate solubilisation, nitrogen fixation, siderophore production, exopolysaccharide synthesis, osmoprotection, ion homeostasis, antioxidant defence, and plant colonisation. In vitro assays showed that the biofertilizer significantly enhanced rice seedling growth and biomass under saline conditions compared with individual strains and liquid consortium inoculation. Pot experiments showed that combining biofertilizer with a 75% reduced chemical fertilizer dose significantly improved plant growth, physiological performance, nutrient uptake, and stress tolerance under both 4 and 6 dS m⁻¹ salinity. Moreover, biofertilizer application improved soil nutrient status while reducing sodium accumulation and oxidative stress, with the magnitude of these benefits greater under salinity stress. These findings demonstrate the potential of genome-enabled microbial biofertilizers for sustainable rice cultivation in saline environments with reduced chemical fertiliser inputs.
