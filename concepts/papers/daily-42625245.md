# Integrating genomic selection into potato breeding: A comparison of genotyping platforms and cross-environmental predictions.

## 摘要
Genomic selection (GS) is a powerful tool for accelerating genetic gain in potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) breeding, particularly for complex traits. In this study, three practical aspects of GS implementation in a potato breeding program were examined. First, the predictive ability of GS models was evaluated for three key traits (total yield, marketable yield, and specific gravity) using two elite potato populations with shared ancestry, tested across seven location-year environments. Two cross-validation strategies were used to reflect practical breeding scenarios: predicting unphenotyped lines in known environments and predicting clonal performance in unknown environments. Four models were evaluated, two of which included genotype-by-environment interactions. Tuber specific gravity showed higher and more consistent prediction accuracy across environments, supporting the evidence that it is a more stable trait. Second, the impact of genotyping platforms and marker density on GS performance were examined, as the two populations were genotyped using two different targeted sequencing platforms: Flex-seq (22K loci) and DArTag (4K loci), sharing ∼4K common loci, that allowed direct comparison. Prediction accuracies were comparable across platforms, indicating that both are suitable for GS implementation, with the choice depending on breeding goals, cost, and throughput considerations. Finally, the long-term impact of GS on genetic gain was assessed through stochastic simulation of a 30-year breeding pipeline, comparing conventional phenotypic selection with GS-assisted selection scenarios. GS scenarios achieved higher long-term genetic gains, though practical deployment should consider both cost and breeding objectives. Our findings for the three aspects of this study support the integration of GS into potato breeding programs, while highlighting key considerations for its effective implementation.

Potato breeders want to rapidly develop better varieties, but this is challenging because yield and quality are influenced by both genetics and the environment. In this study, the effectiveness of genomic selection (GS), a tool in which DNA information is used to predict plant performance, was evaluated in potatoes. First, three important traits were evaluated using two potato populations grown across seven environments. Tuber specific gravity was predicted most reliably, showing it is a more stable trait across environments. Second, two DNA marker platforms, a high‐density and a low‐density system, were compared, and similar prediction results were obtained from both. Finally, to understand its long‐term impact, a 30‐year breeding pipeline was simulated, and higher genetic gains were achieved with GS compared to the conventional approach alone. These results highlight GS as a practical tool that can help potato breeding programs make faster and more informed decisions.

### 核心发现
1. These results suggest that GS is particularly well suited for traits with greater genetic stability, enabling effective early‐stage selection even when phenotypic data originate from environments that differ from the target production region.
2. Collectively, these results indicate that once marker density is sufficient to capture genetic relatedness among individuals, adding markers provides little improvement in genomic prediction accuracy for the population evaluated here.
3. When these genotypes are tested in subtropical states, G × E interactions can be more pronounced not solely due to environmental stress but because the genetic material is not well‐adapted to the target environment, amplifying the interaction effects.
4. It reflects real‐world breeding scenarios where genotypes have been tested in some environments but not in the target environment.
5. The Flex‐seq panel yielded more markers than DArTag, as it targets more genomic regions (22K probes) and can detect multiple variants within a single probe region.
6. To effectively integrate GS into a conventional breeding pipeline, it is essential to optimize the inputs used in GS.
