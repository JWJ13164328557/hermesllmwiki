# Spatially integrated imaging analyses of copper-induced cell death in the root apex of Solanum lycopersicum.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Planta (2026)
- **PMID**: [43037732](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037732/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00425-026-05221-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00425-026-05221-6)
- **第一作者**: Sakshi Chouhan
- **作者**: Sakshi Chouhan, Shilpa Chandra, Abdul Salam, Chayan Kanti Nandi

## 摘要
This study demonstrates organelle dysfunction, oxidative stress, and cell death under Cu exposure, providing a basis for developing future strategies to mitigate Cu toxicity in plants. Copper (Cu) is an essential micronutrient required for photosynthesis, respiration, antioxidant defense, and cellular metabolism. However, excessive Cu accumulation disrupts cellular homeostasis and can trigger programmed cell death (PCD). Despite extensive evidence of Cu toxicity, the coordinated cellular events underlying Cu-induced cell death in plants remain poorly defined. Here, we performed an integrated imaging study to investigate the Cu-induced cell-death pathway in Solanum lycopersicum (tomato) root apex using confocal and super-resolution imaging. Cu exposure progressively disrupted mitochondrial function and altered cytochrome c (Cyt c) distribution, followed by increased intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS), indicating the development of oxidative stress. These changes were accompanied by altered SnRK1/AMPK-TOR/mTOR signaling and enhanced nuclear NRF2 immunoreactive fluorescence, suggesting coordinated metabolic and redox responses. As Cu stress progressed, nuclear architecture and chromatin organization were markedly altered, with distinct reorganization in chromatin. Increased autophagosome formation was also observed, accompanied by a concentration-dependent increase in dead cells in the root apex, indicating progression from cellular stress toward cell death. This study provides an integrated spatial framework for Cu-induced PCD and identifies potential cellular indicators for monitoring Cu toxicity and evaluating mitigation strategies in crops.
