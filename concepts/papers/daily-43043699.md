# Activation of ROPGEFs by a malectin-like receptor kinase is involved in tip growth regulation in Physcomitrium patens.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences of the United States of America (2026)
- **PMID**: [43043699](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43043699/)
- **DOI**: [10.1073/pnas.2620413123](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2620413123)
- **第一作者**: Ruohan Lyu
- **作者**: Ruohan Lyu, Jingtong Ruan, Ying Chen, Haoyu Tian, Peishan Yi

## 摘要
Rho-type GTPases of plants (ROPs) play essential roles in polarized cell growth, cell division, and organogenesis. Their activation depends on two types of guanine nucleotide exchange factors (GEFs): the plant-specific PRONE-domain-containing ROPGEFs and DOCK-type SPKGEFs. While ROPGEFs have been proposed to be autoinhibited in pollen and activated by receptor-like kinases (RLKs), the underlying mechanisms remain incompletely understood. Using the moss Physcomitrium patens, we show that moss ROPGEF4 and ROPGEF6 exhibit low intrinsic GEF activity and are not autoinhibited by non-PRONE regions, whereas the catalytic domain of SPK1 and SPK6 is highly active. Through a candidate screen, we identified MLRK1, a malectin-like receptor kinase, as a ROPGEF-interacting protein. MLRK1 directly phosphorylates ROPGEF4 and ROPGEF6 and stimulates their GEF activities in vitro. These effects are attenuated by substituting key phosphosites (Ser231 in ROPGEF4 and Ser524/Thr526 in ROPGEF6) with nonphosphorylatable residues. Structural modeling, pull-down assays, and functional analyses indicate that phosphorylation enhances ROPGEF-ROP interactions and promotes the membrane localization of ROPGEF4/6. Furthermore, ROPGEFs are not essential for polarized cell growth on their own but act redundantly with SPK2. At the apical membrane, ROPGEFs appear to activate ROPs, reduce their diffusion, promote actin filament assembly and vesicle secretion, and ultimately facilitate cell expansion and growth. Collectively, our findings provide functional evidence that RLK-ROPGEF-ROP signaling module has emerged in early land plants and is involved in cell growth regulation.
