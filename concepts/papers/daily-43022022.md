# Genomic and biochemical dissection of stripe rust resistance in Indian wheat varieties under hotspot conditions in the north-western Himalayas, India.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Molecular breeding : new strategies in plant improvement (2026)
- **PMID**: [43022022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43022022/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s11032-026-01733-x](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11032-026-01733-x)
- **第一作者**: Nikita Aggarwal
- **作者**: Nikita Aggarwal, Sandeep Kumar, Mukesh Rathore, Farkhandah Jan, Mohd Tahir, Ayushi Singh, Waseem Ali Dar, Tariq Rasool Rather

## 摘要
UNLABELLED: Stripe rust, caused by Puccinia striiformis, poses a major threat to wheat production, particularly in hotspot regions such as the north-western Himalayas. In this study, a diverse panel of 252 Indian wheat varieties was evaluated for stripe rust resistance at both seedling and adult plant stages across eight environments in the Kashmir Valley. Significant phenotypic variation was observed for disease severity (DS), infection type (IT), and seedling response (SD), with several genotypes exhibiting stable resistance across environments. GWAS using 1,887 high-quality DArT-SNP markers and multi-locus models (FarmCPU and BLINK) revealed 26 stable MTAs, of which 13 Bonferroni-corrected loci explaining up to 13.76% of the observed phenotypic variation. Several MTAs colocalized with previously reported QTLs/MTAs, whereas others represent potentially novel genomic regions. In-silico candidate gene prioritization identified several putative defense-related genes, of which 12 showed consistent differential expression across multiple publicly available transcriptomic datasets. These prioritized genes represent promising targets for future functional validation studies. Biochemical profiling revealed that resistant genotypes presented significantly higher activity of antioxidant enzymes and lower accumulation of oxidative stress markers compared with susceptible genotypes, indicating a more efficient reactive oxygen species scavenging system. Overall, this integrative approach combining multi-environment phenotyping, GWAS, biochemical profiling, and in-silico expression analysis provides valuable insights into the genetic and physiological basis of stripe rust resistance. The identified resistant genotypes, MTAs, and prioritized candidate genes provide important resources for further validation and marker-assisted breeding aimed at developing durable stripe rust-resistant wheat cultivars.
