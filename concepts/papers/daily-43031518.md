# Agronomic potential of black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens) frass combined with Bacillus spp. for soybean seedling establishment.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of environmental science and health. Part. B, Pesticides, food contaminants, and agricultural wastes (2026)
- **PMID**: [43031518](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43031518/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/03601234.2026.2759894](https://doi.org/10.1080/03601234.2026.2759894)
- **第一作者**: Dennis Ricardo Cabral Cruz
- **作者**: Dennis Ricardo Cabral Cruz, Fenelon Lourenço de Sousa Santos, Itamar Rosa Teixeira, Zeuxis Rosa Evangelista, Isabella Silva Roque de Souza, Maria Eduarda Moreira Lopes, Gabryella Santana Lopes, Bárbara Rodrigues de Melo Barros

## 摘要
Black soldier fly (Hermetia illucens) frass has gained attention as an organic fertilizer due to its nutrient content and bioactive compounds. However, information on its interaction with microbial bioinputs during early soybean development remains limited. This study evaluated the effects of frass rates combined with a commercial consortium of Bacillus subtilis, B. megaterium, and B. aryabhattai on soybean seedling growth. The experiment was conducted under greenhouse conditions in a completely randomized design using a 5 × 2 factorial arrangement, with five frass rates (0, 2.5, 5.0, 7.5, and 10.0 t ha-1) in the absence and presence of microbial inoculation, with ten replicates. At 14 days after sowing, root morphological traits, fresh and dry biomass, and seedling visual appearance were evaluated. Intermediate frass rates promoted greater root development and biomass accumulation, whereas 10.0 t ha-1 reduced seedling growth, particularly without inoculation. Bacillus inoculation improved shoot growth and biomass accumulation at higher frass rates. Overall, intermediate frass rates showed greater potential to promote early soybean growth, while the Bacillus consortium modified plant responses to increasing frass rates. Further field studies are required to establish effective and agronomically safe application rates.
