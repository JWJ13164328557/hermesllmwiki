# Loss of StLOX2a confers an optimized semi-dwarf plant architecture without tuber yield penalty under controlled growth conditions in potato.

## 摘要
Plant architecture (PA) is an important agronomic trait in potato that is closely related to regional adaptation and cultivation fitness for yield optimization. Here, we identified an optimized semi-dwarf mutant, sd6-23, from a potato T-DNA mutant population, with reduced internode length but increased internode thickness and leaf area without tuber weight penalty under controlled growth conditions. Loss of StLOX2a, which encodes a member of the 13-lipoxygenase family, was identified for sd6-23 by whole-genome resequencing and CRISPR/Cas9 approaches. StLOX2a is predominantly expressed in leaves, and its protein is localized to the chloroplast. The loss of StLOX2a resulted in an increase in the number of leaf epidermal cells and stem pith parenchyma cells, but a decrease in the length of stem epidermal cells. Integrated transcriptome and hormone analyses in leaves and stems revealed that (i) StLOX2a mediates the biosynthesis of jasmonates (JAs), which antagonize cytokinins (CKs) biosynthesis to modulate leaf cell division; (ii) alterations in gibberellins (GAs) and CKs biosynthesis, as well as JAs homeostasis, may coordinately regulate stem epidermal cell elongation and pith parenchyma cell division, which could account for the shorter and thicker internodes observed in lox2a mutants. Furthermore, the shortened internode phenotype of lox2a mutants was restored by the exogenous application of GA3. Thus, the loss of StLOX2a achieved plant height reduction while preserving larger leaves, thereby maintaining stable tuber yield. Manipulation of StLOX2a may represent a new strategy to optimize PA for better adaptation to regional environments and cultivation modes in potato breeding.

### 核心发现
1. Here, we report a semi-dwarf mutant sd6-23 resulting from T-DNA insertion in potato, characterized by shorter and thicker internodes, and larger leaves without tuber weight per plant penalty under controlled growth conditions.
2. Furthermore, we found that a series of genes involved in CKs biosynthesis and signaling transduction pathways were significantly up-regulated and the contents of CKs also were increased in the stems of lox2a mutants ( ).
3. Generally, GAs play a critical role in regulating stem elongation.
4. Auxin plays a central role in rapid stimulation of cell expansion as well as sustained growth over extended periods [ ].
5. Two gRNAs were designed to target the first and the second exon of StLOX2a, respectively ( ).
6. Collectively, the loss of StLOX2a attenuated the biosynthesis of JAs but promoted the biosynthesis of CKs in leaves.
7. Two target sites, sgRNA1 and sgRNA2, which were located within the first and second of StLOX2a genome sequence, respectively, were selected for the CRISPR/Cas9 vector construction.
8. Interestingly, alterations in the biosynthesis of GAs and CKs as well as JAs homeostasis in the stems could collectively regulate cell elongation and division, resulting in shorter and thicker internodes in lox2a mutants.
