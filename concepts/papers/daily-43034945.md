# Ecosystem Carbon Pools and Fluxes Under Contrasting Land-Use Systems in the Indo-Burma Biodiversity Hotspot of Northeast India: Enhancing Nature-based Solutions (NbS).

## 摘要
Land-use change profoundly affects carbon (C) stocks and sequestration potential, yet integrated assessments across natural forests, plantations, fallows, degraded forests, and agricultural fields remain scarce in the Indo-Burma Biodiversity Hotspot. We quantified C stocks (biomass and SOC) and sequestration-related processes (litter dynamics, NPP, and carbon turnover) in five contrasting land-use systems (e.g., pine plantation, PP; community-managed watershed, CMWs; fallow land, FL; degraded forest, DF, and paddy field, PF) in Manipur, Northeast India. Total vegetation C stock was highest in PP (124.51 Mg C ha⁻¹) and CMWs (104.23 Mg C ha⁻¹), followed by DF (39.61), FL (8.98), and PF (4.56 Mg C ha⁻¹). However, the SOC (<1.8%) was very low in PP due to recalcitrant pine litter and slow turnover (K = 0.48 year⁻¹), indicating a vulnerable, slow-cycling C stock. In contrast, CMWs showed a reverse J-shaped diameter distribution, high SOC (5.65-5.88%), and rapid litter turnover (K = 0.59 year⁻¹), indicating a resilient, multi-aged C stock with high cycling potential. FL had low biomass but high post-monsoon surface SOC (6.08%) and high stem density, indicating strong potential for natural regeneration. DF showed a discontinuous diameter structure and incomplete recovery. PF, despite high planting density (35,3333 plants ha⁻¹), stored negligible long-term C. For Nature-based Solutions (NbS), conserving CMWs is the highest priority; extended fallows (FL) offer cost-effective restoration; plantations (PP) provide rapid but less persistent C gains; and paddy fields require climate-smart transformation. These findings provide an evidence-based framework for prioritising NbS in a global biodiversity hotspot.
