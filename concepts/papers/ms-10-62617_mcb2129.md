### 核心发现
1. Cytoskeleton plays a critical role in resisting cellular deformation, facilitating intracellular transport, and enabling shape changes during cell motility [14].
2. Cell membrane and cytoskeleton are tightly interconnected, linked via membrane proteins that bind to intermediate filament proteins of the cytoskeleton [24].
3. Cells bind to ECM through specific integrin receptors—transmembrane proteins located on the cell surface—which facilitate the transmission of mechanical cues from ECM to the intracellular cytoskeleton.
4. Cancer cells remodel the ECM and interact with stromal cells to create a microenvironment conducive to their own growth and dissemination.
5. Cells interact with ECMs mechanically, including by pulling which is often through actomyosin-based contractility coupled to the ECM through integrin-based adhesions, and by pushing which is often through actin polymerization and microtubules.
6. To better understand these disease processes, it is essential to investigate the mechanical effects at the single- cell level separately from those at the tissue level [12].
7. Notably, nucleus itself is relatively soft, with a low elastic modulus of approximately 2.01 kPa ± 0.10 kPa, making it more susceptible to deformation in the absence of cytoskeletal support.
8. To describe this, it is necessary to switch from the simple elastic model to more complex models such as the tension integration model and viscoelastic model.
