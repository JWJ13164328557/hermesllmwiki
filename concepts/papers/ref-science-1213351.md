# Sucrose efflux mediated by SWEET proteins as a key step for phloem transport.




**期刊**: 
**DOI**: [10.1126/science.1213351](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.1213351)
**作者**: 

## 摘要
Plants transport fixed carbon predominantly as sucrose, which is produced in mesophyll cells and imported into phloem cells for translocation throughout the plant. It is not known how sucrose migrates from sites of synthesis in the mesophyll to the phloem, or which cells mediate efflux into the apoplasm as a prerequisite for phloem loading by the SUT sucrose-H(+) (proton) cotransporters. Using optical sucrose sensors, we identified a subfamily of SWEET sucrose efflux transporters. AtSWEET11 and 12 localize to the plasma membrane of the phloem. Mutant plants carrying insertions in AtSWEET11 and 12 are defective in phloem loading, thus revealing a two-step mechanism of SWEET-mediated export from parenchyma cells feeding H(+)-coupled import into the sieve element-companion cell complex. We discuss how restriction of intercellular transport to the interface of adjacent phloem cells may be an effective mechanism to limit the availability of photosynthetic carbon in the leaf apoplasm in order to prevent pathogen infections.

### 核心发现
1. We found that AtSWEET11 and 12 are expressed in phloem cells, and that inhibition by mutation reduces leaf assimilate exudation and leads to increased sugar accumu- lation in leaves.
2. The Arabidopsis ge- nome encodes several SWEET paralogs, includ- ing the closely related transporters AtSWEET10, 13, 14, and 15, which we show here to function as sucrose transporters.
3. Using long-term demographic and foraging records, we show that foraging range in wandering albatrosses has shifted poleward in conjunction with these changes in wind pattern, while their rates of travel and flight speeds have increased.
4. We found that the meridional component best explained the increase in flight speed during a foraging trip (Table 1).
5. Taken together, the data indicate that clade III SWEETs are involved in export of sucrose and are responsible for the previously undescribed first step in phloem loading.
6. Clade III SWEETs had previously been im- plicated as key targets of biotrophic pathogens.
