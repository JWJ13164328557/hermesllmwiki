### 核心发现
1. In our previous study, we found that PC deletion occurs in bladder cancer-especially muscle-invasive bladder cancer (MIBC)-and that restoring PC significantly suppresses tumor proliferation (3).
2. In this study, we reveal that primary cilia are significantly reduced in basal-type bladder cancer, and that promoting PC formation inhibits tumor progression.
3. Mechanistically, we show that the NEK2/AURKA axis inhibits IFT88 expression through ubiquitination-mediated degradation.
4. Our data suggest that NEK2 overexpression is a principal cause of PC degradation in basal-type bladder cancer.
5. We found that AURKA interacts and co-localizes with NEK2 at the basal body of primary cilia, with the highest mRNA correlation between the two.
6. Our data support that AURKA acts downstream of NEK2, interacting with both NEK2 and IFT88.
7. Collectively, these data indicate that primary cilia are deficient in basal-type bladder cancer.
