# A new species in the Magnaporthiopsis genus from wild rice enhances rice yield and disease resistance by restructuring the microbiome.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Microbiological research (2027)
- **PMID**: [42994041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42994041/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.micres.2026.128816](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micres.2026.128816)
- **第一作者**: Wanting Li
- **作者**: Wanting Li, Qinping Zhu, Yiling Wu, Jianan Zhu, Xueming Zhu, Lin Li, Fucheng Lin, Zhenzhu Su

## 摘要
Wild rice serves as a rich reservoir of beneficial endophytic fungi. However, the diversity and functional potential of these endophytes remain largely unexplored, limiting their utilization in sustainable rice production. In this work, we isolated and identified a novel endophytic fungus, Magnaporthiopsis oryzae (coded MB-9-27), from wild rice, Oryza granulata, and demonstrated that it directly promotes rice growth (yield increased by 12.66%) and enhances resistance to rice blast (disease rate decreased by 45.51%) in the field. Furthermore, MB-9-27 inoculation reshaped the rice microbiome, simplifying microbial interaction networks while significantly enriching beneficial bacteria (Methylobacterium sp., Sphingomonas sp.) and fungi (Mortierella sp., Rhizophagus sp., Mollisia sp., and Coniothyrium sp.). Among 75 fungi isolated from MB-9-27-treated rice plants, five representative strains (F14: Mortierella sp., F25: Trichoderma sp., F27: Clonostachys sp., F29: Podospora sp., and F30: Pseudeurotium sp.) corresponded to taxa ranked among the top 150 dominant fungal members identified through microbiome analysis. Functional screening revealed that Mortierella sp. (coded F14) markedly promoted rice growth, increasing shoot and root development by 55.8% and 51.6%, respectively, and reducing rice blast severity by 18.2%, indicating its dual role in promoting growth and suppressing disease. Metabolomic profiling of the rice-F14 holobiont identified significant accumulation of several metabolites, including Indole-3-glycerol phosphate (IGP), Adenine, and S-lactoylglutathione (SLG), suggesting alterations in pathways related to tryptophan metabolism, cellular metabolism, and redox homeostasis. Targeted LC-MS analysis further confirmed the production of IGP by F14. Bioassays demonstrated that IGP significantly promoted rice growth, supporting a potential role of tryptophan-derived metabolites in the plant growth-promoting activity of F14. Collectively, our findings demonstrated that MB-9-27 functions not only as a growth-promoting and disease-suppressive endophyte but also as a driver of beneficial microbiota assembly. Our results highlighted the potential of wild rice-derived endophytic fungi and their associated microbiota as valuable resources for microbial-assisted sustainable rice production.
