# Combined Rhizophagus irregularis and Bacillus megaterium inoculation enhances root hydraulics and potassium acquisition and improves maize tolerance to drought stress and potassium deficiency.

- **PMID**: [43069007](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43069007/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00572-026-01330-y](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00572-026-01330-y)
- **第一作者**: María Muñoz-Carrasco

## 摘要
In this study we investigated the effectiveness of combined inoculation with the arbuscular mycorrhizal fungus R. irregularis and the PGPR B. megaterium in improving maize tolerance to combined potassium (K) deficiency and drought stress. Thus, maize plants inoculated with these microorganisms or maintained as uninoculated controls were cultivated either under deficient K supply (0.3 mM K) or under ample K fertilization (6 mM K). Half of plants was maintained under well-watered conditions during the entire experiment and the other half was subjected to drought stress for 15 days before harvest. Results showed that under K deficiency, the combined inoculation of R. irregularis plus B. megaterium allowed maize plants to overcome the problem of K limitation and to reach a similar biomass production than uninoculated control plants cultivated under normal K levels. This effect was observed both, under well-watered and under drought stress conditions. The mechanisms underlying these effects on plant growth include enhanced root hydraulic conductivities, improved photosynthetic capacity and efficiency of photosystem II, reduced oxidative damage to lipids, and enhanced K uptake and accumulation. This indicates that, for important crops such as maize, the inoculated microorganisms can contribute to reducing the use of chemical fertilizers under the influence of additional abiotic stresses such as drought.
