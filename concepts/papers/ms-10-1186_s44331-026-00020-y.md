### 核心发现
1. Unlike Cas9, which performs a single, site-specific cut [13], Cas12 and Cas13 undergo a conformational acti- vation once they recognize their target sequence—DNA in the case of Cas12 and RNA in the case of Cas13 [18, 19].
2. This activation triggers indiscriminate cleavage of surrounding single-stranded nucleic acids, largely spar- ing the original target sequence [20, 21].
3. A Biorecognition elements, including CRISPR-Cas systems (Cas12a for DNA targets and Cas13 for RNA targets), aptamers, and DNAzymes, selectively recognize target nucleic acids or biomolecules.
4. C Target recognition initiates signal generation mechanisms such as collateral cleavage of fluorophore–quencher (FQ) reporters, Förster resonance energy transfer (FRET; e.g., quantum dot-based systems), or electron transfer/redox reactions.
5. When col- lateral activity is initiated, these reporters are cleaved, producing a detectable signal that directly indicates the presence of the target sequence [15, 20].
6. 2 further clarify how SHERLOCK and DETECTR convert target recognition into detectable reporter sig- nals through Cas13- and Cas12a-mediated collateral cleavage, respectively.
7. Target DNA is amplified by recombinase polymerase amplification (RPA), whereas RNA targets undergo reverse transcription RPA (RT-RPA) to generate complementary DNA (cDNA), followed by T7 transcription to produce RNA.
8. Cas13 is complexed with a guide RNA (gRNA) to form a ribonucleoprotein complex that specifically recognizes target RNA.
