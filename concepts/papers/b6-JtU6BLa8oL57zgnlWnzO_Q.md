## 摘要
The phytochrome B (phyB)-transcription factor signaling module plays a critical role in regulating photomorphogenesis. In this study, TCP21, a member of the Class I TCP transcription factor family, was identified as a direct interacting partner of phyB. TCP21 negatively regulates photomorphogenesis through downregulating HY5 expression and repressing the transcriptional activity of HY5. Moreover, phyB physically interacts with TCP21 and inhibits its transcriptional activity. Notably, phyB outcompetes HY5 for binding to TCP21, thereby attenuating the interaction between TCP21 and HY5 and alleviating TCP21-mediated repression of HY5 DNA-binding ability. Collectively, these findings establish TCP21 as a key component within the phyB-HY5 signaling module, contributing to plant adaptation to dynamic light environments.

### INTRODUCTION
Plants, as sessile organisms anchored in the soil, must respond to internal and external stimuli to adapt to constantly changing environments and optimize their growth and fitness. Light, the energy source required for photosynthesis, also serves as a vital environmental signal that regulates various physiological processes across the plant life cycle, ranging from germination to ripening (Paik &amp; Huq, 2019 ; Zhou &amp; Deng, 2025 ). Arabidopsis seedlings have evolved two distinct developmental programs to adapt to changes in light conditions. Upon germination in soil without light, dark‐grown seedlings undergo skotomorphogenesis characterized by elongated hypocotyls and closed cotyledons with an apical hook (Zhou &amp; Deng, 2025 ). In contrast to skotomorphogenesis, Arabidopsis seedlings adopt the developmental program of photomorphogenesis when exposed to light. The hypocotyl elongation is inhibited in light‐grown seedlings, while the cotyledons open, expand, and green with well‐developed chloroplasts (Zhou &amp; Deng, 2025 ).
So far, at least 14 photoreceptors in Arabidopsis, such as phytochromes and cryptochromes, have been identified to sense and transduce light signals (Liu et al., 2025 ). Among these photoreceptors, PHYTOCHROME B (phyB) functions as the predominant red light photoreceptor and has been extensively studied in Arabidopsis (Cheng et al., 2021 ; Viczián &amp; Nagy, 2024 ). In company with the transition from darkness to red light, phyB undergoes light‐induced conformational remodeling from the inactive red light‐absorbing Pr form to the active far‐red light‐absorbing Pfr form (Cai &amp; Huq, 2024 ). Subsequently, light‐activated phyB can translocate from the cytoplasm into the nucleus, where it assembles into photobodies and exerts its biological functions (Cai &amp; Huq, 2024 ). phyB exists as an asymmetric dimeric chromoprotein, with each monomer consisting of an N‐terminal photosensory module (PSM) and a C‐terminal output module (OPM) (Burg

### RESULTS
TCP21 physically interacts with phyB
In our previous work, we conducted a yeast two‐hybrid (Y2H) screen using the C‐terminus of phyB as the bait and successfully identified a series of potential phyB‐interacting factors (Liu et al., 2025 ). TCP21 was among these candidate proteins, and its interaction with phyB was further validated by Y2H assays (Figure 1A,B ). Domain‐mapping assays revealed that the C‐terminal region of TCP21, but not the N‐terminal region, is responsible for mediating the interaction with phyB‐C (Figure S1 ). Next, in vitro pull‐down assays were performed to confirm the interaction between TCP21 and phyB. As shown in Figure 1(C) , the recombinant MBP‐phyB‐C protein, but not MBP protein alone, could specifically pull down His‐TCP21 (Figure 1C ).

### DISCUSSION
Among phytochromes, phyB plays a dominant role in the light‐grown plants. Upon red light illumination, phyB triggers photomorphogenic development via multilevel regulatory mechanisms, including transcriptional control (Cheng et al., 2021 ; Hernando et al., 2021 ). Gene expression profiling has revealed that up to 4857 differentially expressed genes are identified in phyB‐9 mutant, indicating that gene expression control plays a fundamental role in phyB‐mediated responses (Yan et al., 2025 ).
It is well established that light‐activated phyB drives transcriptome reprogramming through multiple mechanisms. First, phyB directly interacts with transcription factors and promotes their degradation, thereby altering downstream gene expression (Liu et al., 2025 ; Zhao et al., 2023 ). Second, phyB can regulate transcription factor activity via direct physical interaction (Legris et al., 2019 ; Park et al., 2012 ; Wu et al., 2018 ). A canonical example is the phyB‐PIF interaction, which directly blocks the DNA‐binding capacity of PIF proteins (Legris et al., 2019 ; Park et al., 2012 ). Consistent with this regulatory paradigm, our study demonstrates that phyB directly interacts with TCP21 and represses its DNA‐binding capacity (Figure 5 ). Furthermore, phyB can influence the interaction between TF and its co‐factors to reshape transcriptional outputs. For instance, phyB directly interacts with both AHL21 and PIF4 and acts as a molecular glue to strengthen the AHL21‐PIF4 interaction, which in turn promotes AHL21‐mediated repression of PIF4 transcriptional activation ability (Liu et al., 2025 ). However, in this study, we uncovered that phyB outcompetes TCP21 for binding to HY5, thus promoting HY5 activity through releasing TCP21‐mediated repression of HY5 transcriptional ability (Figures 4 and 6 ). Collectively, the phyB‐TF signaling module is central to the precise regulation of photomorphogenesis.

### 核心发现
1. Taken together, all these data imply that TCP21 directly interacts with phyB likely in a light-dependent manner.
2. Further, we found that the N-terminal region of HY5 was required for its interaction with TCP21 (Figure S6).
3. Taken together, these ﬁndings strongly suggest that TCP21 inhibits the transcriptional activity of HY5, probably by impairing its ability to bind to the promoters of its target genes.
4. Taken together, these ﬁndings strongly support the conclusion that phyB inhibits TCP21’s transcriptional activity, likely by interfering with TCP21’s capacity to bind to the promoters of its target genes.
5. In this study, we show that the Class I TCP member TCP21 participates in phyB-dependent signaling pathway and negatively regulates photomorphogenesis (Figure S10).
6. SUMMARY The phytochrome B (phyB)-transcription factor signaling module plays a critical role in regulating photomor- phogenesis.
7. Moreover, phyB physically interacts with TCP21 and inhibits its transcriptional activity.
8. Collectively, these ﬁndings establish TCP21 as a key component within the phyB- HY5 signaling module, contributing to plant adaptation to dynamic light environments.
