# Tropical vulnerability and temperate resilience: molecular and physiological insights into cold stress adaptation in oil palm.

## 摘要
Cold stress is a major abiotic constraint limiting the growth and productivity of oil palm (Elaeis guineensis). Its high sensitivity to chilling causes physiological impairment, metabolic disruption, and reduced oil yield and quality. This review summarizes physiological, biochemical, and molecular mechanisms of cold stress responses in oil palm, with comparative insights from rapeseed and soybean. Key pathways, including Ca2+ signaling, MAPK cascades, and the ICE-CBF/DREB network, coordinate membrane lipid remodeling, osmotic adjustment, antioxidant defense, and hormonal regulation. Key genes (EgCBF, EgICE1, EgNCED, EgFAD2, EgSAD, EgP5CS) and transcription factors (EgWRKY, EgNAC, EgMYB) contribute to stress adaptation. However, these responses may be insufficient under prolonged or recurrent chilling, potentially disrupting lipid remodeling, water relations, and plant performance. Advances in GWAS, QTL mapping, multi-omics, genomic selection, and genome editing provide opportunities to improve cold resilience, particularly against episodic chilling events and abrupt temperature fluctuations that may become increasingly important under climate change.

### 核心发现
1. Importantly, cold-induced perturbations in lipid biosynthesis pathways, particularly the downregulation of fatty acid desaturase (FAD) genes, directly compromise oil accumulation and quality, underscoring the dual impact on both yield and industrial value.
2. Cross-talk between Ca +2 and reactive oxygen species (ROS) is exemplified by MsCML10 interacting with MsGSTU8, linking early Ca +2 signals to oxidative stress defense, and CsCIPK11 phosphorylates CsGSTU23 to enhance cold tolerance.
3. Taken together, the available evidence points to differences in signal kinetics and amplification rather than a complete divergence in the components of cold perception.
4. Soybean exhibits similar integration, where GmCBLs and GmCDPKs interact with MAPK modules to enhance downstream TF activation., This cooperative interaction between calcium-dependent kinases and MAPK pathways ensures efficient signal propagation and reinforces transcriptional reprogramming under stress conditions.
5. ICE1 (Inducer of CBF Expression 1), a MYC-like bHLH transcription factor, binds to promoter regions of CBF/DREB genes and initiates a transcriptional cascade that activates downstream stress-responsive genes.
6. These secondary metabolites not only neutralize reactive oxygen species but also interact with lipid bilayers to stabilize membrane structure, reducing oxidative damage and leakage.
7. Addressing this gap will require the integration of multi-omics datasets with high-resolution phenotyping and predictive computational modeling to identify robust genetic targets and optimal gene combinations.
8. Importantly, climate change does not necessarily imply sustained cooling in tropical regions; rather, increasing climatic variability may expose crops to abrupt temperature fluctuations, episodic cold snaps, and recurrent chilling events outside their historical thermal range.
