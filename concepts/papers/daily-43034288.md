# Transcriptional regulatory landscape of plum (Prunus salicina) fruit ripening.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: The Plant cell (2026)
- **PMID**: [43034288](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034288/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plcell/koag350](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koag350)
- **第一作者**: Lufeng Fu
- **作者**: Lufeng Fu, Xueting Ni, Chunzhen Cheng, Xiaoxin Shen, Dongli Cai, Youfeng Jiang, Dafeng Dong, Xiaobo Hu

## 摘要
Fruit ripening in plum (Prunus salicina) involves a complex transcriptional regulatory network, yet the underlying mechanisms remain poorly understood. This study presents a comprehensive transcriptional regulatory landscape of plum fruit ripening by integrating multi-omics data, including transcriptome, chromatin accessibility (DNase-seq), and DNA affinity purification sequencing (DAP-seq) of multiple ripening responsive transcription factors (TFs) from EIL, NAC, bZIP, WRKY, MYB, and C2H2 families. We constructed a position-resolved regulatory network, revealing the dynamic modulation of multiple ripening processes, especially sugar accumulation. The key ethylene signaling TF PsEIN3-1 directly binds to the promoters of PsbZIP44 and PsNAC1, which in turn, further bind to key enzyme genes involved in ethylene biosynthesis, starch degradation, and sugar accumulation. The regulatory effects of PsbZIP44 on plum fruit ripening and sugar accumulation were preliminarily verified through transient transformation experiments. Through comparative analysis with other Prunus species (plum, peach, apricot, and sweet cherry), we uncovered both conserved regulatory circuits governing ripening. Additionally, we developed the Prunus Regulome Atlas http://plantencode.fun/regulome/prunus/that hosts multiple omics data and interactive regulatory networks of Prunus. Our findings provide novel insights into the transcriptional regulation of plum fruit ripening and serve as a foundational resource for future functional studies and breeding improvement in Prunus species.
