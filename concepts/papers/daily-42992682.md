# S-nitrosoglutathione reductase deficiency is associated with altered recovery from repeated climate stress across generations in Arabidopsis thaliana.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of experimental botany (2026)
- **PMID**: [42992682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42992682/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/jxb/erag557](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erag557)
- **第一作者**: R Behl
- **作者**: R Behl, A Ghirardo, J B Winkler, S Jafarian, L Frungillo, S H Spoel, A Fischbach, G Huber

## 摘要
Climate change exposes plants to recurrent and interacting stresses, yet the extent to which these effects persist across generations, and the mechanisms involved, remain unclear. Here, we compared Arabidopsis thaliana wild type (WT, Col-0) plants with gsnor1-3 (hereafter, gsnor-ko), a mutant deficient in S-nitrosoglutathione reductase (GSNOR), an enzyme involved in nitric oxide homeostasis. Plants were propagated for three successive generations (G1-G3) under control, drought, elevated CO2, O3, heat (warm temperature), and combined treatment scenarios, followed by two recovery generations under control conditions (G4-G5). We quantified rosette growth, photosynthetic traits, seed production, and transcriptome dynamics by RNA-seq. Across environments, gsnor-ko exhibited reduced vegetative growth and reproductive output compared with WT. Transcriptomic responses were strongly scenario- and generation-dependent, with the largest differential expression shifts observed under heat and combined-treatment (combination) conditions. Compared with WT, gsnor-ko showed greater transcriptome similarity across generations and among genes differentially expressed in response to the same treatment. Functional enrichment and candidate-gene analyses identified pathways/components linked to DNA methylation, heterochromatin maintenance, histone ubiquitination, m6A RNA regulation, and methyl-donor metabolism. Together, these findings are consistent with a model in which GSNOR activity contributes to transcriptomic recovery after repeated climate stress, whereas GSNOR deficiency is associated with recurrent or reconfigured stress-associated transcriptional states at G1, G3, and G5, suggesting epigenetically associated regulatory reconfiguration.
