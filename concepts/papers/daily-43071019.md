# Ultra-high hydrostatic pressure combined with tea saponin micelles enhances naringin extraction from pomelo peel.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Bioresources and bioprocessing (2026)
- **PMID**: [43071019](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43071019/)
- **DOI**: [10.1186/s40643-026-01164-8](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40643-026-01164-8)
- **第一作者**: Wen Zhu
- **作者**: Wen Zhu, Xiaoxue Nie, Cuiman Tang, Zhicun Liu, Yankun Liu, Shuo Yang, Chaofan Sun, Xiuhua Zhao

## 摘要
Pomelo peel is a citrus-processing by-product rich in naringin, a high-value bioactive flavonoid, but its dense tissue matrix and naringin's limited aqueous solubility constrain efficient extraction. We therefore developed an aqueous process combining ultra-high hydrostatic pressure (UHHP) with tea saponin micelles to address both tissue disruption and solubilization. Conductor-like screening model for real solvents (COSMO-RS) calculations, followed by experimental screening, identified tea saponin as the most suitable candidate among the tested surfactants. Response surface methodology gave optimal conditions of 293 MPa, 80.8 mg/mL tea saponin, a liquid-to-solid ratio of 25 mL/g, three pressure cycles, and a holding time of 4.45 min per cycle. Under these conditions, mean naringin recovery and extraction yield reached 80.3% and 9.58 mg/g, respectively. In a laboratory-scale comparison with ethanol maceration and ultrasound- and microwave-assisted extraction, the UHHP-micellar process gave the highest yield and shorter treatment time, together with lower calculated energy use and CO2 emissions within the laboratory-scale comparison. Scanning electron microscopy indicated marked disruption and increased porosity of the peel matrix, while Fourier transform infrared spectra showed no new major bands attributable to naringin degradation. Density functional theory calculations supported hydrogen-bonding and van der Waals interactions between naringin and the modeled tea saponin structure, and molecular dynamics simulations suggested pressure-induced micellar compaction and retention of naringin after decompression. The enhanced extraction performance is attributed to complementary contributions from tissue disruption, micellar solubilization, and noncovalent association. These findings provide a basis for further scale-up, downstream purification, and life-cycle evaluation of an aqueous route for recovering poorly soluble flavonoids from citrus-processing by-products.
