# CYCLOPHILIN28 associates with Lhcb6 antenna to regulate photosystem II supercomplex accumulation in plants.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: International journal of biological macromolecules (2026)
- **PMID**: [43008985](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43008985/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2026.155206](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.ijbiomac.2026.155206)
- **第一作者**: Linqing Xu
- **作者**: Linqing Xu, Ying Zhong, Yongfeng Sun, Nan Yao, Yuxuan Chen, Yimin Feng, Linsheng Zhang, Juan Chen

## 摘要
The absorption and conversion of light energy into chemical energy is a highly complicated and regulated process in plant chloroplasts. This process involves cyclophilins (CYPs), but the underlying regulatory mechanisms remain elusive. In the present study, we identified the regulatory mechanism of an unusual negative regulator of photosynthesis CYCLOPHILIN28 (CYP28), and investigated its physiological characterization in photosynthesis. Our results showed that CYP28 associates with the minor antenna Lhcb6 (CP24), and plays crucial roles in the dissociation of Lhcb6 from Lhcb4 (CP29) antenna. Deletion of CYP28 slows down light-harvesting complex II (LHCII) degradation, promoting the accumulation of photosystem II-LHCII supercomplex (PSII-LHCII SC). The PSII-LHCII SC accumulation affects the level of LHCII and D1 phosphorylation and the state transition in light reaction, increases light-driven carbon dioxide fixation and starch accumulation in Calvin-Benson-Bassham cycle, and biomass accumulation, and potentially accelerates the cyp28 mutant plant growth. Furthermore, Lhcb4 and Lhcb6 were identified in LHCII trimers. Collectively, our findings indicate that CYP28 is involved in regulating the accumulation of PSII-LHCII SC through Lhcb6 antenna in photosynthesis to potentially affect the plant growth in Arabidopsis, providing a new avenue for improving photosynthetic efficiency and increasing carbon dioxide fixation in green plants.
