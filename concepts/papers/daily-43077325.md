# A novel family of polysaccharide lyase proteins are deployed in rice to negatively control arbuscular mycorrhizal symbiosis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: The Plant cell (2026)
- **PMID**: [43077325](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077325/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plcell/koag365](https://doi.org/10.1093/plcell/koag365)
- **第一作者**: Jianfei Cheng
- **作者**: Jianfei Cheng, Xuan Wang, Tairong Liu, Liyue Zhang, Qian Yan, Yuhan Zeng, He Huang, Huiru Lu

## 摘要
As the core structure of arbuscular mycorrhizal (AM) symbiosis, arbuscules exhibit an unusually short lifespan, yet the underlying mechanisms remain largely obscure. In this study, a novel family of bacteria-originated polysaccharide lyases (designated chitin-binding and lyase proteins, CBLPs) was investigated in rice. After inoculation with the AM fungus Rhizophagus irregularis, OsCBLP1 was specifically induced in arbusculated cells while OsCBLP2 was expressed in both arbusculated and adjacent cells. Loss-of-function of either OsCBLP1 or OsCBLP2 resulted in enhanced root colonization, and the double mutant oscblp1 oscblp2 exhibited an even more exaggerated phenotype, indicating that the two OsCBLPs act additively to negatively regulate AM symbiosis. Overexpression of OsCBLPs significantly decreased arbuscule abundance in rice and Medicago truncatula roots. Both OsCBLPs were localized to the apoplastic space after plasmolysis, and purified OsCBLP1 not only efficiently bound and lysed chitin but also significantly inhibited hyphal elongation of germinated R. irregularis spores. Moreover, OsCBLP2 was induced in rice leaves during symbiosis and conferred effective resistance to the blast fungus. Together, this work reveals a common set of polysaccharide lyase genes that are used by plants to control both symbiotic and pathogenic fungi. It also indicates that arbuscule degradation is likely a consequence of active host defense.
