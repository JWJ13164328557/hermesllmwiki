# The T6P-KIN10-ORE1 regulatory axis acts downstream of TDIF-PXY signaling to connect phloem sugar transport with leaf senescence in Arabidopsis.

## 摘要
The TDIF-PXY signaling module is best known for its role in vascular development, a process closely linked to phloem function and long-distance sugar transport. However, how TDIF-PXY-mediated vascular development connects phloem sugar transport and signaling to leaf senescence remains largely unclear. Here, we show that Arabidopsis mutants defective in TDIF-PXY signaling exhibit accelerated leaf senescence, impaired phloem transport, increased vein callose deposition, transcriptional reprogramming of sugar metabolism, and carbohydrate accumulation. Experiments using inducible PXY knockdown showed that changes in sugar metabolism-related gene expression observed following TDIF-PXY attenuation arise primarily from impaired phloem transport rather than from a direct transcriptional output of this pathway. Genetic and metabolic analyses identified trehalose-6-phosphate (T6P) as a key signal contributing to premature senescence in the pxy mutant. Mechanistically, KIN10, a T6P-inhibited catalytic subunit of SnRK1, interacts directly with the senescence regulator ORE1 and phosphorylates it at S115, thereby promoting its destabilization. Elevated T6P inhibits KIN10 activity, thereby reducing ORE1 phosphorylation at S115 and stabilizing ORE1. During natural leaf aging, the expression of TDIF-PXY pathway genes and phloem transport capacity decline, whereas T6P accumulation and vein callose deposition increase. Maintaining TDIF expression in phloem tissues preserves phloem transport and delays senescence. Together, these findings reveal a T6P-KIN10-ORE1 regulatory axis that links vascular function to leaf aging, suggesting that vascular status serves as an intrinsic cue for the onset of leaf senescence.
