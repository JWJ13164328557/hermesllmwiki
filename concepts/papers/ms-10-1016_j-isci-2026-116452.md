### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Summary
The mammalian testis develops through coordinated differentiation of germline and somatic lineages. How chromatin maturation and epigenetic states guide testis development is not well understood. To address this, we generated paired single-nucleus chromatin and transcriptomic data from mouse testis cells from embryonic day 14.5 through postnatal day 4. Across lineages, epigenetic maturation differed temporally during development, spatially within the genome, and organizationally with bifurcating or linear trajectories. For instance, germ cell chromatin accessibility specifically at distal intergenic regions linked to genes implicated in spermatogonial stem cell function. Sertoli cells within seminiferous tubules or the rete displayed region-specific chromatin profiles. Among interstitial cells, epigenetic maturation marked canonical lineage drivers and modulated diverging cell fates. Finally, motif enrichment demonstrated cell-type specific activity among transcription factors, and cell-to-cell communication inferences illuminated interconnected networks among lineages. These findings highlight distinct maturations guiding testis development and identify candidate regulators with potential relevance to infertility.

### Highlights
• Testis cell lineages experience distinct patterns in chromatin maturation
• Spermatogonial stem cell formation was not intimately linked to chromatin state
• Cell communication shifts developmentally to reflect the onset of spermatogenesis
• Data herein are accessible from a simple web interface at datahub.vetmed.wsu.edu

### Single-nucleus Multi-‘omic analysis captures a diversity of testis cell populations
To broadly study testis development from the completion of sex determination to the onset of spermatogenesis, we performed tandem single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (snATAC-seq), referred to as snMultiOme herein. Testes from duplicate male mice were collected at E14, E18, and P4. Before performing snMultiOme, we devised a strategy targeted at enriching the germ cell population represented in the data ( Figure 1 A). The representation of germ cells is generally low during testis development and comprises approximately 3–10% of the total cell population. 27 , 29 , 41 To enrich for germ cells, we adapted a magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS) approach using an antibody recognizing the cell surface protein epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EPCAM), which is a similar strategy used in other studies. 42 , 43 However, to maintain representation of all cell types within the testis, we combined EPCAM-enriched cell suspensions with the total testis cell suspension to accomplish the overall goal of germ cell enrichment. Serendipitously, we also enriched for rare populations of support cells during testis development (discussed more later in discussion). The resulting cell suspensions from MACS enrichment were processed to capture both snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq information from within the same individual cell.

### Germ cell maturation includes distinct modulation of putative enhancers
To begin evaluating germ cells captured within our snMultiOme atlas, we isolated clusters 9 and 10 from the analysis above ( Figures 2 A–2C), which encapsulated populations enriched for canonical germline markers. Unfortunately, following quality control filtering, only germ cells from E14 and P4 were captured in our dataset. However, by comparing these flanking ages within our dataset and evaluating differentially accessible regions (DARs) between E14 and P4, we discovered a widespread shift in chromatin accessibility ( Figure 2 D). Interestingly, of the 70,858 DARs in total, 77.41% (54,850) were more accessible at P4 compared to E14, suggesting numerous regions of increased accessibility through development. DARs were primarily located at promoter and distal intergenic regions ( Figure 2 E). Remarkably, 95.25% of DARs (or 67,494 out of 70,858) were correlated to the expression of one or more genes, and over 80% of DEGs between E14 and P4 were linked to DARs of chromatin ( Figure 2 F), suggesting a strong link between differential gene expression and chromatin architecture. Gene ontology (GO) analysis highlighted that many DEGs between E14 and P4 linked to changes in chromatin architecture were enriched within male germline-related genes, including those implicated in stem cell development, reproductive development, and meiosis ( Figure 2 G).

### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Chromatin dynamics vary markedly across cell lineages
Our analyses revealed that chromatin remodeling occurs in a lineage-specific and temporally regulated manner. Germ cells in particular undergo extensive changes in chromatin accessibility between E14 and P4, coinciding with the transition from prospermatogonia to spermatogonia and the emergence of the SSC population. 4 , 28 Interestingly, widespread DNA methylation occurs during this developmental window, 82 , 83 which makes our observation of widespread increases in chromatin accessibility surprising, given that DNA methylation is generally associated with gene silencing and chromatin compaction. 84 , 85 A striking majority of DARs in germ cells at this transition mapped to distal intergenic elements, implicating enhancer-driven regulation as a potential mechanism in SSC specification. Indeed, the expression of canonical SSC-associated genes such as Gfra1 , Foxc2 , and Id4 was linked to distal accessible regions, whereas the promoters of such genes remained epigenetically stable. This result highlights the importance of distal regulatory control, including regulation at enhancers, for genes critical for SSC function.
In contrast, Sertoli cells exhibited a remarkably stable chromatin landscape over the same developmental window. While subtle changes in transcription and chromatin were observed between E14 and P4, overall levels of epigenetic remodeling were notably lower compared to germ cells. For example, genes central to Sertoli cell function, including Amh , Fshr , and Inha 86 , carried consistent chromatin accessibility from E14 to P4. These findings suggest that Sertoli cells acquire a mature chromatin state early in testis development, possibly reflecting their roles in initiating sex determination and coordinating seminiferous cord formation and early testis morphogenesis. 87 , 88 This early epigenetic maturity may confer stability to the Sertoli cell transcriptome and enable consistent support of testis development across ages.

### Transcription factor activity reflects lineage progression
A major strength of our multi-‘omic approach is the ability to infer transcription factor activity from the enrichment of transcription factor motifs alongside expression profiling for each transcription factor. This allowed us to not only confirm the activity of established regulators but also to identify previously unstudied candidates that may play important roles in testis development. For instance, in the germline, we observed increased activity of DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 at P4, consistent with their known roles in spermatogonial development and differentiation. 53 , 54 , 55 , 57 , 58 Interestingly, DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 share remarkably similar but indeed unique motifs within the genome ( Figure 2 N), 53 , 57 and thus, may represent a core set of key developmental regulators of neonatal development. In contrast, motifs for SOX2 and TFAP2C, which are involved in fetal germline programming, were depleted at postnatal stages. In terms of transcription factors with previously uncharacterized roles in testis lineages, KLF7 and ZFP281 emerged as differentially expressed and enriched at later developmental stages in Sertoli cells, suggesting potential contributions to postnatal Sertoli cell maturation. Within the interstitial lineage, transcription factor motif enrichment revealed more nuanced patterns than gene expression alone. For example, Tcf21 was broadly expressed across progenitor and terminal populations, consistent with previous lineage tracing studies, 68 but the activity of TCF21 was higher in differentiated myoid and Leydig cells. This suggests that epigenetic remodeling may precede the transcription factor-dependent responses in some instances.

### Germ cell fate heterogeneity may not be linked to chromatin architecture
An unexpected finding of our study was the relatively weak correlation between chromatin accessibility and transcriptional heterogeneity among spermatogonia of different fates. Despite clear transcriptomic distinctions between SSCs, progenitor, and differentiating spermatogonia at P4, only a small subset of DEGs were linked to DARs. Moreover, enhancer elements associated with the expression of SSC-associated genes were not differentially accessible across spermatogonia clusters, despite differences in expression. This suggests that while enhancer priming may be critical for establishing SSC identity during the prospermatogonia-to-spermatogonia transition, broad differences in chromatin accessibility may not be the primary driver of spermatogonial subtypes at P4. A notable limitation, however, is that our analysis focused on chromatin accessibility as opposed to individual epigenetic marks. A recent study of isolated spermatogonia separated into either SSC-enriched or progenitor-enriched populations revealed that SSCs were largely not distinguishable based on ATAC-seq signature; rather, specific histone marks often differentially deposited at certain genomic loci demarcate SSCs and progenitors. 90 Thus, future studies using single-cell approaches, such as single-cell CUT&amp;Tag, may resolve this heterogeneity through development as it pertains to spermatogonial fate.

### Cell signaling and inter-lineage communication
Lastly, our computational analysis of ligand-receptor interactions uncovered dynamic changes in intercellular communication during testis development. Our data indicates that germ cells become increasingly responsive to cues from somatic cells between E14 and P4, which largely coincides with the formation of SSCs and the onset of spermatogenesis. 4 Specifically, many signaling pathways previously implicated in SSC maintenance or differentiation during spermatogenesis, including FGF, GDNF, and BMP signaling, 75 , 76 , 91 , 92 became notably more active in germ cells at P4 due in large part to ligand availability. For instance, FGF2 is known to support replication of SSCs, 76 , 93 , 94 , 95 and the availability of FGF2 from sources such as myoid and interstitial cells only became evident at P4 in our data. Interestingly, previous studies indicated that endothelial cells are a source of FGFs in the adult testis. 76 However, our analyses did not identify a clear contribution of endothelial cells as ligand sources in the prenatal or neonatal testis, which may indicate developmental differences in the source of FGFs.
In addition to the general increase in FGF activity with the onset of spermatogenesis, we observed the activation of the SSC self-renewing cue GDNF from both Sertoli and peritubular myoid cells, consistent with previous findings. 40 , 96 Interestingly, we also observed the onset of BMP signaling, which, intriguingly, indicated significant BMP6 contributions from the Leydig cell population. However, the role of BMP in the onset of spermatogenesis and SSC formation is much less clear (reviewed in 91 , 92 ). Collectively, we observed 50 predicted interactions and pathways that were identified as significantly changed from fetal to neonatal development ( Figure 7 F), including many that have not been explored in testis development and thus offer hypotheses for how intercellular communication shapes lineage specification. ### Method details
Experimental design of snMultiOme
Parallel single-nucleus RNA- and ATAC-sequencing were performed from freshly prepared single-cell suspensions generated from both testes of E14.5, E18.5, and P4 male mice. Each developmental age was collected in duplicate from different litters, representing n = 2 biologically distinct replicates per age. To obtain sufficient cells, testes from E14.5 and E18.5 males were pooled (7 males per replicate for E14.5 and 2–3 males per replicate for E18.5). Paired testes from a single male were used for P4 replicates. Replicates of each age were processed side-by-side on the same experimental day, but each developmental age was collected on different experimental days. Single-cell suspensions and cell enrichment
To generate single-cell suspensions from isolated testes, detunicated testes were incubated in a solution of 0.25% trypsin/EDTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 2 mg/mL deoxyribonuclease I (MilliporeSigma) for 10 min at 37°C with occasional, gentle pipetting. The trypsin digest was quenched with the addition fetal bovine serum (FBS) to a final concentration of 10% and poured through a 40 μm filter. The resulting single-cell suspension was washed and resuspended in DPBS-S (1% FBS, 1 mg/mL glucose, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 10 mM HEPES, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in PBS [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Approximately 1 × 10 9 cells were resuspended in DPBS-S and incubated with 3 μg of a mouse monoclonal antibody recognizing EPCAM (Thermo Fisher, cat. no. 14-5791-8; clone G8.8) for 30 min on ice. Antibody-bound cells were then isolated using a magnetic cell sorting (MACS) Cell Isolation Kit and MS columns (Miltenyi Biotech) according to manufacturer protocol. ### Summary
The mammalian testis develops through coordinated differentiation of germline and somatic lineages. How chromatin maturation and epigenetic states guide testis development is not well understood. To address this, we generated paired single-nucleus chromatin and transcriptomic data from mouse testis cells from embryonic day 14.5 through postnatal day 4. Across lineages, epigenetic maturation differed temporally during development, spatially within the genome, and organizationally with bifurcating or linear trajectories. For instance, germ cell chromatin accessibility specifically at distal intergenic regions linked to genes implicated in spermatogonial stem cell function. Sertoli cells within seminiferous tubules or the rete displayed region-specific chromatin profiles. Among interstitial cells, epigenetic maturation marked canonical lineage drivers and modulated diverging cell fates. Finally, motif enrichment demonstrated cell-type specific activity among transcription factors, and cell-to-cell communication inferences illuminated interconnected networks among lineages. These findings highlight distinct maturations guiding testis development and identify candidate regulators with potential relevance to infertility.

### Highlights
• Testis cell lineages experience distinct patterns in chromatin maturation
• Spermatogonial stem cell formation was not intimately linked to chromatin state
• Cell communication shifts developmentally to reflect the onset of spermatogenesis

### Introduction
Mammalian testis development is a largely asynchronous process, with diverse cell lineages reaching developmental milestones at distinct points during maturation. 1 , 2 Much of this asynchrony is likely attributed to the breadth of cellular heterogeneity within the developing gonad and the unique differentiation pathways of each cell lineage. In general, these differentiation trajectories are incompletely understood. Furthermore, how testicular cells communicate to coordinate testis maturation is not clear. Yet, the formation of a functional germline and supportive somatic environment is essential to sustain spermatogenesis into adulthood. 3 , 4
Ongoing studies generally point to three broad cell lineages within the developing testis: the germ, steroidogenic, and supporting cell lineages. First, the germ cell lineage arises prior to gonad formation around E6.25 in mice and 2–3 weeks of gestation in humans. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are specified outside the gonad from the proximal epiblast before migrating through the embryo to colonize the forming gonad around E7-10 in mice and 5 weeks of gestation in humans. 9 , 10 Around E10 in mice, the genital ridge (future gonad) begins to form from the thickening of the coelomic epithelium adjacent to the mesonephros which serves as the source of both steroidogenic and supporting cells within the gonad. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 Sex determination temporally overlaps early gonadal development, with expression of the key sex-determining gene Sry initiating around E10.5 in mice and 5–6 weeks gestation in humans. 15 , 16 , 17
Sry triggers a cascading genetic program that directs the gonad toward a male fate, which includes the differentiation of PGCs into prospermatogonia and somatic cells into male-specific counterparts, including Sertoli, undifferentiated mesenchymal, and vascular cells.

### Single-nucleus Multi-‘omic analysis captures a diversity of testis cell populations
To broadly study testis development from the completion of sex determination to the onset of spermatogenesis, we performed tandem single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (snATAC-seq), referred to as snMultiOme herein. Testes from duplicate male mice were collected at E14, E18, and P4. Before performing snMultiOme, we devised a strategy targeted at enriching the germ cell population represented in the data ( Figure 1 A). The representation of germ cells is generally low during testis development and comprises approximately 3–10% of the total cell population. 27 , 29 , 41 To enrich for germ cells, we adapted a magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS) approach using an antibody recognizing the cell surface protein epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EPCAM), which is a similar strategy used in other studies. 42 , 43 However, to maintain representation of all cell types within the testis, we combined EPCAM-enriched cell suspensions with the total testis cell suspension to accomplish the overall goal of germ cell enrichment. Serendipitously, we also enriched for rare populations of support cells during testis development (discussed more later in discussion). The resulting cell suspensions from MACS enrichment were processed to capture both snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq information from within the same individual cell.

### Germ cell maturation includes distinct modulation of putative enhancers
To begin evaluating germ cells captured within our snMultiOme atlas, we isolated clusters 9 and 10 from the analysis above ( Figures 2 A–2C), which encapsulated populations enriched for canonical germline markers. Unfortunately, following quality control filtering, only germ cells from E14 and P4 were captured in our dataset. However, by comparing these flanking ages within our dataset and evaluating differentially accessible regions (DARs) between E14 and P4, we discovered a widespread shift in chromatin accessibility ( Figure 2 D). Interestingly, of the 70,858 DARs in total, 77.41% (54,850) were more accessible at P4 compared to E14, suggesting numerous regions of increased accessibility through development. DARs were primarily located at promoter and distal intergenic regions ( Figure 2 E). Remarkably, 95.25% of DARs (or 67,494 out of 70,858) were correlated to the expression of one or more genes, and over 80% of DEGs between E14 and P4 were linked to DARs of chromatin ( Figure 2 F), suggesting a strong link between differential gene expression and chromatin architecture. Gene ontology (GO) analysis highlighted that many DEGs between E14 and P4 linked to changes in chromatin architecture were enriched within male germline-related genes, including those implicated in stem cell development, reproductive development, and meiosis ( Figure 2 G).

### Neonatal germ cell fate may not be overtly connected to chromatin signatures

To accomplish this, we generated unsupervised clusters of P4 germ cells with the goal of enriching for the three fates of neonatal spermatogonia, including SSCs, progenitor spermatogonia, and differentiating spermatogonia ( Figure 3 A). We leveraged transcriptomic data and differential gene expression of established markers of these populations to demonstrate relative enrichment of these subtly different populations among P4 spermatogonia ( Figure 3 B).

### Distinct chromatin signatures delineate interstitial and myoid lineages
Peritubular myoid, Leydig, and other interstitial cells are generally thought to arise from a common population of multipotent progenitors during testis development. To explore the epigenetic maturation of these populations during development, we computationally isolated the peritubular myoid, Leydig, and other interstitial populations based on clustering and marker gene enrichment from above ( Figures 5 A, 5B, and S7 A). The resulting UMAP based on transcriptomic data generated a stark projection of branches that could be ordered along both developmental age and pseudotime trajectory inference ( Figures 5 C and S7 B). Two of these branches terminated in populations of terminally differentiated cell types, either peritubular myoid or Leydig cells. By contrast, the identity of the third branch was not clear. While trajectories were not as clearly resolved with snATAC-seq data of these cells, individual clusters largely segregated based on chromatin profiles ( Figures S7 C and S7D).

### Cell-to-cell communication dynamically modulates testis development
One strength of single-cell transcriptomic approaches is the ability to analyze ligand-receptor combinations to infer potential cell-to-cell communication using computational approaches. Given the breadth of our data, we utilized this computational approach and discovered a wide range of cell interactions at each developmental timepoint captured in our data ( Figures 7 A and 7B). Interestingly, the myoid and interstitial lineage was a central node of cell signaling at each developmental age ( Figures 7 C–7E). By contrast, germ cells functioned far less as “receivers” of communication at E14 and were much more receptive to signals at P4. Overall, we discovered a major shift in the signaling pathways across all cell populations from E14 to P4 ( Figure 7 F).

### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Chromatin dynamics vary markedly across cell lineages
Our analyses revealed that chromatin remodeling occurs in a lineage-specific and temporally regulated manner. Germ cells in particular undergo extensive changes in chromatin accessibility between E14 and P4, coinciding with the transition from prospermatogonia to spermatogonia and the emergence of the SSC population. 4 , 28 Interestingly, widespread DNA methylation occurs during this developmental window, 82 , 83 which makes our observation of widespread increases in chromatin accessibility surprising, given that DNA methylation is generally associated with gene silencing and chromatin compaction. 84 , 85 A striking majority of DARs in germ cells at this transition mapped to distal intergenic elements, implicating enhancer-driven regulation as a potential mechanism in SSC specification. Indeed, the expression of canonical SSC-associated genes such as Gfra1 , Foxc2 , and Id4 was linked to distal accessible regions, whereas the promoters of such genes remained epigenetically stable. This result highlights the importance of distal regulatory control, including regulation at enhancers, for genes critical for SSC function.
In contrast, Sertoli cells exhibited a remarkably stable chromatin landscape over the same developmental window. While subtle changes in transcription and chromatin were observed between E14 and P4, overall levels of epigenetic remodeling were notably lower compared to germ cells. For example, genes central to Sertoli cell function, including Amh , Fshr , and Inha 86 , carried consistent chromatin accessibility from E14 to P4. These findings suggest that Sertoli cells acquire a mature chromatin state early in testis development, possibly reflecting their roles in initiating sex determination and coordinating seminiferous cord formation and early testis morphogenesis. 87 , 88 This early epigenetic maturity may confer stability to the Sertoli cell transcriptome and enable consistent support of testis development across ages.

### Transcription factor activity reflects lineage progression
A major strength of our multi-‘omic approach is the ability to infer transcription factor activity from the enrichment of transcription factor motifs alongside expression profiling for each transcription factor. This allowed us to not only confirm the activity of established regulators but also to identify previously unstudied candidates that may play important roles in testis development. For instance, in the germline, we observed increased activity of DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 at P4, consistent with their known roles in spermatogonial development and differentiation. 53 , 54 , 55 , 57 , 58 Interestingly, DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 share remarkably similar but indeed unique motifs within the genome ( Figure 2 N), 53 , 57 and thus, may represent a core set of key developmental regulators of neonatal development. In contrast, motifs for SOX2 and TFAP2C, which are involved in fetal germline programming, were depleted at postnatal stages. In terms of transcription factors with previously uncharacterized roles in testis lineages, KLF7 and ZFP281 emerged as differentially expressed and enriched at later developmental stages in Sertoli cells, suggesting potential contributions to postnatal Sertoli cell maturation. Within the interstitial lineage, transcription factor motif enrichment revealed more nuanced patterns than gene expression alone. For example, Tcf21 was broadly expressed across progenitor and terminal populations, consistent with previous lineage tracing studies, 68 but the activity of TCF21 was higher in differentiated myoid and Leydig cells. This suggests that epigenetic remodeling may precede the transcription factor-dependent responses in some instances.

### Germ cell fate heterogeneity may not be linked to chromatin architecture
An unexpected finding of our study was the relatively weak correlation between chromatin accessibility and transcriptional heterogeneity among spermatogonia of different fates. Despite clear transcriptomic distinctions between SSCs, progenitor, and differentiating spermatogonia at P4, only a small subset of DEGs were linked to DARs. Moreover, enhancer elements associated with the expression of SSC-associated genes were not differentially accessible across spermatogonia clusters, despite differences in expression. This suggests that while enhancer priming may be critical for establishing SSC identity during the prospermatogonia-to-spermatogonia transition, broad differences in chromatin accessibility may not be the primary driver of spermatogonial subtypes at P4. A notable limitation, however, is that our analysis focused on chromatin accessibility as opposed to individual epigenetic marks. A recent study of isolated spermatogonia separated into either SSC-enriched or progenitor-enriched populations revealed that SSCs were largely not distinguishable based on ATAC-seq signature; rather, specific histone marks often differentially deposited at certain genomic loci demarcate SSCs and progenitors. 90 Thus, future studies using single-cell approaches, such as single-cell CUT&amp;Tag, may resolve this heterogeneity through development as it pertains to spermatogonial fate.

### Cell signaling and inter-lineage communication
Lastly, our computational analysis of ligand-receptor interactions uncovered dynamic changes in intercellular communication during testis development. Our data indicates that germ cells become increasingly responsive to cues from somatic cells between E14 and P4, which largely coincides with the formation of SSCs and the onset of spermatogenesis. 4 Specifically, many signaling pathways previously implicated in SSC maintenance or differentiation during spermatogenesis, including FGF, GDNF, and BMP signaling, 75 , 76 , 91 , 92 became notably more active in germ cells at P4 due in large part to ligand availability. For instance, FGF2 is known to support replication of SSCs, 76 , 93 , 94 , 95 and the availability of FGF2 from sources such as myoid and interstitial cells only became evident at P4 in our data. Interestingly, previous studies indicated that endothelial cells are a source of FGFs in the adult testis. 76 However, our analyses did not identify a clear contribution of endothelial cells as ligand sources in the prenatal or neonatal testis, which may indicate developmental differences in the source of FGFs.
In addition to the general increase in FGF activity with the onset of spermatogenesis, we observed the activation of the SSC self-renewing cue GDNF from both Sertoli and peritubular myoid cells, consistent with previous findings. 40 , 96 Interestingly, we also observed the onset of BMP signaling, which, intriguingly, indicated significant BMP6 contributions from the Leydig cell population. However, the role of BMP in the onset of spermatogenesis and SSC formation is much less clear (reviewed in 91 , 92 ). Collectively, we observed 50 predicted interactions and pathways that were identified as significantly changed from fetal to neonatal development ( Figure 7 F), including many that have not been explored in testis development and thus offer hypotheses for how intercellular communication shapes lineage specification. ### Method details
Experimental design of snMultiOme
Parallel single-nucleus RNA- and ATAC-sequencing were performed from freshly prepared single-cell suspensions generated from both testes of E14.5, E18.5, and P4 male mice. Each developmental age was collected in duplicate from different litters, representing n = 2 biologically distinct replicates per age. To obtain sufficient cells, testes from E14.5 and E18.5 males were pooled (7 males per replicate for E14.5 and 2–3 males per replicate for E18.5). Paired testes from a single male were used for P4 replicates. Replicates of each age were processed side-by-side on the same experimental day, but each developmental age was collected on different experimental days. Single-cell suspensions and cell enrichment
To generate single-cell suspensions from isolated testes, detunicated testes were incubated in a solution of 0.25% trypsin/EDTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 2 mg/mL deoxyribonuclease I (MilliporeSigma) for 10 min at 37°C with occasional, gentle pipetting. The trypsin digest was quenched with the addition fetal bovine serum (FBS) to a final concentration of 10% and poured through a 40 μm filter. The resulting single-cell suspension was washed and resuspended in DPBS-S (1% FBS, 1 mg/mL glucose, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 10 mM HEPES, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in PBS [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Approximately 1 × 10 9 cells were resuspended in DPBS-S and incubated with 3 μg of a mouse monoclonal antibody recognizing EPCAM (Thermo Fisher, cat. no. 14-5791-8; clone G8.8) for 30 min on ice. Antibody-bound cells were then isolated using a magnetic cell sorting (MACS) Cell Isolation Kit and MS columns (Miltenyi Biotech) according to manufacturer protocol. ### Quantification and statistical analysis

### Summary
The mammalian testis develops through coordinated differentiation of germline and somatic lineages. How chromatin maturation and epigenetic states guide testis development is not well understood. To address this, we generated paired single-nucleus chromatin and transcriptomic data from mouse testis cells from embryonic day 14.5 through postnatal day 4. Across lineages, epigenetic maturation differed temporally during development, spatially within the genome, and organizationally with bifurcating or linear trajectories. For instance, germ cell chromatin accessibility specifically at distal intergenic regions linked to genes implicated in spermatogonial stem cell function. Sertoli cells within seminiferous tubules or the rete displayed region-specific chromatin profiles. Among interstitial cells, epigenetic maturation marked canonical lineage drivers and modulated diverging cell fates. Finally, motif enrichment demonstrated cell-type specific activity among transcription factors, and cell-to-cell communication inferences illuminated interconnected networks among lineages. These findings highlight distinct maturations guiding testis development and identify candidate regulators with potential relevance to infertility.

• Spermatogonial stem cell formation was not intimately linked to chromatin state
• Cell communication shifts developmentally to reflect the onset of spermatogenesis

### Introduction
Mammalian testis development is a largely asynchronous process, with diverse cell lineages reaching developmental milestones at distinct points during maturation. 1 , 2 Much of this asynchrony is likely attributed to the breadth of cellular heterogeneity within the developing gonad and the unique differentiation pathways of each cell lineage. In general, these differentiation trajectories are incompletely understood. Furthermore, how testicular cells communicate to coordinate testis maturation is not clear. Yet, the formation of a functional germline and supportive somatic environment is essential to sustain spermatogenesis into adulthood. 3 , 4
Ongoing studies generally point to three broad cell lineages within the developing testis: the germ, steroidogenic, and supporting cell lineages. First, the germ cell lineage arises prior to gonad formation around E6.25 in mice and 2–3 weeks of gestation in humans. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are specified outside the gonad from the proximal epiblast before migrating through the embryo to colonize the forming gonad around E7-10 in mice and 5 weeks of gestation in humans. 9 , 10 Around E10 in mice, the genital ridge (future gonad) begins to form from the thickening of the coelomic epithelium adjacent to the mesonephros which serves as the source of both steroidogenic and supporting cells within the gonad. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 Sex determination temporally overlaps early gonadal development, with expression of the key sex-determining gene Sry initiating around E10.5 in mice and 5–6 weeks gestation in humans. 15 , 16 , 17
Sry triggers a cascading genetic program that directs the gonad toward a male fate, which includes the differentiation of PGCs into prospermatogonia and somatic cells into male-specific counterparts, including Sertoli, undifferentiated mesenchymal, and vascular cells.

### Single-nucleus Multi-‘omic analysis captures a diversity of testis cell populations
To broadly study testis development from the completion of sex determination to the onset of spermatogenesis, we performed tandem single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (snATAC-seq), referred to as snMultiOme herein. Testes from duplicate male mice were collected at E14, E18, and P4. Before performing snMultiOme, we devised a strategy targeted at enriching the germ cell population represented in the data ( Figure 1 A). Serendipitously, we also enriched for rare populations of support cells during testis development (discussed more later in discussion). The resulting cell suspensions from MACS enrichment were processed to capture both snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq information from within the same individual cell.

### Cell-to-cell communication dynamically modulates testis development
One strength of single-cell transcriptomic approaches is the ability to analyze ligand-receptor combinations to infer potential cell-to-cell communication using computational approaches. Given the breadth of our data, we utilized this computational approach and discovered a wide range of cell interactions at each developmental timepoint captured in our data ( Figures 7 A and 7B). Interestingly, the myoid and interstitial lineage was a central node of cell signaling at each developmental age ( Figures 7 C–7E). ### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Chromatin dynamics vary markedly across cell lineages
Our analyses revealed that chromatin remodeling occurs in a lineage-specific and temporally regulated manner. Germ cells in particular undergo extensive changes in chromatin accessibility between E14 and P4, coinciding with the transition from prospermatogonia to spermatogonia and the emergence of the SSC population. 4 , 28 Interestingly, widespread DNA methylation occurs during this developmental window, 82 , 83 which makes our observation of widespread increases in chromatin accessibility surprising, given that DNA methylation is generally associated with gene silencing and chromatin compaction. 84 , 85 A striking majority of DARs in germ cells at this transition mapped to distal intergenic elements, implicating enhancer-driven regulation as a potential mechanism in SSC specification. Indeed, the expression of canonical SSC-associated genes such as Gfra1 , Foxc2 , and Id4 was linked to distal accessible regions, whereas the promoters of such genes remained epigenetically stable. This result highlights the importance of distal regulatory control, including regulation at enhancers, for genes critical for SSC function.
In contrast, Sertoli cells exhibited a remarkably stable chromatin landscape over the same developmental window. While subtle changes in transcription and chromatin were observed between E14 and P4, overall levels of epigenetic remodeling were notably lower compared to germ cells. For example, genes central to Sertoli cell function, including Amh , Fshr , and Inha 86 , carried consistent chromatin accessibility from E14 to P4. These findings suggest that Sertoli cells acquire a mature chromatin state early in testis development, possibly reflecting their roles in initiating sex determination and coordinating seminiferous cord formation and early testis morphogenesis. 87 , 88 This early epigenetic maturity may confer stability to the Sertoli cell transcriptome and enable consistent support of testis development across ages.

### Transcription factor activity reflects lineage progression
A major strength of our multi-‘omic approach is the ability to infer transcription factor activity from the enrichment of transcription factor motifs alongside expression profiling for each transcription factor. This allowed us to not only confirm the activity of established regulators but also to identify previously unstudied candidates that may play important roles in testis development. For instance, in the germline, we observed increased activity of DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 at P4, consistent with their known roles in spermatogonial development and differentiation. 53 , 54 , 55 , 57 , 58 Interestingly, DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 share remarkably similar but indeed unique motifs within the genome ( Figure 2 N), 53 , 57 and thus, may represent a core set of key developmental regulators of neonatal development. In contrast, motifs for SOX2 and TFAP2C, which are involved in fetal germline programming, were depleted at postnatal stages. In terms of transcription factors with previously uncharacterized roles in testis lineages, KLF7 and ZFP281 emerged as differentially expressed and enriched at later developmental stages in Sertoli cells, suggesting potential contributions to postnatal Sertoli cell maturation. Within the interstitial lineage, transcription factor motif enrichment revealed more nuanced patterns than gene expression alone. For example, Tcf21 was broadly expressed across progenitor and terminal populations, consistent with previous lineage tracing studies, 68 but the activity of TCF21 was higher in differentiated myoid and Leydig cells. This suggests that epigenetic remodeling may precede the transcription factor-dependent responses in some instances.

### Germ cell fate heterogeneity may not be linked to chromatin architecture
An unexpected finding of our study was the relatively weak correlation between chromatin accessibility and transcriptional heterogeneity among spermatogonia of different fates. Despite clear transcriptomic distinctions between SSCs, progenitor, and differentiating spermatogonia at P4, only a small subset of DEGs were linked to DARs. Moreover, enhancer elements associated with the expression of SSC-associated genes were not differentially accessible across spermatogonia clusters, despite differences in expression. This suggests that while enhancer priming may be critical for establishing SSC identity during the prospermatogonia-to-spermatogonia transition, broad differences in chromatin accessibility may not be the primary driver of spermatogonial subtypes at P4. A notable limitation, however, is that our analysis focused on chromatin accessibility as opposed to individual epigenetic marks. A recent study of isolated spermatogonia separated into either SSC-enriched or progenitor-enriched populations revealed that SSCs were largely not distinguishable based on ATAC-seq signature; rather, specific histone marks often differentially deposited at certain genomic loci demarcate SSCs and progenitors. 90 Thus, future studies using single-cell approaches, such as single-cell CUT&amp;Tag, may resolve this heterogeneity through development as it pertains to spermatogonial fate.

### Cell signaling and inter-lineage communication
Lastly, our computational analysis of ligand-receptor interactions uncovered dynamic changes in intercellular communication during testis development. Our data indicates that germ cells become increasingly responsive to cues from somatic cells between E14 and P4, which largely coincides with the formation of SSCs and the onset of spermatogenesis. 4 Specifically, many signaling pathways previously implicated in SSC maintenance or differentiation during spermatogenesis, including FGF, GDNF, and BMP signaling, 75 , 76 , 91 , 92 became notably more active in germ cells at P4 due in large part to ligand availability. For instance, FGF2 is known to support replication of SSCs, 76 , 93 , 94 , 95 and the availability of FGF2 from sources such as myoid and interstitial cells only became evident at P4 in our data. Interestingly, previous studies indicated that endothelial cells are a source of FGFs in the adult testis. 76 However, our analyses did not identify a clear contribution of endothelial cells as ligand sources in the prenatal or neonatal testis, which may indicate developmental differences in the source of FGFs.
In addition to the general increase in FGF activity with the onset of spermatogenesis, we observed the activation of the SSC self-renewing cue GDNF from both Sertoli and peritubular myoid cells, consistent with previous findings. 40 , 96 Interestingly, we also observed the onset of BMP signaling, which, intriguingly, indicated significant BMP6 contributions from the Leydig cell population. However, the role of BMP in the onset of spermatogenesis and SSC formation is much less clear (reviewed in 91 , 92 ). Collectively, we observed 50 predicted interactions and pathways that were identified as significantly changed from fetal to neonatal development ( Figure 7 F), including many that have not been explored in testis development and thus offer hypotheses for how intercellular communication shapes lineage specification. ### Summary
The mammalian testis develops through coordinated differentiation of germline and somatic lineages. How chromatin maturation and epigenetic states guide testis development is not well understood. To address this, we generated paired single-nucleus chromatin and transcriptomic data from mouse testis cells from embryonic day 14.5 through postnatal day 4. Across lineages, epigenetic maturation differed temporally during development, spatially within the genome, and organizationally with bifurcating or linear trajectories. For instance, germ cell chromatin accessibility specifically at distal intergenic regions linked to genes implicated in spermatogonial stem cell function. Sertoli cells within seminiferous tubules or the rete displayed region-specific chromatin profiles. Among interstitial cells, epigenetic maturation marked canonical lineage drivers and modulated diverging cell fates. Finally, motif enrichment demonstrated cell-type specific activity among transcription factors, and cell-to-cell communication inferences illuminated interconnected networks among lineages. These findings highlight distinct maturations guiding testis development and identify candidate regulators with potential relevance to infertility.

### Introduction
Mammalian testis development is a largely asynchronous process, with diverse cell lineages reaching developmental milestones at distinct points during maturation. 1 , 2 Much of this asynchrony is likely attributed to the breadth of cellular heterogeneity within the developing gonad and the unique differentiation pathways of each cell lineage. In general, these differentiation trajectories are incompletely understood. Furthermore, how testicular cells communicate to coordinate testis maturation is not clear. Yet, the formation of a functional germline and supportive somatic environment is essential to sustain spermatogenesis into adulthood. 3 , 4
Ongoing studies generally point to three broad cell lineages within the developing testis: the germ, steroidogenic, and supporting cell lineages. First, the germ cell lineage arises prior to gonad formation around E6.25 in mice and 2–3 weeks of gestation in humans. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are specified outside the gonad from the proximal epiblast before migrating through the embryo to colonize the forming gonad around E7-10 in mice and 5 weeks of gestation in humans. 9 , 10 Around E10 in mice, the genital ridge (future gonad) begins to form from the thickening of the coelomic epithelium adjacent to the mesonephros which serves as the source of both steroidogenic and supporting cells within the gonad. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 Sex determination temporally overlaps early gonadal development, with expression of the key sex-determining gene Sry initiating around E10.5 in mice and 5–6 weeks gestation in humans. 15 , 16 , 17
Sry triggers a cascading genetic program that directs the gonad toward a male fate, which includes the differentiation of PGCs into prospermatogonia and somatic cells into male-specific counterparts, including Sertoli, undifferentiated mesenchymal, and vascular cells.

To broadly study testis development from the completion of sex determination to the onset of spermatogenesis, we performed tandem single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (snATAC-seq), referred to as snMultiOme herein. ### Germ cell maturation includes distinct modulation of putative enhancers
To begin evaluating germ cells captured within our snMultiOme atlas, we isolated clusters 9 and 10 from the analysis above ( Figures 2 A–2C), which encapsulated populations enriched for canonical germline markers. Unfortunately, following quality control filtering, only germ cells from E14 and P4 were captured in our dataset. ### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Chromatin dynamics vary markedly across cell lineages
Our analyses revealed that chromatin remodeling occurs in a lineage-specific and temporally regulated manner. Germ cells in particular undergo extensive changes in chromatin accessibility between E14 and P4, coinciding with the transition from prospermatogonia to spermatogonia and the emergence of the SSC population. 4 , 28 Interestingly, widespread DNA methylation occurs during this developmental window, 82 , 83 which makes our observation of widespread increases in chromatin accessibility surprising, given that DNA methylation is generally associated with gene silencing and chromatin compaction. 84 , 85 A striking majority of DARs in germ cells at this transition mapped to distal intergenic elements, implicating enhancer-driven regulation as a potential mechanism in SSC specification. Indeed, the expression of canonical SSC-associated genes such as Gfra1 , Foxc2 , and Id4 was linked to distal accessible regions, whereas the promoters of such genes remained epigenetically stable. This result highlights the importance of distal regulatory control, including regulation at enhancers, for genes critical for SSC function.
In contrast, Sertoli cells exhibited a remarkably stable chromatin landscape over the same developmental window. While subtle changes in transcription and chromatin were observed between E14 and P4, overall levels of epigenetic remodeling were notably lower compared to germ cells. For example, genes central to Sertoli cell function, including Amh , Fshr , and Inha 86 , carried consistent chromatin accessibility from E14 to P4. These findings suggest that Sertoli cells acquire a mature chromatin state early in testis development, possibly reflecting their roles in initiating sex determination and coordinating seminiferous cord formation and early testis morphogenesis. 87 , 88 This early epigenetic maturity may confer stability to the Sertoli cell transcriptome and enable consistent support of testis development across ages.

### Transcription factor activity reflects lineage progression
A major strength of our multi-‘omic approach is the ability to infer transcription factor activity from the enrichment of transcription factor motifs alongside expression profiling for each transcription factor. This allowed us to not only confirm the activity of established regulators but also to identify previously unstudied candidates that may play important roles in testis development. For instance, in the germline, we observed increased activity of DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 at P4, consistent with their known roles in spermatogonial development and differentiation. 53 , 54 , 55 , 57 , 58 Interestingly, DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 share remarkably similar but indeed unique motifs within the genome ( Figure 2 N), 53 , 57 and thus, may represent a core set of key developmental regulators of neonatal development. In contrast, motifs for SOX2 and TFAP2C, which are involved in fetal germline programming, were depleted at postnatal stages. In terms of transcription factors with previously uncharacterized roles in testis lineages, KLF7 and ZFP281 emerged as differentially expressed and enriched at later developmental stages in Sertoli cells, suggesting potential contributions to postnatal Sertoli cell maturation. Within the interstitial lineage, transcription factor motif enrichment revealed more nuanced patterns than gene expression alone. For example, Tcf21 was broadly expressed across progenitor and terminal populations, consistent with previous lineage tracing studies, 68 but the activity of TCF21 was higher in differentiated myoid and Leydig cells. This suggests that epigenetic remodeling may precede the transcription factor-dependent responses in some instances.

### Germ cell fate heterogeneity may not be linked to chromatin architecture
An unexpected finding of our study was the relatively weak correlation between chromatin accessibility and transcriptional heterogeneity among spermatogonia of different fates. Despite clear transcriptomic distinctions between SSCs, progenitor, and differentiating spermatogonia at P4, only a small subset of DEGs were linked to DARs. Moreover, enhancer elements associated with the expression of SSC-associated genes were not differentially accessible across spermatogonia clusters, despite differences in expression. This suggests that while enhancer priming may be critical for establishing SSC identity during the prospermatogonia-to-spermatogonia transition, broad differences in chromatin accessibility may not be the primary driver of spermatogonial subtypes at P4. A notable limitation, however, is that our analysis focused on chromatin accessibility as opposed to individual epigenetic marks. A recent study of isolated spermatogonia separated into either SSC-enriched or progenitor-enriched populations revealed that SSCs were largely not distinguishable based on ATAC-seq signature; rather, specific histone marks often differentially deposited at certain genomic loci demarcate SSCs and progenitors. 90 Thus, future studies using single-cell approaches, such as single-cell CUT&amp;Tag, may resolve this heterogeneity through development as it pertains to spermatogonial fate.

### Cell signaling and inter-lineage communication
Lastly, our computational analysis of ligand-receptor interactions uncovered dynamic changes in intercellular communication during testis development. Our data indicates that germ cells become increasingly responsive to cues from somatic cells between E14 and P4, which largely coincides with the formation of SSCs and the onset of spermatogenesis. 4 Specifically, many signaling pathways previously implicated in SSC maintenance or differentiation during spermatogenesis, including FGF, GDNF, and BMP signaling, 75 , 76 , 91 , 92 became notably more active in germ cells at P4 due in large part to ligand availability. For instance, FGF2 is known to support replication of SSCs, 76 , 93 , 94 , 95 and the availability of FGF2 from sources such as myoid and interstitial cells only became evident at P4 in our data. Interestingly, previous studies indicated that endothelial cells are a source of FGFs in the adult testis. 76 However, our analyses did not identify a clear contribution of endothelial cells as ligand sources in the prenatal or neonatal testis, which may indicate developmental differences in the source of FGFs.
In addition to the general increase in FGF activity with the onset of spermatogenesis, we observed the activation of the SSC self-renewing cue GDNF from both Sertoli and peritubular myoid cells, consistent with previous findings. 40 , 96 Interestingly, we also observed the onset of BMP signaling, which, intriguingly, indicated significant BMP6 contributions from the Leydig cell population. However, the role of BMP in the onset of spermatogenesis and SSC formation is much less clear (reviewed in 91 , 92 ). Collectively, we observed 50 predicted interactions and pathways that were identified as significantly changed from fetal to neonatal development ( Figure 7 F), including many that have not been explored in testis development and thus offer hypotheses for how intercellular communication shapes lineage specification. ### Method details
Experimental design of snMultiOme
Parallel single-nucleus RNA- and ATAC-sequencing were performed from freshly prepared single-cell suspensions generated from both testes of E14.5, E18.5, and P4 male mice. Each developmental age was collected in duplicate from different litters, representing n = 2 biologically distinct replicates per age. To obtain sufficient cells, testes from E14.5 and E18.5 males were pooled (7 males per replicate for E14.5 and 2–3 males per replicate for E18.5). Paired testes from a single male were used for P4 replicates. Replicates of each age were processed side-by-side on the same experimental day, but each developmental age was collected on different experimental days. Single-cell suspensions and cell enrichment
To generate single-cell suspensions from isolated testes, detunicated testes were incubated in a solution of 0.25% trypsin/EDTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 2 mg/mL deoxyribonuclease I (MilliporeSigma) for 10 min at 37°C with occasional, gentle pipetting. The trypsin digest was quenched with the addition fetal bovine serum (FBS) to a final concentration of 10% and poured through a 40 μm filter. The resulting single-cell suspension was washed and resuspended in DPBS-S (1% FBS, 1 mg/mL glucose, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 10 mM HEPES, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in PBS [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Approximately 1 × 10 9 cells were resuspended in DPBS-S and incubated with 3 μg of a mouse monoclonal antibody recognizing EPCAM (Thermo Fisher, cat. no. 14-5791-8; clone G8.8) for 30 min on ice. Antibody-bound cells were then isolated using a magnetic cell sorting (MACS) Cell Isolation Kit and MS columns (Miltenyi Biotech) according to manufacturer protocol. Equal parts of MACS-isolated cells and flow-through were used to generate an EPCAM-enriched population. Nuclear isolation

### Summary
The mammalian testis develops through coordinated differentiation of germline and somatic lineages. How chromatin maturation and epigenetic states guide testis development is not well understood. To address this, we generated paired single-nucleus chromatin and transcriptomic data from mouse testis cells from embryonic day 14.5 through postnatal day 4. Across lineages, epigenetic maturation differed temporally during development, spatially within the genome, and organizationally with bifurcating or linear trajectories. For instance, germ cell chromatin accessibility specifically at distal intergenic regions linked to genes implicated in spermatogonial stem cell function. Sertoli cells within seminiferous tubules or the rete displayed region-specific chromatin profiles. Among interstitial cells, epigenetic maturation marked canonical lineage drivers and modulated diverging cell fates. Finally, motif enrichment demonstrated cell-type specific activity among transcription factors, and cell-to-cell communication inferences illuminated interconnected networks among lineages. These findings highlight distinct maturations guiding testis development and identify candidate regulators with potential relevance to infertility.

### Highlights
• Testis cell lineages experience distinct patterns in chromatin maturation
• Spermatogonial stem cell formation was not intimately linked to chromatin state
• Cell communication shifts developmentally to reflect the onset of spermatogenesis
• Data herein are accessible from a simple web interface at datahub.vetmed.wsu.edu

### Introduction
Mammalian testis development is a largely asynchronous process, with diverse cell lineages reaching developmental milestones at distinct points during maturation. 1 , 2 Much of this asynchrony is likely attributed to the breadth of cellular heterogeneity within the developing gonad and the unique differentiation pathways of each cell lineage. In general, these differentiation trajectories are incompletely understood. Furthermore, how testicular cells communicate to coordinate testis maturation is not clear. Yet, the formation of a functional germline and supportive somatic environment is essential to sustain spermatogenesis into adulthood. 3 , 4
Ongoing studies generally point to three broad cell lineages within the developing testis: the germ, steroidogenic, and supporting cell lineages. First, the germ cell lineage arises prior to gonad formation around E6.25 in mice and 2–3 weeks of gestation in humans. 5 , 6 , 7 , 8 Primordial germ cells (PGCs) are specified outside the gonad from the proximal epiblast before migrating through the embryo to colonize the forming gonad around E7-10 in mice and 5 weeks of gestation in humans. 9 , 10 Around E10 in mice, the genital ridge (future gonad) begins to form from the thickening of the coelomic epithelium adjacent to the mesonephros which serves as the source of both steroidogenic and supporting cells within the gonad. 11 , 12 , 13 , 14 Sex determination temporally overlaps early gonadal development, with expression of the key sex-determining gene Sry initiating around E10.5 in mice and 5–6 weeks gestation in humans. 15 , 16 , 17
Sry triggers a cascading genetic program that directs the gonad toward a male fate, which includes the differentiation of PGCs into prospermatogonia and somatic cells into male-specific counterparts, including Sertoli, undifferentiated mesenchymal, and vascular cells.

### Single-nucleus Multi-‘omic analysis captures a diversity of testis cell populations
To broadly study testis development from the completion of sex determination to the onset of spermatogenesis, we performed tandem single-nucleus RNA-sequencing (snRNA-seq) and single-nucleus assay for transposase-accessible chromatin sequencing (snATAC-seq), referred to as snMultiOme herein. Testes from duplicate male mice were collected at E14, E18, and P4. Before performing snMultiOme, we devised a strategy targeted at enriching the germ cell population represented in the data ( Figure 1 A). The representation of germ cells is generally low during testis development and comprises approximately 3–10% of the total cell population. 27 , 29 , 41 To enrich for germ cells, we adapted a magnetic activated cell sorting (MACS) approach using an antibody recognizing the cell surface protein epithelial cell adhesion molecule (EPCAM), which is a similar strategy used in other studies. 42 , 43 However, to maintain representation of all cell types within the testis, we combined EPCAM-enriched cell suspensions with the total testis cell suspension to accomplish the overall goal of germ cell enrichment. Serendipitously, we also enriched for rare populations of support cells during testis development (discussed more later in discussion). The resulting cell suspensions from MACS enrichment were processed to capture both snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq information from within the same individual cell.

To begin evaluating germ cells captured within our snMultiOme atlas, we isolated clusters 9 and 10 from the analysis above ( Figures 2 A–2C), which encapsulated populations enriched for canonical germline markers. Unfortunately, following quality control filtering, only germ cells from E14 and P4 were captured in our dataset. ### Cell-to-cell communication dynamically modulates testis development
One strength of single-cell transcriptomic approaches is the ability to analyze ligand-receptor combinations to infer potential cell-to-cell communication using computational approaches. ### Discussion
In this study, we generated a data atlas spanning key stages of mouse testis development to investigate how chromatin accessibility and gene expression dynamics underlie cell lineage specification and maturation. Prior studies have leveraged snATAC-seq alone to elucidate chromatin shifts during a similar developmental window, 81 but how chromatin underlies the expression of fate-determining factors and shapes the divergence of lineage trajectories within the testis remains incompletely studied. To address this gap, we generated tandem snRNA-seq and snATAC-seq data from individual cells of E14, E18, and P4 mouse testes. After annotating testis cell lineages captured in our data, we evaluated the connection between chromatin changes and gene expression through development within each cell type. Furthermore, we leveraged the paired chromatin and gene expression data to identify candidate transcription factors that may control developmental transitions within each lineage. Several important themes emerge from our findings.

### Chromatin dynamics vary markedly across cell lineages
Our analyses revealed that chromatin remodeling occurs in a lineage-specific and temporally regulated manner. Germ cells in particular undergo extensive changes in chromatin accessibility between E14 and P4, coinciding with the transition from prospermatogonia to spermatogonia and the emergence of the SSC population. 4 , 28 Interestingly, widespread DNA methylation occurs during this developmental window, 82 , 83 which makes our observation of widespread increases in chromatin accessibility surprising, given that DNA methylation is generally associated with gene silencing and chromatin compaction. 84 , 85 A striking majority of DARs in germ cells at this transition mapped to distal intergenic elements, implicating enhancer-driven regulation as a potential mechanism in SSC specification. Indeed, the expression of canonical SSC-associated genes such as Gfra1 , Foxc2 , and Id4 was linked to distal accessible regions, whereas the promoters of such genes remained epigenetically stable. This result highlights the importance of distal regulatory control, including regulation at enhancers, for genes critical for SSC function.
In contrast, Sertoli cells exhibited a remarkably stable chromatin landscape over the same developmental window. While subtle changes in transcription and chromatin were observed between E14 and P4, overall levels of epigenetic remodeling were notably lower compared to germ cells. For example, genes central to Sertoli cell function, including Amh , Fshr , and Inha 86 , carried consistent chromatin accessibility from E14 to P4. These findings suggest that Sertoli cells acquire a mature chromatin state early in testis development, possibly reflecting their roles in initiating sex determination and coordinating seminiferous cord formation and early testis morphogenesis. 87 , 88 This early epigenetic maturity may confer stability to the Sertoli cell transcriptome and enable consistent support of testis development across ages.

### Transcription factor activity reflects lineage progression
A major strength of our multi-‘omic approach is the ability to infer transcription factor activity from the enrichment of transcription factor motifs alongside expression profiling for each transcription factor. This allowed us to not only confirm the activity of established regulators but also to identify previously unstudied candidates that may play important roles in testis development. For instance, in the germline, we observed increased activity of DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 at P4, consistent with their known roles in spermatogonial development and differentiation. 53 , 54 , 55 , 57 , 58 Interestingly, DMRT1·SALL4 and DMRTB1 share remarkably similar but indeed unique motifs within the genome ( Figure 2 N), 53 , 57 and thus, may represent a core set of key developmental regulators of neonatal development. In contrast, motifs for SOX2 and TFAP2C, which are involved in fetal germline programming, were depleted at postnatal stages. In terms of transcription factors with previously uncharacterized roles in testis lineages, KLF7 and ZFP281 emerged as differentially expressed and enriched at later developmental stages in Sertoli cells, suggesting potential contributions to postnatal Sertoli cell maturation. Within the interstitial lineage, transcription factor motif enrichment revealed more nuanced patterns than gene expression alone. For example, Tcf21 was broadly expressed across progenitor and terminal populations, consistent with previous lineage tracing studies, 68 but the activity of TCF21 was higher in differentiated myoid and Leydig cells. This suggests that epigenetic remodeling may precede the transcription factor-dependent responses in some instances.

### Germ cell fate heterogeneity may not be linked to chromatin architecture
An unexpected finding of our study was the relatively weak correlation between chromatin accessibility and transcriptional heterogeneity among spermatogonia of different fates. Despite clear transcriptomic distinctions between SSCs, progenitor, and differentiating spermatogonia at P4, only a small subset of DEGs were linked to DARs. Moreover, enhancer elements associated with the expression of SSC-associated genes were not differentially accessible across spermatogonia clusters, despite differences in expression. This suggests that while enhancer priming may be critical for establishing SSC identity during the prospermatogonia-to-spermatogonia transition, broad differences in chromatin accessibility may not be the primary driver of spermatogonial subtypes at P4. A notable limitation, however, is that our analysis focused on chromatin accessibility as opposed to individual epigenetic marks. A recent study of isolated spermatogonia separated into either SSC-enriched or progenitor-enriched populations revealed that SSCs were largely not distinguishable based on ATAC-seq signature; rather, specific histone marks often differentially deposited at certain genomic loci demarcate SSCs and progenitors. 90 Thus, future studies using single-cell approaches, such as single-cell CUT&amp;Tag, may resolve this heterogeneity through development as it pertains to spermatogonial fate.

### Cell signaling and inter-lineage communication
Lastly, our computational analysis of ligand-receptor interactions uncovered dynamic changes in intercellular communication during testis development. Our data indicates that germ cells become increasingly responsive to cues from somatic cells between E14 and P4, which largely coincides with the formation of SSCs and the onset of spermatogenesis. 4 Specifically, many signaling pathways previously implicated in SSC maintenance or differentiation during spermatogenesis, including FGF, GDNF, and BMP signaling, 75 , 76 , 91 , 92 became notably more active in germ cells at P4 due in large part to ligand availability. For instance, FGF2 is known to support replication of SSCs, 76 , 93 , 94 , 95 and the availability of FGF2 from sources such as myoid and interstitial cells only became evident at P4 in our data. Interestingly, previous studies indicated that endothelial cells are a source of FGFs in the adult testis. 76 However, our analyses did not identify a clear contribution of endothelial cells as ligand sources in the prenatal or neonatal testis, which may indicate developmental differences in the source of FGFs.
In addition to the general increase in FGF activity with the onset of spermatogenesis, we observed the activation of the SSC self-renewing cue GDNF from both Sertoli and peritubular myoid cells, consistent with previous findings. 40 , 96 Interestingly, we also observed the onset of BMP signaling, which, intriguingly, indicated significant BMP6 contributions from the Leydig cell population. However, the role of BMP in the onset of spermatogenesis and SSC formation is much less clear (reviewed in 91 , 92 ). Collectively, we observed 50 predicted interactions and pathways that were identified as significantly changed from fetal to neonatal development ( Figure 7 F), including many that have not been explored in testis development and thus offer hypotheses for how intercellular communication shapes lineage specification. ### Method details
Experimental design of snMultiOme
Parallel single-nucleus RNA- and ATAC-sequencing were performed from freshly prepared single-cell suspensions generated from both testes of E14.5, E18.5, and P4 male mice. Each developmental age was collected in duplicate from different litters, representing n = 2 biologically distinct replicates per age. To obtain sufficient cells, testes from E14.5 and E18.5 males were pooled (7 males per replicate for E14.5 and 2–3 males per replicate for E18.5). Paired testes from a single male were used for P4 replicates. Replicates of each age were processed side-by-side on the same experimental day, but each developmental age was collected on different experimental days. Single-cell suspensions and cell enrichment
To generate single-cell suspensions from isolated testes, detunicated testes were incubated in a solution of 0.25% trypsin/EDTA (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and 2 mg/mL deoxyribonuclease I (MilliporeSigma) for 10 min at 37°C with occasional, gentle pipetting. The trypsin digest was quenched with the addition fetal bovine serum (FBS) to a final concentration of 10% and poured through a 40 μm filter. The resulting single-cell suspension was washed and resuspended in DPBS-S (1% FBS, 1 mg/mL glucose, 1 mM sodium pyruvate, 10 mM HEPES, 100 units/mL penicillin, and 100 μg/mL streptomycin in PBS [Thermo Fisher Scientific]). Approximately 1 × 10 9 cells were resuspended in DPBS-S and incubated with 3 μg of a mouse monoclonal antibody recognizing EPCAM (Thermo Fisher, cat. no. 14-5791-8; clone G8.8) for 30 min on ice. Antibody-bound cells were then isolated using a magnetic cell sorting (MACS) Cell Isolation Kit and MS columns (Miltenyi Biotech) according to manufacturer protocol. Equal parts of MACS-isolated cells and flow-through were used to generate an EPCAM-enriched population. Nuclear isolation
