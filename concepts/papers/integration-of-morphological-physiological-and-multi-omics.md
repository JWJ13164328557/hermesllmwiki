### 核心发现
1. We found continuous reductions in chloro­ phyll a content, total chlorophyll content, and chlorophyll a:b ratio with increasing planting density, but no significant difference in chlorophyll b content between leaves planted at high and low density (Fig.
2. In our study, we found that, in MD and HD plants, the levels of photosynthesis-related carbohydrates including sugar and starch decreased, along with changes in the expression levels of associated genes.
3. We found that compared with controls, most MYB genes, including MYB3, 4, 5, and 111, were downregulated in HD and upregulated in MD (Fig.
4. In our study, we found that the expression levels of some genes encoding components of SA signaling significantly decreased in HD leaves, but increased in MD.
5. Based on the results of leaf yield and quality, we conclude that MD as the ideal density for opti­ mizing leaf yield and active compound accumulation in G.
6. These results suggest that the MD as the optimal density could not only enhance leaf yield, but also improve active-compound accumulation in G.
7. These results suggest that increasing planting density significantly improved the total leaf production in leaf-use ginkgo seedings.
8. These results suggest that flavonoid biosynthetic pathways may be activated in moderate planting density in leaf-use ginkgo seedlings.
