# The bifunctional Arabidopsis cytosolic FAD synthase contributes to flavin homeostasis and the development of leaf and flower organs

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant Physiology ()
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag867](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag867)
- **作者**: Patrick Schall, Lucas Marutschke, Yuwei Wang, Johanna Rottke, Alexander Gehm
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要
Abstract
                  The flavins FAD and FMN are essential cofactors for many vital enzymes in living organisms. While de novo riboflavin synthesis is specific to plants, fungi and some bacteria, all organisms can synthesize the flavocoenzymes. In plants, the interconversion from riboflavin to FAD has been described in both in plastids and the cytoplasm. However, comprehensive functional analyses of the genes and enzymes involved in the two plant flavin metabolic pathways are still lacking. This includes an investigation into whether the two spatially separate FAD syntheses can compensate for one another. Here, we report on the genetic and physiological consequences of insufficient and enhanced levels of the cytosolic FAD synthase (FADS). The enzyme itself is a 55-kDa bifunctional protein made up of an N-terminal 3'-phosphoadenosine 5'-phosphosulfate reductase domain (with a FAD synthase function) and a C-terminal hydrolase molybdopterin-binding (MPtB) domain. Using T-DNA insertion mutants, FADS-RNAi, virus-induced gene silencing and FADS-overexpressor lines, various mutant phenotypes were identified during floral organ development and in leaf pigmentation. These phenotypic changes correlate with a shift in the FAD-to-FMN ratio and/or reduced FAD and FMN levels compared to wild type. We hypothesize that disrupted flavin homeostasis in turn leads to reduced expression of FAD-dependent enzymes. One significant deficiency phenotype attributable to disrupted FADS expression involves changes in phytohormone levels and signaling functions in flowers, impairing floral organ development.
