# Arctic forcing of European spring productivity and agricultural yields linked to Barents Sea ice loss.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Science advances (2026)
- **PMID**: [43084658](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43084658/)
- **DOI**: [10.1126/sciadv.aei1858](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aei1858)
- **第一作者**: Zhi Huang
- **作者**: Zhi Huang, Jun Wang, Philippe Ciais, Xichen Li, William K M Lau, Ning Zeng, Wenping Yuan, Haijin Dai

## 摘要
Arctic sea ice loss reshapes midlatitude climate, yet its cascading impacts on terrestrial carbon cycling and food security remain unclear. Barents Sea ice area (BSIA) has experienced the Arctic's fastest decline. Using four decades of satellite observations, agricultural statistics, and Earth system model (ESM) simulations, we identify late-winter BSIA as a key driver of European spring productivity. Interannual BSIA reductions substantially enhance gross primary productivity (GPP) and raise national wheat yields by up to 16.5% in Eastern Europe, a global breadbasket. Reanalysis and model experiments reveal that reduced BSIA alters large-scale circulation through stratosphere-troposphere coupling, wave-mean flow interactions, and Rossby wave propagation. Anomalous subsidence drives adiabatic and radiative warming; cyclonic flow strengthens warm-air advection, together alleviating thermal constraints on photosynthesis. Although most ESMs capture atmospheric teleconnections, they systematically underestimate productivity responses due to underrepresented GPP-temperature sensitivity. BSIA decline accounts for approximately half of the four-decade increase in Eastern European productivity, highlighting an overlooked Arctic forcing of midlatitude carbon dynamics.
