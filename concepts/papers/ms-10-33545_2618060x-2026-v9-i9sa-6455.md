### 核心发现
1. Ethylene interacts with auxin and other hormonal pathways to regulate cell division, root primordium initiation and subsequent root emergence [20, 21, 27, 28].
2. Accumulation of ethylene in submerged tissues can interact with reactive oxygen species (ROS), calcium-dependent signalling and other regulatory pathways to promote selective cell death in specific cortical cell files [39, 40].
3. Further research is required to establish whether these traits can be reliably incorporated into routine screening programmes for legume waterlogging tolerance.
4. Therefore, further comparative studies are required to determine whether root hair density, length and persistence provide consistent indicators of adaptive capacity under waterlogged conditions.
