# Growth, development and adaptation of soybean in Saskatchewan

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Canadian Journal of Plant Science ()
- **DOI**: [10.1139/cjps-2026-0054](https://doi.org/10.1139/cjps-2026-0054)
- **作者**: Ayza E E.V. Camargos, Nathaniel Wesley Waldie Ort, Thomas D. Warkentin, Elroy R. Cober, Mehrzad Milad Eskandari
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要
With the growing interest in soybean production and its expansion into Western Canada, this study aimed at characterizing soybean adaptation to central Saskatchewan (SK), which is considered a new north-latitude (51-53°N) frontier for soybean production. A set of soybean genotypes (24) within maturity groups (MG) 000 and 00 was evaluated across 12 site-years from 2019-2022 to assess overall soybean growth and development, corn heat units (CHU) accumulation, response to precipitation, seed yield, protein and oil concentration. On average, soybean required 120 to 135 days to reach physiological maturity and full maturity, respectively, accumulating 2150 to 2240 CHU and receiving 81 to 221 mm of total precipitation. All site-years were drier than the long-term average for Saskatchewan. Seed yield ranged from 1007 to 1247 kg ha-1, protein concentration was 35.9 to 38.8%, and oil concentration was 22.8 to 24.2% across environments. Growth parameters at the seed development stage included aboveground biomass of 5800 kg ha-1, leaf area index of 1.1 to 2.4, and maximum fractional light interception of 83%. Our results indicated that central SK accumulates sufficient CHU for soybean to reach maturity, especially MG 000 genotypes, although low precipitation and early fall frost remain limiting factors.
