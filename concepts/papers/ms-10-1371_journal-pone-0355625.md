### 核心发现
1. We found a comparable proportion of dendritic spines contains an SER tubule in both KO and WT rats (at P80), suggesting the capacity for SER to visit spines is not lost in the Synpo KO.
2. The Synpo KO rat could serve as a valuable tool to assess whether the developmental upregulation of Synpo [40,41] and formation of the spine apparatus [42,43] are necessary for the matura­ tion of synapses, spine clustering, and LTP [33,39].
3. Results We used the CRISPR-Cas9 system to delete the coding region of the Synpo gene in rats (Figs 1A and B, S1 Fig), design­ ing guide RNAs to target the 5’ and 3’ regions flanking the protein-coding exons 2 and 3.
4. These findings suggest that some KO rats may be more susceptible to conditions of kidney stress later in adulthood.
5. These results suggest that KO rats can be studied during development and in young adulthood without concern for kidney malfunction.
6. Axo-axonic GABAergic interneurons, also known as Chandelier cells, selectively target the AIS of excitatory pyrami­ dal neurons in the hippocampal area CA1 and other excitatory pyramidal neurons throughout the brain.
7. The spine apparatus plays a critical role in human health and disease, as demonstrated by its aberrant morphology in neurodegenerative and peritumoral brain tissue [88].
8. These guide RNAs were predicted by CRISPOR to be highly specific, and there are no potential off-target sequences in known exons or on chromosome 18, where Synpo gene is located.
