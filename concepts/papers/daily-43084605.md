# PbCNL1, a CC-NB-LRR R protein from 'duli' pear (Pyrus betulifolia Bunge), mediates resistance to bacterial and fungal pathogens.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43084605](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43084605/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag883](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag883)
- **第一作者**: Qingcui Ma
- **作者**: Qingcui Ma, Gang Li, Pingli Song, Zhe Sun, Haixia Zhang, Hui Ma, Meilin Li, Murray Grant

## 摘要
Nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat (NB-LRR) proteins play vital roles in plant immunity. Using the AtRPM1 amino acid sequence to search the genome of wild-type 'duli' pear (Pyrus betulifolia), we identified a homologous CC-NB-LRR protein, PbCNL1. Encoded by 2,838 bp gene it shares 29.4% identity and conserved domains with AtRPM1. Overexpressing PbCNL1 enhanced resistance to the pear black spot fungal pathogen Alternaria alternata in pear calli, fruits, leaves, and stable transgenic Arabidopsis, as well as to the bacterial pathogen Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato DC3000 (Pst DC3000) in pear fruits, tobacco leaves, and transgenic Arabidopsis. PbCNL1 overexpression also reduced the sensitivity to the microbe-associated molecular pattern (MAMP) flagellin peptide flg22. Disease resistance marker genes (PR1, PR5, NPR1) and salicylic acid (SA)-related metabolic and signaling genes were consistently upregulated in PbCNL1-expressing plants. We further identified three RIN4 homologues (PbRIN4-1/2/3). PbCNL1 interacted with all three PbRIN4s in a plasma membrane-dependent manner, with PbRIN4-2 further enhancing PbCNL1-mediated resistance to both Pst DC3000 and A. alternata. Unlike the classical 'guardee' role of AtRIN4 for AtRPM1 in Arabidopsis, PbRIN4-2 appears to enhance and positively regulate PbCNL1-mediated disease resistance, and this occurs independently of its S-acylation and phosphorylation status. Overexpression of PbRIN4-2 alone also enhanced resistance to both pathogens in transgenic Arabidopsis and pear fruits. Taken together, we have identified a CNL-type R protein, PbCNL1, and three PbRIN4s from wild-type 'duli' pear that confer resistance to both bacterial and fungal pathogens. PbCNL1 interacts with PbRIN4s to positively regulate immunity, offering promising candidate genes for engineering disease-resistant pear rootstocks.
