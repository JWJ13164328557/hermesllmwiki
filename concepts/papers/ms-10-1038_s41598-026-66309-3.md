### 核心发现
1. Our results suggest that prenatal exposure to low-dose cesium-137 enhances susceptibility to HFD-driven tumorigenesis in males, potentially through IR, mediated epigenomic reprogramming of metabolic progenitor cells during development, a mechanism previously demonstrated in mice 49.
2. These findings suggest that in utero exposure to low-dose cesium-137 may potentiate the impact of environmental stressors such as HFD, which is recognized as a major contributor to hepatic carcinogenetic risk through steatosis39,40,46.
3. These results suggest hepatic injury and substantial disruption of lipid metabolism, further aggravated by prenatal exposure to low dose cesium-137.
4. Page 12/25 In conclusion, our study demonstrates for the first time that in utero exposure to low-dose ionizing radiation increases susceptibility to metabolic stress, thereby exacerbating HFD-induced hepatic effects.
5. This study provides the first evidence that prenatal exposure of C57BL/6J mice to low doses of cesium-137 increases the risk of liver tumor development in offspring.
6. Notably, 4 of 29 exposed males (14%) developed score-3 masses, a condition absent in controls.
