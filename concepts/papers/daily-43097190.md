# Receptor-like kinases (RLKs) as key regulators of plant stress adaptation.

## 摘要
Receptor-like kinases (RLKs) are one of the largest gene families in plants, with prominent members such as leucine-rich repeat (LRR), lectin (Lec), lysin motif (LysM), and wall-associated kinase (WAK) proteins. Advances in genome and transcriptome sequencing have rapidly expanded the catalog of RLKs across diverse plant species. These membrane-bound receptors are pivotal in perceiving a wide range of extracellular signals to coordinate plant development and stress adaptation. Among which, LRR-RLKs are recognized for their roles in regulating growth processes and interacting with pathogen-associated molecular patterns to initiate Pattern-Triggered Immunity (PTI). Furthermore, certain RLKs are involved in Effector-Triggered Immunity (ETI), underscoring their multifaceted functions in plant defense. This review synthesizes current knowledge on the diverse RLK families, highlighting their structures, signaling mechanisms, and integrated roles in plant stress resilience and immunity. Despite extensive characterization in model plants like Arabidopsis thaliana and rice, the functional diversity and molecular mechanisms of RLKs in stress signaling remain poorly understood in many non-model and stress-resilient species. In addition, important knowledge gaps remain regarding the mechanisms underlying RLK-mediated signaling. The functional specificity of many RLKs within complex signaling networks remain poorly understood, particularly under simultaneous or sequential exposure to multiple stresses. Addressing this knowledge gap is essential for understanding how RLK-mediated signaling networks contribute to plant adaptability under changing environments. Future research should focus on elucidating the cross-talk between RLK signaling and hormone-mediated stress responses, which could guide genetic and biotechnological strategies for developing climate-resilient crops.
