### Abstract
Drought stress severely limits the growth of perennial trees. Xylem structure is central to water conduction; however, lignin monomer composition driving xylem remodeling for drought adaptation remains enigmatic. By integrating multi-omics analyses, genotype-environment association analysis, and metabolite-based genome-wide association studies, we identified PtoCPK3 as a key gene associated with precipitation-related traits and the aridity index in Populus tomentosa . This variation in association was linked to coniferyl alcohol and ferulic acid, two metabolites related to guaiacyl (G)-lignin monomer biosynthesis. Overexpressing PtoCPK3 enhanced drought tolerance in transgenic poplar by promoting xylem remodeling associated with a decreased lignin S/G ratio. Mechanistically, drought-induced Ca 2+ signaling activates PtoCPK3 to phosphorylate PtoERF72 at Ser 96 , enhancing its activation of PtoWOX13b and direct repression of PtoUGT72AZ2 . Synergistic repression of PtoUGT72AZ2 by PtoWOX13b reduces glycosylation of G-monomer precursors, favoring ferulic acid and coniferyl alcohol accumulation. Furthermore, natural variants in PtoCPK3 and PtoERF72 drive geographic divergence, with the PtoCPK3 II _ PtoERF72 CC genotype conferring superior drought resilience via elevated phosphorylation efficiency. This module links drought signaling to xylem remodeling, providing genetic targets for breeding drought-resilient trees.

### INTRODUCTION
Forests, covering approximately 30% of terrestrial land, play a crucial role in regulating regional climate and global biogeochemical cycles ( 1 , 2 ). However, climate change and human disturbance are intensifying the drought events, leading to widespread tree mortality and compromised ecosystem resilience ( 3 – 5 ). Because trees are long-lived and sessile, their survival depends on structural and physiological adjustments that maintain water transport under abiotic stress. A key strategy is xylem structural adaptation, which involves coordinated changes in vessel structure and cell wall composition ( 6 ). Drought-resistant species typically undergo vessel lumen reduction and cell wall thickening, together with concomitant adjustments in pit architecture, both of which enhance xylem strength and mitigate the risk of cavitation, thereby maintaining water transport ( 7 – 9 ). Lignin, a major component of the secondary cell wall (SCW), not only provides rigidity and hydrophobicity but also determines the mechanical properties of vessels through its monomeric composition. Variations in syringyl-to-guaiacyl (S/G) ratio, coupled with increased lignin deposition, have been consistently observed as adaptive responses to drought stress ( 10 – 12 ).
Beyond structural adaptations, calcium signaling serves as a central regulator of drought responses, where transient calcium ion (Ca 2+ ) fluctuations are decoded by calcium-dependent protein kinases (CPKs) ( 13 , 14 ). Ca 2+ binding to the CPK activation domain activates kinase activity, phosphorylating downstream targets such as transcription factors, facilitating their cytoplasm-to-nucleus translocation, which mediates gene expression and stress adaptation ( 15 – 17 ). The best-characterized function of CPKs in drought adaptation is their role in regulating stomatal closure. ### Multi-omics analyses reveal the phenylpropanoid pathway as the pivotal drought-response axis in Populus tomentosa
To investigate the metabolic responses in the xylem tissue to drought stress, we subjected Populus tomentosa cultivar “1316” to a 15-day water-withholding treatment. Drought resulted in severe wilting, decreased xylem width, and thickened SCWs, indicating that the plants had perceived water-deficit cues and undergone structural remodeling ( Fig. 1, A and B ). Subsequently, broad-targeted xylem metabolomic profiling identified 395 differentially accumulated metabolites (fig. S1A and data S1). Metabolite set enrichment analysis (MSEA) revealed notable enrichment of metabolites associated with phenylpropanoid biosynthesis under drought stress, whereas metabolites related to flavonoid biosynthesis were predominantly enriched in well-watered conditions ( Fig. 1C ). These findings suggest that drought stress preferentially redirects carbon flux toward lignin biosynthesis, thereby enhancing SCW deposition. We further analyzed changes in all metabolites within the phenylpropanoid pathway and found that three general precursors ( l -phenylalanine, cinnamic acid, and p -coumaric acid) of the phenylpropanoid pathway were up-regulated, most flavonoids (53 of 67, 79.10%) were down-regulated, whereas nearly all lignin-related metabolites (9 of 10, 90.00%) were up-regulated under drought stress ( Fig. 1D ). Furthermore, lignin-related metabolites exhibited significantly greater changes than flavonoid metabolites in response to drought ( P = 2.69 × 10 −9 ; Fig. 1E ), suggesting that lignin metabolism plays a predominant role in the drought response.

### Environment drives adaptive selection of lignin metabolites in P. tomentosa population
To further explore the drought adaptability of P. tomentosa , we analyzed environmental differentiation among three genetically and geographically defined subpopulations, south (S; n = 102), northeast (NE; n = 92), and northwest (NW; n = 108) ( 28 ). Analysis of variance (ANOVA) revealed significant differences between the S and NE/NW subpopulations across 24 environmental variables, including 15 precipitation-related traits and the aridity index ( Fig. 2A , fig. S2A, and data S6). GEA identified 260 drought-adaptive SNPs annotated to 215 genes significantly associated with 18 environmental variables ( P &lt; 1.0 × 10 −5 ; Fig. 2B and data S7). Notably, 72 SNPs exhibited pleiotropic effects, with the greatest overlap detected between the drought index and precipitation-related variables (fig. S2B). In addition, drought-adaptive SNPs exhibited significantly higher F st values than neutral variants ( P &lt; 0.0001; Fig. 2C ), indicating spatially varying selection. Mantel and partial Mantel tests further showed that neutral loci displayed isolation by distance (IBD) only within the S subpopulation, whereas adaptive loci displayed both IBD and isolation by environment (IBE) across populations ( Fig. 2, D and E , and fig. S2, C to H), supporting the view that geographic and environmental factors jointly shape adaptive divergence. Partial redundancy analysis (pRDA) showed that precipitation-related variables and the aridity index explained 45.3% of the genetic variance (fig. S2I). Gradient forest modeling identified five key environmental predictors: annual precipitation (Bio12), precipitation in October and November (Prec10, Prec11), precipitation seasonality (Bio15), and aridity index, which highlights contrasting adaptive responses between the humid S and the relatively drier N (NE and NW) subpopulations (fig. ### Synergistic regulation of PtoUGT72AZ2 by PtoWOX13b and PtoERF72 on lignin G-monomer biosynthesis in poplar
To investigate the enzymatic function of PtoUGT72AZ2 in lignin-related metabolism, in vitro assays coupled with high-performance liquid chromatography (HPLC)–MS/MS analysis demonstrated that PtoUGT72AZ2 catalyzes the glycosylation of ferulic acid and coniferyl alcohol using uridine diphosphate (UDP)–glucose as the donor substrate, yielding ferulic acid glucoside and coniferyl glucoside, respectively ( Fig. 7, A and B ). Phylogenetic and alignment analyses confirmed high conservation between PtoUGT72AZ2 and its “84K” orthologs (figs. S10 and S11A). In vivo, the concentrations of ferulic acid and coniferyl alcohol in PtoUGT72AZ2 -OE lines were decreased by 13.88% and 11.61%, respectively, whereas ptougt72az2 -KD lines exhibited increases of 25.47% and 13.23%, respectively, compared with WT ( Fig. 7C and fig. S11, B and C). However, no significant differences in total lignin content (TLC) were detected among the different poplar genotypes ( Fig. 7C ). Additionally, the expression levels of genes involved in the biosynthesis of ferulic acid and coniferyl alcohol, such as Pto4CL , PtoCCR , PtoCAD , and PtoLAC , were down-regulated in PtoUGT72AZ2 -OE lines, while these genes were up-regulated in ptougt72az2 -KD lines (fig. S11D). These results suggest that PtoUGT72AZ2 promotes a metabolic shift toward glycosylation and sequestration of lignin intermediates, which is consistent with the reduced accumulation of monolignols.

### Natural variations in PtoCPK3-ERF72 underpinning drought adaptation divergence
To dissect causal variants underlying lignin biosynthesis, mGWAS analysis identified a synonymous SNP226 (Chr3_6384016) in PtoCPK3 that was significantly associated with ferulic acid and coniferyl alcohol concentrations (data S9). In a natural population of 302 P. tomentosa individuals, the AA genotype exhibited markedly higher metabolite concentrations than the CC genotype of PtoCPK3 (fig. S14A). SNP226 was in complete linkage disequilibrium (LD) with InDel217, a 1-bp deletion at the 23rd nucleotide of the coding sequence (CDS), which caused a frameshift and premature termination ( Fig. 8, A and B , and fig. S14B). InDel217 defined two alleles, PtoCPK3 II (WT, functional) and PtoCPK3 DD (mutant, nonfunctional). The PtoCPK3 II genotype occurred in 53.97% of individuals, while PtoCPK3 DD was present in 34.11% (excluding heterozygotes). Poplars carrying PtoCPK3 II showed a 16.00% higher LC than those carrying PtoCPK3 DD (fig. S14C). Genotypic distribution analysis revealed that PtoCPK3 DD was enriched in the warm, humid S region, whereas PtoCPK3 II predominated in the colder and drier NE and NW regions (fig. S15, A and B). Functional assays confirmed that PtoCPK3 DD was restricted to the cytoplasm and failed to interact with PtoERF72, displaying a complete loss of kinase activity ( Fig. 8, C and E ).

### DISCUSSION
Plant drought adaptation involves multilayered regulation networks. Among these, CPKs play a pivotal role by integrating Ca 2+ sensing with kinase activity, functioning as direct “sensor-responders” that transduce intracellular oscillations into specific phosphorylation events. In Arabidopsis , CPK3 acts as a key positive regulator of ABA- and Ca 2+ -dependent stomatal closure by phosphorylating target proteins such as TPK1 (Ser 42 ) and SnRK2s (Ser 175 ), and its loss in the cpk3cpk6 double mutant severely impairs stomatal closure and drought survival ( 32 , 33 ). These findings establish CPK3 as a central component of guard cell signaling ( 34 , 35 ), yet stomatal regulation constitutes only one aspect of the multifaceted drought response. In woody plants, long-term survival relies less on transient stomatal movements and more on sustaining hydraulic stability over time. Here, we address this gap using P. tomentosa as a model, demonstrating that PtoCPK3 enhances drought tolerance by modulating xylem remodeling and lignin metabolism, which are shaped by both intrinsic molecular regulation and environmentally driven selection across natural populations.
PtoCPK3 enhances drought adaptation in poplar by regulating xylem structure and lignin metabolism

This integrated anatomical pattern aligns with the well-established hydraulic safety-efficiency trade-off observed in woody species, whereby both vessel- and pit-level traits shift toward enhanced resistance to cavitation and embolism spread ( 8 , 36 , 37 ). ### PtoCPK3-PtoERF72 cascade coordinates transcriptional regulation of PtoWOX13b and PtoUGT72AZ2 promoters
Protein phosphorylation is one of the most ubiquitous posttranslational modifications in plants, enabling the rapid and reversible regulation of protein activity, subcellular localization, and intermolecular interactions during stress responses ( Fig. 1 ) ( 53 , 54 ). Y2H-seq and phosphoproteomic analyses identified PtoERF72 as a key phosphorylation target of PtoCPK3 ( Fig. 5 ). Subcellular localization analysis revealed that PtoCPK3 localizes to both the cytoplasm and the nucleus ( 55 ), a dual distribution also observed for other CPKs and regarded as essential for coupling Ca 2+ oscillations with nuclear signaling ( 16 ). Further assays demonstrated that PtoCPK3 physically interacts with PtoERF72 at Ser 96 in a Ca 2+ -dependent manner, and substitution of this residue completely abolished their nuclear colocalization and phosphorylation ( Fig. 6 ). These phosphorylation-dependent nuclear interactions are consistent with observations in other plant systems, where phosphosite mutations similarly disrupt nuclear complex formation, such as SnRK2.6-ABF2 (Ser 126 ) and MPK6-WRKY53 (Ser 229 ) in Arabidopsis , and SAPK2-bZIP46 (Thr 180 ) in rice ( 56 , 57 , 58 ).
Functionally, phosphorylation of PtoERF72 at Ser 96 by PtoCPK3 enhances its transcriptional activity, establishing a regulatory cascade that controls two key downstream target genes: PtoWOX13b (xylem development) and PtoUGT72AZ2 (lignin metabolism). This dual regulatory mode—activation of one target and repression of another—reflects the well-established bidirectional transcriptional activity of ethylene response factor (ERF) family members. For example, ERF72 acts as both a transcriptional activator and repressor, and it suppresses SUS1 and HA2 to regulate iron uptake and carbon allocation ( 59 , 60 ), while activating stress-responsive genes such as RD29A , PAD3 , and CYP71A13 ( 61 ). ### Natural variation of PtoCPK3-PtoERF72 underpins allelic divergence in drought adaptation
Natural allelic variation constitutes the fundamental basis of local adaptation and serves as an essential genetic resource for molecular breeding ( 70 ). For example, natural allelic variation of ZmVPP1 and ZmAbh4 in maize modulates ABA signaling, while variation of Ghd7.1 in rice regulates phenology, collectively influencing WUE and drought tolerance ( 71 – 73 ). In P. tomentosa , natural allelic variation of PtoWRKY68 and PtoRSZ21 has been shown to affect drought responses by modulating stomatal morphology and ABA-responsive gene expression ( 74 , 75 ). Here, functional polymorphisms in PtoCPK3 and PtoERF72 were identified as key determinants of drought adaptation through combined GEA and mGWAS analyses in P. tomentosa ( Fig. 2 ). A frameshift allele of PtoCPK3 (PtoCPK3 DD ) abolished kinase activity and disrupted the nuclear interaction with PtoERF72 AA and PtoERF72 CC , whereas the functional allele (PtoCPK3 II ) retained phosphorylation capacity and promoted xylem reinforcement ( Fig. 8 ). Similarly, a nonsynonymous variant of PtoERF72 (PtoERF72 CC ) introduced an additional phosphorylation site at Thr 126 compared with the PtoERF72 AA allele, enhancing phosphorylation efficiency and strengthening downstream regulation of PtoWOX13b and PtoUGT72AZ2 ( Fig. 8 ).
The combined genotype PtoCPK3 II _PtoERF72 CC conferred the greatest drought tolerance in natural populations, characterized by higher lignin accumulation, increased vessel density, and elevated WUE compared with other allelic combinations. Comparable mechanisms have been reported in other species, where allelic variation in kinases or their substrates reshapes phosphorylation dynamics and modulates stress responses. For example, a promoter deletion in ZmSnRK2.10 (Del-356) diminishes phosphorylation of ZmHAK4 at Ser 5 and consequently alters stress tolerance in maize ( 21 ). ### Multi-omics dataset analysis
Xylem tissue collection and processing
For 4-month-old soil-grown poplars, stem segments were collected from the basal and middle parts of the trunk. The bark (including phloem and cambium) was manually stripped to expose the woody core. Xylem tissues adjacent to the cambium were gently scraped using a sterile razor blade, immediately frozen in liquid nitrogen, and stored at −80°C until further use.
Transcriptomic profiling of xylem tissues from 4-month-old poplars under well-watered and drought conditions was performed by Novogene (Beijing, China). Briefly, mRNA was enriched from liquid nitrogen–ground samples using oligo(dT) beads and fragmented. First-strand cDNA was synthesized using random hexamers, followed by second-strand synthesis utilizing DNA polymerase I and ribonuclease (RNase) H. After purification with AMPure XP beads, double-stranded cDNA underwent end-repair, A-tailing, and adapter ligation. Final libraries were generated via size selection and PCR amplification, then sequenced on the DNBSEQ-T7 platform. RNA-seq data were normalized as fragments per kilobase of transcript per million mapped reads, and DEGs were identified using Cuffdiff ( P &lt; 0.05, |FC| &gt; 2). PtoCPK3 -OE and ptocpk3 -KD lines were subjected to drought treatment for 15 days as described above, and phenotypic detection was conducted.
Proteomic and phosphoproteomic analyses
Proteomic and phosphoproteomic analyses of xylem tissues from the same material and treatments were performed by Qinglian Bio (Beijing, China). Following dilution, samples were digested overnight with trypsin at a 50:1 protein-to-enzyme ratio. Resulting peptides were desalted using C18 columns, eluted with 70% acetonitrile, and lyophilized. ### Association study
Genome resequencing of the association population and population genetic analyses
The whole-genome resequencing dataset comprising 302 P. tomentosa accessions was obtained, and after quality filtering [minor allele frequency (MAF) &gt; 0.05; missing rate &lt; 0.2; heterozygosity &lt; 0.9], 8,686,835 high-confidence SNPs were retained ( 82 , 83 ). Population structure was inferred using ADMIXTURE v1.3.0 using SNPs pruned for LD &lt; 0.2. Cross-validation error indicated K = 3 as the optimal number of subpopulations, consistent with simple sequence repeat (SSR)-based clustering ( 81 ). Kinship was estimated using EMMAX with independent SNPs (LD &lt; 0.2) ( 84 ).
Identification of drought-associated genetic variants

### Determination and distribution of chemical composition in stems
The chemical composition of cell walls from 4-month-old poplar stems was analyzed according to modified National Renewable Energy Laboratory (NREL) protocols ( 106 ). Stem samples were harvested, oven-dried to constant weight, and ground into fine powder before analysis. For removal of soluble compounds, 100 mg of stem powder was extracted with 80% (v/v) methanol under continuous shaking, followed by centrifugation. The insoluble residue was collected, air-dried, and used for subsequent lignin analysis. Cell wall polymers were hydrolyzed using a two-step sulfuric acid hydrolysis procedure. Briefly, the residue was incubated with 72% (w/w) H 2 SO 4 at 30°C for 1 hour with occasional mixing to allow complete impregnation. The reaction mixture was then diluted to a final sulfuric acid concentration of 4% (w/w) and autoclaved at 121°C for 1 hour to complete hydrolysis of structural carbohydrates. After hydrolysis, the reaction mixture was filtered to separate the acid-insoluble residue from the hydrolysate. Acid-soluble lignin (ASL) content was quantified by measuring absorbance at 205 nm using a UV-visible spectrophotometer, with appropriate acid blanks used for baseline correction. Acid-insoluble lignin (AIL) content was determined using the Klason method ( 107 ). TLC was calculated as the sum of ASL and AIL content. All assays were performed in triplicate, and mean values were calculated.

### Xylem anatomical structure analysis
The stem IN15 of well-watered and drought-treated poplars was collected for anatomical and ultrastructural analyses. ### Drought tolerance assessment
A total of 45 P. tomentosa individuals were selected, including 15 PtoCPK3 II _PtoERF72 AA and 15 PtoCPK3 II _PtoERF72 CC genotypes from the NE and NW regions, and 15 PtoCPK3 DD _PtoERF72 AA genotypes selected from the S region, respectively. To ensure genetic uniformity and eliminate environmental maternal effects, explants were obtained from accessions asexually propagated via grafting. These explants underwent surface disinfection and were established as in vitro cultures on 1/2 Murashige-Skoog basal medium. Clonal plantlets were then multiplied through subculturing to produce three identical replicates per accession. Four-month-old seedlings with uniform height and ground diameter were assigned to either a well-watered control group or a drought-stress treatment group. During the initial growth stage, soil RWC was kept at 70% ± 5%. Four-month-old poplars were assigned to well-watered controls and drought treatment (soil RWC reduced to 20% ± 5% for 15 days) before phenotyping.
