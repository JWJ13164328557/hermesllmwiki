### 核心发现
1. Collectively, this review provides an updated framework for understanding the regulatory significance of O-glycosylation in plant nutrient signaling and highlights its emerging potential in future crop improvement strategies.
2. Beyond their metabolic roles, sugars serve as powerful signaling molecules that interact with nitrogen status and energy sen­ sors such as SnRK1 and TOR to regulate growth decisions.
3. In particular, mineral nutrients interact with secondary messengers and phytohormonal pathways to regulate nutrient acquisition, growth, and stress adapta­ tion, thereby contributing to the maintenance of plant per­ formance under adverse environmental conditions (Das and Adak 2025).
4. Among these phytohormones, gibberellic acid (GA) plays a central role in regulating seed germination, vegetative growth, developmental processes, and stress responses.
5. Ubiquitination, mediated by sequential E1-E2-E3 enzyme cascades, targets nutrient signaling components including transcription factors and receptor kinases for precise 26 S proteasome degradation, preventing pathway overactivation while maintaining cellular homeostasis (Zhang and Zeng 2020).
6. Addressing these challenges will require sys­ tems-level approaches that integrate quantitative proteomics with structural biology, ultimately enabling the identifica­ tion of novel molecular targets for improving nutrient-use efficiency in crop breeding.
7. Collectively, the molecular networks of molecules involved in O-glycosylation comprise a central node in the nutrient-responsive signaling.
8. In drought stress, O-GlcNAcylation has been associated with abscisic acid (ABA)-mediated signaling pathways regulating stomatal closure and water-use efficiency, and it may interact with phosphorylation-dependent regulation of SnRK2 kinases (Qin et al.
