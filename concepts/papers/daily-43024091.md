# Nodulation induces systemic defense in Phaseolus vulgaris leaves against Sclerotinia sclerotiorum through ROS accumulation and COBRA-like gene expression.

## 摘要
Mature root nodule symbiosis enhances susceptibility infection to Sclerotinia sclerotiorum by coordinating ROS, defense metabolism, cell-wall carbohydrate profiles, and specific PvCOBL gene responses in Phaseolus vulgaris. Root nodule symbiosis (RNS) enhances plant nutrition and can systemically modify plant defense responses. However, the mechanisms linking RNS to resistance against necrotrophic pathogens remain poorly understood in Phaseolus vulgaris. In this study, we investigated whether nodulation induced by Rhizobium tropici influences systemic resistance to Sclerotinia sclerotiorum and is associated with changes in reactive oxygen species (ROS), defense-related metabolism, cell wall-associated carbohydrates, and COBRA-like (COBL) gene family. A temporal analysis of nodulation (7, 14, 21, and 30 days post inoculation, dpi) revealed that resistance to S. sclerotiorum was strongly dependent on the developmental stage of symbiosis. At the mature nodule stage (21 dpi), nodulated plants (N+) exhibited approximately 7 fold lower necrotic lesion area than non-nodulated (N-) plants, whereas susceptibility was greater at immature (14 dpi) and senescent (30 dpi) stages of nodulation. Enhanced resistance at 21 dpi was associated with a 5-fold increase in H₂O₂ accumulation, elevated phenolic and flavonoid contents, increased chlorophyll levels, and changes in cell wall-associated carbohydrate profiles. Genome-wide analysis identified 14 PvCOBL genes distributed across three phylogenetic clades and revealed substantial conservation in gene structure, and the characteristic CCVS motif. Promoter and transcriptomic analyses revealed that PvCOBL genes exhibit distinct tissue-, developmental-, symbiosis-, and pathogen-associated expression patterns. RT-qPCR further identified PvCOBL9 and PvCOBL10 as promising candidates associated with nodulation-dependent pathogen responses. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that nodule maturation is associated with systemic physiological and transcriptional reprogramming that contributes to reduced susceptibility to S. sclerotiorum. This work provides the first comprehensive characterization of the COBL gene family in P. vulgaris and identifies candidate genes that may link rhizobial symbiosis, cell wall-associated processes, and systemic immunity.
