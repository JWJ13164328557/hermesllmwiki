# Heat shock-responsive genes in Brassica napus: from omics-driven discovery to gene editing.

- **PMID**: [43049903](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049903/)

- **作者**: Aldrin Y Cantila, Gerry N Ramos, Sheng Chen

## 摘要
Heat shock-responsive genes in Brassica napus provide candidate targets for thermotolerance, with genomic clustering, co-expression hubs, polyploid expansion, and epigenetic regulation guiding functional validation and gene-editing-assisted breeding of heat-resilient canola. Heat stress is a major constraint to the productivity and stability of Brassica napus, particularly in the current climate change era. Heat shock-responsive genes (HSRGs), including heat shock transcription factors and heat shock proteins, are candidate regulators of thermotolerance. Integrating multi-omics evidence, we compiled 242 putative HSRGs candidates and identified 27 genomic clusters, suggesting synergistical regulation by gene duplication and evolutionary expansion. Co-expression network analysis across independent RNA-seq datasets revealed six modules and 50 hub genes, with 17 hub genes co-localizing with genomic clusters. Comparative orthology across 32 Brassicaceae species suggests substantial HSRG expansion in polyploids with B. napus exhibiting pronounced expansion ratios and identifies Brassica-specific orthogroups. Pangenomic evidence further indicates that structural variation is associated with heat-adaptive allelic diversity. Epigenetic regulation adds further complexity with HSRGs interacting with chromatin modifications and transcriptional memory to fine-tune stress responses. This integrative framework, connecting gene discovery, network architecture, evolutionary genomics, and breeding application positions HSRGs as candidate targets for developing heat-resilient canola to sustain productivity under future extreme climate challenges.
