# A natural mutation in CsCYP90A1 causes a super-compact plant architecture in cucumber (Cucumis sativus L.).

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43014681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014681/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113589](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113589)
- **第一作者**: Zhengao Zhang
- **作者**: Zhengao Zhang, Jing Wang, Jun Zhao, Tiantian Liang, Gaoyuan Zhang, Yuhong Li, Peng Chen

## 摘要
Plant architecture is a vital agronomic trait for high-density planting and mechanized cucumber production. Here, we identified a naturally occurring super-compact mutant, scp-5, characterized by a compact plant architecture, downward-curled cotyledons, and wrinkled, dark-green leaves. Histological analysis revealed significantly reduced stem cell length, suggesting that reduced cell elongation contributes to the compact architecture. Genetic analysis demonstrated that the scp-5 phenotype is controlled by a single recessive nuclear gene. BSA-seq and linkage analysis using 620 F₂ individuals delimited SCP-5 to a 141.84-kb interval on chr5 containing 11 predicted genes. A specific A596-to-T596 transversion in the first exon of CsGy5G007880/CsCYP90A1 caused an Lys-to-Asn substitution at an evolutionarily conserved residue. This variant was absent among 382 cucumber accessions, indicating that it is rare in the surveyed germplasm. VIGS of CsCYP90A1 produced a phenotype similar to that of scp-5, while exogenous brassinolide partially rescued the compact architecture of scp-5. Together, these findings identify CsCYP90A1 as an additional gene associated with cucumber plant architecture and provide evidence for its involvement in BR-related growth.
