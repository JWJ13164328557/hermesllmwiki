### 核心发现
1. The gut microbiota plays a pivotal role in maintaining intestinal homeostasis, and age-related microbial alterations are closely linked to intestinal dysfunction7.
2. Collectively, these findings indicate that fucoidan can be effectively fermented by intestinal microbiota to generate bioactive metabolites, representing a crucial mechanism for maintaining intestinal homeostasis in aged mice.
3. Notably, the HF exhibited strong, continuous ZO-1 and Claudin-1 fluorescence along the epithelial surface (p < 0.001), correlating with decreased serum permeability markers.
4. Notably, butyric acid, the preferred energy source for intestinal epithelial cells, correlated with the observed improvements in mucosal morphology and barrier function36.
5. Interestingly, the MF showed a less pronounced impact on community composition and diversity compared with the LF, suggesting a non- linear microbiota response to fucoidan43.

7. Notably, Desulfobacterota_I, primarily comprising sulfate-reducing bacteria that produce hydrogen sulfide and compromise intestinal mucosa44, decreased following fucoidan treatment, suggesting a reduction in potential intestinal toxicity.
8. Notably, fucoidan has also been reported to lower the F/B ratio in diet-induced obese mice, suggesting a broader modulatory effect on gut microbiota composition47.
