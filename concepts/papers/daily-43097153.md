# LMS2 regulates chlorophyll biosynthetic homeostasis to modulate photo-oxidative stress and immune response in rice.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: TAG. Theoretical and applied genetics. Theoretische und angewandte Genetik (2026)
- **PMID**: [43097153](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097153/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00122-026-05432-1](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00122-026-05432-1)
- **第一作者**: Yicheng Lu
- **作者**: Yicheng Lu, Yudan Tan, Bingyang Liu, Ao Li, Han Yun, Weiqin Gao, Guanghua He, Ting Zhang

## 摘要
Disruption of the LMS2-OsPORB interaction is associated with Pchlide accumulation, triggering a cytotoxic singlet oxygen burst that drives programmed cell death and activates plant immune responses. Lesion mimic mutants (LMMs) constitute valuable genetic models for dissecting the molecular pathways that underlie light-dependent photodynamic stress, chlorophyll homeostasis, and plant innate immunity. Here, we identified and functionally characterized a rice lesion mimic mutant, lesion mimic stripe 2 (lms2), which develops photoperiod-dependent necrotic leaf lesions appearing as black-and-white stripes, together with reduced photosynthetic pigment content and altered chloroplast ultrastructure. Map-based cloning revealed that LMS2 encodes a chloroplast-localized protein that specifically and physically interacts with protochlorophyllide oxidoreductase B (OsPORB). Loss of LMS2 function disrupts chlorophyll biosynthetic homeostasis, leading to excessive accumulation of protochlorophyllide (Pchlide) in the dark, with the high-risk, non-photoactive Pchlide species predominating. Upon light exposure, this aberrant Pchlide pool triggers a light-dependent burst of singlet oxygen (¹O₂) and consequent reactive oxygen species (ROS) accumulation, ultimately culminating in severe photo-oxidative cell death. Notably, inoculation assays with Magnaporthe oryzae demonstrated that lms2 exhibits significantly enhanced, broad-spectrum resistance to rice blast, accompanied by robust constitutive activation and up-regulation of defense-related signaling transcription factors and pathogenesis-related (PR) genes. Collectively, our findings reveal that LMS2 plays a critical dual role in maintaining chlorophyll metabolic homeostasis to prevent photodynamic damage while modulating immune response networks in rice.
