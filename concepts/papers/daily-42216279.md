Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a staple food, supplyingc. 20% of global caloric and protein needs. This allohexaploid species contains three subgenomes, A, B, and D, each from a different diploid wild progenitor. Many wheat breeding programs focus on developing varieties with traits that mitigate challenges posed by pests, pathogens, and abiotic environmental factors, such as drought and salinity stress. The annual loss in wheat yields due to pests and pathogens alone is estimated to bec. 21.5% of total projected wheat production (Savaryet al.,2019). In addition, drought stress affects wheat agriculture in regions with limited rainfall, and many irrigated areas depend on unsustainable extraction of water from aquifers. Wheat cultivation usually requires agrochemical applications throughout the growing season; if projected yields drop during later stages of growth, these inputs are wasted, representing a sustainability concern. Another focus is enhancing essential micronutrient content (i.e. zinc and iron) in wheat kernels. Gene editing and genome engineering offer powerful tools for developing wheat varieties with superior traits (Ayalaet al.,2024). Here, we provide a foundation for identifying and selecting candidate genes for editing and breeding by creating the atlas of cloned wheat genes, a comprehensive directory of 745 cloned wheat genes. ## 摘要
Bread wheat (Triticum aestivum) production is increasingly threatened by biotic and abiotic stresses. Developing varieties with improved stress tolerance and desirable end-use qualities is crucial for meeting growing global demand. Genome-editing technologies, particularly Clustered Regularly Interspaced Short Palindromic Repeats and CRISPR-associated proteins (CRISPR/Cas)-derived systems, represent powerful tools to accelerate trait discovery and crop improvement. Here, we present a comprehensive atlas of cloned wheat genes and discuss examples and strategies for using it to identify candidate targets for CRISPR/Cas-mediated improvement. Finally, we recommend ways to integrate gene editing into breeding timelines and accelerate the incorporation of desirable alleles.
