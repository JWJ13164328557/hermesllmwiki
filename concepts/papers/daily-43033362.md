# Insect-resistant rice and Metarhizium anisopliae sustain the brown planthopper susceptibility through AMPK-mediated lipid redistribution.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Crop health (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033362](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033362/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s44297-026-00090-7](https://doi.org/10.1007/s44297-026-00090-7)
- **第一作者**: Kai Lin
- **作者**: Kai Lin, Yutao Hu, Guy Smagghe, Wenqing Zhang

## 摘要
The brown planthopper (BPH), Nilaparvata lugens, rapidly overcomes host resistance and develops insecticide resistance, undermining sustainable rice pest control. Here, we show that combining insect-resistant rice IR36 with the entomopathogenic fungus Metarhizium anisopliae CQMa421 provides durable suppression of BPH under laboratory conditions. Across multiple generations, chemical insecticides drove resistance and host adaptation, whereas M. anisopliae maintained susceptibility. Combined exposure redistributed lipids by reducing diacylglycerol (DAG) and adenosine triphosphate (ATP) in the fat body and ovary while increasing DAG in the hemolymph, thereby prioritizing humoral immunity. This redistribution was mediated by AMPK activation in the fat body, which inhibited insulin signaling and lipid catabolism while limiting detoxification and reproduction. AMPK-mediated energy reallocation sustained BPH sensitivity to both resistant rice and fungal infection. Integrating plant resistance with microbial biocontrol therefore suppresses host adaptation and delays resistance evolution, offering a sustainable pest management strategy.
