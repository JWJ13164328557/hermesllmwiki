# A Streptomyces-derived cyclic peptide valinomycin primes two-layered plant local immunity and systemic disease resistance.

## 摘要
Intensive research has led to the identification of various natural compounds as alternative and complementary agents for the chemical management of plant diseases. Here, we propose that valinomycin, a cyclic peptide produced by Streptomyces bacillaris S8, is a putative biocontrol agent against various plant pathogens with dual functions. The compound demonstrated antifungal activity against the plant pathogenic fungus, Botrytis cinerea (B. cinerea), a necrotrophic fungal pathogen that causes gray mold disease in many plant species. Intriguingly, valinomycin boosted plant immunity with priming activity in response to pathogen infection. Plants treated with valinomycin exhibited an increased flg22-mediated reactive oxygen species (ROS) burst, mitogen-activated protein kinase (MAPK) phosphorylation, and callose deposition, known hallmarks of pathogen-associated molecular patterns (PAMPs)-triggered immunity (PTI). Regarding effector-triggered immunity (ETI), valinomycin potentiated hypersensitive response (HR), a representative phenotype of the ETI responses. Furthermore, ROS burst assay revealed that valinomycin simultaneously facilitated PTI and ETI that mutually strengthen each other. Valinomycin increased the resistance against Pseudomonas syringae pv. tomato DC3000 (Pto DC3000), without antibacterial activity. Valinomycin primes plant immunity by modulating plant hormone crosstalk, extending to systemic resistance in Arabidopsis. Notably, valinomycin also enhanced disease resistance in tobacco and soybean, demonstrating its potential for expanded application to many crop species. Therefore, our study highlights valinomycin as a versatile natural compound with potential as a biocontrol agent, combining direct antifungal activity with plant immune-priming function across multiple plant species.
