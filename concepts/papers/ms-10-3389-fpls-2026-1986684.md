# Species-specific regulation of photoperiod and end-of-day far-red light on the growth and quality of vegetable crops

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Frontiers in Plant Science ()
- **DOI**: [10.3389/fpls.2026.1986684](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2026.1986684)
- **作者**: Minggui Zhang, Jun Ju, Youzhi Hu, Xueli Shi, Xiangyu Wang
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要
To investigate the effects of different supplemental far-red (FR) strategies on the growth and quality of vegetable crops, this study systematically compared the effects of photoperiod FR and end-of-day FR (EOD-FR) on the morphology, biomass, photosynthetic pigment, PSII fluorescence, and nutritional quality of six vegetable crop entries (lettuce, Chinese kale, green/red perilla, tomato, and cucumber). The regulatory effects of FR and EOD-FR exhibited high species and organ-specificity. In leafy vegetables, the FR treatment significantly promoted biomass accumulation and stem elongation in all species. In contrast, the EOD-FR treatment induced species-differential responses, effectively promoting biomass and canopy architecture in lettuce and Chinese kale, while solely significantly promoting leaf area in green and red perilla. FR generally reduced the total leaf chlorophyll content and altered energy partitioning by decreasing the effective quantum yield of PSII (Y(II)) and increasing the quantum yield of non-regulated energy dissipation (Y(NO)). The EOD-FR treatment selectively elevated the chlorophyll a/b ratio and enhanced regulated thermal dissipation (Y(NPQ)) in lettuce and Chinese kale. The FR treatment increased the soluble sugar content in all leafy vegetables, and also increased the contents of soluble protein, vitamin C, polyphenols and flavonoids in green and red perilla, while decreasing these in lettuce and Chinese kale. For fruit vegetable seedlings, the FR treatment promoted stem elongation but significantly decreased the root-to-shoot ratio and seedling index of tomato and cucumber seedlings, Furthermore, FR increased the accumulation of malondialdehyde (MDA) and reduced antioxidant enzyme activities in tomato, whereas it exhibited a divergent response in cucumber by increasing POD activity and decreasing MDA levels. In contrast, the EOD-FR treatment moderately stimulated leaf expansion while maintaining balanced biomass allocation and stable morphological traits. Furthermore, the EOD-FR treatment improved PSII photochemical yield and energy partitioning, specifically by increasing Y(II) and reducing both Y(NO) and the quantum yield of regulated thermal dissipation (Y(NPQ)) in tomato seedlings. These findings demonstrate that customized FR lighting strategies should be developed for specific crop species in controlled environment agriculture. Continuous photoperiod FR supplementation is more suitable for increasing biomass in specific leafy vegetables, and can simultaneously improve nutritional quality in species like perilla, while comes with nutritional quality trade-offs in lettuce and Chinese kale. Conversely, the EOD-FR treatment serves as a more appropriate targeted lighting strategy for fruit vegetable seedlings to balance morphological expansion and robust seedling cultivation.
