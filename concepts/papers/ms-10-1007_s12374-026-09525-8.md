### 核心发现
1. Early studies relied heavily on RNA silencing approaches, which enabled rapid functional analyses but often resulted in incomplete suppression of gene expression and raised concerns about off-target effects (Watson et al.
2. Unlike earlier genome-editing systems, which relied on engineered DNA-binding proteins for target recognition (Urnov et al.
3. As a result, target sites can be specified simply by changing the guide RNA sequence, greatly accelerating mutant genera­ tion and extending functional genetics to any transformable species including crops (Shan et al.
4. Conventional CRISPR-based editing still depends on stable transformation and tissue-culture regeneration, which remain inefficient or unavailable in most wild spe­ cies, and can still produce mosaic or off-target edits.
5. Notably, these plants attracted herbivore species that were rarely observed on wild-type individuals, demonstrating that manipulating a single gene could alter the composition of the herbivore community associated with a plant.
6. By generating introgression lines in which a functional AN2 allele produces anthocy­ anin-pigmented purple flowers and a non-functional allele yields white flowers, they demonstrated that manipulating this single gene was sufficient to shift pollinator preference between bumblebees and hawkmoths.
7. Using VIGE-gener­ ated knockout lines, researchers produced myc2/3 double knockout mutants and demonstrated that these jasmonate- responsive transcription factors are required for herbivory- induced pith lignification (Choung et al.
