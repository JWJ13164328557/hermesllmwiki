# Systematic analysis of sweet potato glutathione S-transferase genes identifies key roles of IbGST4 and IbGST63 for anthocyanin accumulation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant cell reports (2026)
- **PMID**: [43102626](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43102626/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00299-026-04058-3](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00299-026-04058-3)
- **第一作者**: Can Huang
- **作者**: Can Huang, Feifan Li, Yuqi Wang, Weiwei Liu, Yifei Wang, Yumeng Lv, Siyuan Liu, Ping Ding

## 摘要
IbGST genes were systematically identified in sweet potato, revealing the key roles of IbGST4 and IbGST63 in anthocyanin accumulation in coordination with multiple IbMYB members. Glutathione S-transferases (GSTs) constitute a multifunctional superfamily of enzymes that play a pivotal role in plant anthocyanin accumulation. However, a comprehensive analysis of the GST gene family in sweet potato (Ipomoea batatas) remains lacking. Herein, a total of 106 putative IbGST proteins were identified and phylogenetically classified into ten subfamilies, supported by analyses of gene structures, conserved motifs, and functional domains. The expansion of IbGSTs was primarily propelled by tandem duplications, and collinearity analysis of GSTs between sweet potato and other plants further depicted the evolutionary insights. Subsequently, IbGSTs closely related to anthocyanin accumulation were identified by RNA-seq screening and qRT-PCR validation, showing expression levels of IbGST4 and IbGST63 positively correlated with anthocyanin content across varieties differing in tuberous root and leaf coloration. Functional assays demonstrated that co-expression of either IbGST4 or IbGST63 with transcription factors IbMYB2 or IbMYB3 in tobacco (Nicotiana tabacum) leaves markedly promoted anthocyanin production. Besides, dual-luciferase reporter assays confirmed that IbMYB1, IbMYB2, and IbMYB3 acted as transcriptional activators of the IbGST4 promoter. Collectively, the findings provide viable candidate IbGSTs for purple sweet potato improvement, and establish a foundation for further molecular decipherment of IbGSTs in anthocyanin accumulation.
