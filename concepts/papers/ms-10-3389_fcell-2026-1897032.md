2. Vaccines against hepatitis B virus and human papillomavirus target foreign viral antigens before malignant transformation and have reduced the incidence of virus-associated cancers (Chang et al., 2009; Arbyn et al., 2018).
3. Within precision oncology, a useful vaccine target is not defined only by mutation, overexpression, or computational prediction.
4. Recurrent alterations in KRAS, BRAF, APC, TP53, and mismatch repair genes can also influence antigen generation, immune escape, and target retention (Yoon et al., 2025).
5. Priority was given to peer-reviewed articles that provided mechanistic, translational, or clinical insight into antigen generation, antigen presentation, tumor immune escape, vaccine target selection, functional validation, and biomarker-guided patient stratification.
6. Some changes generate antigens shared across patients; others create private targets unique to one tumor.
7. These targets are useful because they can support off-the-shelf approaches, but they often face partial immune tolerance and, in some cases, the risk of off-tumor toxicity (Buona et al., 2020; Coulie et al., 2014).
