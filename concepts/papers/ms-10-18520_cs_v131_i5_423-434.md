### 核心发现
1. PEPC, a key cytosolic enzyme involved in carbon assimilation for C4 and crassulacean acid metabolism (CAM) plants, has been detected in tree stems, where it plays a critical role in bark photosynthesis50.
2. These findings suggest that bark anatomical properties and spec- tral light quality play important roles in regulating bark photosynthesis and respiratory CO2 refixation.
3. These findings suggest that conventional estimates of respiratory carbon loss may be overestimated without understanding bark photosynthesis in forest carbon budgets.
4. Un- derstanding this trait is crucial for comprehending whole-tree carbon dynamics, restoring degraded ex- treme sites, and developing climate resilience strategies using species with high bark photosynthetic capacity.
5. An effective chloroplast structure, light, carbon dioxide, water, nutrients and other enzymes essential for photosynthesis are required to drive the CO2 assimilation metabolism in the barks2.

7. This, in turn, reduces photosynthetic carbon assimilation, increasing the deficit in NSC, which are essential for osmoregulation and maintenance of cell turgor76.
