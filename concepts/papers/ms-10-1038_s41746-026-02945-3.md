### 核心发现
1. Our findings show that QoQ-Med3 trained with DRPO achieves an overall balanced accuracy of 71.3% and a F1 of 0.349, a 14.7 percentage point improvement over the strongest baseline, with particularly pronounced gains in understudied modalities such as ultrasound and mammography.
2. Our results show that QoQ-Med3 pretrained on non-EHR data (CLIMB) achieves 3.4-fold higher BioBERT similarity and 8.1% higher accuracy than the base model on MIMIC-IV in a zero-shot setting, with further gains when combined with MIMIC-IV finetuning.
3. As shown in Figure 6, we found taking full inputs across ECG, chest X-ray images, and electronic health records (EHR) gives better performance than any ablation of these modalities, signaling that QoQ-Med3 is able to effectively aggregate information across all modalities.
4. Specifically, we found vision and text contribute more to the final accuracy and F1 scores than ECG.
5. Collectively, our findings highlight both the potential of multimodal reasoning-based clinical foundation models and the critical next steps required to make them robust and reliable for real-world deployment.
6. In conclusion, we developed two multimodal reasoning–based foundation models and tested them on a a held-out MIMIC-IV and a private JHU PMAP dataset collected at Johns Hopkins University hospital.
7. Reasoning allows models to think explicitly in a more logical and systematic way with evidence from the inputs and their own knowledge48, all of which are essential for robust clinical diagnosis and doctor-in-the-loop usage49,50.
8. Notably, we observe consistent gains in both accuracy and F1 score on the length-of-stay (LOS), 30-day mortality, and in-hospital mortality tasks.
