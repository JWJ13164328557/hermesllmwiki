### 核心发现
1. Ecosystems such as coral reefs, man- groves, seagrass meadows, and salt marshes often collectively referred to as “blue carbon ecosystems” play a critical role in regulating global biogeochemical cycles, stabilizing shorelines, supporting fisheries, and sequestering atmospheric carbon [4, 28, 68, 82].
2. Alterations in the acidity and deoxygenation of the oceans can also interact with microplastics to have a com- pounding response on microbial stress.
3. Importantly, climate change impacts in marine systems are not governed by single stressors but by the interaction of multiple drivers that operate across spatial and tem- poral scales [26, 27, 36].
4. Embedding these tactics in the context of larger climate mitigation is necessary to maintain ecosystem functions and services on reefs.
5. Comprehensive policy frameworks that com- bine the imperatives of ecological resilience with socio-economic goals are necessary to ensure continued provision of coastal ecosystem services under increasing climatic change [54, 73].
6. More importantly, functionally significant groups of microorgan- isms, including nitrogen-fixing bacteria, sulfate reducers, and symbiotic ones, can be decreased, disrupting key biogeochemical activities, including nutrient cycling and degradation of organic matter [90, 105].
7. Since these factors often occur simultaneously, understanding their interactive and cumulative impacts is essential for accurately pre- dicting ecosystem responses and resilience under future climate scenarios.
