# Evaluation of the M444/Solanidine Metabolic Ratio for CYP2D6 Activity Assessment: Comparison With Dextromethorphan Phenotyping.

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Interindividual variability in drug response is largely influenced by CYP2D6, which determines the metabolism of 20% of marketed drugs. CYP2D6 is currently assessed by genotyping or drug phenotyping, through costly and specialized methods. Solanidine, a potato-derived alkaloid metabolized by CYP2D6 into several metabolites, including M444, is a promising endogenous biomarker reflecting real-time CYP2D6 activity, capturing both genetic and environmental factors.

OBJECTIVES: The objective of this study is to evaluate whether the plasma solanidine metabolic ratio (M444/solanidine) can serve as a surrogate marker of CYP2D6 activity by comparison with the reference dextromethorphan metabolic ratio.

METHODS: In this retrospective study, solanidine and M444 were semiquantified by LC-MS/MS in 69 patients previously phenotyped to optimize analgesic or psychotropic therapy.

RESULTS: Eleven patients were excluded because solanidine and M444 were below the limit of detection, leaving 58 patients to analysis. The solanidine metabolic ratio strongly correlated with the dextromethorphan metabolic ratio (dextrorphan/dextromethorphan) (r = 0.67; p < 0.0001). It differed across CYP2D6 phenotype groups (p < 0.0001) and discriminated poor metabolizers (PM) from other phenotypes. Receiver operating characteristic (ROC) analysis for the identification of CYP2D6 PM yielded an area under the curve of 0.992, with an optimal metabolic ratio cut-off of 0.04 (Youden index: 0.98), corresponding to 100% sensitivity and 97.9% specificity.

CONCLUSION: Although validation in larger studies, particularly under controlled potato intake, is needed, this study provides the first evidence in French patients that the solanidine metabolic ratio is a promising biomarker for identifying CYP2D6 PM and could support large-scale, cost-effective phenotyping for personalized medicine.
