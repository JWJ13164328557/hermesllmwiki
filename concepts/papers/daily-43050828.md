# S-Nitrosation and tyrosine nitration accompany salinity-induced programmed cell death in Pisum sativum L.

- **PMID**: [43050828](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050828/)

- **作者**: Swapnil Ombale, Rohi Bhatt, Abhishek Sharma, Budhi Sagar Tiwari

## 摘要
Programmed cell death (PCD) is a physio-biochemical process common to multicellular organisms in which target cells undergo self-directed demise to control cell number, remove damaged cells, and maintain tissue homeostasis. PCD is developmentally regulated and is also induced by biotic and abiotic stress, in which reactive oxygen and nitrogen species (ROS and RNS) generated within the exposed cell are widely implicated. Most mechanistic studies of stress-induced PCD have examined ROS or RNS individually, whereas nitric oxide (NO) and superoxide (O2•-) react rapidly to form peroxynitrite (ONOO-), a reactive oxidant/nitrating species capable of modifying proteins by two chemically distinct routes: S-nitrosylation, the addition of an NO group to reactive cysteine thiols (or, at lower frequency, transition-metal centres), and tyrosine nitration, the addition of a nitro group to tyrosine residues. These two modifications differ fundamentally in their chemistry and reversibility. S-nitrosylation can arise directly from NO and is enzymatically and non-enzymatically reversible, whereas tyrosine nitration requires peroxynitrite or related nitrating radicals and is essentially irreversible under physiological conditions. Information on how these two RONS (reactive oxygen and nitrogen species)-associated modification classes are jointly distributed across a defined PCD time course remains limited in plants, including in pea, despite prior reports of each modification individually in this species. In the present study, we characterised the temporal accumulation of ROS, RNS, and peroxynitrite alongside established PCD hallmarks (membrane damage, caspase-3-like activity, DNA fragmentation, nuclear and chloroplast morphological change) in salinity-stressed pea (Pisum sativum L.) seedlings and isolated mesophyll protoplasts, and used affinity enrichment coupled to mass spectrometry to identify candidate S-nitrosylated and nitrotyrosinated proteins across this time course. Forty candidate S-nitrosylated proteins were identified across 12-48 h, distributed across functionally distinct temporal stages, while forty-six candidate nitrotyrosinated proteins were detected exclusively at 48 h. The two modification classes showed no protein-level overlap but converged at the level of shared functional pathways. These temporal and functional patterns are consistent with a model in which S-nitrosylation, arising from an early NO-rich phase, and tyrosine nitration, arising once peroxynitrite accumulation is sustained, act as two distinct but temporally coordinated arms of the nitro-oxidative response accompanying salinity-induced PCD in pea. We emphasise that the protein identifications reported here derive from selective affinity enrichment coupled to MS/MS-based protein identification, without a dedicated mass-shift search for either PTM, and should be regarded as candidate modification targets pending residue-level and functional validation, and that the temporal associations reported between ROS/RNS accumulation, candidate PTMs, and PCD hallmarks are correlative; a causal requirement for peroxynitrite or NO in the observed PCD progression has not been directly tested in this study.
