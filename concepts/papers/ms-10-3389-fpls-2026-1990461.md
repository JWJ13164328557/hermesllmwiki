# Spatiotemporal dynamics of reserve metabolism and vascular development during soybean seed germination

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Frontiers in Plant Science ()
- **DOI**: [10.3389/fpls.2026.1990461](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2026.1990461)
- **作者**: Yujiao Zhang, Ming Xing, Jian You, Yueming Zhao, Wei Zhao
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要

                    Introduction
                    Seed germination is a pivotal developmental phase involving multiple tissues, yet a systematic understanding of their spatiotemporal dynamics in metabolism and reserve mobilization remains lacking.
                  
                  
                    Methods
                    
                      Here, by integrating morphophysiological and multi-tissue transcriptomic analyses, we profiled the spatiotemporal dynamics of five soybean (
                      Glycine max
                      ) seed tissues: cotyledon, plumule-proximal end, middle hypocotyl, radicle-proximal end, and testa across different germination stages.
                    
                  
                  
                    Results
                    The period 36-48h after imbibition represented a key temporal window, marked by the most intense transcriptional reprogramming. During germination, these tissues shared a common basis of stress adaptation and differed in metabolic functional specialization, reflecting both shared responses and tissue specificity. Notably, the testa displayed a temporal transcriptional signature during germination, shifting from basal metabolism and antioxidant defense to cell wall loosening, and eventually to cellular component degradation and defense. Transcriptomic and physiological analyses suggest a sequential relay of carbon reserves: early mobilization of stored soluble sugars, followed by protein and starch degradation, and finally potential lipid-derived gluconeogenesis. The vascular tissues of the embryonic axis and cotyledon originated from distinct anatomical origins; however, their vascular differentiations shared similar temporal patterns and was associated with the expression of genes involved in cell wall remodeling and lignin biosynthesis.
                  
                  
                    Discussion
                    In summary, we propose a spatiotemporal model that depicts the shared and tissue-specific responses of multiple seed tissues during soybean germination, deepening our understanding of germination physiology.
