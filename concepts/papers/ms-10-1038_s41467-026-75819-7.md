### 核心发现
1. Taken together, these results suggest that ACSS2 deficiency alleviates MASH, at least partially, by attenuating hepatocellular senescence.
2. This ultimately initiates a vicious cycle of chronic inflammation, which directly promotes the progression from simple steatosis to MASH.
3. ACSS2 also directly regulates fibrosis: Steinberg and colleagues21 demonstrated that ACSS2 controls hepatic stellate cell activation and fibrogenesis.
4. Moreover, owing to its pivotal role in controlling inflammatory and fibrotic responses, especially in amplifying dysregulated inflammation, ACSS2 presents a compelling target for therapeutic intervention20.
5. Previous studies have shown that SREBP1 can directly bind to the SRE element (−475 to −483 bp) in the ACSS2 promoter to drive its transcription47; and that ChREBP activates the target gene Acss2 to regulate hepatic acetate metabolism48.
6. Collectively, these data demonstrate that hepatocyte-specific ACSS2 knockout alleviates MCD-induced liver inflammation and injury.
7. Therefore, these findings suggest that ACSS2 regulates MASH mainly through inflammatory modulation rather than lipid metabolism.
8. Collectively, these lines of evidence demonstrate that AIF1 is a key downstream effector of ACSS2.
