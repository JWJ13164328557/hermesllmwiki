# Thermotolerance of natural Arabidopsis thaliana demes growing under salinity.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of plant physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43008421](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43008421/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jplph.2026.154914](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jplph.2026.154914)
- **第一作者**: Davide Giordano
- **作者**: Davide Giordano, Carlos González-Cobo, Milos Bartak, Charlotte Poschenrieder, Silvia Busoms

## 摘要
Climate change is intensifying abiotic stresses, with salinity and short-term heat stress emerging as constraints on plant growth and productivity. Although individual stress responses are well studied, plants frequently experience multiple stresses simultaneously. Natural genetic diversity plays a key role in shaping plant resilience under these conditions. Here, we investigated natural variation in thermotolerance under moderate salinity among Arabidopsis thaliana demes from Catalonia, where inland sites experience higher temperatures than coastal sites. Two screenings of 18 demes revealed divergence in heat tolerance that was not explained by the main axes of population structure, and demes were exposed to combined heat and salt stress. Under moderate salinity (25 mM NaCl), the inland deme A1 maintained relative biomass and photosystem II performance after heat shock, together with greater induction of HSFA2, HSP101, and HSP60-2. This advantage was attenuated at 50 mM NaCl, where heat increased leaf Na+ in A1 and reduced induction of root SOS1 and HKT1;1. In contrast, the coastal deme SFG matched A1 in relative biomass at 50 mM NaCl and showed the lowest leaf Na+ and highest K+:Na+ ratio after heat, coinciding with maintenance of SOS1 and HKT1;1 expression. Sequence analysis of 53 HSPs and 23 HSFs identified synonymous, missense, and regulatory-region variants differentiating tolerant and sensitive demes, while a genome-wide FST scan identified candidate regions. Together, these findings show that natural variation within a gene pool is associated with contrasting physiological and molecular responses to combined heat and salinity stress, with candidate mechanisms involving proteostasis and ionic homeostasis.
