# Phytosulfokine peptide regulates the growth-immunity trade-off through CAMTA3-mediated transcriptional signaling in Arabidopsis.

## 摘要
Although phytosulfokine (PSK) has been recognized as a crucial phytohormone for over three decades, its transcriptional signaling pathway remains largely unexplored. Here, we reveal a PSK-triggered transcriptional signaling pathway that fine-tunes the trade-off between growth and immunity in Arabidopsis thaliana. Through forward genetics, we identified Growth Retardation and Autoimmunity 1 (GRA1), which encodes tyrosylprotein sulfotransferase, an enzyme essential for the biosynthesis of sulfated peptides. Notably, like the gra1 mutant, the PSK receptor double mutant pskr1 pskr2 exhibits a phenotype characterized by growth retardation and autoimmunity. Application of exogenous PSK rescued the growth defects of gra1 mutants, and overexpression of the PSK precursor gene PSK3 significantly enhanced plant growth. Integrated transcriptomic, genetic, and biochemical analyses demonstrated that PSK promotes plant growth by repressing salicylic acid (SA)-dependent autoimmunity via the transcription factor CAMTA3. Mechanistically, PSK signaling attenuates the phosphorylation of CAMTA3, enhancing its protein stability and facilitating its nuclear accumulation to constrain SA-mediated autoimmunity. CaM6 and CaM7, two calmodulins that interact with the PSK receptor PSKR1, directly bind to and stabilize CAMTA3. Ectopic expression of CAMTA3 triggers autoimmunity in the cam6 cam7 double mutant but enhances plant growth in wild-type plants, demonstrating that CaM6/7 are indispensable for CAMTA3-dependent growth promotion downstream of PSK signaling. In summary, this study reveals a previously uncharacterized transcriptional regulatory pathway through which PSK orchestrates the balance between plant growth and immunity.
