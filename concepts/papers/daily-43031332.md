# A wild soybean-derived endophytic synthetic community for broad-spectrum crop protection and growth promotion.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Pest management science (2026)
- **PMID**: [43031332](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43031332/)
- **DOI**: [10.1002/ps.71479](https://doi.org/10.1002/ps.71479)
- **第一作者**: Linye Tang
- **作者**: Linye Tang, Wenmin Song, Yi Wei, Ram Prasad, Shihong Zhang

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Agricultural domestication has increased crop yields, yet it also has profoundly reshaped the host's symbiotic microbial communities, leading to the loss of stress-resistant phytomicrobiome. As an ancestor of cultivated soybeans, wild soybeans (WS) harbor a highly promising reservoir of high-quality microbial resources.

RESULT: This study systematically analyzed the succession patterns of endophytic microbial communities and their counterparts. Amplicon sequencing and symbiotic network analysis revealed significant asymmetry in the endosymbiotic communities' responses to domestication: bacterial communities remained relatively stable, whereas fungal community diversity in cultivated soybeans declined severely, and network topology became increasingly fragile. By contrast, WS maintained a highly connected cross-boundary symbiotic network. Based on network topology predictions and multidimensional in vitro functional validation, we isolated and reconstructed a cross-kingdom synthetic community, SynCom TL, derived from WS, comprising four bacterial strains and one fungal strain. In vivo validation demonstrated that SynCom TL not only significantly promotes the growth of the homologous host soybean, but also exhibits strong resistance to the soil-borne pathogen Fusarium solani.

CONCLUSION: SynCom TL transcends host phylogenetic limitations, demonstrating exceptional broad-spectrum growth-promoting capabilities in the monocot food crops rice and maize, while also significantly activating the expression of conserved systemic defense genes in these heterologous hosts. This study not only provides robust ecological evidence for the rewilding of the plant microbiome, but also offers an innovative paradigm for developing a new generation of broad-spectrum agricultural microbial formulations with cross-species universality. © 2026 Society of Chemical Industry.
