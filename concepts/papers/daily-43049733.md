# Six-Year Field Evaluation of the Defense-Inducing Biofungicide Bacillus mycoides isolate J for Grapevine Downy and Powdery Mildew Management.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43049733](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049733/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-09-25-1825-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-09-25-1825-RE)
- **第一作者**: Kaitlin Gold
- **作者**: Kaitlin Gold, Angela Paul, David Combs, Kathleen Kanaley, Wayne F Wilcox

## 摘要
Microbial biofungicides that activate host defenses could diversify grapevine fungicide programs, but their multiseason field performance is not well characterized. From 2017 through 2022, we evaluated Bacillus mycoides isolate J (BmJ; LifeGard WG; FRAC P06) alone and in rotation with conventional fungicides for grapevine downy mildew (Plasmopara viticola) and powdery mildew (Erysiphe necator) control near Geneva, New York, USA. Trials used randomized complete block designs with four blocks. Disease incidence and severity were assessed at harvest on 20 leaves and 20 clusters per replicate. Corrected-logit percentages were analyzed with year, program type, and their interaction as fixed effects and block nested within year as a random intercept. Significant program-by-year interactions were detected for all eight disease-tissue-endpoint combinations (P = 1.47 × 10-18 to 2.45 × 10-4). Whenever rotation and conventional programs were both represented, none of their within-year differences was significant after Holm correction. BmJ-only programs frequently reduced disease relative to untreated controls, but were less effective than conventional programs in multiple 2017-2019 comparisons. In 2021, BmJ-only programs produced lower powdery mildew severity than some conventional programs. BmJ-containing rotations frequently achieved control that was not detectably different from conventional-only programs, but performance varied among years and endpoints. BmJ is promising as an integrated-management component for grapevine powdery and downy mildew control, but not a fixed substitute for conventional fungicides. These results support continued evaluation of biological fungicide rotations to diversify grape disease-management programs.
