# Subfunctionalization with Partially Overlapping Roles of AGAMOUS Paralogs RdAG1 and RdAG2 of Rhododendron delavayi in Floral Organ Development.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43014682](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014682/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113595](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113595)
- **第一作者**: Qian Ao
- **作者**: Qian Ao, Weiwei Zuo, Liang Zeng, Qiqiang Guo, Bin Zhang, Hong Nan, Chao Gao, Huie Li

## 摘要
Rhododendron delavayi is an important ornamental plant in Southwest China that produces large showy flowers, yet the molecular basis of its complex floral morphology remains poorly understood. Here, we investigated the functional divergence of two AGAMOUS (AG) paralogs, RdAG1 and RdAG2, to elucidate floral organ development. Our results support a subfunctionalization model with partially overlapping functions: RdAG1 likely acts as a C-class gene mainly for stamen and carpel identity, whereas RdAG2 likely acts as a D-class gene primarily for pistil and ovule development. Heterologous expression in Arabidopsis thaliana and Nicotiana benthamiana revealed both divergent and overlapping phenotypic effects: the two genes differed in their tendencies to induce stamen-like and pistil-like structures, yet both induced homeotic conversion of sepals into carpels and were associated with downregulation of class A genes. Since an efficient regeneration system has not yet been established for R. delavayi, the C/D-class functional assignment is based on heterologous expression and awaits validation in native floral tissues. Yeast two-hybrid assays confirmed the physical interaction between RdAG1 and RdAG2, while DAP-seq in floral buds identified RdGIK as a shared downstream target. Transcriptomic analysis of calli generated via Agrobacterium-mediated CRISPR/Cas9 editing revealed that the majority of downstream targets were classified as showing additive responses, while a smaller subset deviated from this additive expectation. Collectively, these findings define a subfunctionalization model for RdAG1 and RdAG2, providing a foundation for further investigation of their finely tuned functions in floral organ development in R. delavayi.
