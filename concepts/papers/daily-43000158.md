# Pantoea Species Isolated from Onion Plants and Bulbs Across Different Regions of Chile.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43000158](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43000158/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PHYTO-04-26-0148-SA](https://doi.org/10.1094/PHYTO-04-26-0148-SA)
- **第一作者**: Gonzalo Calderón
- **作者**: Gonzalo Calderón, Valentina Muñoz-Quiroz, Ingrid-Nicole Vásconez, Michael Seeger, Jeannette Guajardo, Michelle Pena MacLelland, Bhabesh Dutta, Verónica Guajardo

## 摘要
Onions are one of the main vegetables cultivated in Chile. In recent years, changing climate conditions have favored the emergence of new bacterial diseases, in which Pantoea species have been reported as causal agents of onion leaf blight and bulb rot in central Chile. The objective of this work was to study the abundance of Pantoea spp. isolated from symptomatic onion plants sampled in different regions of Chile. The identification of Pantoea species was determined based on gyrB, leuS, and rpoB gene sequences, revealing P. agglomerans as the most frequently isolated species, followed by P. ananatis and P. eucalypti. Pathogenicity tests were performed. Only 3 of the 20 isolated strains showed clear symptoms of bulb damage, with P. ananatis exhibiting the most severe symptoms. The complete genome of a pathogenic strain of each identified species was sequenced. These genomes were used for the implementation of a PCR-high-resolution melting-based system for determining characteristic profiles of each species to facilitate the identification of the isolates in onion samples. Six primer pairs of housekeeping genes previously used in multilocus analysis were used to test their ability to differentiate among Pantoea species. The gyrB and leuS primer pairs showed the greatest discrimination among the Pantoea species. In this study, the identification of Pantoea species isolated from onion crops in Chile revealed that only a small proportion were pathogenic, even in diseased plants. This highlights complex plant-bacteria interactions and suggests PCR-high-resolution melting as a useful tool to study these populations and better understand disease development.
