# Mechanistic insights into the selective ligninolysis of wheat straw by the grass-litter decomposer Agrocybe pediades: A multi-omics and chemical study.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Microbiological research (2026)
- **PMID**: [43025161](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025161/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.micres.2026.128818](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micres.2026.128818)
- **第一作者**: María Isabel Sánchez-Ruiz
- **作者**: María Isabel Sánchez-Ruiz, Rashid Babiker, Ander Peña, Delphine Chaduli, Anna Lipzen, Jorge Rencoret, Gisela Marques, Ana Gutiérrez

## 摘要
Agrocybe pediades, a saprotrophic grass-litter-decomposing fungus of the order Agaricales, was studied for its potential to degrade all major components of plant cell walls (PCWs) as inferred from the enzymes encoded in its genome. When grown on wheat straw, this fungus preferentially degraded lignin and hemicelluloses while largely preserving cellulose. To uncover the molecular basis of this selective ligninolysis, we combined genomic, transcriptomic, exoproteomic and metabolomic analyses. Early stages of lignocellulose degradation were characterized by strong mobilization of H2O2-producing enzymes together with ligninolytic class-II peroxidases (PODs). Among ligninolytic PODs, manganese peroxidases were the most abundant. Their ligninolytic activity was related to the potential oxidation of phenolic lignin via Mn³ ⁺ chelates and of non-phenolic lignin through unsaturated lipid peroxidation. Notably, a highly transcribed high-redox-potential lignin peroxidase was also secreted during wheat straw delignification. This oxidative stage was accompanied by sequential transcription and secretion of hemicellulose-active enzymes, suggesting a stepwise xylan degradation. Enzymes targeting other PCW polysaccharides increased during growth. Although most lytic polysaccharide monooxygenases were transcribed, they were not detected in the exoproteome, suggesting that their contribution to cellulose degradation may have been limited. This, together with possible modulation of Fenton chemistry, may have contributed to the limited cellulose degradation observed. Additionally, insights were gained into the intracellular metabolism of sugars and aromatics released during the process. This multi-omics approach, supported by 2D-NMR and chemical analyses, provides valuable insights into selective delignification by A. pediades, contributing to our understanding of selective ligninolysis, an underexplored process with potential for plant biomass valorization.
