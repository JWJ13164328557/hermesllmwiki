# The impact of Thinopyrum ponticum group 7 segmental introgressions on durum wheat early root system development and adaptation to limited water availability.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant Science (2027)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113538](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113538)
- **作者**: Ljliana Kuzmanović, Maria Lia Di Romana, Maria Elena Virili, Alessandra Capoccioni, Miriam Negussu
- **来源**: crossref


## 摘要
To cope with increasingly frequent stresses, primarily drought, root system architecture (RSA) represents a key adaptive trait for efficient soil exploration and resource use. Unlike cultivated germplasm, wild wheat relatives harbour vast untapped variation in RSA traits, with perennial wheatgrasses typically showing deep, widely spreading, and highly branched RSA. Here, multiple root phenotyping approaches were used to evaluate RSA in seedlings/young plants of three durum wheat (DW) near-isogenic recombinant lines (NIRLs+) carrying segmental introgressions of the tall wheatgrass Thinopyrum ponticum 7el1L arm on 7AL, under normal and water-limited conditions. Among them, the R112 + NIRL (28%-long 7el1L segment) displayed the most evident RSA improvements compared with its R112 control and the other NIRLs+. Significant increases were observed in root length, thickness, spread angle, biomass (RDW) and root-to-shoot biomass ratio (R/S) under well-watered semi-hydroponic and rhizotron systems. Under water limitation, enhanced root development allowed R112 + to maintain below- and above-ground plant growth. Consistently, RDW, R/S, and root number were higher in R112 + in a pot drought experiment. The effects on RSA were associated with the proximal 23-28% region of the 7el1L segment, where the LRD-7el1 allele has been mapped and shown in silico to have a highly conserved structure across Triticeae species. As in the case of a 7el1-7D bread wheat introgression, R112 + showed reduced LRD expression in the root differentiation zone, particularly under drought. These results highlight the potential of the 7el1L introgression as carrier of a gene/QTL cluster (including LRD) contributing to improved RSA and drought adaptation in DW.
