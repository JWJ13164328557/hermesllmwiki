# MAX3 deficiency recruits protective Pseudomonas via modulating the SL-ABA-flavonoid axis to suppress soil-borne pathogen infection.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Nature communications (2026)
- **PMID**: [42637765](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42637765/)
- **DOI**: [10.1038/s41467-026-75807-x](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-026-75807-x)
- **第一作者**: Mimi Tian
- **作者**: Mimi Tian, Yiran Zheng, Wenhui Cao, Xinrun Yang, Tianjie Yang, Xiaofang Wang, Yangchun Xu, Alexandre Jousset

## 摘要
Strigolactones (SLs) are carotenoid-derived hormones that regulate plant development and abiotic stress responses, but their role in regulating plant-microbe interactions remains unclear. Here, we show that Arabidopsis thaliana loss-of-function mutants of two SL biosynthetic genes, MORE AXILLARY GROWTH 3 (MAX3) and MAX4, exhibit opposite responses to soil-borne pathogen Ralstonia solanacearum, with max3 mutants displaying enhanced resistance, whereas max4 mutants are hypersusceptible. Exogenous SL analog rac-GR24 restores resistance in max4 mutants supporting a role for canonical SL-dependent immunity, while max3 mutants mediated resistance is SL-independent. Multi-omics analyses suggest that MAX3 deficiency is associated with enhanced abscisic acid (ABA) and flavonoid pathways under natural conditions, coinciding with the enrichment of beneficial Pseudomonas in rhizosphere. Both in vitro and in planta validations suggest that the ABA-flavonoid axis cooperatively enhances Pseudomonas-mediated niche competition and antibiotic biosynthesis, thereby potentially contributing to pathogen suppression. Our findings support a model in which MAX3 is associated with the modulation of rhizosphere-mediated defense, linking hormone signaling, secondary metabolism, and microbiome assembly in the context of soil-borne disease resistance.

### 核心发现
1. Here, we show that Arabidopsis thaliana loss-of-function mutants of two SL biosynthetic genes, MORE AXILLARY GROWTH 3 ( MAX3 ) and MAX4, exhibit opposite responses to soil-borne pathogen Ralstonia solanacearum, with max3 mutants displaying enhanced resistance, whereas max4 mutants are hypersusceptible.
2. Our findings support a model in which MAX3 is associated with the modulation of rhizosphere-mediated defense, linking hormone signaling, secondary metabolism, and microbiome assembly in the context of soil-borne disease resistance.
3. Despite their sequential roles, our results suggest a striking functional divergence in their contributions to plant immunity.
4. Our results support a model in which coordinated metabolic and microbiome-associated processes involving components related to both SL and ABA pathways contribute to rhizosphere-associated disease resistance, rather than direct SL-ABA crosstalk.
5. These findings suggest that the integration of SL and ABA pathways varies with environmental and physiological context, but their interaction under biotic stress remains largely unexplored.
6. Together, these results suggest that exogenous SL can enhance disease resistance in an SL-deficient context, as exemplified by the restoration of resistance in max4-8 plants.
7. These findings suggest that canonical PTI/ETI-associated immune signaling is attenuated in the SL-deficient mutants, and that the enhanced resistance observed in max3-11 plants is likely independent of classical PTI/ETI pathways.
8. Together, these findings suggest that MAX3 -deficient plants, despite lacking canonical immune activation, exhibit a coordinated metabolic reprogramming characterized by rhizosphere accumulation of ABA and flavonoids, which is associated with enhanced resistance against bacterial wilt.
