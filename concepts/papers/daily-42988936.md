# Comprehensive meta-QTL analysis uncovers stable loci for fruit traits, morphological development, anthocyanin regulation, and biotic stress resistance in eggplant.

- **PMID**: [42988936](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42988936/)

## 摘要
Eggplant (Solanum melongena L.) is a crop of significant nutritional value within the Solanaceae family; however, advancements in breeding for fruit quality, stress resilience, and architectural traits are limited due to the quantitative nature of inheritance for these traits. Meta-QTL (MQTL) analysis provides a powerful approach for integrating QTL information from various studies for identifying stable genomic regions. We integrated 620 initial QTLs derived from 23 independent studies that encompass traits related to morphology, fruit characteristics, anthocyanin content, prickles, and resistance to biotic stress. A high-density consensus genetic framework was constructed, onto which 425 QTLs (68.54%) were successfully projected. Meta-analysis identified 97 stable MQTLs across LG1-LG12, with the mean confidence interval decreasing from 10.29 cM for the initial QTLs to 3.42 cM for the MQTLs, corresponding to an approximately threefold reduction in interval width (66.8% narrowing). Candidate gene mining within 40 prioritized MQTLs resulted in the retrieval of 1,440 gene models, which were subsequently refined to 68 high-confidence candidates. This refinement was based on domain architecture, synteny conservation with tomato and potato, ortholog analysis, and expression profiling. Key genomic hubs were identified across several chromosomes, most notably on Chromosome 6, which acts as a multi-trait super-region housing major loci for anthocyanin pigmentation (MQTL6.4_A), morphological traits (MQTL6.3_M), and prickle development (MQTL6.3_P). Other critical intervals include Chromosome 3 (MQTL3.1_Bs) for biotic stress resistance, and Chromosomes 1 and 4 (MQTL1.1_F, MQTL4.3_F) for fruit quality traits. These hotspots are enriched for coordinated, multi-layered regulatory networks and functional modules, including pattern-recognition receptors (e.g., LRR-RLKs), transcriptional regulators (WRKY, MYB), scaffolding proteins, and specialized metabolic and structural enzymes (such as GDSL esterases, laccases, F-box proteins, and SWEET transporters). The significant conservation of synteny with both tomato and potato, along with functional insights from Arabidopsis orthology, underscores the evolutionary stability and functional importance of these co-adapted candidate gene complexes across the Solanaceae. This study represents the first comprehensive MQTL analysis in eggplant, establishing a robust genomic framework for trait improvement, providing key markers and candidate genes to accelerate marker-assisted selection and genomic breeding aimed at developing climate-resilient, high-quality varieties.
