### 核心发现
1. In summary, these data suggest that FAO is not required to supply acetyl-CoA for histone acetylation of differentiation-related genes during basal epidermal keratinocyte differentiation.
2. Here, we reveal that early mitochondrial reprogramming, characterized by the activation of oxidative phosphorylation, is a determinant of differentiation initiation.
3. Using genetic loss-of-function experiments and pharmacological inhibition of FAO both in vitro and in vivo, we show that blocking this metabolic pathway disrupts EpSC differentiation, thereby impairing epidermal renewal and compromising stratiﬁcation.
4. These results uncover an unrecognized role for metabolic reprogramming in epidermal stem cell fate and highlight fatty acid oxidation as a promising therapeutic target for restoring differentiation defects in disease.
5. Collectively, our data surprisingly indicate that commitment to dif- ferentiation and the loss of the stem cell phenotype occur before the well-characterized transcriptional changes traditionally used to deﬁne the process of EpSC differentiation.
6. Taken together, the change in these parameters implies active mitochondrial restruc- turing and fragmentation during commitment to differentiation43,44.
7. Given that etomoxir has been shown to exhibit a variety of off-target effects49, we also suppressed mitochon- drial FAO by inhibiting CPT1 expression through siRNA-mediated knockdown.
8. Taken together, our ﬁndings demonstrate thatmitochondrial FAO is speciﬁcally required for the early commitment to epidermal differentiation.
