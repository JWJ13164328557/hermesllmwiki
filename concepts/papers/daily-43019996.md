# Coupled RNA editing and splicing within plant chloroplasts by the MORFs-CFM2 interaction module.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology and biochemistry : PPB (2026)
- **PMID**: [43019996](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43019996/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111944](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111944)
- **第一作者**: Ping Li
- **作者**: Ping Li, Ying-Qiu Tong, Su-Li Ji, Yi Li, Yu-Xuan Hu, Fen Xiang, David P Molloy, Chao Huang

## 摘要
RNA editing and splicing are two important post-transcriptional processing steps in plant chloroplasts. Evidence gathered in recent years indicates that these two steps are often tightly connected, although the underlying mechanism remains unclear. Here, we elucidate that the splicing protein CFM2 is associated with the MORF2/MORF9 editors and is involved in editing-splicing coupling within plant chloroplasts. Using immunoprecipitation-mass spectrometry (IP-MS) and in vitro/in vivo interaction assays, we identified the chloroplast splicing factor CFM2 as a direct physical interactor of the core editosome components MORF2 and MORF9. Disruption of CFM2 in Arabidopsis thaliana results in an albino-lethal phenotype, accompanied not only by impaired splicing of specific group II introns (e.g., ycf3.1, ndhA, and ndhB) but also by severe defects in RNA editing across multiple chloroplast sites, phenocopying morf2 and morf9 mutants. Conversely, mutations in the MORF genes analogously compromise splicing efficiency. Furthermore, we demonstrate that this mechanistic coupling is evolutionarily conserved between dicots and monocots, as loss of the rice homolog OsCFM2 drastically impairs site-specific editing. Together, our findings provide compelling evidence that, the MORF-CFM2 complex forms a physical association, coordinating the dual maturation of chloroplast transcripts to ensure photosynthetic function and plant viability.
