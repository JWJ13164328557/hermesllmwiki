# Quantitative trait locus-seq analysis identifies candidate genomic regions associated with seedling root growth responses to phosphate deficiency in rice (Oryza sativa).

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43072358](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43072358/)
- **DOI**: [10.1071/FP25388](https://doi.org/10.1071/FP25388)
- **第一作者**: Duong Thi Thuy Dang
- **作者**: Duong Thi Thuy Dang, Tam Thi Thanh Tran, Chi Thanh Nguyen, Nhue Phuong Nguyen, Rabina Sharma, Thi Mai Huong To

## 摘要
Phosphorus deficiency severely limits the productivity of rice (Oryza sativa), and improving phosphate acquisition and utilisation remains a major breeding goal. To elucidate the genetic basis of root growth responses to phosphate deficiency, we evaluated 200 F2/3 individuals derived from a cross between two different Vietnamese rice genotypes for root biomass and length under low-Pi conditions: (1) G22 (phosphate-sensitive); and (2) G299 (phosphate-tolerant). Using quantitative trait locus (QTL)-seq on bulks representing the phenotypic extremes, we identified three significant genomic regions on Chromosomes 1, 3, and 12, with the most prominent candidate region on Chromosome 1. Extreme filtering and annotation for single nucleotide polymorphism (SNP) revealed 11 putative candidate genes within this interval, representing auxin-mediated growth regulators, phosphate metabolism, lipid-derived signalling components, and transcription factors. Gene expression profiling showed distinct transcriptional responses between parental genotypes. Genotype G22 exhibited elevated expression of auxin biosynthesis and signalling genes (OsYUCCA3, OsIAA6), while genotype G299 showed stronger induction of OsPIN9 and phosphate-starvation-associated genes such as OsPAP10a and OsVIP1-like. These expression patterns are consistent with genotype-dependent regulatory responses associated with contrasting seedling root traits under Pi-withdrawal conditions. This study identifies SNPs and promising candidate genes associated with a potential QTL for seedling root growth response under Pi withdrawal, providing a basis for further functional characterisation.
