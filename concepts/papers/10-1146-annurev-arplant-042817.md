### 核心发现
1. Indeed, energy supply in the form of sucrose and perception of light signals from outside of the shoot meristem are required to stimulate WUS expression, with both factors acting through TARGET OF RAPAMYCIN (TOR) kinase, a conserved integrator of nutrients, energy, and growth factors (89).
2. Another KNOX gene target is ISOPENTENYL TRANSFERASE7 (IPT7), a gene involved in cytokinin biosynthesis (52, 134).
3. More in-depth studies found that WUS directly regulates additional cytokinin- and auxin-related genes, possibly con- trolling the balance between these two hormones, and also regulates a class of transcriptional corepressors involved in auxin signaling (15).
4. HOMEODOMAIN LEUCINE ZIPPER (HD-ZIP III) genes, whose protein products interact with the type-B RRs and bind the same WUS promoter region (140).
5. Another target gene of WUS is CLV1, but its expression is repressed by WUS, not activated as predicted by the CLV–WUS feedback model; therefore, this regulation is likely only for the fine-tuning of CLV1 levels (15).
6. In contrast, in direct confirmation of the CLV–WUS feedback prediction, one study found CLV3 as a direct positively-regulated target of WUS since its expression increased 2 h after WUS induction (133).
7. CIKs also interact with other CLV receptors and are phospho- rylated by CLV1, suggesting that they act as a direct downstream response to CLV3 binding by CLV1.
8. CT2/Gα interacts with the β and γ subunits (130), and, indeed, Gβ mutants in Arabidopsis have enlarged meristems and function in CLV signaling, through interactions with the RPK2 receptor (48).
