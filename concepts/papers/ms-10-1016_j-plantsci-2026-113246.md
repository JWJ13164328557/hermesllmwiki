# Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses reveal the involvement of AtGASA1 and AtGASA4 in salicylic acid pathway-associated immune responses in Arabidopsis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant Science (2026)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113246](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113246)
- **作者**: Jian-Bo Song, Ya-Ru Li, Zi-Xin Zhao, Xian-Zhi Zuo, Cai-Feng Wang
- **来源**: crossref


## 摘要
The gibberellic acid-stimulated Arabidopsis (GASA) gene family encodes a class of cysteine-rich proteins characterized by an N-terminal signal peptide and a conserved C-terminal GASA domain containing 12 cysteine (Cys) residues. These small plant-specific proteins have been identified across various species, where they primarily regulate plant growth and abiotic stress responses. However, whether AtGASA family members also contribute to immune responses against bacterial pathogens remains largely unexplored. In this study, we found that the gasa1 and gasa4 mutants exhibited increased susceptibility to P. syringae infection compared to Col-0. To elucidate these roles in plant immunity, we performed transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses. Compared with Col-0, transcriptomic profiling identified 957 differentially expressed genes (DEGs) in the gasa1 and gasa4 mutants upon pathogen infection, while metabolomic analysis revealed 900 differentially accumulated metabolites (DAMs). Integrated transcriptomic and metabolomic analyses further demonstrated that DEGs and DAMs were co-enriched in key pathways, including plant hormone signal transduction and biosynthesis of various plant secondary metabolites. Notably, within the plant hormone signal transduction pathway, multiple genes associated with salicylic acid (SA) biosynthesis and signaling, including ICS1, EDS1, PAD4, PR1, and PR5, were differentially expressed in the mutants. Consistently, SA levels in the gasa1 and gasa4 mutants were significantly lower than those in Col-0. Together, these findings suggest that AtGASA1 and AtGASA4 participate in SA pathway-associated immune responses during P. syringae infection.
