# Dual-target in silico screening of a phytochemical library against tomato brown rugose fruit virus coat and movement proteins.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Functional plant biology : FPB (2026)
- **PMID**: [43085330](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43085330/)
- **DOI**: [10.1071/FP26151](https://doi.org/10.1071/FP26151)
- **第一作者**: Serap Demirel
- **作者**: Serap Demirel, Abdullah Güller, Mustafa Usta

## 摘要
We evaluated 98 phytochemicals, structurally related derivatives, and reference antiviral compounds against the coat protein (CP) and movement protein (MP) of Tomato brown rugose fruit virus (ToBRFV, Tobamovirus fructirugosum) using a dual-target in silico screening approach. CP and MP were selected because they are associated with virion assembly, particle stability, and viral movement. Three-dimensional protein models were generated using AlphaFold, SWISS-MODEL, and Robetta, and the most suitable models were selected according to structural validation parameters. Molecular docking analysis showed binding energy values ranging from -7.5 to -3.9 kcal/mol for CP and from -8.1 to -4.2 kcal/mol for MP. Among the screened compound groups, flavonoid glycosides and alkaloids produced the strongest overall binding profiles. Diosmin, scutellarin, prunin, sanguinarine, and rutaecarpine met the dual-target selection criteria and were selected for detailed interaction analysis. These ligands formed hydrogen bonds, hydrophobic contacts, and π-related interactions with residues located in the predicted binding regions of both viral proteins. ADMET evaluation showed clear differences among the selected ligands in terms of polarity, lipophilicity, absorption-related properties, and drug-likeness parameters. Diosmin showed the strongest predicted dual-target affinity, whereas rutaecarpine displayed a more balanced profile when docking results and ADMET outputs were considered together. These findings identify selected flavonoid glycosides and alkaloids as preliminary ToBRFV antiviral candidates and provide a basis for further molecular dynamics and experimental validation studies.
