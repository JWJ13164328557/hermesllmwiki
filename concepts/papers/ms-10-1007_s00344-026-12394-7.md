### 核心发现
1. Interestingly, we found that for prolonged chilling and salinity stress, the presence of malonic acid (VIP score > 2) increased in both stress condi­ tions, whereas for iminodiacetic acid whose level was high under chilling stress at any time, raised slightly only upon prolonged and high salt concentration.
2. For example, at 14 days, we found that several nitrogen containing compounds, nico­ tinic acid, nicotinamide arginine and hydroxyurea were markedly reduced with salt, instead aminoacids like cys­ teine, methionine, isoleucine and the polyamine putrescine were increased with higher salt levels.
3. To assess the balance between growth and defense, we measured the enzymatic activity of glucose-6-phosphate dehydrogenase (G6PDH), a key regu­ latory enzyme of the oxidative pentose phosphate pathway (OPPP), which plays a central role in governing carbon flux during stress responses.
4. Collectively, these preprocessing steps improve data structure, optimise the performance of multivariate analyses, and facilitate more reliable biological interpretation.
5. These findings suggest a progressive and broad reprogramming of both primary and stress-responsive metabolic processes under chilling stress.
6. Taken together, these changes show that under chilling stress, regardless of exposure duration, cardoon calli undergo a metabolic shift away from growth and toward stress adaptation.
7. Overall, our data provide new insights into the metabolic capacity of cardoon cells to adapt to abiotic stresses.
8. Interestingly, the data suggest the activation of the glucuro­ nate pathway, as evidenced by the accumulation of related intermediates, indicating a potential shift in energy metabo­ lism (i.e., gluheptonic and phenylpyruvic VIP score > 2.6) along with phenylalanine (VIP > 1.6) which indicates the activation of the shikimate pathway.
