### 核心发现
1. Interestingly, when examining the published RNA-seq data (NCBI SRA: SRR12868314) of XZ1516, we found 31 “G to A” mismatches between the mRNA sequence and the genomic sequences in the sept-1(XZ1516) 3′UTR (Fig 4C).
2. In the XZ1516 genome, we found inversion and duplication events that led to sequence divergence of the toxin sup-35 and a deletion event that likely inactivated the antidote pha-1.
3. Using RT-qPCR, we found that the heat-shock promoter induced similar mRNA expression of sept-1(N2) and sept-1(XZ1516) (S5A Fig), but western blot results indicated that the same heat-shock condition resulted in higher expres­ sion of SEPT-1(XZ1516) proteins than SEPT-1(N2) (both are fused with HA-tags; S5B Fig).
4. We do not know why our sept-1(XZ1516) probes did not produce any signal in wild-type XZ1516 animals, but upon close inspection of the sept-1(XZ1516) mRNA sequence, we found a very long stem-loop structure in the 795-nt 3′UTR region (Fig 4B).
5. We found that the sept-1(XZ1516) mRNA levels only decreased by 5% (not significant) in adr-2(-) mutants, which deleted the only active adenosine deaminase in C.
6. We found that if the offspring of the N2/ XZ1516 hybrids carried the extrachromosomal array, they can survive even if they did not have XZ1516 chrV (Fig 3C).
7. For the non-Hawaiian JU1652 and NIC166, we found no nonsynonymous mutations in peel-1 despite the loss of zeel-1.
8. Upon close inspection of the assembled genome of DL238, we found an open reading frame for sup-35, and the predicted SUP-35(DL238) proteins showed significant sequence divergence from the SUP-35(N2) protein (42.9% identity and 57% similarity; S8A and S8B Fig).
