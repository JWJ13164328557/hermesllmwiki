# Design of experiments-guided optimization of supercritical emulsion extraction for salycilic acid microencapsulation and controlled release.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Colloids and surfaces. B, Biointerfaces (2026)
- **PMID**: [43013905](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43013905/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.colsurfb.2026.116324](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.colsurfb.2026.116324)
- **第一作者**: Maria Chiara Iannaco
- **作者**: Maria Chiara Iannaco, Adelinda Cavaliere, Stefania Mottola, Iolanda De Marco

## 摘要
Effective encapsulation of bioactive compounds is crucial for enhancing their stability and controlled release in agriculture. This study investigates Supercritical Emulsion Extraction (SEE) to encapsulate salicylic acid (SA), a plant resistance inducer. Unlike previous studies mainly demonstrating agroactive loading through supercritical emulsion systems, this work combines an O/W SEE process with Design of Experiments (DoE) optimization, droplet-to-particle modelling, dual-pH release analysis, and preliminary biological evaluation. A DoE approach was used to investigate the influence of polymer amount, emulsification speed, and emulsification duration on median particle size and encapsulation efficiency. Emulsification speed primarily governed particle size, whereas polymer amount significantly influenced encapsulation efficiency. Under optimal conditions, spherical microcapsules with a median diameter of 0.92 μm were produced. A simplified Monte Carlo model indicated limited droplet coalescence and approximately 10% size-independent shrinkage, supporting the role of emulsion droplets as templates for final particle formation. The suspension remained stable for over four weeks. Encapsulation prolonged SA release from 1.53 to 7.50 h at pH 5 and from 1.33 to 3.33 h at pH 7. Preliminary radish microgreen tests suggested that controlled release may mitigate early growth inhibition while modulating pigmentation-related responses. However, these findings require confirmation under soil-simulated and field conditions.
