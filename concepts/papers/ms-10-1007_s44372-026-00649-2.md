### 核心发现
1. Interestingly, among the constant R: B treatments, the lower proportions of red light in LT5 to LT7 were associated with reduced lignin levels, indicating that a minimal proportion of red light is required to effectively stimulate lignin biosynthesis.
2. Our findings support this idea, as the treatment involving a gradual increase in R light from 70% to 85% during the seed­ ling stage (LT4) resulted in the most vigorous growth.
3. In summary, light quality during the seedling phase significantly influenced the cold tolerance of studied plant materials.
4. Different numbers of R and B LEDs were installed on individual Plexiglas plates (55 × 45 cm) to obtain the target ratios, and each plate was powered by a constant-voltage driver (Sunpower, SP-60 A 12 V, UK).
5. Taken together, these molecular responses suggest that varying proportion of B and R light can influence cold tolerance, a pattern consistent with our observations.
6. Membrane integrity, a primary target of cold stress, was compromised in treatments with higher EL values.
7. Overall, this study demonstrates that cold tolerance in Viola × wittrockiana ‘Blue Bloch’ seedlings can be enhanced through precise spectral light management dur­ ing early developmental stages.
8. Studies on Arabidopsis, rice, and tomato have demonstrated that the activ­ ity of Phy B, as the primary R light receptor, is crucial for the expression of cold-induced gene such as CBFs, which regulate the expression and accumulation of cold-responsive (COR) genes, leading to membrane stabilization under cold conditions [7, 8].
