### 核心发现
1. Note the signiﬁcant downregulation of pancreas beta cells and E2F target pathways across multiple inhibitory clusters and strong upregulation of transforming growth factor-beta (TGF-beta) signaling, angiogenesis, and IL6-JAK-STAT3 signaling in IN_Early_1, IN_Mid_1, and IN_SST clusters.
2. Notably, TGF-β signaling may serve as an instructive signal for neurons to switch from a “growth state” to a “synaptogenic state” [43, 44]—in line with downregulation of insulin-responsive genes (i.e., “hallmark pancreas beta cells”) with both SPs.
3. Novel, rapid-acting antidepressants are urgently needed, but developing therapeutic targets using animal models has proven challenging.
4. iPSC studies of ketamine found increased neural progenitor proliferation via cAMP-IGF2 signaling [13] and enhanced structural plasticity via AMPAR-driven brain-derived neurotrophic factor (BDNF) and mechanistic target of rapamycin (mTOR) signaling [12], paralleling observations in animal models.

Because the primary intention was to identify overlapping iPSC targets that correspond to the “ground-truth” (i.e., human CSF post-ketamine), and because of limited assay coverage of DEGs in the Olink platform (see Discussion), differential CSF protein expression was FDR-corrected for signiﬁcance, while DEGs from the iPSC conditions were kept uncorrected (p < 0.05).

Pathways governing metabolism, growth, and proliferation, including mTORC1 signaling, oxidative phosphorylation, fatty acid metabo- lism, glycolysis, and MYC target pathways (V1 and V2) were signiﬁcantly upregulated in the excitatory EX_Mature_1 cluster and simultaneously downregulated in the inhibitory IN_Mid_2 cluster.
8. Together, these ﬁndings suggest that while both SPs and glutamatergic modulators ultimately converge on increased synaptic plasticity, they appear to engage distinct cellular targets and molecular pathways to achieve these effects.
