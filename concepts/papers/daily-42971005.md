# Biochemical and functional divergence of phenylalanine ammonia-lyase isoforms involved in specialized metabolism in Salvia miltiorrhiza.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Bioorganic chemistry (2026)
- **PMID**: [42971005](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42971005/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.bioorg.2026.110710](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.bioorg.2026.110710)
- **第一作者**: Shiyao Su
- **作者**: Shiyao Su, Xiang Wang, Dachuan Zhang, Jingmei Wang, Shuting Tan, Lei Li, Maochun Xiao, Rongting Min

## 摘要
Phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL) catalyzes the first committed step in phenylpropanoid biosynthesis, but the functional specialization of PAL isoforms in Salvia miltiorrhiza remains unclear. We characterized SmPAL1, SmPAL2, and SmPAL3, using kinetic analysis of recombinant enzymes, molecular docking, and analyses of transgenic hairy-root lines. Under their respective optimal conditions, each isoform displayed a distinct maximum apparent catalytic performance. For SmPAL1, the apparent catalytic efficiency was relatively high (kcat/Km = 6645 ± 336 M-1·s-1; kcat = 16.68 ± 0.52 s-1; Km = 2.51 ± 0.10 mM). For SmPAL2, the apparent Km was relatively low (1.05 ± 0.04 mM) but the kcat was low (2.90 ± 0.29 s-1). For SmPAL3, the kcat/Km was 5956 ± 481 M-1·s-1. Molecular docking produced similar docking scores for the three isoforms (-6.5 to -6.2 kcal·mol-1). In hairy roots, SmPAL1 overexpression significantly increased rosmarinic acid and salvianolic acid B (2.33 times the corresponding contents in the empty-vector (EV) control) and elevated protocatechuic aldehyde and caffeic acid (2.42- and 1.70-fold, respectively). SmPAL2 overexpression increased protocatechuic aldehyde (2.66-fold) and tanshinone IIA (1.56-fold), whereas SmPAL3 overexpression elevated protocatechuic aldehyde and caffeic acid (2.70- and 1.57-fold, respectively) but did not significantly alter rosmarinic acid or salvianolic acid B. These findings indicate functional divergence among SmPAL isoforms and highlight SmPAL1 as a promising target for enhancing selected phenolic acids. Whether SmPAL2 influences tanshinone biosynthesis or carbon allocation requires further flux analysis.
