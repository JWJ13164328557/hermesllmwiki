## 摘要
With the escalating degradation of global environmental conditions, soil salinization has emerged as a significant threat to global food security, resulting in substantial losses in crop yields. As a staple food crop of paramount importance, the stability of rice yields and quality warrants close examination. Salinity induces acute osmotic stress and ion toxicity, leading to the excessive accumulation of reactive oxygen species (ROS). These oxidative stresses and the subsequent physiological cascades cause irreversible cellular damage throughout the growth and developmental cycle, severely limiting grain yield. This review summarizes recent advances in understanding the physiological and molecular responses of rice to salinity stress, focusing on developmental plasticity, spatiotemporal regulatory mechanisms of gene expression, and the complex networks of phytohormone crosstalk from 2020 onwards. Furthermore, this review identifies the current challenges of elucidating salt tolerance mechanisms and translating these insights into breeding programs, and proposes potential strategies for enhancing salinity adaptation. The aim of this work is to provide a comprehensive theoretical framework and actionable insights for future molecular-targeted breeding and germplasm innovation in rice.
