# Indole Coordinates Ecological Defence and Quality-Related Metabolism in Tea Plants.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant, cell & environment (2026)
- **PMID**: [43096712](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43096712/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/pce.71009](https://doi.org/10.1111/pce.71009)
- **第一作者**: Lintong He
- **作者**: Lintong He, Fengjing Liu, Hui Zhang, Yujie Liu, Lingjia Feng, Siye Chen, Hui Yan, Jianyan Huang

## 摘要
Herbivore-induced plant volatiles (HIPVs) play pivotal roles in mediating plant defence, yet their ecological efficacy and metabolic consequences under realistic field conditions remain poorly resolved. Here, we conducted field experiments in a tea plantation to assess whether the HIPV indole can coordinate ecological defence and quality-related metabolism in tea plants (Camellia sinensis). Using slow-release exposure and foliar spraying approaches, we surveyed pest populations and natural enemy assemblages in 2022 and 2023, and characterised key taste-related metabolites and transcriptomic responses using leaf samples collected in 2023. Indole application consistently suppressed major piercing-sucking pests, including the tea green leafhopper, whitefly, and citrus spiny whitefly, while enhancing the abundance of predatory spiders and parasitoid wasps. Importantly, indole exerted selective effects across herbivore taxa, indicating ecologically precise modulation rather than indiscriminate suppression. Beyond its effects on arthropod communities, indole markedly reprogrammed tea metabolic profiles by reducing bitterness-associated catechins and caffeine while increasing umami- and sweetness-related amino acids, particularly theanine. Transcriptomic analyses further showed that indole redirected phenylpropanoid and flavonoid metabolism, supporting a coordinated shift toward defence-associated branches while suppressing catechin biosynthesis. Foliar spraying produced stronger ecological, metabolic and transcriptional responses than slow-release exposure under the tested field conditions. Together, these results provide the first comprehensive field-based evidence that a single HIPV can simultaneously contribute to pest suppression and reshape quality-related metabolite profiles, with metabolic changes potentially associated with reduced bitterness/astringency and increased umami/sweetness. Our findings position indole as a multitiered regulator linking ecological defence with metabolic optimisation, highlighting the broader potential of volatile-based strategies for sustainable pest management and functional crop improvement.
