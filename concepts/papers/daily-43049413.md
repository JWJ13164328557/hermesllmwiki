# Moderate Phosphorus Application Reshapes Rhizosphere Microbial Ecology and Coordinates Host Metabolic Responses to Improve Sweet Potato Yield and Quality: A Multiomics System-Level Analysis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of agricultural and food chemistry (2026)
- **PMID**: [43049413](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049413/)
- **DOI**: [10.1021/acs.jafc.6c10961](https://doi.org/10.1021/acs.jafc.6c10961)
- **第一作者**: Jinyang Zhao
- **作者**: Jinyang Zhao, Yujie Li, Muhammad Shahid, Kashif Khan, Luqman Khan, Chengcheng Si

## 摘要
Sweet potato is an important food, feed, and industrial crop whose storage root development and quality depend on phosphorus (P) availability, yet plant-soil-microbe responses to P fertilization remain unclear. We integrated agronomic, metagenomic, transcriptomic, metabolomic, and WGCNA analyses comparing moderate P application (P110; 110 kg P2O5 ha-1) with no P (P0). P110 increased soil organic carbon by 45.03% and available potassium by 9.58%; organic/inorganic P declined, while available P remained unchanged. Rhizosphere shifts included higher Acidobacteriota, Basidiomycota, and fungal diversity. P-cycling genes pckA, gcd, and ppk were higher, indicating altered metagenomic potential for soil P turnover. P110 increased storage roots per plant by 20.31-27.83%, yield by 31.88-41.45%, and plant P accumulation by 134.01%. Multiomics-linked hub genes (ARR6, LRR4, LRR3, FKF1, TIC100) and pathways to root traits, yield, and quality. Moderate P coordinates rhizosphere microbial ecology with host transcriptional and metabolic responses, improving P-use, yield, and quality.
