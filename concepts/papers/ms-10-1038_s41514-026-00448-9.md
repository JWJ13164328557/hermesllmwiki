### 核心发现
1. These findings suggest spermidine as not only a "caloric restriction mimetic" with its supplementation mimicking the beneficial effects of nutrient deprivation but also a downstream effector of the possibly geroprotective effects of fasting and rapamycin.
2. The proximal pharmacological target of spermidine is indeed the same as that of salicylic acid, the active metabolite or aspirin, both suppressing EP300 by competing for acetyl coenzyme binding 14.
3. These findings suggest that spermidine supplementation may serve as a viable therapeutic solution for obesity.
4. As higher plasma polyamine levels are associated with increased risk of poststroke cognitive impairment (PSCI) among patients with ischemic stroke, plasma polyamines may be implicated in the pathophysiologic processes of PSCI and represent potential intervention targets for PSCI.
5. Knockdown of ODC, SRM and SMOX reduced α-synuclein toxicity, while inhibition of SAT1 and SMS exacerbated the toxicity, underscoring the critical role of polyamine pathways in regulating the effect of α-synuclein and offering novel therapeutic targets for Parkinson's Disease (PD) 52.
6. Moreover, spermidine may have beneficial effects, which can be attributed to its metabolites, since spermidine supplementation significantly enhances plasma spermine levels in healthy adults without affecting the concentration of spermidine per se or putrescine 59.
7. Nutritional interventions including calorie restriction (CR) and rapamycin extend healthspan via molecular mechanisms converging on suppression of the mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) 74, 75, a molecule that antagonizes autophagy induction 76, 77.
8. Notably, fortified foods have their benefits over supplementation, such as that they can merge into existing diets effortlessly and do not require humans to buy separate pills, a strategy distinct from simply administering spermidine supplements to volunteers.
