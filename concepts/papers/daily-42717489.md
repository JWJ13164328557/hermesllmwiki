# The Jumonji C domain-containing proteins GmJMJ19 and GmJMJ20 link florigen signaling with epigenetic regulation of photoperiodic flowering and post-flowering plant height in soybean.

## 摘要
Soybean (Glycine max) is a photoperiod-sensitive legume whose latitudinal adaptation depends on precise control of flowering time and plant height. Histone demethylases of the JUMONJI C domain-containing (JMJ) protein family have been implicated in these processes across plant species, but their specific roles in soybean remain largely unexplored. Here, we identified soybean GmJMJ19 and GmJMJ20, two closely related JMJD5/KDM8 orthologs, as master epigenetic regulators that coordinately control both photoperiodic flowering and post-flowering plant height. Both genes exhibit intrinsic, rhythmic expression that peaks at zeitgeber time 12, and their encoded proteins physically interact with the florigen proteins FT2a and FT5a. Loss-of-function mutants display delayed flowering under long days (LDs) and increased plant height under both LDs and short days (SDs), and overexpression phenocopies the mutant flowering phenotype, revealing a critical dosage requirement for proper function. Mechanistically, GmJMJ19 and GmJMJ20 are recruited by the FT (FLOWERING LOCUS T)/FD (FLOWERING LOCUS D) transcriptional complex to directly activate the expression of AP1a and AP1c through chromatin modulation. Population genomic analyses revealed that the two proteins show distinct selection signatures: GmJMJ19 has undergone sustained directional selection during cultivation, whereas GmJMJ20 experienced an early domestication sweep, followed by little subsequent change. Haplotype analysis revealed coordinated latitudinal clines, with the JMJ19H1/JMJ20H1 combination predominating at high latitudes, promoting early flowering and limiting height, and the JMJ19H2/JMJ20H2 and wild JMJ19H3/JMJ20H3 alleles predominating at low latitudes, conferring later flowering and increased height. Our findings establish GmJMJ19 and GmJMJ20 as central chromatin regulators that link florigen signaling to the expression of downstream target genes, providing valuable allelic resources for breeding of regionally adapted soybean varieties across a wide range of latitudes.
