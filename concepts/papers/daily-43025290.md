# Pre-harvest putrescine application modulates berry composition in wine grapes under drought stress: Regulating sugar-acid balance, phenolic profiles, and volatile compounds.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Food chemistry (2026)
- **PMID**: [43025290](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025290/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.foodchem.2026.151845](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.foodchem.2026.151845)
- **第一作者**: Hao Chen
- **作者**: Hao Chen, Jiaqi Zhao, Lei Lai, Shiqiu Liu, Yufeng Gao, Yantao Feng, Zhenwen Guan, Muyang Li

## 摘要
Global climate change has exacerbated drought conditions in various wine-producing regions, and the resulting imbalance in sugar-acid balance poses serious challenges to viticulture. Putrescine is involved in various metabolic regulatory processes and can delay senescence, thereby supporting plant adaptation to abiotic stress. However, the role of putrescine in modulating grape berry quality under drought conditions remains unclear. This study investigated the impact of foliar application of putrescine on Cabernet Sauvignon grapes and wine under drought conditions over a two-year period. Putrescine mitigated drought stress in grapevines through improved water-use efficiency. Putrescine reduced the reducing sugar content in mature grapes and the alcohol content in wines, while increasing the malic acid content in the berries. Linear regression indicated that putrescine enhanced the antioxidant activity of berries and wine, which was linked to its effect on increasing total phenol and anthocyanin contents. In the CIELAB color space, putrescine enhanced the redness (a*) of grape skins and wine, and improved the lightness (L*) of the wine, as reflected by the rise in individual anthocyanin concentrations. Malvidin-3-O-glucoside was identified by PCA and PLS-DA analyses as a common differential metabolite in berries and wines across different treatments. Furthermore, analysis of volatile compounds and sensory aroma assessments confirmed that putrescine improved wine quality under drought conditions.
