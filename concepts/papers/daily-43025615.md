# The AIB/JAM1 transcription factor participates in glucose responses during early development in Arabidopsis thaliana.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43025615](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43025615/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113593](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113593)
- **第一作者**: María-Isabel Cruz-López
- **作者**: María-Isabel Cruz-López, Gustavo Rodríguez-Alonso, Kenny-Alejandra Agreda-Laguna, Patricia León, Elizabeth Cordoba

## 摘要
Plants have developed the ability to sense sugar levels and elicit appropriate responses through diverse signaling pathways to tightly coordinate growth and development to maintain sugar energy homeostasis. To identify components of sugar signaling pathways, particularly those mediated by glucose (Glc), we characterized the role of the AIB/JAM1 (ABA INDUCIBLE BHLH TYPE TRANSCRIPTION FACTOR/JASMONATE-ASSOCIATED MYC2-LIKE1) transcription factor in regulating sugar responses. The jam1-1 and jam1-2 allelic mutants displayed a Glc-hypersensitive phenotype and exhibited altered expression of the Glc-repressed STP1 gene, supporting a function for AIB/JAM1 in sugar signaling. Transcriptomic analysis of seedlings grown on Glc supplemented media showed differential accumulation of 3,502 transcripts in the jam1-2 mutant compared to wild-type plants. Down-regulated genes were enriched in processes related to the organization and function of chloroplasts, while up-regulated genes were associated with cellular respiration. This transcriptomic reprogramming correlated with the Glc-hypersensitivity phenotype displayed by jam1-2 plants and suggests alterations in carbon metabolism and sugar signaling. Altogether, our results suggest that AIB/JAM1 is a component of the sugar signaling network, acting as an effector that modulates transcriptional responses to Glc and may contribute to the transition from heterotrophy to autotrophy during early seedling establishment.
