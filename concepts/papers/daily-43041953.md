# Smaller and denser stomatal trend: a strategy for growth and abiotic stress resistance in wheat.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant phenomics (Washington, D.C.) (2026)
- **PMID**: [43041953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43041953/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plaphe.2026.100299](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphe.2026.100299)
- **第一作者**: Zhuangzhuang Sun
- **作者**: Zhuangzhuang Sun, Haijiang Min, K H Cheng, Hengtong Wang, Yunlin Song, Qing Li, Xiao Wang, Qin Zhou

## 摘要
Variations in stomatal morphology (density and size) positively influence wheat growth and stress resistance. However, due to the limitations of traditional stomatal phenotyping techniques, systematic field evidence characterizing variation in stomatal size and density across nitrogen treatments, developmental stages, and breeding eras remains limited. This study systematically investigated the genetic variability and plasticity in stomatal traits in 210 wheat varieties, developed and introduced in China, under different nitrogen treatments and growth stages by integrating high-throughput stomatal phenotyping technology and genome-wide association study (GWAS). The results indicated that the adaxial leaf surface generally has a higher stomatal density and a smaller individual stomatal area than on the abaxial surface. Under nitrogen deficiency stress, wheat stomata consistently increased in density and decreased in individual area on both adaxial and abaxial leaf surfaces. During growth, the stomatal density increased gradually while the stomatal size decreased, presenting remarkable developmental plasticity. The analysis of dynamic stomatal phenotypes within the observed leaf area indicated that smaller stomata closed faster than larger ones, providing an advantage in water conservation. Across breeding eras, the cultivar panel also showed a shift toward smaller and denser stomata, which paralleled a trend of increasing grain yield under N240. GWAS identified 53 candidate loci associated with adaxial stomatal traits and 12 with abaxial ones; functional annotations of the candidate genes revealed their involvement in membrane components, transmembrane transport, transcriptional regulation, and other biological processes. This study applied high-throughput phenotyping technology to characterize the trend toward smaller and denser stomata in wheat and to explore its potential relevance to abiotic stress responses and yield formation, highlighting the utility of this approach and providing a theoretical and technical basis for further research and future genetic improvement of wheat.
