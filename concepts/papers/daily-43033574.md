# Characterization, Epidemiology, and Host Range of Alternaria solani f. sp. lactucae, the Causal Pathogen of Alternaria leaf spot of lettuce.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033574](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033574/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-03-26-0493-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-03-26-0493-RE)
- **第一作者**: Steven T Koike
- **作者**: Steven T Koike, Maria Ferrer Ruiz, Olivia Gutierrez, Hanane Stanghellini, Mahnaz Rashidi, Jennifer Guerrero, David Miller, Lilly D Cosio

## 摘要
An undescribed leaf spot disease was first seen on lettuce grown in coastal California in 2023. Caused by a species of Alternaria, the symptoms were round to irregular, gray green to brown leaf spots having concentric rings; these symptoms were distinct from those of other known foliar diseases of lettuce in the state. Inoculation experiments showed that 15 different cultivars of iceberg, romaine, green leaf, red leaf, and butterhead lettuce types were susceptible. When 22 non-lettuce crops in seven plant families were inoculated, this Alternaria sp. caused disease on only endive, potato, and tomato. Conidial morphology of the lettuce pathogen resembled that of the potato early blight pathogen, A. solani. When an A. solani isolate from potato was inoculated onto potato and lettuce, the potato developed severe early blight, but the lettuce remained symptomless. In 8- to 11-month-old Petri plate cultures, the lettuce Alternaria sp. isolates formed microsclerotia; when inoculated onto lettuce, the microsclerotia caused the leaf spot disease. Whole-genome sequencing and calculation of average nucleotide identity indicated that the lettuce Alternaria sp. and A. solani isolates clustered together, although the lettuce isolates were distinct from the potato pathogen and represented a new forma specialis, A. solani f. sp. lactucae, within the A. solani species complex. We present a near-complete telomere-to-telomere genome assembly for this pathogen. Secretome and effector differences between A. solani f. sp. lactucae and potato A. solani isolates provide the foundation for future molecular studies into differential host pathogenicity.
