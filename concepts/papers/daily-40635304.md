# The GoSPGF-GoPGF-GhWRKYs transcriptional cascade positively regulates pigment gland formation in the stem of upland cotton.

## 摘要
The pigment gland in cotton (Gossypium hirsutum) is a specialized structure that serves as a reservoir for gossypol and its derivatives. The morphogenesis of the pigment gland is complex and involves sophisticated but poorly understood interactions among genes that regulate cell differentiation and development. In this study, we identified a distinct gland/gossypol cluster which includes a key regulator of cotton pigment gland formation, GoPGF, and elucidated the developmental trajectory of gland cells through spatial transcriptome and single-cell RNA integrative analysis. Focusing on GoPGF, we identified a GRAS transcriptional factor GoSPGF that acts upstream of GoPGF through directly binding to the 'AGAC' motif of its promoter. This leads to production of GoPGF that in turn binds to G-box and MYC elements within the promoters of WRKY genes thereby activating their expression. Downregulation of WRKY genes by VIGS in cotton led to dramatic reductions in the number of glands in stems and petioles. Overall, our results delineate a core transcriptional pathway, the GoSPGF-GoPGF-GhWRKYs cascade, that confers pigment gland formation in the stem of cotton. These findings offer new insight into the mechanisms and regulatory pathways underlying cotton gland formation and highlight potential candidates for breeding low-gossypol (glandless) cotton.

### 核心发现
1. In summary, these findings suggest that the normal GoPGF protein directly binds to the promoters of multiple WRKY genes, thereby activating their transcription.
2. Collectively, these findings suggest that the downregulation of WRKY genes decreases the number of glands in stems and petioles without affecting gland formation in leaves.
3. Notably, we found several critical genes for gossypol biosynthesis, such as 2‐ODD‐1, CDNC, CYP71BE79, HMGR, CYP706B1, CYP82D113, FPS and IPPI, exhibit a continuous increase in expression along the differentiation trajectory (Huang et al., ) (Figure ), with the highest expression level in the apoptotic cells.
4. This leads to production of GoPGF that in turn binds to G‐box and MYC elements within the promoters of WRKY genes thereby activating their expression.
5. Yeast cells harbouring normal GoSPGF with bait‐P1 and bait‐P3 grew robustly on SD/‐Ura/‐Leu medium supplemented with 60 ng/mL AbA (Figure ), indicating that the P1 and P3 fragments of the GoPGF promoter possess potential target sites for GoSPGF.
6. These results suggest that GoSPGF normally typically acts as an activator of GoPGF, whereas the mutated version lacking the GRAS domain acts as a repressor.
7. Together, these results conclusively demonstrate that GoSPGF can directly and specifically bind to the ‘AGAC’ motif in the GoPGF promoter.
8. Additionally, GO analysis of the candidate target genes revealed enrichment in the term ‘membrane part’, with associated genes typically expressed in apoptotic cells (Liu et al., ) (Figure ).
