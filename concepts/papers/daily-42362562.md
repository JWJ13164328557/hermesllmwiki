## 摘要
Isoflavones, characterized by their distinctive C6-C3-C6 skeleton, represent an important class of plant secondary metabolites with considerable pharmacological and physiological relevance. Although their biosynthetic pathways have been extensively elucidated in Fabaceae species, the molecular mechanisms governing isoflavone production in other plant families remain largely unexplored. Herein, we uncovered an isoflavone biosynthetic pathway in Iridaceae plants. Notably, it involves an uncharacterized CYP736D family isoflavone synthase, a non-CYP450 (iso)flavone 6-hydroxylase, two cation-dependent type I O-methyltransferases, and a newly identified CYP75F isoflavone 3'-hydroxylase. Evolutionary analyses revealed that Iridaceae have retained an ancient and conserved isoflavone biosynthetic pathway, potentially representing a prototypical route in land plants. In contrast, soybean utilizes a specialized pathway unique to Fabaceae. Our findings not only resolve a long-standing gap in our understanding of isoflavone biosynthesis in Iridaceae plants, but also provide insights into the independent evolutionary trajectory of isoflavone biosynthetic mechanisms in Iridaceae and Fabaceae.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, these findings suggest that Iridaceae employs an isoflavone biosynthetic route that is distinct from the well-characterized Fabaceae pathway or recently reported wheat pathway, and that this route involves a CYP93C-family IFS enzyme closely related to canonical C4Hs.
2. These results confirm that the five enzymes are sufficient to catalyze the complete cascade from naringenin to iristectorigenin A, achieving heterologous biosynthesis of this isoflavone in N.
3. Furthermore, we found genes encoding upstream enzymes producing the isoflavone precursor, i.e., naringenin, are broadly conserved across land plants, whereas those governing isoflavone skeleton formation and downstream modification show lineage-specific divergence.
4. Despite an exhaustive search of genome and transcriptome datasets, we found no homologs of CYP93C or CYP71F, the gene families previously reported to encode IFS in Fabaceae and Poaceae, respectively,,.
5. In this study, however, we found that IFSs from three examined Iridaceae species all belong to a previously uncharacterized P450 family: CYP736D (Supplementary Fig.
6. Although high-resolution structural data or theoretical calculations are still needed to definitively resolve the catalytic trajectory of CYP736D, our results suggest that the catalytic mechanisms of CYP736D differ from CYP93C, at least in terms of key catalytic residues.
7. Collectively, these findings imply that the origin of isoflavone biosynthesis may be much older than previously thought, and could have originated during the transition of plants from aquatic to terrestrial habitats.
