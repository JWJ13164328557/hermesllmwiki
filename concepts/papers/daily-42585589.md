# Two clones, two paths: divergent intrinsic xylem traits and hydraulic responses to prolonged drought in poplar.

## 摘要
Climate change is increasing the frequency and intensity of droughts, challenging the growth, hydraulic function and survival of woody plants. Poplar is a fast-growing species widely cultivated for biomass and cellulose production, but it is also highly sensitive to water deficit, with substantial variation among species and clones.

This study compared the drought responses of two Populus × canadensis clones, 'Tucano' and 'San Martino', under gradually imposed, prolonged water stress. Physiological traits and intrinsic wood anatomical characteristics revealed different strategies between clones for coping with drought.

Compared with the 'San Martino' clone, the 'Tucano' clone exhibited faster stomatal closure under moderate stress and a greater resistance to embolism formation. The resistance was further supported by specific intrinsic wood anatomical traits, including higher vessel density, increased vessel clustering, greater theoretical hydraulic conductivity, thicker cell walls and higher wood density. Conversely, the delayed stomatal closure, the less negative P50 value and the distinct xylem anatomical structure observed in 'San Martino' suggest a significantly higher vulnerability to prolonged water deficit.

'Tucano' adopts an effective drought-avoidance strategy, integrating stomatal regulation, intrinsic wood anatomy and carbon allocation to balance hydraulic safety with growth and wood quality. The study highlights specific traits as key determinants of drought resistance and supports the selection of a specific clone for poplar cultivation in future climate scenarios characterized by water scarcity.

### 核心发现
1. Although avoidance strategies are often associated with reduced productivity, our results show that in gradual and prolonged drought conditions, ‘Tucano’ successfully reconciles hydraulic safety with growth and wood quality.
2. The metabolic cost of hydraulic safety is compounded further by the essential role of non-structural carbohydrates (NSCs), because they play a central role in maintaining plant hydraulic function during drought.
3. Therefore, identifying whether a given clone is better adapted to drought is particularly relevant in areas where poplar cultivation plays a key role in the local economy and is expected to face increasingly frequent and prolonged drought events in the near future ( ).
4. Therefore, the resistance of the tree to xylem embolism is crucial for preventing hydraulic failure.
5. Our results deepen our understanding of poplar responses to prolonged drought under climate change and provide new insights that might support more effective management and the selection of more drought-resilient clones for future plantation systems.
