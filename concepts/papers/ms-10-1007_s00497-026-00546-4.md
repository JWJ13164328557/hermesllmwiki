### 核心发现
1. Taken together, these results suggest that impaired timely degeneration of tapetal cells lead to defective pollen development in Sbtdr-1 anthers.
2. Here, we report the morphological characterization of anther development in wild-type sorghum BTx623 and the Sorghum bicolor tapetal degeneration retardation (Sbtdr) mutant, as well as identification of the causal gene SbTDR.
3. In this study, we report the isolation and characterization of the Sbtdr male sterile mutant in sorghum, whose causal gene is orthologous to the rice TDR gene.
4. We found two mutant lines designated ARS-67 and ARS-84 that carried point mutations in the sorghum orthologue of the rice TDR gene (gene ID: Sorbi_3004G017500).
5. UDT1 activates the TIP2 transcription, while the TIP2-TDR het­ erodimer directly regulates expression of EAT1 and TDR via a feed-forward regulatory mechanism (Fu et al.
6. Addi­ tionally, EAT1 directly activates expression of two aspartic protease genes AP25 and AP37, which might induce tapetal programmed cell death (Ko et al.
7. Our findings demonstrate that the SbTDR gene plays a key role in pollen development by controlling tapetal degeneration in sorghum.
8. Collectively, these results indicate that Sbtdr-1 is a male sterile mutant.
