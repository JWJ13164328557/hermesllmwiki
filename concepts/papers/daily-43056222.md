# A glycosyltransferase pair UGT74F1 and UGT74F2 enhances antiviral resistance independent of phytohormone and RNA silencing pathways.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43056222](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43056222/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag868](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag868)
- **第一作者**: Wenqi Mao
- **作者**: Wenqi Mao, Jiajia Lin, Linxi Li, Yanjun Zhang, Xinxin Fang, Ziying Guo, Jinjin Xu, Jianping Chen

## 摘要
Glycosylation by uridine diphosphate-dependent glycosyltransferases (UGTs) regulates metabolite activity and plant immunity, but their roles in plant virus infection remain elusive. Here, we report that Arabidopsis UGT74F1 and UGT74F2, established salicylic acid (SA) glycosyltransferases, function as potent inhibitors of turnip mosaic virus (TuMV). Overexpression of either enzyme attenuates TuMV local and systemic infection, while loss of UGT74F2 enhances susceptibility. AtUGT74F1 is recruited to viral replication complexes and suppresses viral replication. Strikingly, this antiviral activity is independent of SA and jasmonic acid signaling, as the resistance is fully retained in sid2-4, coi1-23, and NahG backgrounds. Furthermore, the catalytically inactive mutant that cannot produce SA glucosides retains wild-type antiviral activity, definitively uncoupling enzymatic and immune functions. The resistance mechanism also likely operates independently of RNA silencing, as UGT74F1 suppresses TuMV equally in DCL2/4i and rdr6i plants. Our findings reveal a previously unrecognized, hormone- and RNAi-independent antiviral mechanism mediated by metabolic enzymes, expanding UGT functional diversity beyond phytohormone regulation and providing genetic resources for engineering virus-resistant crops.
