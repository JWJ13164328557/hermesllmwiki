# An 18 bp deletion inside CmaDUF1997 is associated with pale yellow flower color in Cucurbita maxima.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: TAG. Theoretical and applied genetics. Theoretische und angewandte Genetik (2026)
- **PMID**: [43097169](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43097169/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00122-026-05449-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00122-026-05449-6)
- **第一作者**: Yuetong Hou
- **作者**: Yuetong Hou, Qianrong Hao, Zhen Zhang, Ruoying Wang, Chaojie Wang, Ming Xu, Shuping Qu

## 摘要
Flower color is a crucial trait in Cucurbit plants, contributing to floral organ protection and energy balance. In this study, two Cucurbita maxima inbred lines '174' (yellow flower) and '164' (pale yellow flower) were used as plant materials. HPLC analysis showed significantly higher petal carotenoid content in '174' than in '164' on the day of flowering. Genetic analysis indicated that flower color was controlled by a single dominant locus CmaFC (Flower Color). BSA-seq initially mapped the CmaFC locus to a 2.08 Mb interval on chromosome 14, which was subsequently narrowed to 75.7 kb containing 19 genes using SNP and InDel markers in 1,270 F₂ and 384 BC₁P₂ individuals. Among them, CmaCh14G005810 (CmaDUF1997), encoding a Domain of Unknown Function 1997 protein, was identified as a candidate gene. The expression level of CmaDUF1997 was significantly higher in yellow flower than in pale yellow flower. Sequence analysis revealed that an 18 bp in-frame deletion in the coding sequence of CmaDUF1997 in '164' results in the loss of six amino acids. Further analysis of allelic diversity in germplasm accessions revealed pale yellow color to be associated with the 18 bp deletion of CmaDUF1997. Transcriptomic analysis revealed downregulation of carotenoid biosynthesis genes in '164', which may be associated with reduced carotenoid accumulation and the pale yellow flower phenotype. These findings lay the foundation for further investigation into the genetic mechanisms underlying flower color variation and the development of breeding strategies for flower coloration in C. maxima.
