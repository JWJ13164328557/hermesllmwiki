# Plant essential oils as a control strategy against Ascosphaera apis: mechanisms, efficacy, and practical applications in apiculture.

- **PMID**: [43034048](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43034048/)

- **作者**: Štěpán Ryba, Petr Mráz

## 摘要
Chalkbrood, caused by Ascosphaera apis (Maassen ex Claussen) Olive & Spiltoir (Ascomycota: Onygenales: Ascosphaeraceae), is a globally distributed fungal brood disease of the western honey bee, Apis mellifera L. (Hymenoptera: Apidae). Despite chronic economic losses, no chemotherapeutic agent is currently registered for chalkbrood control in major beekeeping jurisdictions, creating a clear treatment gap relative to varroosis and bacterial brood diseases. Plant essential oils (EOs) and related botanical products have been repeatedly proposed as candidate antifungal agents, but the strength and translational maturity of the supporting evidence remain inconsistent. This review synthesizes current data on the chemistry, antifungal mechanisms, efficacy, bee safety, formulation, residue behavior, and regulatory status of EOs and related plant-derived compounds tested against A. apis. In vitro evidence consistently identifies thymol, carvacrol, cinnamaldehyde, eugenol, and several whole oils from Lamiaceae, Myrtaceae, and Lauraceae as the most potent candidates, acting through membrane disruption, ergosterol interference, cell-wall and spore-germination inhibition, oxidative stress, and impaired energy metabolism. However, colony-level evidence is sparse and methodologically heterogeneous, and bee safety, formulation, and residue behavior are still under-reported. We propose a minimum reporting framework covering pathogen and inoculum, chemistry, in vitro and in vivo endpoints, toxicology, formulation, and field-trial design, and place EOs within an integrated pest and disease management strategy combining hygienic-behavior selection, nutrition, sanitation, and emerging diagnostics. EOs currently support cautious development rather than immediate adoption as stand-alone chalkbrood treatments.
