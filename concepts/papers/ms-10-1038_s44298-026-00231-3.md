### 核心发现
1. We found a smaller number of viral sequences that could be classiﬁed in these plants, with unclassiﬁed sequences being identiﬁed in all samples.
2. We found that some virus taxa showed strong association with certain plant families.
3. Similarly to the study of Bernardo et al.12, we found viruses classiﬁed in a larger number of viral taxa in the area with a lower degree of human interference(PESB) comparedto the area with a higher degree(UFV-CRP).
4. Despite the fact that virus identiﬁcation was achieved primarily at the order level, our results support the conclusion that plant-associated viral diversity is greater in regions with lower levels of human disturbance, while viral prevalence remains broadly similar across landscapes.
5. Collectively, both direct and indirect human- mediated ecological disturbances tend to favor the emergence of viruses at the interface between managed and natural ecosystems6–8.
6. These results suggest that viral communities associated with the PESB core are more diverse and hetero- geneous (Table S6).
7. Collectively, these works identiﬁed a vast number of novel plant RNA viruses in different environ- ments, and suggested that the true diversity of these agents remains largely untapped.
8. Interestingly, ﬁve orders were associated exclusively with samples collected atPESB:Algavirales,Geplafuvirales,Goujianvirales, Jingchuvirales, and Serpentovirales.
