# Physiological, metabolic and agronomic responses of potato plants to the application type and dosage of a flesh-coloured potato extract under Rhizoctonia solani inoculation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of the science of food and agriculture (2026)
- **PMID**: [43035796](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43035796/)
- **DOI**: [10.1002/jsfa.71242](https://doi.org/10.1002/jsfa.71242)
- **第一作者**: Francisca Gómez
- **作者**: Francisca Gómez, Sebastián Valencia, Felipe González, Christian Santander, Cledir Santos, Boris Contreras, Antonieta Ruiz

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: Potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) is a major global food crop whose productivity is affected by fungal pathogens such as Rhizoctonia solani. Conventional control relies on synthetic fungicides, which present limitations including pathogen resistance and adverse physiological effects. Natural alternatives, particularly phenolic-rich extracts, have emerged as a sustainable strategy. Flesh-coloured potato (FCP), rich in anthocyanins and hydroxycinnamic acids, represents a potential source of bioactive compounds.

RESULTS: This study evaluated the effects of FCP extract (20 and 675 mg L-1 total phenolics) on potato plants inoculated with R. solani, considering different application methods (immersion, soil application and spraying) and commercial fungicides as control. Photosynthetic traits, antioxidant responses, organic acid exudation and agronomic yield were assessed. Compared with inoculated controls and fungicide treatments, FCP treatments were associated with higher photosynthetic performance. Spraying at low dose increased photosynthetic rate and stomatal conductance, whereas soil application at high dose was associated with higher water use efficiency and tuber yield. Antioxidant enzyme activity and phenolic accumulation increased under FCP treatments, while fungicide treatments showed limited physiological responses. FCP also reduced oxalic acid exudation. Principal component analysis (PCA) indicated a dose and method dependent pattern in plant responses.

CONCLUSION: Overall, FCP extract was associated with dose and method dependent physiological, metabolic and agronomic responses in potato plants under R. solani inoculation, supporting its potential as an alternative approach for crop management. © 2026 Society of Chemical Industry.
