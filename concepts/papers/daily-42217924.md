# Synergistic effects of zinc and iron to reduce cadmium uptake in wheat: Insights into soil immobilization and physiological regulatory mechanisms.

## 摘要
Soil heavy metal pollution tends to cause excessive accumulation of cadmium (Cd) in wheat grains, posing serious threats to food safety and human health. Therefore, it is urgent to develop strategies for effective remediation of Cd-contaminated soils. Iron (Fe) and zinc (Zn) can antagonize Cd uptake for safer agriculture production. However, the regulatory mechanisms by which Zn and Fe affect Cd uptake remain elusive. This study systematically investigated the effects of ZnSO4 and FeSO4 on Cd accumulation in wheat plants via pot and hydroponic experiments. The results indicated that appropriate co-application of ZnSO4 and FeSO4 significantly reduced Cd accumulation in wheat through some synergistic mechanisms involving soil immobilization, rhizosphere adsorption, and competitive uptake for Cd. Notably, the co-application of ZnSO4 and FeSO4 reduced soil Cd availability, promoted the formation of poorly crystalline Fe(III) oxides, which facilitated the Cd immobilization on wheat rhizosphere, and increased available Zn and Fe contents, thereby enhancing the antagonistic interactions between Cd2+ and Fe2+/Zn2+ in the root. Specifically, the co-application of 0.1 % FeSO4 and 0.5 % ZnSO4 reduced soil available Cd content by 26.9 %, and decreased the Cd content in wheat roots, shoots, and leaves by 41.8 %, 72.3 %, and 63.9 % in the pot experiment, respectively. More importantly, the Cd content in wheat grains was reduced from 0.24 mg/kg to 0.07 mg/kg, which meets China's food safety standard (0.1 mg/kg). This study provides theoretical guidance for micronutrient-based strategies to ensure safe and high-quality production of wheat in Cd-contaminated weakly alkaline soils.
