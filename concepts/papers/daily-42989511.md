# Mechanism and regulation of Arabidopsis polyamine acetylation by NATA1 and NATA2.

## 摘要
Polyamines are crucial for sustaining plant growth and stress resilience. Spermine/spermidine N1-acetyl transferases (SSATs) regulate polyamine abundance by catalyzing their N-acetylation, thereby reducing the pool of polyamines and producing other bioactive components. Arabidopsis encodes two SSAT paralogues, N-acetyltransferase activity (NATA) 1 and 2, whose physiological differences and regulatory mechanisms remain poorly understood. Here, we report eight crystal structures of NATA1 and NATA2, providing a detailed view of substrate recognition and conformational dynamics during catalysis. Biophysical analyses reveal that NATA2 is markedly more thermostable than NATA1, suggesting a compensatory role for NATA2 under heat stress. Combining structural and biochemical approaches, we further show that stress-induced acidic metabolites, including glutamate and γ-aminobutyric acid, competitively occupy the NATA active site and collectively attenuate polyamine acetylation. Based on these findings, we propose an autoregulatory mechanism in which stress-associated metabolites jointly inhibit SSAT activity to preserve polyamine pools under adverse conditions, while maintaining basal acetylation capacity. This work clarifies SSAT activity and regulation in Arabidopsis and suggests a previously unrecognized link between stress metabolism and polyamine homeostasis in plants.
