Single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, and spatial metabolomics techniques have been combined to analyze plant development. ### Abstract
Plants contain a large number of cell types and exhibit complex regulatory mechanisms. Studies at the single-cell level have gradually become more common in plant science. Single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, and spatial metabolomics techniques have been combined to analyze plant development. These techniques have been used to study the transcriptomes and metabolomes of plant tissues at the single-cell level, enabling the systematic investigation of gene expression and metabolism in specific tissues and cell types during defined developmental stages. In this review, we present an overview of significant breakthroughs in spatial multi-omics in plants, and we discuss how these approaches may soon play essential roles in plant research.
Key words: mass spectrometry imaging, plants, single-cell transcriptomics, spatial transcriptomics, spatial metabolomics
### Introduction
Differentiation during plant cell development produces heterogeneity among cells and cell types, which then associate with each other during development. Expression levels of specific genes that determine the course of cell development differ significantly among cell developmental stages ( Liu et al., 2022b ), making it important to study this process at the single-cell level.
Many recent studies have therefore made use of single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) ( Butler et al., 2018 ; Wu et al., 2019 ; Moncada et al., 2020 ), in which the transcriptomes of isolated single cells are amplified and sequenced. The ability to obtain gene expression profiles of single cells is a key advantage of scRNA-seq relative to previous transcriptomic techniques ( Melnekoff and Laganà, 2022 ). Although scRNA-seq enables the study of transcriptomic heterogeneity among cells and the identification of specific cell types, its requirement for tissue dissociation to isolate single cells means that information on the original spatial locations of the cells is lost. This drawback limits the study of cell development in both temporal and spatial dimensions. To address this limitation, a novel sequencing technology (spatial transcriptomics) that can retain both gene expression and location information has been developed ( Crosetto et al., 2015 ).
