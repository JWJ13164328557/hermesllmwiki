### 核心发现
1. In summary, our findings suggest that diminished low-frequency structure- function coupling within the SMN is associated with impaired long-range signaling between frontal motor- initiating regions and parietal state-representing regions, which may hinder the forward model from generating vital outcome predictions.
2. Our findings reveal a significant reduction in low-frequency coupling within the sensorimotor network (SMN) in patients with schizophrenia compared to healthy controls, and notably, this low-frequency coupling is significantly correlated with the severity of delusional symptoms.
3. Our results suggest that disruptions in local, fast diffusion processes within the DMN may contribute to dysfunctions in higher-order brain functions.
4. Second, at the level of signal propagation, we found markedly decreased low- frequency coupling between the DMN and SMN, which was closely associated with hallucination severity.
5. Taken together, these findings demonstrate that frequency-specific SC–FC decoupling may underlie the core symptoms of schizophrenia, providing mechanistic insight and a potential foundation for precision-targeted interventions.
6. Collectively, the findings indicate that within schizophrenia, the SMN—a core network for sensorimotor information processing—exhibits broadly diminished capacity of structural connections to support functional signaling across different diffusion modes.
7. Together, these results suggest that structure-function decoupling in schizophrenia is not a uniform phenomenon but exhibits both frequency- and topology-specific characteristics, likely reflecting frequency-specific ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS disruptions in neural activity arising from hierarchical structural impairments.
8. Finally, this study reveals multiscale coupling abnormalities within the sensorimotor network (SMN) in individuals with schizophrenia.
