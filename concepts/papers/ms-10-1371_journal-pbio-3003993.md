### 核心发现
1. We found that the progression of maturation from the tip of the leaf downward was delayed in jaw-D relative to wild type based on several markers of maturation, in agreement with the role of TCP transcription factors in pro­ moting maturation.
2. Thus, our results suggest wild-type plants coordinate growth across the mediolateral axis leaf blade to main­ tain flatness.
3. We conclude that our live imaging of the early stages of leaf development (3–8 DAS) captures the initial development of organ shape at cellular resolution.
4. We conclude that the entire jaw-D leaf curves, not just the margins, and that curvature is initiated at 6 DAS.
5. We found a striking shift in the distribution of areal growth in jaw-D leaves along the proximal–distal axis, with growth occurring at a more constant rate all the way along the proximal–distal axis.
6. Based on the model, we conclude that different proximal–distal growth patterns can yield similar leaf lengths.
7. If we quantify the overall local spatial and temporal variability by averaging cells throughout the leaf, we found that wild type and jaw-D are largely indistinguishable (Fig 5C and 5D), indicating that the localized pattern, not the global pattern is important in the case of mediolateral growth.
8. We found that there was less large- scale variation in growth rate across the organ in jaw-D than wild type (Fig 5E and 5F).
