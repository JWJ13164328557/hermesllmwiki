# Cadmium/arsenic co-contamination drives delayed succession of root endosphere bacteria and aligns with the metal partitioning stage in maize.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of environmental sciences (China) (2026)
- **PMID**: [43014893](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43014893/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.jes.2026.02.056](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jes.2026.02.056)
- **第一作者**: Guili Hua
- **作者**: Guili Hua, Liping Geng, Dinghao Li, Yidan Zhao, Peipei Gao, Hongxin Sun, Shushen Yang, Quanli Zhao

## 摘要
Root-associated microbiota have been shown to mediate cadmium (Cd) and arsenic (As) transfer from soil to crops, however, their temporal dynamics and linkage to metal accumulation in maize under different Cd-As co-contamination levels remain unclear. A field experiment was conducted with low (LS) or high (HS) Cd-As co-contamination levels to elucidate the dynamics of root-associated bacteria and metal accumulation across maize growth stages. The results revealed that while the rhizosphere community remained stable, the temporal succession of root endosphere bacteria was delayed by one growth stage under high contamination rather than merely showing a reduction in diversity. The peak diversity (Shannon) and richness (Chao) occurred at the tasselling stage (TS) in the LS condition but was delayed until the grain-filling stage (GS) in the HS condition. This delay was synchronized with a shift in metal partitioning that Cd was highly retained in roots at the TS, with the translocation factor (TF) decreasing by 33.73 %-238.61 % (HS) and 31.34 %-191.20 % (LS) compared with the other stages, while As following a similar pattern at the GS, with the TF decreasing by 36.36 %-272.24 % (HS) and 56.25 %-146.44 % (LS). This metal-specific patterning was associated with the stage-delayed enrichment of Actinobacteria. Specifically, enrichment of Streptomyces, Agromyces, and Microbacterium at the TS was linked to the inhibition of Cd translocation, whereas enrichment of Lechevalieria and Mycobacterium at the GS was corresponded to the restriction of As translocation. These findings provided a stage‑synchronized framework for microbiome‑based remediation in Cd-As co‑contaminated soils.
