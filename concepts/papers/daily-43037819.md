# Microbial succession and functional gene repertoires in Brassica rapa fermentation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: World journal of microbiology & biotechnology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43037819](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43037819/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s11274-026-05409-8](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11274-026-05409-8)
- **第一作者**: Anna Rossi
- **作者**: Anna Rossi, Michela Maifreni, Nadia Innocente, Elisabetta Trossolo, Fabio Marroni, Marilena Marino

## 摘要
Traditional fermented vegetables are complex microbial ecosystems whose dynamics are shaped by processing conditions, yet many regional European fermentations remain poorly characterized. This study combined shotgun metagenomics, culture-dependent analyses, and chemical measurements to characterize microbial succession, fermentation dynamics, and metagenomic functional potential during brovada fermentation and to compare one semi-industrial and one artisanal production system over 30 days. The two systems showed distinct trajectories. In the semi-industrial system, lactic acid bacteria (LAB) increased to 8.30 ± 0.50 log CFU/g by day 30, together with rapid sugar depletion, lactic acid accumulation, and a final pH of 3.94 ± 0.09. In the artisanal system, LAB peaked at 5.63 ± 0.38 log CFU/g at day 9 and declined to 3.92 ± 0.26 log CFU/g, while the final pH remained higher (4.51 ± 0.08); yeasts became more abundant than LAB at the end of fermentation. Metagenomic profiling showed a faster transition from Proteobacteria- to Firmicutes/LAB-dominated communities in the semi-industrial system, whereas the artisanal system retained greater microbial diversity and a longer persistence of Pseudomonas, Acinetobacter, and other environmental taxa. Functional profiling identified genes associated with bile salt hydrolysis, adhesion, exopolysaccharide production, gamma-aminobutyric acid (GABA) synthesis, plantaricin production, myrosinase-like activity, nitrate/nitrite metabolism, and amino acid and folate biosynthesis. Overall, the study shows that brovada fermentation can follow markedly different microbial and metabolic trajectories under contrasting production conditions, with corresponding differences in functional gene repertoires. These findings highlight the close relationship between processing conditions, microbial succession, and the functional potential of traditional vegetable fermentations.
