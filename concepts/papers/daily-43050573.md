# Impact of catalytic and non-catalytic domains of GUX enzymes on xylan glucuronidation in Arabidopsis.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43050573](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050573/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag861](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag861)
- **第一作者**: Katharine Davis
- **作者**: Katharine Davis, Jan J Lyczakowski, Theodora Tryfona, Joel Wurman-Rodrich, Alberto Echevarría-Poza, Paul Dupree

## 摘要
Xylan is one of the most abundant secondary cell wall polysaccharides in Angiosperms. The xylan backbone is decorated with glucuronic acid (GlcA) by Glucuronic acid substitution of Xylan (GUX) enzymes. While GUX1 adds evenly spaced GlcA residues to long stretches of the xylan backbone, GUX2 is responsible for shorter regions of clustered GlcA. While it is known that GUX1 and GUX2 have a role in creating discrete domains of xylan that are decorated differently, how these domains arise in vivo is unknown. Through investigating the activity of chimeric GUX enzymes expressed in gux1 gux2 Arabidopsis, we have revealed that both the non-catalytic N-terminal and catalytic C-terminal domains of GUX enzymes have an influence on protein activity in vivo. We also observed differences in activity for the chimeric GUX enzymes in vitro when compared to in vivo, suggesting that GUX activity is impacted by the environment within the Golgi. We present a hypothesis in which patterning by GUX1 and GUX2 enzymes is influenced both by the specificity of their catalytic C terminal domain and by their position in the "production line" of xylan backbone synthesis and decoration within the plant Golgi, which could be affected by the amino acid sequence of the non-catalytic N terminus. As plant cell wall polysaccharides constitute the majority of living biomass, this contribution to our understanding of cell wall biosynthesis has relevance for enabling the future design of tailored biorenewable products.
