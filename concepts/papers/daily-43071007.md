# Dietary and Lifestyle Modifiers of Toxic-Metal Exposure and Selenium-Dependent Defense: A Critical Narrative Review.

## 摘要
Exposure to toxic metals and metalloids arises from contaminated food and water, tobacco, consumer products, environmental pollution, and occupational activities. Although dietary and lifestyle "detoxification" strategies are increasingly promoted, their effects on body burden and health outcomes are often uncertain. This narrative review evaluates diet, nutritional status, smoking, exercise, sweating, food preparation, environmental remediation, and workplace controls in relation to lead, mercury, arsenic, cadmium, chromium, nickel, manganese, aluminum, and thallium, with iron and copper overload used as counterexamples. Across metals, the most effective intervention is generally identification and removal of the exposure source. Nutritional measures are metal-specific: adequate calcium and iron status may reduce lead uptake or mobilization; iron status also influences cadmium and manganese absorption; folate can modify arsenic methylation and lower blood arsenic in some exposed populations; lower-mercury fish selection and arsenic-reducing rice preparation directly reduce dietary exposure; and smoking cessation prevents continued cadmium accumulation. Metals can be detected in sweat, but current evidence does not show that exercise or sauna meaningfully reduce tissue stores or improve clinical outcomes. Small human studies of garlic and spirulina have been reported, while animal studies of garlic, curcumin, spirulina, selenium, and related compounds suggest protection against oxidative or inflammatory injury; these findings generally do not establish removal of stored metal. Occupational engineering controls and exposure prevention remain better supported than post-exposure dietary measures. Lifestyle and nutritional interventions are best viewed as adjuncts to source control, biomonitoring, and medically indicated treatment. Selenocysteine-dependent redox enzymes can be affected by certain electrophilic metals, especially mercurials, but this mechanism is not universal.
