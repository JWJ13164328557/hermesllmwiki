### 核心发现
1. In summary, our results show that cGAS functions to maintain heterochromatin organization in the nucleus, independent of its cytoplasmic role as a DNA sensor or its catalytic activity, with implications for geroprotective strategies targeting this pathway.
2. Surprisingly, our findings show that knockdown or KO of cGAS, both in vitro and in vivo, results in aberrant L1 transcription and cytosolic cDNA accumulation.
3. Unexpectedly, we found that cGAS KO mice exhibit an accelerated-aging phenotype, with induction of inflammation in multiple organs.
4. We found 168 upregulated and 86 downregulated genes in cGAS KO compared to WT (adjusted P (Padj) < 0.05, log2(fold-change) (log2(FC)) > 0.5, Fig.
5. Crucially, we found that both STING KO and PYHIN KO mice maintain repression of the L1MdA family (Extended Data Fig.
6. Our data show that cGAS-deficient mice display a premature aging phenotype charac- terized by increased frailty and heightened induction of inflammation.
7. We found that the AIM2 inflammasome may be partially involved in compensating for the loss of cGAS; however, AIM2 knockdown did not fully reduce inflammation in cGAS KO cells, indicating that other DNA or RNA sensing pathways are likely active.
8. Here we show that loss of cGAS leads to changes in the distribution of H3K9me3 and a general opening of heterochromatin and L1 derepres- sion.
