1. 5.6 Comparison with Existing Approaches Our results show that the TTLD model outperforms all baseline models in the task, achieving the highest F1 score (90.42%) in identifying Urdu texts that utilize threatening language.
2. This approach allows models to perform well even with limited labeled data, as they transfer previously learned representations from the pre-training stage to the target task.

For example, in ""( "ﺗﻢ ﻧﮯ ﻣﯿﺮﯼ ﺑﺎﺕ ﻧﮧ ﺳﻨﯽ ﺗﻮ ﻣﯿﮟ ﺗﻤﮩﯿﮟ ﻧﮩﯿﮟ ﭼﮭﻮﮌﻭﮞ ﮔﺎIf you don't listen to me, I will not spare you"), the model assigns the highest attention (0.59) to the explicit threat verb phrase "( ﻧﮩﯿﮟ ﭼﮭﻮﮌﻭﮞ ﮔﺎwill not spare"), with a secondary peak (0.14) on the direct target reference "( ﺗﻤﮩﯿﮟyou").

This qualitative inspection shows that the model consistently assigns higher attention weight to explicit threat verbs, negation and conditional cues, and target references, which is consistent with these tokens carrying the most decisive information for distinguishing threatening from non-threatening intent.
