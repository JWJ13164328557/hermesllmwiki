# Dissecting the Genetic Architecture of White Spot Resistance in Tropical Maize Reveals Novel Loci and Valuable Breeding Resources.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43077564](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077564/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-03-26-0469-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-03-26-0469-RE)
- **第一作者**: Yaqi Bi
- **作者**: Yaqi Bi, Jiao Kong, Fuyan Jiang, Ranjan K Shaw, Jiachen Sun, Jing Wang, Xingming Fan

## 摘要
Maize white spot (MWS) is a severe foliar disease that threatens maize production and grain quality worldwide. Understanding the genetic basis of resistance is crucial for the development of durable resistant varieties. In this study, a large multi-parent population (MPP) comprising 904 recombinant inbred lines (RILs), derived from six tropical/subtropical maize inbred lines crossed with a temperate line, was used to identify genetic variations underlying MWS resistance. Phenotypic data collected over 4 years, combined with 6,390,967 high-quality single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) obtained from whole-genome resequencing, established a comprehensive framework for dissecting the genetic basis of MWS resistance using genome-wide association studies (GWAS) and linkage analysis. Population structure (Q) and kinship (K) were incorporated to control for relatedness. The combined analysis identified five promising candidate genes (Zmhsftf25, Zm00001eb107230, Zmmpk8, Zmmmp86, and Zm00001eb168510), which encode heat-stress transcription factors, remorin proteins, mitogen-activated protein kinases (MAPKs), and leucine-rich repeat (LRR) proteins involved in defense signaling and stress responses. qRT-PCR further confirmed the early induction of these genes following infection. Haplotype analysis identified superior resistance haplotypes derived from tropical parents, which can be directly utilized in marker-assisted selection (MAS) for temperate maize improvement. Additionally, a putative "membrane-to-nucleus" signaling model was proposed to elucidate the molecular mechanism underlying maize immune response to MWS. This study not only identified novel loci associated with MWS resistance but also provided valuable genetic resources and a theoretical basis for overcoming the genetic bottleneck of temperate maize through marker-assisted breeding.
