# Crop Management and Seasonal Soil Health Dynamics Are Associated With Soybean Cyst Nematode Reproduction.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant disease (2026)
- **PMID**: [43031417](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43031417/)
- **DOI**: [10.1094/PDIS-08-26-1661-RE](https://doi.org/10.1094/PDIS-08-26-1661-RE)
- **第一作者**: Ambria Small
- **作者**: Ambria Small, Sandip Mondal, Elizabeth Hawkins, Jim Ippolito, Horacio D Lopez-Nicora

## 摘要
Soybean cyst nematode (SCN) remains the most economically damaging soybean pathogen in North America. While its relationship with soil texture is well documented, less is known about how soil health properties and crop management practices are related to SCN population dynamics under field conditions. This study evaluated SCN reproductive factor (RF) in relation to: 1) soil texture and its interaction with crop management, 2) key soil physicochemical properties and their seasonal changes, and 3) the predictive performance of individual soil properties versus the Comprehensive Assessment of Soil Health (CASH) soil health indices. In 2023 and 2024, soil samples from 185 Ohio fields were collected during spring and fall. Hierarchical modeling and zero-inflated negative binomial regression were used to evaluate relationships among soil texture, management practices, and SCN RF. Soil texture explained little variation in SCN RF, whereas crop rotation with non-host crop was the strongest management factor, with non-host crops associated with a 79% reduction in RF. Seven variables were significantly associated with increased or decreased SCN RF, including seasonal changes in permanganate oxidizable carbon (POXC), pH, and macroaggregates, spring measurements of potassium, pH, and POXC, and the presence of a non-host crop. The final individual soil property model (RF6) explained 2.8 times more variation in SCN RF than the best-performing model based on composite CASH indices. These findings reinforce the importance of crop rotation with non-host crop, and continued SCN monitoring as part of integrated management strategies for soybean production systems, and suggest that seasonal changes in soil properties merit further study as indicators of SCN dynamics.
