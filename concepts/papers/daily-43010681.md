# HiEGP: An environment-informed genomic prediction framework that facilitates soybean genebank germplasm evaluation and climate-smart breeding.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Molecular plant (2026)
- **PMID**: [43010681](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43010681/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.molp.2026.11.003](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.molp.2026.11.003)
- **第一作者**: Yingpu Che
- **作者**: Yingpu Che, Huihui Li, Xiuliang Jin, Chaosen Zhao, Qiang Wang, Hongmei Qiu, Wen Huang, Qingsong Zhao

## 摘要
The biodiversity preserved in gene banks drives crop improvement and strengthens climate resilience for feeding the future. However, only a limited fraction of germplasm has been well characterized, because phenotyping remains low-throughput and costly, especially for region-specific yield-related traits. This gap hinders the utilization of gene bank biodiversity in breeding, particularly for photoperiod and temperature-sensitive crops like soybean. Here, we constructed a multi-environment soybean dataset integrating phenotypes, genotypes, and environmental covariates for 1,421 diverse soybean germplasm accessions. It includes six key breeding traits collected across nine locations (latitudes 28.53°-45.64°), along with bioclimatic and soil data. We developed HiEGP (High-dimensional Environmental Factor Integration for Genomic Prediction), a feature-engineered hierarchical G×E prediction framework that integrates environmental main effects, genotype-specific environmental responses, genomic background, and environmental similarity. HiEGP achieved high predictive performance under both validation settings, reaching average trait-wise pooled correlations of 0.871 for predicting untested genotypes in observed environments and 0.775 for predicting untested genotypes in unobserved environments, outperforming the evaluated methods overall. In the external USDA soybean panel, HiEGP showed stable prediction for 100-seed weight, with environment-wise correlations ranging from 0.70 to 0.81. HiEGP enabled environment-response genome-wide association studies, recovering known maturity loci and identifying 175 candidate regions associated with environmental-response patterns. Phenotypic evidence from ethyl methanesulfonate mutants supported the prioritization of selected candidate genes. HiEGP supported regional adaptation assessment under the observed environmental conditions. This capability offers a scalable strategy to improve the characterization and breeding use of underexplored gene bank germplasm for environment-informed soybean breeding.
