# Determinants of Occupational Injury in Plantation Settings: A Systematic Review.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of agromedicine (2026)
- **PMID**: [43053664](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43053664/)
- **DOI**: [10.1080/1059924X.2026.2764313](https://doi.org/10.1080/1059924X.2026.2764313)
- **第一作者**: Annes Waren
- **作者**: Annes Waren, Deni K Sunjaya, Dewi S Soemarko, Ardini S Raksanagara

## 摘要
OBJECTIVES: Plantation systems represent a structurally distinct segment of agriculture characterized by estate-based monoculture, labor-intensive harvesting, and hierarchical work organization. Despite the documented burden of agricultural injuries, plantation-specific determinants are often combined with evidence from heterogeneous agricultural settings. This review aimed to synthesize evidence on the determinants of occupational injury in plantation monocrop systems, a distinct occupational setting.

METHODS: We conducted a systematic review with a structured narrative synthesis in accordance with PRISMA 2020 guidelines. Four electronic databases (PubMed, Scopus, Cochrane Library, and EBSCOhost) were searched for studies published between 2015 and 2025 examining occupational injury outcomes among plantation workers. Eligible designs included observational epidemiological and registry-based studies analyzing determinants or risk factors. Data were synthesized narratively using a structured domain-based framework that grouped determinants into environmental, task-related, behavioral/individual, and structural domains. Methodological quality was assessed using Joanna Briggs Institute tools.

RESULTS: Nine studies covering multiple commodities and geographic regions met the inclusion criteria. Task-related and behavioral/individual determinants were each reported in six of the nine studies, including sharp tool use, repetitive harvesting, work at height, PPE non-use, risk-taking behaviors, and demographic or socioeconomic factors. Environmental factors were examined in three studies; among two sugarcane record-based analyses, one suggested an increased injury risk at higher Wet Bulb Globe Temperature, whereas the later within-day analysis found no significant association. Structural and socioeconomic determinants, including tenant farming status and socioeconomic conditions, were reported in two studies. Most evidence was derived from cross-sectional designs with self-reported outcomes.

CONCLUSION: Occupational injuries in plantation systems appear to be embedded within concentrated manual task exposure, environmental heat stress, and labor organization structures. Plantation monoculture may concentrate occupational hazards within a wider agricultural sector. Prevention strategies should prioritize engineering controls, heat-adaptive scheduling, strengthened safety governance, and improved injury surveillance to address plantation-specific risk.
