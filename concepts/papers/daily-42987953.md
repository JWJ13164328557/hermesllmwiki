# Positive feedback between cytokinins and sugars controls correlative inhibition in two-branched pea seedlings.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Journal of experimental botany (2026)
- **PMID**: [42987953](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42987953/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/jxb/erag544](https://doi.org/10.1093/jxb/erag544)
- **第一作者**: Andrey A Kotov
- **作者**: Andrey A Kotov, Liudmila M Kotova

## 摘要
The work proposed a correlative inhibition (CI) model based on positive feedback (PF) between sugars (Sug) and cytokinis (CKs) - CK activates Sug uploading to sink organs, and Sug increases CK content and activates CK signalling. This creates an unstable PF system capable of triggering the development of dominant and suppressed shoots on the same plant. The predictions of the Sug-CK model were tested on two-branched pea seedlings. Sug deficiency (removal of part of the cotyledons) and CK deficiency (treatment with an inhibitor of CK synthesis) in accordance with the model increased the occurrence of unequal-branched plants. Surprisingly, auxin was found to be inactive in the regulation of CI. In the apical buds of lateral shoots, a Sug deficiency reduced the CK signal, as measured by the expression of PsRR5 gene, whilst inhibition of CK synthesis reduced Sug accumulation, thereby confirming the presence of PF between CK and Sug. A deficiency of Sug enhanced the effect of a CK deficiency in reducing CK status, explaining the synergistic effect of both deficiencies on CI. A deficiency of strigolactones (SL) in the rms5 and rms1 mutants and SL insensitivity in the rms4 mutant completely abolished the effects of CK and Sug deficiencies on CI. It was demonstrated on the rms5 mutant that a CK deficiency did not affect Sug levels thus explaining the insensitivity to CK-Sug deficiencies in SL mutants. The BRANCHED1 (BRC1) gene is an important regulator of plant branching, and its absence in the brc1 mutant resulted in a lack of response to Sug deficiency with regard to both CI and CK status. It has thus been shown that SL signalling and the BRC1 gene are involved in the Sug-CK interaction, thereby playing a role in the regulation of CI.
