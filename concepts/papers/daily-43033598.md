# Spatial-temporal mechanisms of reactive oxygen species and redox governing plant development.

- **PMID**: [43033598](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033598/)

- **第一作者**: Lior Tal
- **作者**: Lior Tal, Yosef Fichman

## 摘要
Reactive oxygen species (ROS) and redox systems are integral components of plant metabolism and development. This review examines recent findings showing how spatial and temporal redox signals regulate developmental transitions from seed germination to root morphogenesis, reproduction, and seed formation. During germination, ROS, nitric oxide, thioredoxins, and related oxidoreductase systems coordinate reserve mobilization, respiration, hormone responses, and the decision between dormancy release and growth arrest. In roots, localized redox landscapes define meristem activity, differentiation, root hair growth, and lateral root plasticity under changing water availability. Recent work further indicates that glutathione content, not only redox potential, is required for Arabidopsis root apical meristem activity and root elongation. In reproductive tissues, ROS interact with nitric oxide and antioxidant systems to control ovule development, pollen tube guidance, self-incompatibility responses, and stress tolerance during pollen growth. Redox regulation also shapes seed development, as shown by studies linking ROS homeostasis to basal endosperm transfer layer differentiation and kernel viability. Across these contexts, ROS act within narrow cellular domains and signaling windows, rather than as nonspecific stress byproducts, to influence proliferation, differentiation, and programmed cell death. The review demonstrates how small redox shifts fine-tune developmental outcomes at cell-type resolution and across different developmental stages. Defining these mechanisms will be important for explaining developmental plasticity and for improving crop resilience under fluctuating environments.
