# MhAFB2-MhIAA1_like module mediates auxin-associated modulation of apple immunity against Alternaria alternata.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant science : an international journal of experimental plant biology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43020380](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43020380/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113597](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113597)
- **第一作者**: Yumeng An
- **作者**: Yumeng An, Deqian Hong, Ruimin Lu, Kaixu Hu, Shenchun Qu, Xinyi Yu

## 摘要
Alternaria alternata is a devastating pathogenic fungus in apple, leading to leaf necrosis and premature defoliation. Although auxin signaling is known to participate in plant immunity, the specific roles of the TIR1/AFB-Aux/IAA signaling module in apple-pathogen interactions remain largely elusive. Here, we identified and characterized MhAFB2 (a TIR1/AFB auxin receptor homolog) and MhIAA1_like (an Aux/IAA transcriptional repressor homolog) cloned from Malus hupehensis. Protein interaction assays, including yeast two-hybrid, GST pull-down, BiFC, and LCI, demonstrated that MhAFB2 interacted with MhIAA1_like in an auxin-dependent or auxin-promoted manner, exhibiting a more robust and readily detectable interaction tendency compared to MhTIR1. Using transgenic 'Orin' calli and transiently transformed 'Gala' leaves as the functional evaluation system, we conducted genetic manipulation that revealed distinct disease phenotypes. MhAFB2 overexpression compromised apple resistance to A. alternata, whereas the RNAi-mediated silencing conferred an enhancement. Conversely, overexpression of MhIAA1_like significantly restricted lesion expansion, while its silencing increased the susceptibility. These altered disease phenotypes were tightly coupled with physiological and transcriptional defense responses. Resistant lines maintained higher levels of H2O2 accumulation and exhibited elevated expression of key salicylic acid- (PR1, PAD4, and EDS1), jasmonic acid-, and ethylene- defense pathway genes, whereas susceptible lines showed opposite patterns alongside increased expression of early auxin-responsive genes (GH3.1 and ARF4). Furthermore, exogenous indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) treatment suppresses immune responses primed by A. alternata infection, an effect that is partially attenuated upon MhAFB2 silencing. Together, these findings reveal that MhAFB2 and MhIAA1_like act as core components in the auxin-associated modulation of apple immunity, and the MhAFB2-MhIAA1_like module is involved in coordinating the balance between auxin signaling and multi-hormone-associated defenses, providing new insights into the roles of auxin receptor-repressor pairs in fine-tuning plant immune responses.
