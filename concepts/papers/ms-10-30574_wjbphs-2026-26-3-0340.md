3. Unlike single-target synthetic drugs, luteolin exhibits multi-target interactions, enabling simultaneous modulation of inflammation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, and immune responses [5].
4. Given the expanding body of literature and the increasing relevance of multi-target natural compounds in modern drug discovery, a consolidated understanding of luteolin’s molecular mechanisms and therapeutic scope is required.
5. Molecular mechanisms of luteolin Luteolin exerts its pharmacological effects through a complex and interconnected network of molecular targets involved in inflammation, oxidative stress, apoptosis, autophagy, cell cycle regulation, immune responses, and cellular metabolism [5-9].
6. Unlike single-target therapeutic agents, luteolin functions as a pleiotropic signaling modulator capable of simultaneously regulating multiple intracellular pathways [3-5].
7. This multi-target activity underlies its broad therapeutic potential across cancer, neurodegenerative disorders, cardiovascular diseases, metabolic syndromes, and chronic inflammatory conditions [10-12].
8. Experimental studies have demonstrated that luteolin interacts with transcription factors, protein kinases, cytokines, growth factor receptors, and redox-sensitive signaling molecules, thereby restoring cellular homeostasis under pathological conditions [13,14].
