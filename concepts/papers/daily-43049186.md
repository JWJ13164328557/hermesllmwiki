# Lanosterol is the biosynthetic precursor of solanoeclepins, root-secreted hatching factors for potato cyst nematodes.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: The New phytologist (2026)
- **PMID**: [43049186](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43049186/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/nph.71760](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.71760)
- **第一作者**: Ryota Akiyama
- **作者**: Ryota Akiyama, Bubwoong Kang, Shota Suzawa, Atsuhiko Kushida, Keiji Tanino, Masaharu Mizutani

## 摘要
Solanoeclepins are highly modified triterpenoids secreted from tomato and potato roots that act as potent hatching factors for potato cyst nematodes and are therefore important targets for the development of sustainable control strategies. However, their biosynthetic origin has remained unknown, limiting our understanding of their biological functions and practical exploitation. In this study, we identified the initial step of solanoeclepin biosynthesis using tomato hairy roots lacking lanosterol synthase, together with chemical complementation and stable isotope feeding experiments using deuterium-labeled lanosterol to validate its precursor role. Disruption of lanosterol synthase abolished lanosterol production and greatly reduced solanoeclepin accumulation. Exogenous lanosterol restored solanoeclepin production, and deuterium-labeled lanosterol was directly incorporated into solanoeclepins, providing definitive evidence that lanosterol is their biosynthetic precursor. Our results establish the entry point to the solanoeclepin biosynthesis and reveal a previously unrecognized function of plant lanosterol metabolism in the biosynthesis of root-secreted signaling molecules. These findings provide a foundation for elucidating the complete solanoeclepin biosynthetic pathway, exploring the biological roles of lanosterol-derived metabolites in plants, and enabling metabolic engineering approaches for sustainable potato cyst nematode control.
