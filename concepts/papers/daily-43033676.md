# Genomic and functional analysis of the endophytic Flavobacterium johnsoniae UMJL46B reveals integrated growth-promotion strategies in Triticum aestivum.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Microbiological research (2026)
- **PMID**: [43033676](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43033676/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.micres.2026.128826](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.micres.2026.128826)
- **第一作者**: José Luis Ávila-Oviedo
- **作者**: José Luis Ávila-Oviedo, Lourdes Macías-Rodríguez, Arturo Ramírez-Ordorica, Sergio de Los Santos-Villalobos, Perla García-Juárez, Gustavo Santoyo, Eduardo Valencia-Cantero

## 摘要
Members of the genus Flavobacterium are abundant in the wheat endosphere; however, their functional roles in plant systems remain largely unresolved. In this study 11 endophytic Flavobacterium strains were isolated and evaluated for their effects on Triticum aestivum. Plant growth promotion assays revealed contrasting responses, ranging from growth inhibition to growth promotion. Among these strains, UMJL46B consistently exhibited plant growth promoting effects, including increases in shoot biomass (21%) as well as a notable increase in the number of lateral roots (84%). Whole genome sequencing confirmed that UMJL46B belongs to the F. johnsoniae species and revealed multiple plant associated functional traits, including genes involved in phytohormone biosynthesis, nutrient metabolism, stress tolerance, and volatile organic compound emission, as well as a putative cytochrome P450 associated with gibberellin biosynthesis. Functional assays validated these predictions. UMJL46B produced tryptophan-dependent indole-3-acetic acid, which correlated with modifications in root system architecture, including a 24% increase in root number. At later developmental stages, bacterial inoculation increased tiller formation by 7.4-fold and enhanced leaf development by 32%. In addition, UMJL46B synthesized gibberellic acid, significantly accelerating seed germination within 24 h and representing an unequivocal report of GA₃ production within the genus Flavobacterium. Finally, UMJL46B promoted root and shoot development in vitro through volatile mediated mechanisms, including acetoin and 3-methyl-butanol emission as complementary mechanisms by which UMJL46B enhances plant growth. The integration of genomic and functional analyses identifies F. johnsoniae UMJL46B as a plant growth-promoting endophyte and demonstrates the value of genome guided strategies for predicting beneficial plant microbe interactions.
