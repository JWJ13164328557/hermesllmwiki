### 核心发现
1. They enable testing whether hypothesized key players and their interactions are necessary and sufficient to reproduce a given phenomenon and help unravel the mechanisms underlying how these components collectively orchestrate root growth and architecture.
2. Mechanistic models are particularly powerful in revealing emergent properties that cannot be understood from separate players or processes (6, 60, 66, 70, 102, 111, 125), offering novel insights into the selforganizing principles governing root development.
3. Importantly, with the rise of biological big data, the term model can mean different things.
4. These models are often differential equation models or use some other formalism to describe the temporal evolution of system variables and their interdependencies, aiming to rebuild in silico the mechanism underlying a biological process in order to understand its inner workings.
5. Computational models have played a critical role in unraveling the mechanisms underlying auxin pattern formation.
6. Importantly, these models have thus far mostly been applied on already fully formed root tip topologies.
7. Zooming out from auxin alone, modeling studies have played a key role in elucidating the effects of the interplay between auxin, cytokinin, and ethylene signaling on their respective patterning (55, 75, 76).
8. Surprisingly, more can still be learned from studying PIN-mediated transport in more detail.
9. Notably, while PIN1 localized apolarly with a basal bias, PIN3 localized apolarly with an apical bias.
10. Surprisingly, these results indicate that while PIN polarity is important, differences in PIN transport rate also play an important role in generating physiological auxin patterns.

12. Similarly, for longitudinal zonation, the interplay between auxin and cytokinin was demonstrated to play a key role in patterning the root developmental domains.
13. Moving beyond auxin and cytokinin, modeling was applied to unravel the key roles of ethylene signaling in shaping auxin and cytokinin patterning (55, 75, 76).
14. Integration of mechanical details has allowed for novel investigations into the mechanisms by which plants regulate shape formation.
