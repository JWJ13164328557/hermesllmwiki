2. These findings suggest that loss of SFTPB expression is associated with tumor progression and may be linked to biological processes related to metastatic dissemination in AD.
3. These findings suggest that the prognostic value of SFTPB may extend beyond recurrence- related events and could reflect biological factors influencing long-term disease progression, treatment response, or survival following recurrence.
4. Overall, these findings suggest that the prognostic significance of SFTPB is highly dependent on histologic subtype and lineage context.
5. Collectively, these findings support the concept that SFTPB functions not simply as a structural surfactant protein but also as a lineage-associated biomarker reflecting distinct differentiation states in NSCLC.
6. Given these distinct molecular backgrounds, subtype-specific biomarkers are necessary to better understand tumor biology, refine prognostic stratifica- tion, and potentially guide subtype-specific therapeutic strategies.
