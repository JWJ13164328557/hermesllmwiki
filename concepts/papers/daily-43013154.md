# Genome-wide characterization and analysis of the VviFBW gene family in regulating grape berry coloration.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Molecular biology reports (2026)
- **PMID**: [43013154](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43013154/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s11033-026-13114-w](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11033-026-13114-w)
- **第一作者**: Yong-Ke Wu
- **作者**: Yong-Ke Wu, Ding-Ding Zuo, Tong-Lu Wei, Hai-Nan Liu, Mao-Song Pei, Da-Long Guo

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: F-box proteins serve as the substrate-recognition subunits of the SCF E3 ubiquitin ligase complex, playing pivotal roles in plant development, hormone signaling, and stress responses via the ubiquitin-proteasome system. However, a genome-wide characterization of the F-box/WD40 (FBW) subfamily in grapevine (Vitis vinifera L.) remains largely unexplored.

METHODS AND RESULTS: In this study, we systematically identified and characterized eight FBW subfamily genes (designated as VviFBW1 to VviFBW8) in the grape genome. Phylogenetic analysis clustered these VviFBW proteins into three distinct subgroups alongside homologs from Arabidopsis and rice. Promoter analysis revealed an abundance of light-, stress-, and phytohormone-responsive cis-acting elements, suggesting that VviFBW genes are integrated into complex regulatory networks. Combined RNA-seq and RT-qPCR analyses revealed stage-specific expression profiles during fruit development; VviFBW8 was significantly upregulated during late ripening phases. The overexpression of VviFBW8 can promote the ripening and coloration of grape berry. Regulatory network analysis placed VviFBW genes at central nodes interacting with 84 transcription factors. Subcellular localization assays demonstrated that the VviFBW8 protein predominantly reside in the cytoplasm. GO enrichment analysis showed that VviFBW genes were mainly enriched in plant growth and hormone pathway.

CONCLUSION: In summary, this study elucidate the evolutionary, structural, and expression characteristics of the VviFBW subfamily in grapevine, providing candidate target genes and a theoretical foundation for further deciphering the molecular mechanisms of FBW-mediated fruit development and stress adaptation.
