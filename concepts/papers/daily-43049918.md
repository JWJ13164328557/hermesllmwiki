# Hormone network hubs orchestrate growth-stress-yield trade-offs in rice.

## 摘要
Abiotic stresses, including drought, salinity, flooding, and heat, threaten rice productivity by disrupting growth, physiological processes, and reproductive development. Plant hormones coordinate adaptive responses to these stresses, but protective mechanisms frequently restrict growth and compromise yield formation. This review examines how interactions among abscisic acid (ABA), gibberellins (GA), auxin, cytokinins, ethylene, and brassinosteroids (BRs) regulate growth-stress-yield trade-offs in rice. Particular emphasis is placed on hormonal crosstalk and its consequences for panicle development, spikelet fertility, and grain filling. Under drought and salinity, ABA signaling contributes to stress adaptation while interacting with growth-regulatory pathways. Flooding responses involve ethylene-mediated regulation of survival and growth strategies, whereas heat stress disrupts reproductive development through complex physiological and hormonal responses. Regulatory components, including the DELLA protein SLR1, cytokinin oxidase OsCKX2, and auxin transporter OsPIN1, illustrate how hormone signaling, metabolism, and transport influence developmental and stress-responsive processes. However, their combined effects on stress resilience and yield remain incompletely understood. We further discuss how multi-omics integration, quantitative hormone profiling, computational modeling, and natural genetic variation could improve the identification and characterization of hormone-related regulatory mechanisms. Genomic selection and precision gene editing offer complementary opportunities to investigate candidate genes and improve rice germplasm, although their agronomic benefits require validation across genetic backgrounds and field environments. Integrating mechanistic understanding with functional genetics and field phenotyping may facilitate breeding strategies that strengthen stress resilience while maintaining reproductive productivity under increasingly variable climatic conditions and supporting sustainable rice production.
