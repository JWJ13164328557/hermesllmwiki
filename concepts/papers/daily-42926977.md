## 摘要
Soybean paste is a classic fermented soybean condiment. Microbial fermentation generates soybean paste functional peptides (SPFPs), which regulate flavor and exert bioactivities, determining the sensory quality and health benefits of soybean paste. This review systematically introduces SPFP formation mechanisms and summarizes techniques for their extraction, isolation, purification and characterization. Recent advances concerning their flavor functions and biological activities are outlined. Gastrointestinal stability, in vivo absorption efficiency and bioavailability of SPFPs are also discussed. Additionally, the value of multi-omics integrated molecular regulation for SPFP research is highlighted, and future research strategies are proposed. This work supports high-value utilization and innovative development of traditional fermented soybean products.

1. Other challenges include translating in vitro activity into in vivo efficacy and achieving cost-effective enrichment of target peptides.
2. Collectively, the types, activities and stage-specific action characteristics of microbial proteases are the pivotal factors determining the profile, structural features and functional properties of bioactive peptides in soybean paste ( ).
3. Optimized conditions for protein degradation do not necessarily maximize the concentration or bioactivity of target peptides.
4. Recent soybean-paste peptide discovery provides sequence-specific examples of how hydrophobic and charged groups may contribute to target recognition.
5. Specific sequence motifs may influence peptide–target interactions and activity by shaping local conformation and the spatial distribution of hydrophobic, charged, and hydrogen-bonding groups.
6. However, their effects depend on the surrounding sequence, target structure, and experimental conditions, so the presence of a motif alone does not establish activity.
7. Their biological relevance depends on their stability during gastrointestinal digestion, transport across the intestinal epithelium, systemic exposure, and ability to reach a target tissue at an effective concentration.
8. Local intestinal effects require consideration of digestion and luminal dilution, whereas systemic effects require measurements of intact peptides or defined active metabolites in circulation or target tissues.
