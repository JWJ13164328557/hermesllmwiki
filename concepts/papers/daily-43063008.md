# Moss is more: The moss Physcomitrium patens as an ideal chassis for plant synthetic biology.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43063008](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43063008/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/plphys/kiag876](https://doi.org/10.1093/plphys/kiag876)
- **第一作者**: Rebecca Wolters
- **作者**: Rebecca Wolters, Sze Wai Tse, James P B Lloyd

## 摘要
Physcomitrium patens, commonly known as the spreading earthmoss or Physco, has gained increasing attention as a chassis for synthetic biology in plants. This review explores its advantages over flowering plants traditionally used more often for biotechnology, such as Arabidopsis thaliana and maize, including shorter life cycles and ease to handle experimentally, being haploid, and much higher efficiency for integration of transgenes into its genome by homologous recombination. In addition, Physco's ability to maintain episomes also introduces an additional possibility for transgene expression and manipulation. With these advantageous traits, Physco has already been used for synthetic genomes, recombinant protein production, metabolic engineering and artificial organelle engineering. These applications have been enabled by various toolkits developed for construction of transformation cassettes, DNA and RNA editing, as well as different reporters to allow microscopy imaging or quantification of gene expression. While Physco does have limitations, such as its high number of duplicated genes which can present issues when tweaking of native genes is involved, these certainly do not prevent Physco becoming a useful synthetic biology model for further applications on Earth and future space explorations.
