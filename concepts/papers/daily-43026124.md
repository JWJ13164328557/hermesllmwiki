# Understanding plant UPR from structural and comparative perspectives.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43026124](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43026124/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.tcb.2026.10.010](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tcb.2026.10.010)
- **第一作者**: Ningjing Liu
- **作者**: Ningjing Liu, Qingqiu Gong

## 摘要
The endoplasmic reticulum (ER) is central to protein, lipid, Ca2+, and redox homeostasis. ER stress occurs when oxidative protein-folding capacity is exceeded or lipid composition becomes abnormal. To restore ER and cellular homeostasis, the cell initiates the unfolded protein response (UPR)-a transcriptional response that amplifies the ER's capacity for protein folding, degradation, and lipid biosynthesis. Our understanding of plant UPR activation mechanisms remains limited compared with yeast and mammalian systems. Here, we compare the structural composition of the plant UPR sensors, inositol-requiring enzyme 1 and basic-region leucine zipper 28, with their yeast and animal homologs; predict their potential activation and regulatory mechanisms; and discuss which small-molecule inhibitors developed for mammalian UPR research may be applicable to plant UPR studies.
