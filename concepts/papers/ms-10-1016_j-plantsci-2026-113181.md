# Low-intensity near-infrared light supplementation promotes chloroplast remodelling and biomass in tobacco, whereas far-red light induces shade-type acclimation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant Science (2026)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113181](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plantsci.2026.113181)
- **作者**: Renan Falcioni, Werner Camargos Antunes, Evanilde Benedito, José Alexandre M. Demattê, Marcos Rafael Nanni
- **来源**: crossref


## 摘要
Far-red (FR) photons can increase photosynthesis by improving the PSII-PSI excitation balance; however, the extent to which longer near-infrared (NIR) wavebands (>750 nm) elicit coordinated acclimation of photosynthetic traits and chloroplast organisation remains poorly characterised. We grew Nicotiana tabacum L. under broad-spectrum white light (200 μmol m-2 s-1, 400-700 nm) either alone or supplemented with narrowband radiation centred at 740 nm (FR), 850 nm (NIR) or 940 nm (NIR) at low supplemental photon fluxes (3-5 μmol m-2 s-1). We quantified growth and biomass allocation, leaf reflectance, transmittance and absorptance (350-2500 nm), pigments, gas exchange-coupled fluorescence responses, leaf structure and ultrastructure, and energy yield. FR supplementation promoted an elongated, pale-leaf phenotype, with 26.5% lower total chlorophyll per area and 3-5-fold lower stomatal density than the white control, reduced PSII efficiency and JIP performance indices, increased nonphotochemical quenching, and decreased Amax, Vcmax and Jmax, resulting in the lowest biomass and energy yield. In contrast, compared with FR, NIR supplementation (850 and 940 nm) increased the spongy mesophyll contribution and chloroplast abundance (palisade chloroplasts +60% at 850 nm), was associated with qualitatively denser thylakoid organisation in representative sections and frequent chloroplast-mitochondria juxtaposition, and improved PSII operating efficiency and electron transport; 850 nm most consistently increased photosynthetic capacity, whereas 940 nm maximised biomass and energy yield. Switching NIR light on/off under a white background caused little instantaneous change in CO2 assimilation, and NIR alone supported negligible assimilation, indicating that the long-term benefits observed here arise primarily from acclimation rather than from direct instantaneous NIR-driven photochemistry under these conditions.
