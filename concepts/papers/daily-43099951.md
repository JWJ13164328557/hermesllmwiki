# Effects of Methyl Jasmonate and Salicylic Acid on Volatile Composition of Tea Tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) Leaves.

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43099951](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43099951/)
- **DOI**: [10.1002/cbdv.71944](https://doi.org/10.1002/cbdv.71944)

- **作者**: Anita Apriliani Dwi Rahayu, Ian A Southwell, Carolyn Raymond, Ramil P Mauleon, Lei Liu

## 摘要
Phytohormones such as methyl jasmonate (MeJA) and salicylic acid (SA) have been found to induce the production of valuable secondary metabolites in plants. Tea tree (Melaleuca alternifolia) produces volatile secondary metabolites that are distilled to tea tree oil and widely used as antiseptic, antimicrobial, anti-inflammatory, and disinfectant agents. The effects of foliar application of MeJA or SA on the volatile components produced in tea tree leaves were investigated in this study. Rooted cuttings from six clones were sprayed with MeJA or SA in three different concentrations (0.5, 1.0, and 2.0 mM). Three weeks after application of MeJA or SA, fresh leaves were extracted by ethanol. The extracted volatile compounds were measured using gas chromatography-flame ionization detector (GC-FID). The 1 mM SA increased significantly the yield of terpinen-4-ol in mature leaves from clone 2 compared to the control or other treated plants. MeJA and SA were found to have subtle effects on the composition of some volatile components such as α-terpineol. The variation in some volatile components such as terpinen-4-ol, γ-terpinene, and α-terpinene was mainly contributed by genotype. In conclusion, the foliar application of MeJA and SA may only increase the production of commercially valuable terpenoids in some tea tree genotype.
