### 核心发现
1. Moreover, 17 amino acids in the N-terminal sequence of HTT interact with the nuclear pore protein, Translocated Promoter Region (TRP), which plays a critical role in the nuclear export of proteins to the cytoplasm.
2. In HD, the mHTT pro­ tein fails to interact with REST/NRSF, leading to nuclear accumulation of REST/NRSF and downregulation of neurotrophin expression, such as BDNF [49, 104].
3. The polyQ creates a Polar Zipper structure and binds to transcription factors that contain polyQ regions [39, 41].
4. Furthermore, it can interact with multiple transcription factors and regula­ tory proteins, thereby influencing transcription.
5. Wild-type HTT also interacts with β-tubulin and the dynein/dynactin complex and binds to microtubules to regulate intracellular trafficking, thus acting as a scaffold molecule [39, 51].
6. In contrast, the autophagy-lys­ osome system is primarily located in the cytoplasm and plays a crucial role in degrading long-lived proteins and large structures, such as protein aggregates and organelles [77, 78].
7. The UPS primarily targets the nuclear mHTT fragments and is also involved in wild- type HTT processing, whereas the autophagy-lysosomal pathway targets the mHTT [79, 80].
8. These shorter repeats are known to silence specific target genes [89–91].
