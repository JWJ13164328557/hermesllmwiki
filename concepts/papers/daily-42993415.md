# Evolutionary conservation and abiotic stress response of calmodulin-binding protein 60 family proteins in Zea mays.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Molecular biology reports (2026)
- **PMID**: [42993415](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42993415/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s11033-026-13037-6](https://doi.org/10.1007/s11033-026-13037-6)
- **第一作者**: Yue Wang
- **作者**: Yue Wang, Yanxiao Li, Zhen Liu, Weifeng Dong, Mingzhe Sun, Yang Shen, Xiaoli Sun

## 摘要
BACKGROUND: The calmodulin-binding protein 60 (CBP60) family of transcription factors plays crucial roles in regulating plant stress responses, growth, and development. Although extensively studied in many plant species, its potential biological functions in maize (Zea mays) remain unexplored.

METHODS AND RESULTS: In this study, we reidentified 13 ZmCBP60 genes in the maize genome. Evolutionary analyses revealed a closer phylogenetic relationship and stronger collinearity with monocots (especially rice) than with dicots, a pattern further supported by distinctive codon usage patterns. Promoter cis-element analysis uncovered an abundance of stress-responsive and hormone-responsive elements. Expression profiling demonstrated that several ZmCBP60 genes were differentially regulated under cold, osmotic, and salt stresses as well as by ABA treatment. Notably, the expression patterns under abiotic stresses closely mirrored those under ABA, suggesting their possible involvement in an ABA-mediated signaling pathway. Furthermore, ZmCBP1 and ZmCBP60c-4 exhibited coordinated expression under salt stress, indicating potential functional redundancy.

CONCLUSION: Together, these results offer a genome-wide investigation of the ZmCBP60 gene family in maize and suggest putative involvement of its members in abiotic stress responses, laying a foundation for future functional validation.
