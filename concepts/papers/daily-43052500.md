# Natural polyacetylenes induce heat shock protein gene expression and enhance heat tolerance in Arabidopsis thaliana.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Protoplasma (2026)
- **PMID**: [43052500](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43052500/)
- **DOI**: [10.1007/s00709-026-02298-4](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00709-026-02298-4)
- **第一作者**: Hiroki Murano
- **作者**: Hiroki Murano, Toshihiro Hayakawa, Masakazu Hara

## 摘要
Heat waves negatively impact crop growth and quality. Chemical priming, which enhances plant stress tolerance through treatment with natural compounds, offers a promising strategy. While established chemical priming agents such as acetic acid and ethanol are well-researched, it is crucial to identify novel compounds. To that end, we investigated the stress response-inducing activity of various wild plant extracts using an Arabidopsis thaliana reporter gene assay. This revealed that falcarindiol, a polyacetylene found in Chamaele decumbens, induces a heat stress response (HSR). Arabidopsis treated with falcarindiol exhibited increased expression of three genes of different HSP families and improved heat tolerance. Atractylodin, another polyacetylene that induced HSR in Arabidopsis, inhibited the chaperone activity of wheat germ extract in a dose-dependent manner. In contrast, 2,4-hexadiyne - 1,6-diol, a polyacetylene that did not enhance HSR, showed no inhibitory activity. Since previous studies indicate that inhibitors of HSP chaperone activity induce HSR in plants, falcarindiol and atractylodin may alleviate heat stress through a similar mechanism. In conclusion, it was shown that some natural polyacetylenes exhibit a chemical priming effect.
