# Genome-wide identification of carboxylesterase gene family in tomato (Solanum lycopersicum).

## 论文信息

- **PMID**: [43074726](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43074726/)
- **DOI**: [10.1002/tpg2.70349](https://doi.org/10.1002/tpg2.70349)

- **作者**: Jing Shang, Fujun Li, Jihan Wang, Yue Ji, Yanan Li, Qinglian Liu, Xiaoan Li, Ling Li

## 摘要
Carboxylesterases (CXEs) catalyze the hydrolysis of a wide range of ester compounds and play pivotal roles in plant hormone metabolism, volatile compound regulation, and stress responses. Despite their functional significance, a comprehensive characterization of CXEs in tomato (Solanum lycopersicum) remains lacking. In this study, a total of 24 SlCXE genes were identified through genome-wide analysis, and their phylogenetic relationships, gene structures, and chromosomal distributions were systematically characterized. Collinearity analysis revealed that SlCXEs exhibit closer evolutionary relationships with homologs in dicotyledonous species. Transcriptome profiling indicated that several SlCXE genes are highly expressed during fruit ripening. Among these, Solyc01g108540 (SlCXE6) was selected for further analysis due to its representative expression pattern, conserved structural features, and relatively diverse cis-regulatory elements. Subsequent expression analyses demonstrated that SlCXE6 displays tissue-specific expression patterns, is dynamically regulated during fruit development, and responds differentially to pathogen infection, low-temperature stress, and hormone treatments. These findings provide a comprehensive overview of the SlCXE gene family in tomato and offer novel insights into their evolutionary diversification and functional involvement in fruit ripening and stress adaptation.

Carboxylesterases are enzymes that help plants regulate chemical compounds involved in growth, flavor formation, and stress responses. However, this gene family has not been systematically studied in tomato. In this study, we identified 24 carboxylesterase genes in the tomato genome and analyzed their evolutionary relationships, gene structures, chromosome locations, conserved protein features, and expression patterns. Several genes showed high expression during fruit development and ripening. Among them, SlCXE6 was selected for further analysis because of its conserved structure, active expression during fruit ripening, and potential responses to hormones and stresses. The results suggest that SlCXE genes may play important roles in tomato fruit ripening, aroma‐related metabolism, and adaptation to environmental stress.
