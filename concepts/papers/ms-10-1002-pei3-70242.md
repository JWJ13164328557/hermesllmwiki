## 摘要
ABSTRACT
                  
                    In crop plants, epidermal flavonoids play a predominant role in photoprotection. To date, however, there has been no attempt to exploit the flavonoids as a biomarker in breeding for heat tolerance because routine phenotyping is neither practicable nor cost‐effective. A portable, non‐destructive fluorescence device, the Dualex Scientific sensor, has been used to estimate total epidermal flavonoids in a wide range of plant species. Our objectives in the current research were to investigate which of the flavonoids in wheat (
                    
                      Triticum aestivum
                    
                    L.) can be predicted from sensor readings obtained using the device, test its potential as a target trait to breed for heat tolerance and investigate the genetics through genome‐wide quantitative trait loci (QTL) and prediction analyses. A targeted analysis of several metabolites found that Flav index from the Dualex sensor was highly correlated with the isoorientin content of the leaf (
                    R
                    2
                     = 0.9) and furthermore, cultivars rated as heat‐tolerant versus sensitive showed significant differences in isoorientin contents when exposed to artificial heat stress in the field. Using the Flav index as a proxy, a genome‐wide QTL analysis identified eight single‐nucleotide polymorphic markers associated with the trait. The individual loci explained between 2% and 30% of the phenotypic variation, and the major‐effect locus mapped close to a wheat ortholog of the maize gene (
                    ZmUGT708A6
                    ), which encodes a
                    C‐glycosyltransferase
                    and requires further validation. We further investigated genomic selection using molecular markers alone and found the prospects to be high, with a prediction accuracy of 0.69 ± 0.003. In conclusion, our results suggest that high concentration of isoorientin in wheat leaves, which could be accurately predicted using fluorescence imaging, are associated with heat tolerance in wheat. Furthermore, the wheat crop could be a valuable source of this health‐promoting compound.
