# White-backed chalky grains of japonica rice caused by heat stress during maturation is associated with morphological and epigenetic regulation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Annals of botany (2026)
- **PMID**: [43053588](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43053588/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/aob/mcag367](https://doi.org/10.1093/aob/mcag367)
- **第一作者**: Takuya Kobayashi
- **作者**: Takuya Kobayashi, Chetphilin Suriyasak, Hue Thi Nong, Yui Oyama, Wun-Jin Chen, Arata Yasuda, Xinyue Yang, Norimitsu Hamaoka

## 摘要
BACKGROUND AND AIMS: Rice (Oryza sativa L.) is strongly affected by heat stress during grain filling. Heat stress is a significant contributor to chalky grain, one of the main causes of quality and yield reductions in rice. Among several types of chalky grains, the white-backed type often occurs under heat stress, but the mechanism underlying this defect is not well studied. Therefore, a better understanding of how heat stress induces the white-backed type of chalky grains is necessary to improve grain quality under heat-stress conditions.

METHODS: After flowering, rice plants were exposed to either control (25 °C) or heat (30 °C) temperature treatments. The occurrence and extent of chalkiness and morphological changes of the aleurone layer were evaluated, together with gene transcription levels and epigenetic regulation.

KEY RESULTS: Heat stress during grain filling significantly increased the occurrence of chalky grains after harvest, compared with the control. White-backed chalky grain was the predominant type observed. The expression of α-amylase isozymes was greatly up-regulated in the heat-stressed grains compared to the control. An increase in the thickness of the dorsal aleurone layer was observed in the white-backed chalky grains. In particular, heat stress at the early stage (0-7 days after flowering) significantly increased the occurrence of white-backed chalky grains and increased the thickness of the dorsal aleurone layer. Importantly, heat stress at this stage also significantly up-regulated α-amylase gene expression at a later phase of development, even after the heat stress had ended. This prolonged increase in OsAmy3D gene expression was found to be associated with H3K4me3 enrichment, suggesting the involvement of epigenetic regulations.

CONCLUSIONS: Heat stress during grain filling, especially at an early stage, significantly increased the occurrence of white-backed grains, attributable to increased number of dorsal aleurone cells and epigenetically up-regulated α-amylase expression, although the association between these two changes warrants further study.
