# Red, blue and far-red light regimes shape vegetative growth and flowering responses in wild rocket: potential implications for vertical farming and speed breeding

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Frontiers in Plant Science ()
- **DOI**: [10.3389/fpls.2026.2015467](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpls.2026.2015467)
- **作者**: Kambiz Baghalian, Zohreh Soltani
- **来源**: crossref

## 摘要

                    This study compared vegetative growth and flowering incidence in wild rocket (
                    Diplotaxis tenuifolia
                    ) under contrasting spectral and photon-supply conditions. Two varieties, Dragon’s Fire and Saturn, were exposed for four weeks to ambient-light Control and four LED regimes: R70:B30, R50:B50, red-blue-far-red (RBF) and low-photon white (LFW), supplying total photon fluxes of 215, 160, 230 and 50 µmol m
                    -2
                    s
                    -1
                    , respectively, including far-red where present. RBF supplied red:blue:far-red photon fluxes in a 155:60:15 ratio. The white LED treatment served as a reference with deliberately constrained photon supply. Light regime affected shoot fresh weight and stomatal conductance (both p &amp;lt; 0.001), whereas total phenolic content did not differ significantly among light treatments (p &amp;gt; 0.05). The red-blue treatments supported the strongest vegetative performance. Mean shoot fresh weight was 3.47 and 2.63 g plant
                    -1
                    under R70:B30 and R50:B50, respectively, compared with 0.75 g plant
                    -1
                    under RBF and 0.62 g plant
                    -1
                    under LFW. However, the red-blue treatments did not differ significantly for shoot fresh weight within either variety. RBF produced the highest mean stomatal conductance (g
                    s
                    ; 6.273 and 9.080 mm s
                    -1
                    in Dragon’s Fire and Saturn, respectively) but weak biomass accumulation and flowering, despite receiving the highest photon flux. Under R70:B30 and R50:B50, mean flowering incidence ranged from 16.07 to 21.21% in Dragon’s Fire and from 31.90 to 38.49% in Saturn. No flowering occurred under LFW, although this was accompanied by a substantial biomass penalty. PCA separated the red-blue treatments from RBF and LFW. Shoot fresh weight was positively associated with flowering incidence across treatments and varieties (R
                    2
                    = 0.621). Overall, wild rocket responded to the complete light regime rather than spectral composition or photon supply independently.
