# Graphene oxide remodels the rhizosphere microbiome and induces metabolic reprogramming of flavor-related compounds in tea leaves.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Food chemistry. Molecular sciences (2026)
- **PMID**: [42872044](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42872044/)
- **DOI**: [10.1111/nph.17928](https://doi.org/10.1111/nph.17928)
- **第一作者**: Jing Yang
- **作者**: Jing Yang, Qiyuan An, Shangjia Du, Fan Wu, Junxia Niu, Guangzhen Zhou

## 摘要
The flavor quality of Camellia sinensis is strongly influenced by their secondary metabolites and associated microbial communities. Graphene oxide (GO) is a carbon-based nanomaterial with unique physicochemical properties. While its potential to modulate plant physiology has been recognized, its specific effects on tea plant metabolism and associated rhizosphere microbiomes remain largely unexplored. To address this gap, a pot experiment was established to evaluate the impact of varying GO concentrations (0, 25, 50, 100 mg/L) on both the foliar metabolic profiles of tea plants and the structural composition of their rhizosphere microbial communities. Untargeted metabolomics and 16S rRNA sequencing were integrated to systematically analyze changes in metabolite accumulation and microbial composition under GO treatments. The results revealed that GO treatments significantly altered the metabolite composition in tea leaves, with key flavor-related compounds such as flavonoids, phenolic acids, amino acids, and their derivatives showing concentration-dependent accumulation. Furthermore, GO application notably increased the richness and diversity of the rhizosphere microbial community and shifted its structure. LEfSe analysis identified distinct microbial biomarkers across GO concentrations, including beneficial taxa (Micrococcaceae, Rhizobiales) in the low-dose group and stress-tolerant taxa (Rhodanobacter, γ-proteobacteria) in the high-dose group. GO treatments also enhanced microbial metabolic pathways associated with secondary metabolite biosynthesis, antibiotic production, and nutrient transport. Co-occurrence network analysis further revealed a reorganization of microbial interactions in the rhizosphere under GO treatment. This study uncovers novel mechanistic insights into the regulatory effects of graphene oxide on the metabolic pathways of tea plants and the compositional dynamics of their rhizosphere microbiomes.

### 核心发现
1. Collectively, these findings confirm that GO can regulate secondary metabolic flux in tea plants, offering a promising technical approach for the targeted modulation of tea flavor and functional quality.
2. Collectively, these results confirm that the effect of GO on the tea rhizosphere microbial community is highly concentration-specific.
3. This study uncovers novel mechanistic insights into the regulatory effects of graphene oxide on the metabolic pathways of tea plants and the compositional dynamics of their rhizosphere microbiomes.
4. These results suggest that GO treatment may enhance the plant-side amino acid transport capacity and may promote theanine translocation from roots to leaves.
5. These results suggest that GO, as a carbon-based nanomaterial, possesses the ability to extensively regulate the metabolic flux towards quality-related pathways.
6. Flavonoids, as the major constituents of tea polyphenols, play a key role in imparting astringency and antioxidant properties to tea, whereas free amino acids are largely responsible for its characteristic umami and sweetness.
7. Taken together, the dose-dependent shifts in microbial community composition—particularly the enrichment of stress-tolerant taxa at higher GO concentrations—likely indicate a rhizosphere-level stress response rather than acute phytotoxicity to the plant itself.
8. Notably, nano‑selenium treatment also promoted the expression of the theanine transporter gene ( CsAAP1, CsAAP5, CsAAP6 ), indicating that nano‑selenium not only facilitated the synthesis of theanine but also promoted its transport from the roots to the leaves (; ).
