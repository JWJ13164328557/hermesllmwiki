# LWD1 acts as a mobile regulator of Arabidopsis circadian clock.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant & cell physiology (2026)
- **PMID**: [43062837](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43062837/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/pcp/pcag159](https://doi.org/10.1093/pcp/pcag159)
- **第一作者**: Yi-Chen Wu
- **作者**: Yi-Chen Wu, Ying Wang, Shu-Hsing Wu

## 摘要
In Arabidopsis, the circadian clock generates ~24-h rhythms that enable plants to anticipate daily environmental changes. Increasing evidence shows that different tissues have distinct rhythmic programs, and the vascular-tissue clocks are viewed as the central hubs that asymmetrically regulate neighboring tissues via mobile signals. However, the identities and roles of these signals remain largely elusive. We previously reported that Arabidopsis mutants defective in LWD1 and LWD2 have a shortened ~18-h period length and reduced amplitude, indicating LWD1 and LWD2 are required for a stable and robust clock. Here we show that the promoters of LIGHT-REGULATED WD1 (LWD1) and LWD2 are preferentially active in vascular tissues. Our data support that LWD1 acts as a mobile factor that migrates cell-to-cell and tissue-to-tissue to regulate the circadian clock in neighboring or distal parts of the plant body. Results indicate that LWD1 mobility is driven, at least in part, by LWD1 mRNA moving from vascular tissue into adjacent mesophyll cells, where it can function in regulating the circadian clock. Grafting experiments further demonstrate long-distance movement of shoot-derived LWD1 mRNAs to the roots of the lwd1 lwd2 mutant and partially rescue the circadian defects in lwd1 lwd2 roots. Taken together, LWD1 mRNAs can be mobile signaling molecules that coordinate circadian communication locally and systemically in Arabidopsis.
