### 核心发现
1. We show that this reduced SA responsiveness in gsnor1 plants arises from impaired vascular-­associated movement of SA, thereby compromising systemic acquired resistance (SAR).
2. While we cannot fully explain the basis for Cao et al.’s (71) findings, our results support the conclusion that SA is a major signal present in PEX, while NHP levels are extremely low or absent.
3. GSNO plays a pivotal role in mediating protein S-­nitrosylation, serving as a relatively stable form that can transport NO bioactivity throughout the cell.
4. These findings suggest that GSNOR1 plays a key regulatory role in SA signaling.
5. These results suggest that the elevated NO levels observed in gsnor1 plants are independent of regulation by NOA1 or NIA2.
6. Together, these results suggest that the gsnor1 mutation is epistatic to camta1/2/3 and that GSNOR1 plays a major role in the regulation of SARD1 expression, and in turn SARD1-­mediated SA and Pip biosynthesis.
7. Collectively, these findings indicate that the mutant’s SA-­ insensitive phenotype primarily reflects reduced foliar uptake and phloem loading/long-­distance movement of SA, rather than impaired NPR1-­dependent perception (18), and they underscore the centrality of SA transport in SAR.
8. Notably, NO regulates SAR in a dose-­dependent manner, with both low and high NO levels compromising SAR (35).
