# Insecticide increases glyphosate resistance in Ipomoea purpurea.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Evolution; international journal of organic evolution (2026)
- **PMID**: [43050592](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43050592/)
- **DOI**: [10.1093/evolut/qpag207](https://doi.org/10.1093/evolut/qpag207)
- **第一作者**: Grace M Zhang
- **作者**: Grace M Zhang, Regina S Baucom

## 摘要
The evolution of plant resistance naturally occurs in complex, multifaceted environments that consist of multiple simultaneous stressors. Understanding how shifting environmental contexts may shape resistance evolution requires empirical studies that consider the combined effects of interacting potential stressors on fitness and selection. Here, we examined how exposure to an insecticide impacts the evolution of resistance to the herbicide glyphosate in Ipomoea purpurea (common morning glory). Through a factorial field experiment, we manipulated glyphosate and an insecticide to estimate selection on glyphosate and herbivory resistance. We found that glyphosate, not herbivory, acted as the primary agent of selection, favoring higher levels of glyphosate resistance. In the presence of glyphosate alone, positive correlational selection favored a combination of higher glyphosate and herbivory resistance, supporting prior work that suggested these traits may be linked. Importantly, insecticide exposure increased the level of glyphosate resistance and marginally weakened the strength of selection acting upon the trait. Together, our results indicate that the expression of herbicide resistance is context-dependent, and that additional chemicals like insecticide have the potential to alter the evolutionary trajectories of plant defense.
