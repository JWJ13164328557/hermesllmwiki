## 摘要
CrRNA engineering has emerged as a pivotal strategy for extending CRISPR-Cas13a biosensing. However, structural modulation of the direct repeat (DR) region remains exceptionally challenging due to its intricate architecture and the high energetic barrier of the Cas13a-crRNA interface, which is conventionally viewed as a rigid and immutable scaffold. Here, we demonstrate that the DR region is instead a programmable topological element with unexpected structural plasticity. By systematically engineering the DR through sequence insertion and structural splitting, we identified multiple DR variants that retain robust catalytic activity. Crucially, this topological reconfiguration enables Cas13a activity to be precisely gated by unmodified nucleic acid blockers, a level of regulation unattainable with the wild-type crRNA. Building on this flexible modulation, we developed Dre-CRISPR, a DR-engineered platform that couples target-triggered DR restoration to a self-reinforcing autocatalytic loop. This self-amplifying system provides a 2 × 106-fold sensitivity enhancement over nonamplified systems. Furthermore, the Dre-CRISPR platform extends the diagnostic scope of Cas13a to a broader spectrum of analytes, ranging from microRNAs to enzymatic activities and heavy metal ions. Our findings redefine the crRNA scaffold as a versatile signaling node and provide a generalizable framework for developing high-sensitivity, self-amplifying CRISPR biosensors through topology-driven guide RNA engineering.

1. CrRNA plays a central role in Cas13a function by directing target recognition and promoting catalytic activation [, ].
2. These results suggest that the high intrinsic affinity between Cas13a and the canonical crRNA, combined with the extreme stability of the active RNP–target complex, renders the wild-type system largely refractory to simple nucleic acid interference.
3. By systematically investigating how structural reconfigurations such as sequence insertion and splitting modulate the catalytic activities of Cas13a, we reveal an unexpected degree of structural plasticity within the tightly bound RNP complex.
4. Within this framework, we found that a 9-nt loop provided the ideal balance between minimal background activation and efficient gating.
5. Through systematic engineering of the lateral and apical loop regions, including sequence insertion and site-specific splitting, we found that multiple crRNA variants preserved robust cis- and trans -cleavage activities comparable to those of the wild-type (WT) guide.
6. Instead, our results reveal that Cas13a activity depends on the adoption of a functional conformation rather than the preservation of a rigid scaffold architecture, thereby redefining the DR as a conformationally and topologically programmable gate for Cas13a activation.
7. Guided by a specific CRISPR RNA (crRNA), Cas13a undergoes conformational rearrangements upon target binding to activate its HEPN nuclease domains, triggering cis- cleavage of the target RNA accompanied with more attractive collateral trans -cleavage of surrounding nontarget RNAs [ – ].
8. A typical Cas13a crRNA consists of a conserved direct repeat (DR) region that mediates Cas13a protein recruitment and ribonucleoprotein (RNP) assembly, and a variable spacer region responsible for sequence-specific target recognition.
