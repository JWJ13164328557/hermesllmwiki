### 核心发现
1. Over the past two decades, major therapeutic advances, including biologics and kinase inhibitors that target inflammatory mediators such as tumor necrosis factor (TNF), interleukin (IL)-6, and the Janus kinase (JAK)-signal transducer and activator of transcription (STAT) pathway, have produced remarkable clinical benefits [1, 2].
2. Collectively, these shifts transform an adaptable equilibrium into a self-sustaining inflammatory ecosystem—an inflammatory attractor state maintained by interlocking metabolic and epigenetic feedback loops.
3. Collectively, these insights position immune tolerance as a self-organizing systems equilibrium regulated by dynamic interactions between immune cells and their environment.
4. In contrast, effector immune cells—including Th1, Th17, cytotoxic T cells, and inflammatory macrophages—adopt glycolytic metabolism characterized by rapid glucose uptake, lactate production, and activation of mechanistic target of rapamycin complex 1 (mTORC1) and hypoxia-inducible factor-1 alpha (HIF-1α) signaling pathways [82, 85].
5. Mitochondrial function plays a central role in maintaining this epigenetic-metabolic coherence.

7. Importantly, this epigenetic-metabolic programming operates not only within individual cells but also across multicellular immune networks.
8. Understanding these differences is critical for developing targeted and effective therapeutic strategies.
