# CsCRY2 mediates blue light-triggered theanine accumulation in new shoots of tea plants in late-spring.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Plant physiology and biochemistry : PPB (2026)
- **PMID**: [43003041](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43003041/)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111943](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plaphy.2026.111943)
- **第一作者**: Zhi Long
- **作者**: Zhi Long, Jing Lei, Qingyuan Zuo, Yuanyuan Zhao, Siyu Xin, Mao Xia, Zhi Zhou, Wenlong Han

## 摘要
Theanine is a non-protein amino acid in tea plants and is the key compound responsible for the fresh and crisp quality of tea leaves. Its content is highest in the new shoots of tea plants in early-spring and decreases significantly as the season progresses into late-spring. The accumulation of theanine in the new tea shoots is regulated by various environmental factors, among which blue light serves as a key photoreceptor signal promoting its accumulation. Cryptochrome (CRY), as the primary blue light receptor in plants, plays a central role in photoreceptor signaling. However, the regulatory mechanisms underlying CsCRY-mediated theanine accumulation in late-spring new tea shoots remain poorly understood. This study systematically analyzed the origin and evolutionary history of CsCRYs. Combined with analyzes of conserved motifs and cis-acting elements, the study revealed the molecular basis for its evolutionary conservation and functional diversification. Spatiotemporal expression pattern analysis showed that the expression of CsCRY2 is highly correlated with the patterns of theanine accumulation, suggesting that it may positively regulate theanine accumulation in the new tea shoots. The results of the blue light supplementation, CsCRY2 transient overexpression and silencing experiments confirmed that CsCRY2 responds to blue light signals, and by inhibiting the degradation process of theanine, it promotes the accumulation of theanine in late-spring new tea shoots. This study elucidated the evolutionary characteristics of CsCRYs and clarifies the central role of CsCRY2 in blue light-regulated theanine accumulation, providing a theoretical basis and technical support for improving tea quality via late-spring blue light supplementation.
