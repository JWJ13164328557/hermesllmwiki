### 核心发现
1. This study establishes BEND4 as a clinically relevant single gene expression-based biomarker and a potential therapeutic target in AML.
2. We found significantly higher expression of BEND4 in three independent datasets, GSE103424 (n=36) [27], GSE83533(n=38) [28], GSE165430(n=268) [29], and in previously used BEAT- AML(n=329) (Figure 1I-L).
3. We found significantly higher (p=0.001) BEND4 protein expression in relapse AML bone marrow biopsy as compared to their corresponding de novo counterpart (Figure 1O).
4. We found that doxorubicin IC50 was higher only in KG1a and THP-1 cells—the two cell lines that exhibited elevated BEND4 expression (Figure 3C).
5. BEND4 expression leads to altered cell proliferation and colony formation ARTICLE IN PRESS ARTICLE IN PRESS Since we found that BEND4 overexpression leads to chemoresistance in AML cells, we further examined the other tumorigenic properties of AML cells post-BEND4 genetic perturbation.
6. Discovery of BEND4 as a novel single- gene prognostic marker and therapeutic target for adverse AML.
7. However, to broaden the scope beyond binary identification of expression-based prognostic markers and toward the identification of therapeutic targets as well, here, we used the Z-score-based statistical approach and identified a gene expression-based candidate prognostic marker, BEN domain-containing protein 4 (BEND4).
8. Therefore, BEND4 holds promise as both a prognostic indicator and a potential therapeutic target in adverse AML.
