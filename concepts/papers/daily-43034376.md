# Artificial Flowering Induction Practices in Pineapple (cv. Sugarloaf) Production in Southern Benin: Comparative Analysis of Effectiveness of Induction Agents and Effects on Fruit Quality.

## 摘要
Flowering Induction Treatment (FIT) is an essential component of the commercial fresh pineapple production system. In Benin Republic and despite the efforts of the government, FIT is still carried out using calcium carbide, which is harmful to human health and the environment. To provide fresh pineapple producers with an eco-friendly FIT, we assessed the effect of 18 FITs on the flowering emergence and fruit quality parameters of cv. Sugarloaf. FITs consisted of zero application, calcium carbide 1%: 22 kg/ha (widely used), activated charcoal enriched with Ethylene (promoted by the government for organic pineapple production), reduced doses of calcium carbide by 25%-50% plus activated charcoal, fine clay or/and petroleum, and reduced doses of Ethrel plus urea and/or sodium bicarbonate or activated charcoal, and activated charcoal enriched with ethylene. Two experiments were carried out in southern Benin using a randomized complete block design with four repetitions. Results showed that treatments including calcium carbide mixed with activated carbon and clay, and Ethrel mixed with urea and/or bicarbonate, induced flowering in more than 95% of cv. Sugarloaf pineapple plants. With these treatments, the length and weight of pineapple fruit, infructescence, and crown, as well as pH and total soluble solids content, were significantly affected. The number of planting materials was also significantly affected by the FITs. Results indicated that treatments with "Calcium carbide 0.75% + Activated carbon 0.5%" and "Ethrel 200ppm + Urea 4% + Sodium bicarbonate 0.4%" offer interesting prospects for conventional cv. Sugarloaf, in terms of flowering incidence, days to flower, and fruit quality, with potential minimal risks for producers and the environment. The potential implications for producer health, environmental safety, and economic benefits were not directly assessed in this study and should be explored through further investigation.
