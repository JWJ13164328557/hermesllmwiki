# Bioprotective potential of Sargassum muticum extracts against Stemphylium vesicarium in tomato.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Bioresources and bioprocessing (2026)
- **PMID**: [43077067](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43077067/)
- **DOI**: [10.1186/s40643-026-01165-7](https://doi.org/10.1186/s40643-026-01165-7)
- **第一作者**: Tânia F L Vicente
- **作者**: Tânia F L Vicente, Carina Félix, Eloísa Toledo, Marta M Correro, Daniela Batista, Pedro D M Dias, Aurelio Gómez-Cadenas, Carolina Clausell-Terol

## 摘要
Stemphylium vesicarium, the pathogen responsible for brown spot disease, poses a significant challenge to pear production and threatens a variety of crops, including onion, parsley, and sunflower. While synthetic fungicides remain the primary control method, their environmental risks, association with microbial resistance, and potential health effects underscore an urgent need for sustainable alternatives. This study explores the potential of Sargassum muticum (Yendo) Fensholt, an invasive seaweed, as a valuable source of bioactive compounds for the development of sustainable plant-protective products for controlling S. vesicarium infections. Native to East Asia and now widespread along European coastlines, S. muticum represents an abundant and underutilized biomass resource rich in minerals, phytohormones, and phenolic acids, including salicylic acid, which may contribute to the observed protective effect following extract application, making it a promising source for eco-friendly agricultural applications. The findings reported here, obtained through controlled infection assays with Solanum lycopersicum L., suggest that treatments with S. muticum extracts, particularly root irrigation and potential seed priming, may have beneficial effects on plant growth, potentially associated with milder disease symptoms. These results support the valorization of invasive seaweed biomass as a source of bio-based agricultural products and contribute to the development of environmentally friendly strategies for the sustainable management of S. vesicarium infections and other crop diseases.
