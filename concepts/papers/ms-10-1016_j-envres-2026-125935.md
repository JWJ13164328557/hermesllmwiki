# Engineered Escherichia coli nrfA reduces cadmium accumulation in rice by modulating bacterial-driven nitrogen cycling and root iron plaque formation.

## 论文信息
- **期刊**: Environmental Research (2026)
- **DOI**: [10.1016/j.envres.2026.125935](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.envres.2026.125935)
- **作者**: Xiaoyu Li, Mingxin Yuan, Peng Yan, Qi Yang, Ying Shang
- **来源**: crossref


## 摘要
Nitrogen supply in the rhizosphere affects cadmium (Cd) accumulation in rice. To study bacterial nitrogen transformation effects on Cd uptake in rice, Escherichia coli MG1655 overexpressing nrfA, which encodes cytochrome c nitrite reductase involved in dissimilatory nitrite reduction to ammonium (DNRA), was constructed using a Tn5 transposon system. The nrfA genomic copy number in the engineered strain was approximately 3-fold that in wild-type, and RNA-seq analysis confirmed significantly increased nrfA transcript. Transcriptomics showed enrichment of ribosomes, oxidative phosphorylation, and carbon metabolism pathways. In pot experiments, inoculation with the nrfA-overexpressing strain selectively enriched nitrogen-transformation and iron-cycling taxa, including Actinobacteria and Flavobacterium. Organic nitrogen metabolism gene abundances increased following strain inoculation, particularly in the nrfA-overexpressing treatment; in contrast, denitrification gene abundances decreased, with the greatest reduction in the nrfA-overexpressing treatment. Consistent with these results, soil ammonium nitrogen was 79.4% and 34.1% higher in the nrfA-overexpressing treatment than in uninoculated control and wild-type treatment, respectively. The nrfA-overexpressing strain also promoted iron plaque formation on rice root surfaces, enhancing root Cd interception Cd by >20% versus both other groups. Consequently, compared with uninoculated control, inoculation with nrfA-overexpressing E. coli significantly reduced Cd concentration in rice roots, stems, and grains by 36.2%, 61.0%, and 57.4%, respectively. Collectively, nrfA-overexpressing E. coli reshaped rhizosphere nitrogen cycling toward sustained ammonium nitrogen enrichment, potentially suppressing root Cd uptake through cation competition and strengthening root-surface Cd retention, achieving simultaneous nitrogen enrichment and Cd reduction in paddy soil. This study may offer theoretical support for microbial remediation of Cd-contaminated farmland.
