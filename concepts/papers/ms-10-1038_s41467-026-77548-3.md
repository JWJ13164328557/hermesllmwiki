### 核心发现
1. Here we report a spatiotemporally pro- grammed oral nanovaccine that implements a dual-site vaccination strategy through a sequential and causally linked immune cascade, initiating immune priming in Peyer’s patches of the small intestine and subsequently amplifying immunity at colorectal tumor sites.
2. Furthermore, we found that the NIR-mediated tumoricidal effect was distance-dependent: increasing the irradiation distance from direct exposure to 2 and 4 cm progressively reduced tumor cell killing (Supplementary Fig.
3. The nanovaccine (DPML@OVA-CpG), composed of antigen- and CpG-loaded mesoporous polydopamine nano- particles stabilized by β-glucan and plant-derived lipids, resists gastrointestinal degradation and targets gut-associated lymphoid tissues.
4. The GI stability of oral nanomedicines plays a pivotal role in determining their therapeutic efﬁcacy12.
5. To determine whether DPML@OVA-CpGs could effec- tively target the PPs, we fabricated a carboxymethyl cellulose-modiﬁed nanovaccine (CPML@OVA-CpG) as a control and compared its tar- geting performance with those of PML@OVA-CpGs and DPML@OVA- CpGs.
6. MLNs, as the primary draining lymph nodes of the intestinal immune system, play a central role in orchestrating gut-associated immune responses.
7. Together, these ﬁndings conﬁrm the ability of DPML@OVA-CpGs to target the key intestinal immune inductive sites and effectively initiate the ﬁrst site vaccination in the small intestine.
8. In the context of oral vaccination, the MLNs serve as the principal draining lymph nodes responsible for initiating anti-tumor immunity, whereas the spleen plays a central role in orchestrating systemic immune responses23.
