# Impact of multiple infections and cross-kingdom interactions on rice virus population structure: field and experimental insights from Burkina Faso.

- **期刊**: Philosophical transactions of the Royal Society of London. Series B, Biological sciences (2026)
- **PMID**: [43094418](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/43094418/)
- **DOI**: [10.1098/rstb.2025.0298](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.2025.0298)
- **第一作者**: Estelle Billard
- **作者**: Estelle Billard, Martine Bangratz, Abdoul Kader Guigma, Abalo Itolou Kassankogno, Julien Simonin, Emilie Thomas, Eugenie Hebrard, Gaël Thébaud

## 摘要
Multiple infections of hosts by various pathogen species or genotypes are common and may affect individual host health, yet their population-scale consequences remain relatively unexplored. Here, we investigate implications for pathogen evolution by testing whether the genetic structure of one pathogen is influenced by another in co-infection. We examined the evolution of an Africa-endemic rice virus of major agricultural importance, building on previous work documenting its genetic diversity and experimental evidence of interactions with a rice-infecting bacterium. We tested whether multiple infections, both within viral species (between viral genetic lineages) and between the two pathogens (virus and bacteria), impacted the structure of viral populations and fitness proxies, by combining field surveys with experimental work. We revealed high local viral diversity, documented multiple infections with distinct viral lineages in the field and detected various past recombination events between lineages. We found that the structure of viral genetic diversity is influenced not only by the location and year, but also by the presence of the bacteria in co-infections. Further, experimental infections show the contrasted performance of some viral lineages when evaluated in single versus multiple infections. Overall, these results suggest that multiple infections contribute to shape the genetic composition of viral populations. This article is part of the theme issue 'Ecological and evolutionary interactions between parasites across scales'.
