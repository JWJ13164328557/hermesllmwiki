## 摘要
Understanding and predicting complex traits in plants remains a fundamental challenge due to the emergent nature of most phenotypes and their dependence on genetic, regulatory, and environmental interactions. Accurate prediction of traits and identification of underlying genetic elements have broad applications for plant breeding, systems biology, and biotechnology. Here, we tested if multi-omic datasets could improve predictive accuracy of 129 diverse maize phenotypes across 9 environments using genomic markers, field-based transcriptomic data from 2 locations, and drone-derived phenomic data of vegetative indices. Analysis of model feature weights further indicated that predictive signal is distributed across many genes, consistent with complex traits such as yield arising from coordinated, network-level processes rather than a small number of dominant loci. These findings demonstrate that integrating transcriptomic and phenomic data with genotypes enhances trait prediction, improves model generalizability across environments, and provides deeper insight into the genetic and regulatory architecture of agriculturally important traits in maize.

### 核心发现
1. Our results show that combining these data types improves prediction accuracy for most traits and that the additional omics inputs provide complementary, nonredundant information.
2. For every phenotype, we found that transcriptomic features were significantly more likely to appear in the top 5% of weighted predictors than genomic markers ( ).
3. By analyzing feature importance, we found that gene expression features dominated multi-omics models, with transcripts consistently overrepresented among the top predictors compared to genetic markers.
4. Our results suggest that while multi-omics integration is beneficial, defining optimal strategies will require substantially more data across environments, tissues, and developmental timepoints.
5. These findings suggest that integrating biologically rich datasets across environments can help explore the basis of complex trait variation, enhance predictive modeling for crop improvement, and uncover additional loci for targeted breeding or functional analysis.
6. More broadly, these results suggest that improving predictive accuracy may depend more on incorporating larger-scale datasets or more biologically informative data layers than on increasing model complexity.
7. While individual genes with high feature weights highlight biologically interesting pathways associated with trait variation and provide targets for follow-up work, they represent only a small fraction of the predictive signal captured by the models.
8. Importantly, while these individual genes provide biologically meaningful insights into pathways associated with yield variation, predictive performance is not driven by single genes.
