# Mining rare haplotypes to accelerate precision breeding in rice.

## 摘要
Rice (Oryza sativa L.) genomics has made significant progress, with over 13000 genome sequences available and more than 4500 genes functionally characterized. However, limited haplotype-level information has constrained the effective deployment of these genes in breeding programs. Here, we investigated haplotype variation of 1644 functionally characterized genes representing six major trait categories using the 3000 Rice Genome panel. High haplotypic diversity was observed, with more than 300 haplotypes, which clustered into 2-15 major haplogroups. Several haplotypes showed subpopulation or region-specific distributions, whereas others were broadly distributed across the panel. To identify rare haplotypes (frequency < 0.1) with potential breeding value, we prioritized 180 genes relevant to defined product concepts and identified 210 rare haplotypes across 51 genes. Notably, a rare haplotype of DEP1 (DEP1-H2: frequency 0.07) associated with increased panicle length was introgressed into the mega rice variety Swarna through haplotype-based backcross breeding. The resulting introgression lines showed a 29%-34% increase in panicle length compared to the recurrent parent, confirming the functional advantage of the rare haplotype.

Rice contains thousands of genes, and variation within these genes shapes key agronomic traits such as yield, grain quality, and stress tolerance. In this study, we analyzed 1644 functionally characterized genes in the 3K Rice Genomes panel to understand how gene variants, known as haplotypes, are distributed and how they affect trait performance. We identified 210 rare haplotypes present in only a few rice accessions, some of which showed potential agronomic advantages. To evaluate their breeding potential, a rare haplotype of the DEP1 gene (DEP1‐H2) was introgressed into a widely grown variety, Swarna. The resulting lines showed a 29%–34% increase in panicle length compared with Swarna. These results demonstrate that rare allelic variants can provide measurable benefits and can be effectively used in breeding programs.
