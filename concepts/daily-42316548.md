Common wheat (Triticum aestivum) is a staple crop grown around the world and accounts for approximately half of the world's caloric intake (Erenstein et al.2022). However, the success of common wheat comes at a cost, which is largely associated with intensive agricultural practices. Intermediate wheatgrass (IWG) has been receiving increased attention as a perennial complement to common wheat. Much of this increased attention is due to the recent success of perennial rice which has seen an increase in yield similar to that of its common counterpart and an increase in consumer demand for more nutritious and functional foods (Zhang et al.2023; Canadian Foundation for Dietetic Research2025). Therefore, it is essential that perennial grains, such as IWG, be utilized not only to improve the nutritional value of common wheat products but also to contribute to a more regenerative agricultural system. The potential to continually improve common wheat is present; however, there is an urgency to fortify common wheat flour to diversify food systems. In the face of our changing climate, wheat is seeing decreases in yield, variation in protein content, and suboptimal plant health in years of drought and high temperatures (Hellemans et al.2018; Jaldhani et al.2021).
