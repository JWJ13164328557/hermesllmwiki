# A yield-reducing introgression linked to sd1 in rice.

## 摘要
The semi-dwarf (sd1) allele was central to the Green Revolution, but its widespread use in US tropical japonica rice (Oryza sativa L.) may have introduced unintended effects through linkage drag. This study examined the genomic region surrounding sd1 using genome-wide association and haplotype analyses in a multiparent population (MP6/8) evaluated across environments and in a biparental population (MP-D) for validation. Genomic and phenotypic evidence indicated that an indica-derived haplotype upstream of sd1 persists in US tropical japonica and global indica semi-dwarf germplasm and is associated with reduced grain yield. Genome-wide association analysis confirmed sd1 as the primary determinant of reduced plant height and identified a quantitative trait locus at 36.69 Mb upstream of sd1 that was consistently associated with reduced grain yield (0.6-0.9 t ha

The semi‐dwarf gene, sd1, played a pivotal role in the Green Revolution; however, its incorporation into US tropical japonica rice (Oryza sativa L.) also introduced linked genomic regions from traditional indica donor varieties. We identified an unfavorable genomic segment upstream of the semi‐dwarf gene that is consistently associated with reduced grain yield across populations and environments. This segment originated from the Taichung Native 1 lineage and persists in some modern US germplasm and is widespread in global indica germplasm. These findings demonstrate that linkage drag introduced during early breeding can have long‐lasting effects, while providing breeders with clear genomic targets to retain the benefits of semi‐dwarfism while eliminating associated yield penalties. By identifying this hidden yield penalty, breeders can remove unfavorable linked DNA while retaining the height‐reducing allele that underpins modern rice germplasm.
