Global wheat production underpins the food security of approximately 35% of the world's population, yet it faces escalating threats from climate change‐driven extremes. While individual heat and drought stresses have been extensively characterized, their simultaneous occurrence during critical developmental windows—particularly anthesis and early grain filling—presents a far more complex and devastating scenario, triggering irreversible floret abortion, pollen sterility and catastrophic yield losses (Li et al.2025; Prasad et al.2011). Understanding the genetic and physiological architecture of tolerance to combined heat‐drought (HD) stress is therefore an urgent imperative for sustaining wheat productivity in an increasingly volatile climate. Despite decades of research, the genetic dissection of combined HD tolerance has lagged considerably, reflecting both the practical challenges of imposing reproducible combined stress treatments and the intricate coordination of tolerance mechanisms spanning photosynthetic maintenance, osmotic adjustment and carbon remobilization (Kumar et al.2022; Mahrookashani et al.2017). The ‘stay‐green’ phenotype—maintained chlorophyll content and photosynthetic competence under stress—has emerged as a promising proxy for stress tolerance, yet its genetic basis and mechanistic relationship to yield stability under combined HD stress remain incompletely understood (Christopher et al.2016). Moreover, identifying stable quantitative trait loci (QTLs) that confer tolerance across diverse environments remains a critical bottleneck in translating physiological insights into actionable breeding targets. J. Zhang et al. (2026) address these challenges through a comprehensive investigation integrating detailed phenotyping, physiological profiling and genome‐wide association mapping in a diverse panel of 345 wheat genotypes. Their study stands out for its rigorous experimental design—encompassing controlled glasshouse HD stress, a late‐sowing field trial and a non‐stress control—enabling the identification of environment‐stable marker‐trait associations (MTAs). Equally importantly, the authors probe deeply into physiological determinants of tolerance, measuring chlorophyll dynamics, photosynthetic electron transport, leaf architecture and detailed yield components. The result is a rich dataset providing both immediate breeding resources and fundamental insights into the genetic architecture of HD tolerance. The diverse germplasm panel of 345 genotypes provides ample statistical power and allelic richness for robust GWAS. The combined use of three complementary models (FarmCPU, GLM and MLM) with a stringent cross‐validation criterion effectively minimizes false‐positive associations.
