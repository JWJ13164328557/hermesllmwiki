Seed coat color and pattern are important traits in common bean because they define market classes, influence consumer preference, and determine crop value. These traits are controlled by multiple major genes that interact in complex ways, making it challenging for breeders to maintain the desired seed coat appearance when introducing new traits. In this study, we developed and tested a set of DNA‐based markers to help breeders identify and track key genes controlling seed color and pattern. Markers were created for nine major genes, and an existing marker for the slow‐darkening trait was validated. We also developed a marker to distinguish the two major genetic groups of common bean, Middle American and Andean. Testing across diverse bean collections showed that most markers were highly reliable, although some genes required multiple markers to capture their variation. Together, these tools help breeders to develop improved bean varieties while preserving market‐class quality.
