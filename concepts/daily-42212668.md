Pathogenesis‐related 1 (PR1) proteins contribute to plant immunity through antimicrobial activity and the regulation of target protein stability and condensation. In wheat,TaPR1‐2has been widely used as a molecular marker of immune responses to rust fungi. However, it remains unclear whether other members of the PR1 family are involved in wheat resistance to leaf rust. Here, we show that the transcript levels ofTaPR1‐11are induced following inoculation withPuccinia triticina(Pt). Overexpression ofTaPR1‐11significantly enhanced wheat resistance toPt, accompanied by reduced fungal growth, whereasTaPR1‐11‐RNAi wheat lines displayed increased susceptibility. TaPR1‐11 physically interacts with TaPR4‐1, a Barwin‐like protein that also positively regulates wheat resistance toPt. Further analysis revealed that TaPR1‐11 stabilizes TaPR4‐1 in vivo, while TaPR4‐1 reciprocally promotes TaPR1‐11 protein accumulation duringPtinfection. Collectively, our findings identify a previously unreported PR1‐PR4 protein partnership in wheat, showing that TaPR1‐11 stabilizes TaPR4‐1 to enhance resistance againstPt. This work expands current understanding of the molecular mechanisms by which PR1 proteins contribute to wheat defence responses. Through long‐term co‐evolution with pathogenic microorganisms, plants have developed a sophisticated immune system in which pathogenesis‐related (PR) proteins play a vital role. PR proteins are rapidly induced and accumulate in plants in response to pathogen infection or abiotic stress, contributing to hypersensitive response (HR) and systemic acquired resistance (SAR) (Durrant and Dong2004; Han and Schneiter2024). As core components of the plant immune system, PR proteins exhibit diverse enzymatic and antimicrobial activities, including chitinase, peroxidase, hydrolase, protease inhibitor and antimicrobial peptide activities (Zribi et al.2021). Known PR proteins can be grouped into 17 families (van Loon et al.2006; Sels et al.2008). While the specific roles of PR proteins vary across families and species, most function in defence signalling to enhance plant resistance to pathogens. PR1, one of the earliest identified PR protein families, belongs to the CAP (Cysteine‐rich secretory protein, Antigen 5, Pathogenesis‐related‐1) superfamily.
