Red sour soup (RSS) is a distinctive fermented condiment in China. Based on fermentation substrates, RSS can be divided into three mainstream types, including tomato based RSS (T-RSS), tomato-chili based RSS (M-RSS), and tomato-chili-rice acid based RSS (R-RSS). Difference in fermentation substrate is an important factor causing discrepancies in the physicochemical properties, flavor profiles, bioactive constituents and quality attributes of final RSS products. Existing studies have explored the quality characteristics of single types of RSS. In physicochemical properties, compared with chili based RSS, T-RSS exhibits higher acidity and amino acid nitrogen content (Lin et al., 2022), but lower reducing sugar content (Liu et al., 2025). According to Zheng (Zheng et al., 2024), sugar addition in T-RSS promotes the accumulation of lactic acid and enhances acidity. In flavor profile, substrate formulas significantly affect the flavor fingerprint of RSS. ## 摘要
Red sour soup (RSS) is a characteristic Chinese fermented food. This study analyzed tomato-based RSS (T-RSS), tomato-chili-based RSS (M-RSS), and tomato-chili-rice-acid based RSS (R-RSS) for sensory, physicochemical, bioactive, and safety properties. Among the samples, M-RSS exhibited significantly higher levels of polyphenol (161.89 mg GAE/100 g) and total flavonoid (150.85 mg RE/100 g DW). Capsaicinoids were exclusively found in M-RSS and R-RSS, with nordihydrocapsaicin showing the highest level at 1125.61 μg/g. In most samples, lycopene (0.125-3.560 μg/g) was the main carotenoid and lactic acid the primary organic acid (14-54%). Multivariate analysis revealed that capsorubin, zeaxanthin, cryptoxanthin, and lutein were significantly correlated with both RSS redness (a* value, p < 0.05) and specific organic acids, while capsaicin and dihydrocapsaicin were positively associated with polyphenols, vitamin C, tartaric/oxalic acids, and total flavonoids. This study characterized the quality profiles of RSS with different substrates, providing a reference for its quality evaluation and targeted functional improvement.
