Kuntze is a woody economic crop widely cultivated, commonly propagated by seed or cuttings. Cutting propagation is a preferred method due to its ability to produce clonal plants that retain advantageous genetic characteristics and it also enables rapid propagation and uniform traits, addressing issues in seed propagation such as self‐incompatibility and trait segregation (Wang et al.2022; Wei et al.2019). To date, most commercial tea cutting propagation is still performed in the field, since the cost of building and operating green factory facilities is comparatively high. However, young cuttings used for propagation are quite vulnerable to open‐field conditions, and delicate cultivation practices are required, such as low‐intensity illumination, high humidity, and clean, fertile, and healthy soil. Certain tea cultivars have low rooting abilities. Adventitious rooting is essential for achieving successful propagation by cuttings, while this process usually takes a couple of months and is very susceptible to the environment (Wei et al.2019). In some parts of China, despite the harsh weather, winter propagation is adopted due to lower labor costs and the availability of spare labor in the off‐season. ## 摘要
Development of adventitious roots is crucial for successful tea propagation, yet it is often inhibited by low temperatures in winter. To enhance tea rooting efficiency during winter, we investigated the effects of different shade treatments (different colors and shade levels) on the root development of Camellia sinensis cv. Longjing 43. Blue and red nets with 95% shade percentage achieved the highest rooting rates (> 95%) and longer roots, followed by silvery and black nets at 55% shading, while the 95% black net showed the poorest performance. The underlying mechanisms were investigated using transcriptome and phytohormone profiles through weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA) and KEGG analysis. The results showed blue net shading primarily affected zeatin metabolism, and red net shading promoted indole-3-acetic acid (IAA) metabolism, both of which regulated hormone signaling and hormone composition. Relevant key genes significantly correlated with zeatin and IAA levels were identified using Mantel tests. These findings provide a practical and cost-effective strategy to improve the winter rooting efficiency of tea cuttings under cold conditions by optimizing field growth environments.
