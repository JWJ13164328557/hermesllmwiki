A noncanonical laccase catalyzes the core skeleton formation in theMorus albabenzofuran natural product biosynthesis. The benzofuran core structure is commonly found in bioactive natural products and pharmaceuticals (1–3). To date, more than 30 benzofurans or their derivatives have been approved by the US Food and Drug Administration as drugs, including fruquintinib, vilazodone, and benzbromarone (Fig. 1A) (2,4). Therefore, developing novel chemical catalysts and strategies for benzofuran synthesis has received considerable attention (1,5). Meanwhile, bioactive benzofuran–type natural products are also produced by nature, mainly found in higher plants such as Asteraceae (6), Fabaceae (7) and Moraceae plants (8). Despite the fact that advances have been made in furanocoumarins biosynthesis (9–11), biosynthetic pathways of other benzofuran-type natural products in plants remain largely unexplored, hampering their efficient synthesis via metabolic engineering strategy.
