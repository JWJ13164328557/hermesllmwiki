Limited evidence exists on the prevalence and determinants of concurrent secondhand smoke (SHS) and thirdhand smoke (THS) exposure in Chinese households, particularly in Beijing, where highly urbanized districts and urban-rural transition zones coexist. Among the 1,839 surveyed households, 41.3% of preschool-aged children (3–6 years) were exposed to THS, with 21.6% experiencing combined SHS-THS exposure and 19.7% exposed to THS alone. Lower parental awareness of tobacco hazards significantly increased the likelihood of combined exposure. While partial indoor smoking bans were associated with a reduced likelihood of SHS exposure, only comprehensive indoor smoking bans effectively mitigated THS exposure. Children living in completely smoke-free homes were 3.3 times more likely to be entirely free from tobacco smoke exposure than those living in homes lacking any restrictions. Tobacco control initiatives targeting families of preschoolers should include THS prevention and emphasize the limitations of partial indoor smoking bans. Public health initiatives should prioritize full smoke-free homes to eliminate children's household tobacco exposure. Targeted, culturally tailored interventions are required for high-risk groups like multigenerational households to translate hazard awareness into consistent protective behaviors. Keywords:Tobacco Smoke Pollution, Thirdhand Smoke, Preschool-aged Children, Beijing, Influencing Factors Introduction:Preschool-aged children (3–6 years old) are particularly vulnerable to household tobacco smoke exposure. This study investigated the prevalence of secondhand smoke (SHS) and thirdhand smoke (THS) exposure among preschoolers in Beijing and identified the associated socioeconomic and behavioral risk factors, including residential area, parental education level, and household structure, to guide targeted prevention and control strategies. Methods:We conducted a cross-sectional survey among 1,839 parents in 2023 using stratified random cluster sampling across 20 kindergartens in Beijing, China. Structured questionnaires were administered to collect data on demographics, tobacco-related knowledge, and household smoking restrictions. Results:The overall prevalence of THS exposure among preschoolers was 41.3% (759/1,839), comprising 21.6% combined SHS-THS exposure and 19.7% isolated THS exposure. Multinomial logistic regression adopted isolated THS exposure as the reference group.
