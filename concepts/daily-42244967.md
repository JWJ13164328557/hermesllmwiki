Secondhand smoke (SHS) exposure remains a significant public health concern among college students, even where smoke-free campus policies are in place. This report documented SHS exposure across five campus settings among Chinese college students in 2025, revealing substantial variation in prevalence by setting. School-level factors — particularly the comprehensiveness of smoke-free policies and teachers' smoking behavior on campus — were more strongly associated with student exposure than the broader tobacco-control environment. Colleges should strengthen comprehensive smoke-free policy implementation, as has been achieved in primary and secondary schools, to reduce student SHS exposure. Keywords:Secondhand Smoke Exposure, Smoke-Free Policy Implementation, College Students, Campus Settings Introduction:Secondhand smoke (SHS) exposure remains a major public health concern among college students in China. This study characterized SHS exposure across five campus settings among Chinese college students and examined whether campus-level indicators of policy implementation were associated with setting-specific exposure. Methods:Using data from the 2025 National Survey on Tobacco Use among Chinese College Students, this study estimated the prevalence of SHS exposure with 95% confidence intervals (CIs) across five campus settings. Multilevel multivariable logistic regression models assessed associations of school smoking restrictions, observed teacher smoking on campus, and provincial adult SHS exposure with setting-specific SHS exposure. Results:In 2025, the prevalence of SHS exposure was highest in dormitory buildings (31.5%), followed by teaching buildings (26.8%) and playgrounds (25.8%), and lowest in administrative buildings (13.3%) and canteens (10.3%). Students who reported partial, absent, or unclear school smoking restrictions, as well as those who observed teachers smoking on campus, had higher SHS exposure across all settings. Provincial adult SHS exposure showed no significant association with setting-specific SHS exposure. Conclusions:Despite progress in establishing smoke-free primary and secondary schools, SHS exposure remains prevalent across college campus settings. Comprehensive smoke-free policies, enhanced teacher management, and routine enforcement may help reduce SHS exposure in college settings. College students occupy a critical transitional stage between adolescence and adulthood during which health behaviors and social norms take shape. Campuses — encompassing academic, residential, and recreational environments — represent key settings for tobacco control. Although smoke-free campus policies have been widely promoted across China and substantial progress has been achieved in smoke-free primary and secondary schools (1–2), evidence on secondhand smoke (SHS) exposure across specific campus settings among Chinese college students remains limited (3–4).
