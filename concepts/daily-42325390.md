This study investigated the effects of 0.5% Artemisia sphaerocephala Krasch gum (ASKG) on precooked alkaline noodles during refrigeration and reheating. Results showed ASKG significantly reduced recooking loss, suppressed hardness increases by 2.77 N, and boosted tensile strength by approximately 30% after 4-day storage. In recent years, the growing demand for convenience foods has stimulated the development of precooked noodles, which can be stored under refrigerated conditions and reheated before consumption (Joyner et al., 2016). Such products meet the demand for convenience while maintaining the sensory qualities of fresh noodles. However, the cold-chain process frequently leads to textural deterioration, including increased hardness, loss of elasticity, and surface stickiness, largely due to starch retrogradation and gluten network weakening (Gong et al., 2019;Liu et al., 2025). These effects are mainly associated with starch retrogradation, gluten network weakening, and water redistribution within the noodle matrix, posing major challenges to the industrial production and quality control of refrigerated noodle products (Liu et al., 2023). Precooked alkaline noodles represent an important category within Asian wheat-based foods. Products such as Wuhan hot-dry noodles and Japanese ramen are recognized for their strong hardness, elasticity, and characteristic yellow color. These attributes are closely linked to the addition of alkaline salts – mainly sodium (Na2CO3) and potassium (K2CO3) carbonates (Tao et al., 2019).
