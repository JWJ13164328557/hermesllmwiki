# Integrated analysis of multimodal single-cell data

---

## 1. Scientific Context

### Existing Consensus (Before This Paper)

By 2021, single-cell genomics had matured considerably with multiple modalities becoming experimentally accessible — scRNA-seq, scATAC-seq, CITE-seq, and others — but integration methods were predominantly **unimodal**. Key prior consensus:

- **Seurat v3** ([[xr-10-1016-j-cell-2019-05-031|Stuart 2019]]) had established anchor-based integration for scRNA-seq data, enabling cross-dataset alignment and label transfer via CCA-based mutual nearest neighbors (MNN)
- **CITE-seq** ([[cr-s13059-014-0550-8|Stoeckius 2017]]) enabled simultaneous measurement of transcriptome and surface protein epitopes by combining scRNA-seq with antibody-derived tags (ADTs), but analysis was limited to independent processing of each modality
- **Multimodal data analysis** was largely ad hoc: researchers either analyzed modalities separately and compared results, or concatenated features from all modalities into one matrix, ignoring differences in data type, noise structure, and information content
- **TotalVI** (Gayoso 2021, scvi-tools) had introduced a probabilistic model for joint CITE-seq analysis using variational autoencoders, but lacked a unified clustering framework and did not learn cell-specific modality weights
- **MOFA+** (Argelaguet 2020) used factor analysis for multi-omics integration but was designed for bulk assays, scaling poorly to hundreds of thousands of single cells
- No principled framework existed for defining **cellular states based on multimodal data** where each modality contributes differently to each cell's identity

### Existing Models

- **Separate analysis**: Each modality processed independently (PCA/UMAP on RNA, CLR-normalized PCA on ADTs), then results compared side by side — loses cross-modal relationships
- **Feature concatenation**: RNA and ADT features merged into one matrix for joint PCA — treats modalities equally, ignores different noise characteristics, and RNA's higher dimensionality (~20,000 genes) dominates over ADTs (~200 proteins)
- **TotalVI**: Probabilistic VAE jointly modeling RNA counts and ADT counts — learns shared latent space but uses equal weighting across modalities
- **MOFA+**: Multi-omics factor analysis — flexible but not optimized for single-cell scale or CITE-seq specifically

### Knowledge Gaps

- No method to **learn cell-specific modality weights** — in some cells, protein data may be more informative than mRNA (e.g., surface markers for T-cell subsets); in others, RNA may be more discriminating
- No framework for **unsupervised multimodal clustering** that uses information from all modalities to define cell states
- No **multimodal reference atlas** of the human immune system against which new data could be mapped
- No principled approach to interpret **immune responses** (vaccination, COVID-19) using multimodal single-cell data
- No method to identify **cell states uniquely visible in multimodal space** — subpopulations that are invisible in any single modality alone

### Why This Paper Matters

This paper introduced **Weighted-Nearest Neighbor (WNN)** analysis in Seurat v4, the first unsupervised framework that learns the relative information content of each modality on a per-cell basis, then integrates modalities through a weighted combination of modality-specific nearest-neighbor graphs. It produced a multimodal CITE-seq reference atlas of 211,000 PBMCs with 228 antibodies and demonstrated clinical utility by interpreting immune responses to COVID-19 vaccination. WNN became the de facto standard for multimodal single-cell analysis, fundamentally changing how the field defines cellular identity — from unimodal transcriptional states to **multimodal cellular states**.

## 2. Research Questions

### Primary Question

Can an unsupervised algorithm learn the relative utility of each data modality on a per-cell basis, enabling integrated multimodal analysis that defines cellular states more precisely than any single modality alone?

### Secondary Questions

1. Does WNN analysis on CITE-seq PBMCs (RNA + 228 surface proteins) resolve cell states that are invisible in RNA-only or protein-only analysis?
2. Can WNN identify and validate previously unreported lymphoid subpopulations through multimodal definition?
3. Can a multimodal PBMC reference atlas (211K cells) serve as a scaffold for mapping new datasets and interpreting immune perturbations?
4. Does WNN analysis of COVID-19 vaccine response reveal coordinated transcriptional and protein-level immune dynamics?
5. How does WNN compare to existing methods (separate analysis, MOFA+, TotalVI) for multimodal clustering and visualization?

### Explicit Hypotheses

- Different cells will have different modality weights — surface proteins will be more informative for defining immune cell lineages (T cells, B cells, monocytes) with well-established surface markers, while RNA will dominate for finer transcriptional states within lineages
- A weighted combination of modality-specific k-nearest neighbor graphs will produce a joint representation that captures the best of each modality
- Cells of the same biological type will have similar modality-weight profiles, providing an internal validation of the WNN approach

### Implicit Hypotheses

- The information content of each modality varies systematically across cell populations and can be estimated from within-modality affinity structures
- Multimodal integration will reveal new biology — cell states defined by the intersection of transcriptional and proteomic features that are invisible to either modality alone
- A multimodal reference atlas will generalize beyond the training data, enabling rapid annotation and interpretation of new single-cell experiments
- The immune system's response to vaccination involves coordinated changes across transcriptome and surface proteome that WNN will capture holistically

```
    ↓

    ↓

             identifies novel lymphoid subpopulations not visible in unimodal analysis;
             validates with independent flow cytometry
    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

    ↓

             multiple immune cell types after vaccination; reveals vaccine-responsive
             cell states missed by RNA-only analysis
    ↓
Conclusion: WNN is a general unsupervised framework for multimodal single-cell analysis
            that defines cellular states more comprehensively than unimodal approaches
            and enables clinical interpretation of immune perturbations
```

### Why WNN?

- **Cell-specific weighting**: Unlike global concatenation or equal-weight VAE approaches, WNN acknowledges that cell identity is defined by different modalities in different contexts — surface proteins are highly informative for major immune lineages, RNA for subtle activation states
- **Graph-based fusion**: kNN graphs naturally capture local neighborhood structure; fusing graphs from different modalities via weighted averaging preserves modality-specific topology while creating a unified manifold
- **Unsupervised weight learning**: Modality weights are learned from within-modality prediction accuracy (how well does the RNA graph predict protein neighbors, and vice versa?) — no labeled data required
- **Seurat ecosystem integration**: Built into Seurat, the most widely used single-cell toolkit, ensuring immediate community adoption

### Why Three Demonstrations?

- **CITE-seq atlas (211K PBMCs)**: Establishes the method's core capability — resolving cell states from multimodal data at scale; the atlas itself is a community resource
- **Reference mapping**: Demonstrates that WNN-defined multimodal states generalize — the atlas can annotate and interpret new experiments, a critical use case
- **COVID-19 vaccine response**: Demonstrates translational utility — WNN analysis not just a clustering method but a framework for interpreting complex immune perturbations with clinical relevance

## 4. Method Analysis

### Core Algorithm: Weighted-Nearest Neighbor (WNN) Multimodal Integration (Seurat v4)

**Problem formulation**: Given a single-cell dataset with $M$ modalities measured in the same cells, learn cell-specific modality weights $\{w_{i,1}, w_{i,2}, ..., w_{i,M}\}$ for each cell $i$, and construct an integrated k-nearest neighbor graph that optimally combines information across modalities for downstream clustering, visualization, and analysis.

**Algorithm overview**:

#### Step 1: Modality-Specific Dimensionality Reduction

For each modality $m$, preprocess data using modality-appropriate normalization:
- **RNA**: SCTransform (regularized negative binomial regression) → PCA on top 3,000 variable genes
- **ADT (proteins)**: Centered log-ratio (CLR) normalization → PCA on all antibodies

This produces modality-specific low-dimensional embeddings $Z_m$ for each cell.

#### Step 2: Modality-Specific k-Nearest Neighbor Graphs

For each modality $m$, construct a kNN graph $G_m$ where edges connect each cell to its $k$ nearest neighbors in modality $m$'s PCA space. Edge weights use the Jaccard similarity of shared nearest neighbors (SNN).

- **Innovation**: Building separate graphs preserves the unique neighborhood structure of each modality — RNA neighbors may differ from protein neighbors, and these differences contain information about modality informativeness.

#### Step 3: Learn Cell-Specific Modality Weights

For each cell $i$ and each modality $m$, compute a **within-modality prediction accuracy**: how well does the RNA-based graph predict the cell's protein neighborhood, and vice versa?

Specifically, WNN computes the **cross-modality prediction ratio**:
- For each cell $i$, identify its nearest neighbors in modality $m$ ($NN_m(i)$)
- Count how many of those neighbors are also among $i$'s nearest neighbors in the other modality — this measures how much the modalities agree on $i$'s local neighborhood
- Cells where both modalities agree on neighbors receive balanced weights; cells where one modality provides more coherent local structure receive asymmetric weights favoring the more reliable modality

The weights are normalized per cell: $\sum_m w_{i,m} = 1$.

- **Innovation**: This is the key intellectual contribution — learning modality weights from the data itself, without supervision. The approach is based on the intuition that if a cell's protein neighbors and RNA neighbors are similar, both modalities are equally informative; if they diverge, the modality with more internally consistent neighborhood structure (higher within-modality prediction accuracy) is weighted more heavily.

#### Step 4: Construct Weighted-Nearest Neighbor Graph

Construct the integrated WNN graph $G_{WNN}$ where the edge weight between cells $i$ and $j$ is:

$$W_{ij} = \sum_{m=1}^{M} w_{i,m} \cdot A_{ij}^{(m)}$$

where $A^{(m)}$ is the adjacency matrix of modality $m$'s kNN graph (after Jaccard-based SNN weighting).

- **Innovation**: A simple weighted average, but the per-cell weights make it powerful — each cell's neighborhood is informed primarily by the modality that best defines its identity.

#### Step 5: Joint Clustering and Visualization

Use the WNN graph for:
- **Louvain/Leiden clustering**: Identify multimodal cell states
- **UMAP/t-SNE visualization**: Project cells into 2D using the WNN graph as the input affinity matrix
- **Differential analysis**: Within each WNN-defined cluster, identify differentially expressed genes and differentially abundant proteins

#### Step 6: Supervised Principal Component Analysis (SPCA) for Reference Mapping

For reference-based analysis, WNN uses **supervised PCA** — a dimensionality reduction that identifies axes of variation that best distinguish the WNN-defined clusters in the reference. New query datasets are projected into this supervised PCA space, then anchor-based label transfer (from Seurat v3, [[xr-10-1016-j-cell-2019-05-031|Stuart 2019]]) maps reference annotations to query cells.

- **Innovation**: SPCA ensures that the reference projection space captures the multimodal cluster structure, making mapping more accurate than projecting onto unsupervised PCA.

### Key Algorithmic Contributions

| Innovation | Significance |
|---|---|
| **Cell-specific modality weights** | Core conceptual advance — different cells rely on different modalities for identity definition |
| **Unsupervised weight learning** | Weights learned from cross-modality prediction accuracy; no labeled data needed |
| **Graph-based fusion** | Preserves modality-specific topology while creating unified multimodal manifold |
| **WNN-weighted UMAP** | Enables multimodal visualization that reflects the integrated cellular landscape |
| **Supervised PCA for reference mapping** | Optimizes projection space for cluster discrimination, improving mapping accuracy |
| **Modality-appropriate preprocessing** | SCTransform for RNA, CLR for ADTs — acknowledges different data distributions |

### Comparison with Contemporary Methods

| Aspect | WNN (Seurat v4) | TotalVI (scvi-tools) | MOFA+ | Feature Concatenation |
|--------|----------------|----------------------|-------|----------------------|
| **Approach** | Graph fusion | Probabilistic VAE | Factor analysis | Matrix merge |
| **Cell-specific weights** | Yes, unsupervised | No (uniform latent space) | No (uniform factors) | No |
| **Modality-appropriate processing** | Yes (SCTransform + CLR) | Yes (negative binomial + log-normal) | Partially | No |
| **Scalability** | 200K+ cells | 100K+ cells | <50K cells | 200K+ cells |
| **Reference mapping** | Yes (SPCA + anchors) | No | No | Possible |
| **Uncertainty quantification** | No | Yes (posterior) | Yes (factor variances) | No |
| **Adoption** | Very high (Seurat ecosystem) | High (scvi-tools) | Moderate | Common (ad hoc) |

### Limitations of the Algorithm

- **Requires matched multimodal data**: All modalities must be measured in the same cells — cannot handle unpaired multi-omics (separate cells for RNA and ATAC)
- **Heuristic weight learning**: The cross-modality prediction ratio, while intuitive, lacks formal statistical grounding
- **kNN parameter sensitivity**: The number of neighbors $k$ affects both within-modality prediction accuracy and the final WNN graph; no automated selection
- **Linear preprocessing**: PCA-based dimensionality reduction assumes linear structure in each modality's feature space
- **SCTransform assumptions**: Relies on regularized negative binomial regression for RNA normalization; may not be optimal for all technologies
- **Two-modality focus**: Demonstrated primarily for CITE-seq (RNA + proteins); extension to >2 modalities requires additional validation
- **No cross-modality interaction modeling**: WNN fuses graphs from separate modalities but does not model interactions between RNA and protein features directly

## 7. Critical Evaluation

### Strengths

- **Conceptual breakthrough**: The idea that different cells rely on different modalities for identity — and that this can be learned unsupervised — is genuinely original and changed how the field thinks about cellular states
- **Immediate practical impact**: Built into Seurat v4, WNN has been adopted by thousands of labs for CITE-seq, ASAP-seq, and other multimodal analyses
- **Multimodal reference atlas as a community resource**: The 211K-cell PBMC reference with 228 antibodies is a landmark dataset, used extensively for annotation and benchmarking
- **Clinically relevant demonstration**: COVID-19 vaccine response analysis shows WNN's utility beyond method development — it can generate biological and translational insights
- **Principled preprocessing**: SCTransform for RNA and CLR for proteins acknowledges the fundamentally different data distributions of each modality
- **Validation of novel findings**: Previously unreported lymphoid subpopulations were validated by independent flow cytometry, confirming that WNN does not simply overfit to data structure
- **Integration with Seurat ecosystem**: WNN works seamlessly with Seurat's existing functionality (clustering, DE, visualization, label transfer), lowering the barrier to adoption
- **Reference mapping generalizability**: The supervised PCA framework enables rapid multimodal annotation of new experiments without re-running the full WNN pipeline

### Weaknesses

- **Heuristic weight learning**: The cross-modality prediction ratio is intuitive but lacks formal statistical justification; no confidence intervals or significance tests for modality weights
- **Restricted to matched multimodal data**: WNN requires all modalities measured in the same cells — cannot integrate unpaired multi-omics (e.g., scRNA-seq from one batch + scATAC-seq from another), which is a common experimental design
- **Two-modality demonstration**: While the framework generalizes to $M$ modalities, all demonstrations are for $M=2$ (RNA + proteins); performance with 3+ modalities (e.g., RNA + ATAC + proteins) is not validated
- **No cross-modality interaction modeling**: WNN fuses independent modality graphs but does not model how RNA and protein features interact — e.g., cell-surface protein abundance regulating transcription factor activity
- **SCTransform black-box**: The regularized negative binomial regression in SCTransform is complex and its assumptions may not hold for all RNA-seq protocols
- **Computational memory**: Building and storing multiple kNN graphs (one per modality) plus the WNN graph may be memory-intensive for very large datasets
- **Limited comparison to deep learning methods**: Benchmarking against TotalVI and MOFA+ is present but not comprehensive; no systematic comparison across diverse datasets and metrics

### Missing Controls

- No systematic analysis of how WNN behaves when one modality is substantially lower quality (e.g., low-plex protein panels or shallow RNA sequencing)
- No demonstration of WNN with non-CITE-seq multimodal data (e.g., ASAP-seq, SHARE-seq, 10x Multiome)
- No failure mode analysis — when does WNN degrade to unimodal performance?
- No quantitative metric for "multimodal improvement" — how much better is the WNN clustering compared to unimodal, beyond visual inspection?
- No assessment of WNN's sensitivity to the number of antibodies (228 is a large panel; does WNN work with 10-20 antibodies?)
- No negative control showing that WNN fails to identify biologically meaningful clusters in random or shuffled data

## 8. Research Insight

### What Changed After This Paper

- **Multimodal became the new standard**: Before WNN, CITE-seq data was typically analyzed by processing RNA and ADTs separately. After WNN, integrated multimodal analysis became the default expectation
- **Cellular identity was redefined**: The concept that cell states should be defined by multiple modalities rather than RNA alone shifted the field's ontology — a "T cell" is now understood through its transcriptome, surface proteome, chromatin accessibility, and more
- **Multimodal reference atlases proliferated**: The PBMC CITE-seq atlas inspired similar efforts for other tissues (bone marrow, tumor microenvironments, lymphoid organs) and other multimodal technologies (ASAP-seq, TEA-seq, 10x Multiome)
- **Seurat v4 became the defacto multimodal toolkit**: WNN solidified Seurat's position as the go-to framework for single-cell analysis, now covering spatial (v1 → v3), integration (v2 → v3), and multimodal (v4)
- **Vaccine immunology embraced single-cell**: The COVID-19 vaccine demonstration catalyzed widespread adoption of single-cell multimodal analysis in vaccine studies, including for influenza, HIV, and cancer immunotherapy
- **Modality weighting as a design principle**: The idea of learning cell-specific feature importance influenced subsequent methods — totalVI extended with cell-specific scaling, MultiVI (scvi-tools) for paired+unpaired multi-omics

### How It Connects to Current Knowledge

- **Anchored in Seurat v3**: WNN directly extends the anchor-based integration framework ([[xr-10-1016-j-cell-2019-05-031|Stuart 2019]]) from cross-dataset to cross-modality integration, using the same MNN anchoring concept but within-cell rather than across-dataset
- **Complementary to deep learning methods**: WNN (graph-based, heuristic) and TotalVI/MultiVI (VAE, probabilistic) represent complementary approaches to multimodal integration — WNN is more interpretable (cell-specific weights are directly visualizable), VAE methods provide uncertainty quantification
- **Precursor to "bridge integration"**: WNN's reference mapping via supervised PCA anticipated Seurat v5's "bridge integration" framework, which maps query data to references across modalities using dictionary learning
- **Connected to spatial multimodal**: The WNN framework has been adapted for spatial multimodal data (e.g., [[xr-10-1038-s41587-022-01474-0|spatial CITE-seq]], spatial ATAC-RNA), integrating imaging features with molecular modalities
- **Atlas-scale multimodal analysis**: The PBMC atlas established the reference-based analysis paradigm now standard in the Human Cell Atlas and HuBMAP, where multimodal references define canonical cell states

## 9. Future Research Opportunities

- **Multi-modality ($M>2$) WNN**: Systematic evaluation and optimization of WNN for 3+ modalities — RNA + ATAC + proteins + methylation — with rigorous weight-learning across all modalities simultaneously rather than pairwise
- **Nonlinear modality fusion**: Replace PCA-based dimensionality reduction with autoencoder-based latent spaces per modality, enabling WNN to capture nonlinear modality structures
- **Probabilistic WNN**: Extend the framework to output posterior distributions over modality weights and cluster assignments, enabling uncertainty-aware downstream analysis (e.g., differential testing that accounts for cluster assignment uncertainty)
- **Cross-modality interaction modeling**: Extend WNN beyond graph fusion to explicitly model interactions between modalities — e.g., a surface protein's expression pattern regulating its own gene's transcription — using graphical models or neural networks
- **Unpaired multimodal WNN**: Develop an extension that can integrate modalities measured in different cells (e.g., scRNA-seq from one batch + scATAC-seq from another), bridging the gap between matched and unpaired integration
- **Automated modality weight interpretation**: Develop statistical tests and visualization tools that formally identify which biological processes drive high vs. low modality weights in specific cell populations
- **Dynamic WNN for temporal data**: Extend WNN to time-series multimodal data, learning how modality weights change during developmental processes, immune responses, or disease progression
- **WNN for clinical diagnostics**: Develop supervised extensions of WNN that learn multimodal cell-state signatures predictive of disease outcomes, treatment responses, or adverse events
- **Scalable WNN for billion-cell atlases**: Memory-efficient graph construction and storage strategies that enable WNN analysis of atlas-scale multimodal datasets (e.g., the full Human Cell Atlas)
- **Multi-species multimodal references**: Build cross-species multimodal atlases that leverage WNN's per-cell modality weighting to identify evolutionarily conserved and divergent multimodal cell states
