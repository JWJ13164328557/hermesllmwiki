Rice aroma, a critical determinant of consumer acceptance, is influenced by the degree of milling (DOM). In this work, the volatile fingerprints of 11 Daohuaxiang rice samples across a DOM gradient were characterized using gas chromatography-olfactometry-mass spectrometry, chemometrics, and quantitative aroma structural metrics. Of 50 identified volatiles, 24 differentiated DOM levels, and 12 were key odor-active compounds. As DOM increased, 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline supplanted nonanal and 2-pentylfuran as the dominant aroma contributor. The dominant odor index (DOI) increased from 0.26 to 0.39, whereas Shannon diversity (H′) and Pielou's evenness (J′) decreased from 1.718 and 0.883 to 1.522 and 0.782, indicating a simplified aroma profile. The 3.40% bran degree sample achieved the highest sensory score, characterized by the dominance (DOI = 0.39) of 2-acetyl-1-pyrroline and moderate diversity (H′ = 1.537) and evenness (J′ = 0.790) of key odor-active compounds. A DOM-oriented aroma-quality framework was established, linking DOM-induced changes in proximate composition to key odor-active compound profiles and aroma quality. Keywords:Degree of milling, GC-O-MS, Odor-active compound, Aroma structure, Aroma regulation framework Milling changed the dominant aroma contributor from lipid-derived to 2-AP. Milling increased dominance of 2-AP but reduced diversity and evenness. BD3.40 achieved highest odor score by balancing 2-AP dominance and complexity. Higher heptanal and octanal of BD3.40 provided more complexity than commercial rice. A framework was established to regulate aroma quality of cooked rice through DOM. Rice is a crucial staple food, especially in Asia, serving as a fundamental component of daily diets and a vital nutrient source (Wang et al., 2011). Nutrient distribution in rice kernel was non-uniform, as proteins, lipids, fiber, and minerals were concentrated in the embryo, bran, and outer endosperm (Lamberts et al., 2007). Therefore, although milling improved texture and eating quality, it also removed valuable nutrients. Conversely, retaining more bran could substantially enhance nutritional quality and increase yield (Billiris et al., 2012;Lanning & Siebenmorgen, 2011). Driven by consumer health consciousness, a lower degree of milling has become an emerging trend (Qu et al., 2025;Roy et al., 2008). However, this adjustment impacted not only yield and nutrition but also notably altered rice aroma (Choi et al., 2018).
