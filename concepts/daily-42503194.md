Revised 2026 Jun 16; Received 2025 Dec 3; Accepted 2026 Jun 25; Issue date 2026 Jul. Cirsium boluenseP.H.Davis & Parris (Kartal Kangalı) is a local endemic species in Bolu, Türkiye. Members of theCirsiumgenus have long been used for cancer, bronchitis, and cough in Anatolia. This study investigated the antimutagenic, antibacterial, and antioxidant properties of methanol extracts obtained from 1st year (leaf and root) and 2nd year (capitulum, stem, leaf, and root) plant materials, along with total phenolic–flavonoid levels and LC‐MS/MS profiles. Roots from both growth stages and 2nd year leaves demonstrated the most effective antimutagenic activity, which may be relevant to traditional uses associated with cancer prevention. The 2nd year leaf had the strongest free radical scavenging activity, followed by the capitulum. Leaves showed the highest phenolic–flavonoid content overall. Five bacterial strains were inhibited, with the capitulum showing the most notable antibacterial effect. LC‐MS/MS analysis identified apigenin, chlorogenic acid, 1,5‐dicaffeoylquinic acid (cynarin), and luteolin as major phenolics in capitulum and leaves. The findings validate traditional medicinal uses and indicate significant pharmacological and nutraceutical potential forC. boluense. Keywords:antibacterial, antimutagenic, antioxidant, Asteraceae, chemoprevention,Cirsium, endemic plant, phenol Distinct phenolic profiles in different parts of the endemicCirsium boluenseare associated with strong antioxidant, antimutagenic, and antibacterial activities, supporting its traditional use and potential as a natural bioactive resource. Cirsium boluenseP.H.Davis & Parris (Kartal Kangalı), belonging to the Asteraceae family, is a local endemic plant that is only found in Kartalkaya, Bolu, Türkiye [1]. It is a biennial plant, meaning it lives for 2 years and blooms in the 2nd year. They form a basal rosette of leaves in their 1st year, followed by an extended inflorescence stem in their 2nd year [1]. According to the IUCN Red List Categories and Criteria [2],C. boluenseis currently classified as vulnerable (VU), reflecting its limited distribution and potential conservation concerns. Cirsiumis derived from the Greek word “khirsos,” meaning “swollen vein,” and refers to vein illnesses. Tournefort, citing Dioscorides, reported that the roots of this plant were employed to treat venous problems. Additionally, this herb has been used to alleviate pain from varicose disease [3]. Ethnobotanical records in Anatolia have documented that various parts ofCirsiumspecies, including roots, stems, flowers, and seeds, are traditionally used to treat a wide range of ailments. The roots are commonly prepared as a decoction for use as a tea and employed in the treatment of unspecified forms of cancer, while the aerial parts are used for conditions such as bronchitis, cough, peptic ulcers, diabetes, constipation, rheumatic pain, prostate disorders, and hemorrhoids [4,5,6,7,8,9,10,11].
