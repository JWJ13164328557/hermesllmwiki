Salinity stress is a major constraint limiting crop productivity by inducing osmotic imbalance, ion toxicity, and oxidative damage. The present study evaluated the effectiveness of nanochelated fertilizers, including potassium-rich NPK (12–12–36; NPK1), balanced NPK (20–20–20; NPK2), and a micronutrient complex, on wheat and oat under varying salinity levels in a pot experiment, categorized as Non-saline (ECe=1.7 dS m-1), Slight-saline (ECe=2.9 dS m-1), Moderate-saline (ECe=6.2 dS m-1), and High-saline (ECe=12.5 dS m-1). Salinity significantly reduced growth and physiological traits. In wheat, shoot height decreased by 17.4% and biomass by 41.8% under high salinity, while in oats, reductions reached 23.5% and 41.0%, respectively. Photosynthetic rate (Pn) declined by 54.6% in wheat and 49.1% in oats, while chlorophyll content index (CCI) decreased by 23.0% and 51.9%, respectively.
