## 摘要
INTRODUCTION: Experimental optimization of seed-peanut shelling mechanisms is limited by the low signal-to-noise ratio of biological materials, because variations in pod size, shell thickness, shell-kernel clearance, and rupture behavior can mask the effects of claw geometry. This study proposes a flexible peanut shelling claw and a mechanism-constrained D-optimal response surface optimization (MC-DORSO) framework to reduce biological-variability-induced noise while lowering shelling force and kernel damage.
