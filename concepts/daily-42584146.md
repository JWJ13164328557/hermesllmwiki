is a renewable and abundant resource for

products. Among these, 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF) and furfural
(FA), generated through catalytic dehydration of biomass-derived sugars,
serve as pivotal intermediates for enzymatic synthesis of valuable
platform chemicals like 2,5-furandicarboxylic acid (FDCA). In recent
studies, glyoxal oxidases (GlyOx, EC 1.2.3.15) have emerged as promising
candidates for the biotransformation of furan derivatives. This study
focused on the recombinant production and characterization of a GlyOx
from the medicinal basidiomyceteGanoderma lucidumcapable of oxidizing HMF and FA with 51.5% and 23.0% conversion,
respectively, after 48 h. Furthermore, the enzyme oxidized HMF derived
from hexose-rich wheat straw hydrolysates, leading to 32.4% conversion
after 72 h, as well as FA derived from beechwood hemicellulosic hydrolysates,
leading to furoic acid with 17.0% conversion after 48 h, marking the
first time such enzymatic activity has been demonstrated on furans
originating from genuine lignocellulosic biomass sugar streams. Keywords:biocatalysis, oxidoreductases, furan derivatives, HMF, FDCA, lignocellulose valorization, fructose
dehydration With
the depletion of fossil fuels and the escalating effects of
climate change, there is an increasing demand for sustainable alternatives
that can reduce the reliance on petroleum-based products. Lignocellulosic
biomass, a readily available and abundant organic material, composed
mostly of polysaccharides such as cellulose and hemicellulose, along
with lignin, constitutes a promising source for the production of
value-added biobased compounds, such as biofuels, biomaterials and
fine chemicals which can replace their petrochemical counterparts. Among the most promising biomass-derived chemicals
are 5-hydroxymethylfurfural (HMF) and furfural (FA) both serving as
platform molecules for the synthesis of polymers, organic acids and
functionalized derivatives, such as 2,5-furandicarboxylic acid (FDCA)
and furoic acid. FDCA is primarily used
as a monomer for the synthesis of biobased polyesters and polymers,
such as poly­(ethylene furanoate), while
furoic acid serves as a versatile intermediate for the synthesis of
pharmaceuticals, surfactants, coatings and other chemicals. HMF and FA can be produced from lignocellulosic

enzymes. In this process, cellulose is first depolymerized to C6 sugars,
