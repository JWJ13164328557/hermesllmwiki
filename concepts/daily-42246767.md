Nutrient contents in plant tissues reflect adaptations and environmental constraints, varying across and within species along ecological gradients. Interspecific differences reflect long‐term adaptations, while intraspecific variations may indicate acclimation to present conditions. Examining these responses across elevational ranges is therefore crucial for understanding how nutrients influence species performance. We investigated whether organ nutrients play a significant role at the upper limits of the world's highest occurring herbaceous angiosperms by analysing intraspecific and interspecific variations in leaf carbon (C), nitrogen (N), and phosphorus (P) contents in 22 species, and root N and P contents in 15 species along a gradient from 4560 to 6150 m a.s.l. in the Western Himalaya. Elevated N and P content, especially at the highest localities, imply that the upper limits of the world's highest‐growing plants may not be limited by nutrient acquisition constraints.
