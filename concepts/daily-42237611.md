Topically applied double-stranded RNA (dsRNA) can trigger antiviral RNA interference in plants, but naked dsRNA is unstable and poorly taken up after foliar application. Here, we developed an eco-friendly chitosan–tripolyphosphate (CS-TPP) nanocarrier to enhance dsRNA delivery using turnip crinkle virus (TCV) as a model and tested it inArabidopsis thaliana. A dsRNA targeting the TCV-coat protein (CP) gene was produced inEscherichia coli, and complexation with CS-TPP was induced by ionic gelation. Complete complexation occurred at a 1:1 CS-TPP:dsRNA ratio, producing cationic nanoparticles that protected dsRNA from RNase digestion and maintained its integrity during incubation at 37°C. Using Cy3 fluorescence imaging, we observed stronger epidermis-associated fluorescence in plants treated with the CS-TPP@dsRNA complex than in those treated with naked dsRNA, and RT-qPCR confirmed higher CP-target RNA fragment accumulationin planta. Small RNA sequencing revealed increased levels of TCV-derived, 21–24 nt siRNA molecules targeting the CP region, suggesting the applied dsRNA was processedin planta. Importantly, a CS–TPP@TCV-dsRNA foliar spray application reduced systemic CP RNA levels at 7 and 14 days post-treatment and alleviated disease symptoms more effectively than naked dsRNA. These results demonstrate that CS–TPP nanocomplexation improves dsRNA stability and delivery, enabling improved and more durable antiviral protection. Keywords:chitosan-tripolyphosphate nanoparticles, double-stranded RNA, RNA interference, turnip crinkle virus Plant viruses remain a persistent constraint on agricultural productivity and crop quality worldwide. Viruses replicate inside host cells and depend extensively on host processes, limiting the use of direct chemical control options, such as those commonly used for fungal or bacterial diseases. Management, therefore, relies largely on preventive strategies, such as the use of virus-free planting materials and resistant hosts, vector control, and sanitation (Voloudakis et al., 2022). However, such approaches can be insufficient when resistance is unavailable or rapidly overcome, when multiple viruses co-occur, or when environmental conditions and vector pressure fluctuate. Accordingly, there is strong interest in the development of complementary, environmentally benign antiviral strategies that can be deployed flexibly and that align with the increasing demand for sustainable crop protection (Cagliari et al., 2019;Vetukuri et al., 2021).
