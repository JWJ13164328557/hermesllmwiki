In 2021, the prevalence of current smoking and current daily smoking among Chinese college students was 7.8% and 4.7%, respectively. The prevalence of current cigarette smoking among college students in China declined from 7.8% in 2021 to 7.3% in 2025; however, this rate remained substantially higher than that of senior high school students. Stress relief, curiosity, and socializing were the primary reasons for smoking initiation among college students. Strengthening tobacco control measures for college students is essential, including raising the legal age for tobacco sales to restrict access and implementing targeted interventions that address stress, curiosity, and social influences driving smoking initiation. Keywords:Cigarette, Current smoking, College students, Tobacco control Introduction:College students represent a key population at risk for smoking initiation and continued use. During the transition from high school to college, increased autonomy and legal access to tobacco may elevate smoking prevalence, yet tobacco control policies specifically targeting this group remain limited. Methods:This study analyzed smoking prevalence [with 95% confidence intervals (CIs)] and reasons for smoking initiation among college students using data from the 2025 national survey. Survey-weighted prevalence estimates were calculated using complex sampling procedures, and Rao–Scott χ2tests were applied to assess differences across subgroups. Results:In 2025, the prevalence of current and daily smoking among Chinese college students was 7.3% (95%CI: 6.7%, 7.9%) and 4.8% (95%CI: 4.4%, 5.2%), respectively, with males substantially exceeding females [current smoking: males 12.9% (95%CI: 12.0%, 13.8%), females 1.4% (95%CI: 1.3%, 1.5%); daily smoking: males 8.7% (95%CI: 8.1%, 9.4%), females 0.7% (95%CI: 0.6%, 0.8%)]. Higher Vocational Colleges had higher rates [current smoking: 10.0% (95%CI: 8.9%, 11.1%), daily smoking: 6.7% (95%CI: 6.0%, 7.4%)] than Provincial Colleges [5.0% (95%CI: 4.5%, 5.5%) and 3.2% (95%CI: 2.8%, 3.6%)] and Colleges Directly Under the Central Ministries and Commissions or Co-sponsored by Province and Ministry [3.2% (95%CI: 2.9%, 3.6%) and 1.9% (95%CI: 1.7%, 2.2%)]. Stress relief (73.1%), curiosity (11.3%), and socializing (10.8%) were the leading reasons for smoking initiation. Conclusion:Concerted efforts are needed to reduce tobacco use among college students, including strengthening campus-level tobacco control policies, addressing smoking initiation during the transition to college, considering stronger age-related sales restrictions, and delivering tailored interventions targeting stress, peer influence, and curiosity-driven behaviors.
