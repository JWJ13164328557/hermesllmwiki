In nature, plants are continually challenged by various invaders, including bacteria, fungi, viruses, herbivorous animals, and parasitic plants. To survive, plants have evolved sophisticated defense systems to counteract these invaders, including herbivorous insects (Snoecket al.,2022; Wanget al.,2022). Plants often enhance their resistance to insects through direct or indirect strategies, such as erecting physical barriers, synthesizing toxic compounds, and emitting volatiles to attract natural enemies (Erb & Reymond,2019). During feeding, piercing‐sucking herbivores inject two types of saliva into plant tissues: gelling saliva and watery saliva. Gelling saliva rapidly solidifies to form a continuous and sealed salivary sheath, which provides mechanical support and lubrication for the stylet (Wanget al.,2008; Qiet al.,2025). Although some sheath proteins are dispensable for sheath formation, they play crucial roles in modulating insect feeding efficiency and plant defenses (Huanget al.,2023). Watery saliva contains various detoxifying enzymes, proteases, nucleic acids, and other proteins capable of interacting with the host plant (Carolanet al.,2009; Huanget al.,2018; Chenet al.,2020).
