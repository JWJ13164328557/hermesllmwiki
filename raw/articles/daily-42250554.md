# Environmental factors drive rutin accumulation through a synergistic network promoting flavonoid biosynthesis and suppressing hydrolysis in tea plants (Camellia sinensis).

As an important glycosylated flavonoid, rutin contributes to the sensory quality and health benefits of tea. However, the relationship between rutin accumulation and environmental factors remains poorly characterised. Integration of meteorological, soil, and multi-omics data was used to assess rutin accumulation responses to environmental factors. The results showed that average daily rainfall, maximum temperature, and soil pH are key drivers, significantly affecting transcription and metabolite levels in basal metabolism, signal transduction, and flavonoid pathways. Specifically, the favourable environment enhances gene expression and precursor contents such as quercetin and isoquercitrin in the flavonoid pathway. Simultaneously, the hydrolysis genes CsGH1 and CsBGLU11 are downregulated, collectively promoting rutin accumulation. In summary, key environmental factors drive rutin accumulation through a combined network that enhances flavonoid biosynthesis and reduces hydrolysis. This study reveals the key environmental factors and regulatory mechanisms that drive rutin accumulation, providing a basis for improving the functional properties of tea products.
