# Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi regulate cadmium uptake and detoxification in winter wheat via Cd dose-dependent molecular and cellular mechanisms.

Arbuscular mycorrhizal fungi (AMF) enhance cadmium (Cd) tolerance and promote wheat growth, yet their regulatory mechanisms for Cd uptake and detoxification require further elucidation. A pot trial with Cd gradients (0-4 mg/kg) in soil, based on China's environmental quality standard, was conducted to investigate AMF-mediated modulation of Cd uptake, transport and detoxification in winter wheat. Compared to non-inoculation (Nm), AMF significantly (p < 0.05) enhanced wheat biomass and antioxidant enzyme activities, while reducing hydrogen peroxide (8.28 %-27.51 %) and malondialdehyde (7.97 %-25.12 %) contents under high Cd levels (2-4 mg/kg). AMF regulated Cd uptake in dose-dependent manners: under low Cd levels (0-0.6 mg/kg), AMF upregulated TaNramp5 and TaHMA2 expression in roots, increasing Cd concentration in roots (5.05 %-8.12 %) and shoots (10.65 %-24.44 %); whereas under high Cd levels, AMF downregulated these genes, decreasing Cd concentration in roots (18.22 %-23.91 %) and shoots (10.26 %-16.73 %).
