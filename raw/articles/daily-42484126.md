# One-step preparation of polymeric guanidine-modified straw via internal mixing for green control of soil-borne fungal diseases.

A solvent-free, one-step internal mixing strategy was developed to graft polyhexamethylene guanidine hydrochloride (PHMG) onto straw using epoxidized soybean oil (ESO) as a bio-based crosslinker. This high-temperature, high-shear process concurrently achieves surface functionalization and thermal sanitization of the biomass. The resulting PHMG-modified straw (GMS) was evaluated for controlling soil-borne fungal diseases, with a focus on Fusarium oxysporum f. sp. cubense tropical race 4 (Foc4), the causative agent of devastating banana Fusarium wilt. Notably, GMS maintained the soil concentration of Foc4 spores below the critical disease threshold for at least 40 days and reduced disease incidence by 92.1% in pot trials at the optimal application rate. Beyond disease suppression, GMS effectively improved soil structure and fertility. After 150 days of application, GMS increased soil organic matter (OM), total nitrogen (TN), alkali-hydrolysable nitrogen (AN), available phosphorus (AP), and available potassium (AK) by 28.1%, 31.9%, 24.2%, 14.0%, and 41.0%, respectively. Crucially, these benefits were realized without disrupting soil microbial homeostasis or inducing acute toxicity in earthworms and zebrafish under the tested conditions. This work presents a green polymer-grafting approach for converting straw into a multifunctional soil amendment, thereby integrating biomass recycling with sustainable management of soil-borne diseases.
