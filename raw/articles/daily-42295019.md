# Educational Gaps and Communication Priorities for the Healthfulness of Carbohydrate Foods.

BACKGROUND: Although shared understanding between consumers and experts, and within experts themselves, is essential for healthier food choices, no existing method systematically prioritizes foods based on the magnitude of health education gaps.

OBJECTIVE: To develop and apply a quantitative belief-mapping methodology to identify commonly consumed foods that warrant prioritization for research and nutrition communication.

METHODS: Based on survey data and a meta-nutrition profiling system that represents transparent and harmonized expert healthfulness ratings, consumer misalignment and misperception relative to experts, as well as expert-only uncertainty, were quantified across 20 commonly consumed carbohydrate-rich foods. Foods were then ranked by jointly considering misalignment, expert uncertainty, and national consumption prevalence.

RESULTS: Among the 20 foods evaluated, 85% received a consumer-to-expert Alignment Grade of C or D, and 75% received an Uncertain expert-only healthfulness classification. Among consumers, the most negatively misperceived foods-those rated less healthy by consumers than by experts-included all surveyed potato based foods (French fries, chips, and baked potatoes) and unbuttered, air-popped popcorn. In contrast, honey was the most positively misperceived food, being rated as healthier by consumers than by experts. Most foods (70%) exhibited a mixture of negative and positive misperception (≥15/85%). When misalignment, expert uncertainty, and high U.S. consumption were considered jointly, white rice, white bread, corn tortilla, and fruit juice with vitamin C emerged as priority foods for future research and nutrition education.

CONCLUSIONS: Substantial consumer-expert misalignment and expert uncertainty were observed across commonly consumed carbohydrate-containing foods. This scalable belief-mapping methodology prioritizes foods for future nutrition research, communication efforts, and policy.

A first-of-its-kind quantitative belief-mapping methodology was developed to jointly characterize consumer–expert misalignment, expert-only uncertainty, and national consumption for 20 commonly consumed carbohydrate-containing foods.Across these 20 foods, 85% received a low consumer-to-expert Alignment Grade (C or D) and 75% received an “Uncertain” expert classification, revealing widespread and previously unquantified educational gaps.Negative consumer misperception (consumers rating foods as less healthy than experts) was approximately 2.3× larger in magnitude than positive misperception; foods including honey oat cereal, fruit juices, whole wheat bread, and corn tortilla exhibited complex mixtures of both directions, indicating that single-direction messaging is insufficient.Joint application of poor alignment, expert uncertainty, and high U.S. consumption identified white rice, white bread, corn tortilla, and fruit juice with vitamin C as priority foods for nutrition education and research.The interactive belief-mapping tool (publicly hosted on the FRESH platform) is extensible to additional foods, populations, and health attributes and can support the design of more effective nutrition communication, labeling, and policy interventions.
