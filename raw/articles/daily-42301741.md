# A mechanistic framework integrating morphological, physiological, and redox-mediated defenses underlying sesame resistance to Antigastra catalaunalis (Lepidoptera: Crambidae).

Antigastra catalaunalis Duponchel (Lepidoptera: Crambidae), a host-specialized lepidopteran pest of sesame, represents a major biotic stress in sesame, yet the coordinated plant defense mechanisms conferring resistance remain inadequately understood. We investigated thirteen sesame genotypes to elucidate plant-mediated resistance through an integrated assessment of morphological, physiological, biochemical, and insect performance traits. Significant genotypic variation was observed in foliar and capsule damage, larval establishment, oviposition preference, and developmental success. Resistant genotypes (IC-500343, Swetha, and IC-205775) exhibited reduced feeding injury, limited larval settlement and oviposition, and pronounced antibiosis, manifested as prolonged larval duration, decreased pupation, and reduced adult emergence. These responses were associated with higher trichome density, increased specific leaf weight, and reduced relative water content, indicating structural and physiological constraints on herbivory. Resistant genotypes also displayed elevated constitutive and inducible activities of antioxidant enzymes (POX, CAT, APX, GR, SOD, PAL, and PPO), which are key components of plant defense through regulation of herbivory-induced oxidative stress, and enhanced redox metabolite dynamics (malondialdehyde, glutathione, and ascorbic acid), suggesting rapid activation of oxidative defense pathways following infestation. Multivariate analyses, including principal component analysis and K-means clustering, clearly discriminated resistant and susceptible genotypes and revealed synergistic interactions among morphological, physiological, and redox-regulated defense components. These integrated responses highlight the combined role of antixenosis and antibiosis mechanisms in shaping genotype-specific resistance. Collectively, these findings advance a mechanistic understanding of sesame-A. catalaunalis interactions and identify key resistance traits with potential utility in resistance breeding and sustainable pest management strategies.
