# Streptochlorin from Streptomyces youssoufiensis YMF3.862 as a potential ecological fungicide for controlling Fusarium head blight on wheat.

Fusarium head blight (FHB), caused by Fusarium graminearum, is one of the most devastating fungal diseases of cereal crops, resulting in substantial yield losses and mycotoxin contamination. In this study, streptochlorin, which showed broad-spectrum antifungal activity against phytopathogens, was isolated from the fermentation supernatant of Streptomyces youssoufiensis YMF3.862. Streptochlorin displayed strong antifungal activity against F. graminearum PH-1 and caused crumpling and collapse of PH-1 conidia and mycelia, as well as conidial devitalization. Transcriptomic analysis suggested that streptochlorin might affect the biosynthesis of the cell membrane and cell wall, and impair peroxisome function, leading to increased ROS. Streptochlorin destroyed the integrity of both the cell wall and the cell membrane, increased intracellular ROS levels, disrupted energy metabolism by inhibiting succinate dehydrogenase (SDH) and NADH oxidase (NOX), and reduced DON production by inhibiting trichothecene gene expression. Furthermore, application of 40 μg/mL streptochlorin provided significant control efficacy against FHB and reduced DON production in both wheat spikelets and grains. These findings suggest that streptochlorin could be used as a potential ecological fungicide for controlling FHB.
