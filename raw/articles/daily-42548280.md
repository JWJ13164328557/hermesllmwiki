# Managing yield and soil trace gas fluxes in rice through furrow irrigation and sulfur-enriched urea.

Rice (Oryza sativa L.) cultivation presents a significant opportunity to reduce methane and nitrous oxide fluxes by adopting optimal irrigation strategies and smart N fertilization. A 3-year field study was conducted in Northeast Arkansas to evaluate the impacts of flooded and furrow irrigation with either urea or sulfur-enriched urea (Urea-S) at 168 kg N ha

Flooded rice fields use large amounts of water and are a major source of methane, a soil trace gas that contributes to climate change. This study evaluated whether furrow irrigation, a water‐saving production system, can reduce trace gas fluxes while maintaining rice yields. Across three growing seasons, furrow irrigation greatly reduced methane fluxes compared with flooded fields and produced similar grain yields. However, nitrous oxide fluxes increased when soils dried and were later rewetted, especially after fertilizer applications. Using sulfur‐enriched nitrogen fertilizer reduced nitrous oxide fluxes under stable soil moisture conditions, but results varied depending on seasonal rainfall. These findings show that furrow irrigation can help farmers reduce water use and climate impacts of rice production, but successful adoption requires careful fertilizer timing and soil moisture management to avoid increased nitrous oxide fluxes.
