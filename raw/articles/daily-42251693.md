# Healing Without Wheat: Navigating the Nutritional Landscape of Gluten-Free Flours in Celiac Disease.

Celiac disease (CD) is a persistent autoimmune condition triggered by gluten, which comprises a set of storage proteins found in wheat, barley, and rye. A complete solution, supported by medical science, is for patients to avoid gluten for the rest of their lives. For patients with CD, foods obtained from gluten-containing grains, such as wheat, are replaced by gluten-free options, such as rice, almond, and tapioca flour. The purpose of this study was to evaluate the nutritional basis of gluten-free flours, particularly with regard to their macronutrients and micronutrients, dietary fiber, and potential to support the health of the gastrointestinal system. These flours present advantages in terms of symptom control and gut health maintenance for patients with CD, although they involve problems such as nutrient deficiency, taste and texture alterations, additional financial costs, and concerns over contamination. Some existing issues have been addressed through innovations in flour fortification, manufacturing mechanisms for gluten-free foods, and regulation of allergen labels. To guarantee the best nutritional and functional results from using gluten-free products, this review emphasizes the need to use superior flours and continue researching the well-being of individuals with CD.
