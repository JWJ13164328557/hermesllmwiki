# Theaflavin-3,3'-digallate improves porcine oocyte maturation and independently enhances embryo development in vitro via modulating redox homeostasis and lipid-associated metabolism.

Oxidative stress and metabolic imbalance are major limiting factors for oocyte maturation and early embryonic development under in vitro conditions. Theaflavin-3,3'-digallate (TFDG), a natural polyphenolic compound derived from black tea, exhibits potent antioxidant and lipid-regulatory activities. However, its potential role in mammalian embryo development remains unclear. In this study, we investigated the effects of TFDG supplementation on porcine oocyte maturation and embryonic development using two independent in vitro experimental settings. Our results showed that supplementation with 10 μM TFDG in vitro maturation (IVM) significantly enhanced cumulus expansion and first polar body extrusion rates accompanied by reduced intracellular reactive oxygen species levels and increased glutathione content. Meanwhile, supplementation with 5 μM TFDG in vitro culture (IVC) markedly increased the blastocyst formation rate and improved embryo quality. Mechanistically, TFDG reduced intracellular reactive oxygen species (ROS) levels, and elevated glutathione (GSH) content in both cumulus cells and oocytes. Concurrently addition with TFDG enhanced mitochondrial membrane potential and biogenesis, and decreased lipid droplet accumulation during embryo early development. In parallel, genes involved in β-oxidation were upregulated, whereas lipogenesis-related genes were downregulated. In addition, TFDG treatment attenuated excessive autophagy and apoptosis in blastocysts. Collectively, these findings demonstrate that TFDG improves oocyte maturation when applied during IVM and independently enhances embryo developmental competence when supplemented during IVC. These effects may be mediated, at least in part, through modulation of redox homeostasis and lipid-associated metabolic pathways.
