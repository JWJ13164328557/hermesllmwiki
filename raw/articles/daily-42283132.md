# Desorption of Phosphate from Iron-Bearing Soil Minerals by a Plant Secondary Metabolite.

In soils, phosphorus readily adsorbs to the surfaces of ubiquitous iron (oxyhydr)oxide minerals, rendering it less accessible to plants and microorganisms. Plants have a number of strategies to access iron, among them the secretion of redox-active metabolites from their roots. Although these strategies likely increase the bioavailability of surface-adsorbed phosphorus through reductive dissolution, their effect on phosphorus cycling has not yet been investigated.
