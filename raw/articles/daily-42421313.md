# Two atypical resistance proteins confer plant resistance to Melanaphis sorghi in sorghum.

Aphids are among the most common insect pests that decrease worldwide crop production. However, breeding aphid-resistant crops is currently hindered by scarcity of resistance genes. This problem is particularly prominent in sorghum for which resistant cultivars are now urgently needed to control the aphid Melanaphis sorghi (MES). Here we report map-based cloning of RMES1A and RMES1B, encoding two atypical resistance proteins conferring strong defense against MES. Analysis of knockout mutants and natural variations demonstrates that RMES1A and RMES1B are both required for sorghum resistance against MES. The two genes are induced by MES feeding, with their expression upregulated in the sclerenchyma cells and vascular bundles.
