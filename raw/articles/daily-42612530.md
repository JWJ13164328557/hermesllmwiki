# Synergistic regulation of cadmium tolerance by a 2 C protein phosphatase and nano molybdenum by modulating cellular structure, metal transport, and redox homeostasis in polyploid rice.

Cadmium (Cd) contamination in rice systems poses a significant risk to food safety and human health, necessitating the identification of key genetic regulators and develop effective mitigation strategies. In this study, the role of the protein phosphatase 2 C gene NY1 in Cd tolerance was investigated using a CRISPR/Cas9-derived ny1 mutant in neo-tetraploid rice, and the potential of molybdenum oxide nanoparticles (MoO₃-NPs) to alleviate Cd toxicity was evaluated. Under Cd stress, loss of NY1 function significantly increased plant sensitivity, as evidenced by greater reductions in growth and photosynthetic pigments, higher Cd accumulation, and increased oxidative damage compared to the wild type.
