# Elucidating the chemical and perceptual basis of lychee-like aroma in grapes through multi-omics and molecular sensory analyses.

Lychee-like aroma in grapes is a distinctive fruit trait with great market potential, yet its chemical composition and molecular basis remain unclear. Using an integrated multi-omics and sensory strategy, this study elucidated its chemical foundation, perceptual mechanism, and biosynthetic regulation. Metabolomic profiling identified cis-rose oxide and citronellol as key volatiles responsible for the lychee-like note. Electroencephalography (EEG) during grape consumption revealed neural activation associated with olfactory-gustatory integration, highlighting the role of cross-modal perception in flavor experience. Model solution assays and molecular docking suggested that these volatiles may enhance sweetness and sourness by modulating taste receptor-ligand interactions. Integrated metabolomic and transcriptomic analyses revealed sugar-acid-terpenoid metabolic networks and identified Vitvi037507 as a candidate gene for the biosynthesis of cis-rose oxide and citronellol. Overall, this work elucidates the chemical and perceptual mechanisms of lychee-like aroma in grapes, offering insights into flavor chemistry and a foundation for breeding and metabolic engineering.
