# Effects of stocking density on the pharmacokinetics of enrofloxacin and its metabolite ciprofloxacin in rice field eels (Monopterus albus).

This study investigated the effects of stocking density on the pharmacokinetics of enrofloxacin (ENR) and its active metabolite ciprofloxacin (CIP) in rice field eels (Monopterus albus) reared at low (1 kg/m2), medium (1.7 kg/m2), and high (3.5 kg/m2) densities. With increasing stocking density, ENR absorption was delayed (Tmax prolonged), systemic exposure enhanced (AUC increased), tissue distribution restricted (Vz decreased), metabolic conversion to CIP impaired (CIP/ENR AUC ratio reduced), and elimination markedly slowed (T1/2z prolonged, CLz decreased). Mechanistically, high-density rearing activated the hypothalamic-pituitary-interrenal axis, leading to sustained cortisol elevation, which compromised antioxidant capacity (reduced superoxide dismutase), suppressed non-specific immunity (decreased lysozyme, upregulated TNF-α and IL-1β), and caused severe hepatic damage (vacuolation, inflammatory infiltration, melanin deposition). These multi-level disruptions collectively impaired drug metabolism and clearance. To safeguard animal welfare and food safety, it is recommended to control stocking density within an optimal range and establish a withdrawal period of no less than 4224 °C·d in intensive rice field eel aquaculture. This value is a conservative estimate derived under high-density, wild-stock conditions and may be shortened under lower densities or domesticated stocks.
