# Relationships between sequential MRI in the neonatal period and early motor repertoire in very preterm infants.

BACKGROUND: Infants born very preterm frequently experience neurodevelopmental challenges. Early detection enables provision of targeted interventions to improve outcomes. Early neonatal and term-equivalent age (TEA) MRI and 3-5-month corrected age (CA) Prechtl Motor Optimality Score - revised (MOS-R) are useful prognostic tools, however structure-function relationships between them are not well established.

METHODS: Infants born <31 weeks' gestation underwent MRI at 32-weeks postmenstrual age and TEA. Modified Kidokoro MRI scoring generated white matter, cortical grey matter, deep grey matter and cerebellar scores which were summed to produce a global score. Infant MOS-R was assessed from videos recorded at 3-4 months' CA. Relationships between MRI abnormality scores and MOS-R and absent fidgety general movements were modelled using regression analysis.

This has implications for prioritising infants with poorer MRI scores for early assessment and that poorer MOS-R scores may be a manifestation of changes to brain structure and due to preterm birth.
