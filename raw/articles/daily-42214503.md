# A zinc finger protein gene TksC3H20 positively regulates salt tolerance in Taraxacum kok-saghyz under salinity.

Soil salinity severely limits agricultural productivity globally. Taraxacum kok-saghyz has attracted attention as an alternative rubber-producing crop because of its high capacity for natural rubber biosynthesis and its adaptability to marginal lands. However, the molecular mechanisms underlying its response to salt stress remain largely unexplored. We therefore examined both physiological responses and transcriptional changes in T. kok-saghyz under four salt treatments 0, 100, 200, and 300mM NaCl. The findings indicated that exposure to salt stress suppressed growth metrics and photosynthetic processes in T. kok-saghyz, while increasing antioxidant enzyme activities and the contents of proline and malondialdehyde. Weighted gene co-expression network analysis identified the C3H-type zinc finger protein TksC3H20 as a hub gene that was strongly induced by NaCl in leaves. Genome-wide analysis revealed 49 TksC3H members, followed by a systematic characterization of this gene family. Experimental assays demonstrated that TksC3H20 localizes to the nucleus and functions as a transcriptional activator. Under salt stress, overexpressing TksC3H20 improved plant salt endurance by strengthening the reactive oxygen species-scavenging systems in both T. kok-saghyz and Arabidopsis thaliana. Quantitative real-time PCR assays revealed that salt stress upregulated the expression of multiple genes in transgenic plants, such as TksWRKY33, TksP5CS1, and TksPRX1, which in turn facilitated proline accumulation and boosted peroxidase enzymatic activity. Conclusively, our findings demonstrate that ectopic expression of TksC3H20 in A. thaliana and T. kok-saghyz confers improved salt tolerance via attenuation of oxidative damage. This work identifies promising candidate genes for targeted breeding efforts to bolster salinity resilience in T. kok-saghyz.
