# Efficacious removal of aflatoxin M1 from milk and environmental samples using green spinel manganese ferrite-rice hull embedded by L-cysteine/cellulose-based nanocomposite biosorbent.

Aflatoxin M1 (AFM1) contamination is a significant factor contributing to economic losses in the dairy industry, and it can also pose human health risks. AFM1 can be transported easily to aquatic environments from agricultural fields by rain or re-enter the food cycle through sewage. Here, a facile wet co-participation/impregnation method was applied to fabricate rice hull-spinel manganese ferrite embedded in a porous L-cysteine-trichloro-s-triazine-cellulose scaffold (CTCR-MCF) to develop a bio-adsorbent for AFM1. The rice hull waste was deaggregated with spinel ferrite beads and bound to the S/N/O-H conjugates-cellulose template to synthesize an amphiphilic and pH-responsive nano-biosorbent. The modified cellulosic polymer was comprehensively characterized using structural and morphological techniques. The adsorption capacity of engineered CTCR-MCF was 0.18520 mg g-1, and it effectively removed 99.3% (and ∼ 75.2%) of AFM1 from 50.0 μg mL-1 AFM1 solution (and spiked cow milk) within 45.0 min under neutral pH. The Freundlich adsorption isothermal model (R2 = 0.982) and pseudo-2st-order kinetic model (R2 = 0.984) demonstrated that the removal occurs as a multi-layer on a non-uniform surface without chemical/physical interactions between molecules in a spontaneous/endothermic entropy-increasing process. Additionally, environmental compatibility, chemical/thermal stability, and regeneration were assessed in detail. The results showed that the durable bio-adsorbent had no significant effects on milk nutritional qualities and was successful in removing unsafe heavy metals from milk by using a less energy-intensive isolation method. These findings offer an efficient platform for "treating toxins with biosorbent" to mitigate their hazardous effects in environmental and food samples.
