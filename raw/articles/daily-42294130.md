# Beyond contextual smoking norms: The importance of family-level nicotine use risk factors among rural and nonrural youth living in Arkansas.

OBJECTIVE: This study identified the associations between home-based nicotine use risk factors (sibling nicotine use; permissive home/car smoking rules; and parental approval of youth nicotine use) and nicotine use among youth in Arkansas, a rural state overburdened by tobacco, after accounting for county-level adult smoking rates.

METHODS: Data were from the 2021 Arkansas Prevention Needs Assessment, a state-wide, school-based survey (

RESULTS: In the full and nonrural models, higher county-level adult smoking rates were associated with youth nicotine use. In all models, after adjusting for county-level adult smoking, youth with permissive smoke-free home/car rules, perceived parental approval of child nicotine use, and sibling nicotine use were more likely to report lifetime and past-month nicotine use.

CONCLUSIONS: Addressing home-based nicotine use risk factors may reduce nicotine use among rural and nonrural youth, regardless of the prevalence of adult smoking in their communities. Multi-level interventions are needed to reduce youth exposure to smoking in their communities and home-based nicotine use risk factors.
