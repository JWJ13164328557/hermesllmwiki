# Natural occurrence and dietary risk assessment of Alternaria toxins in wheat flour and its products in Shanghai, China.

Alternaria toxins are increasingly recognized as emerging food safety concerns; however, systematic investigations into their prevalence and associated health risks in wheat flour-based products remain scarce. In this study, 372 commercially available wheat flour and its products samples from Shanghai were analyzed using ultra-high performance liquid chromatography-tandem mass spectrometry (UPLC-MS/MS) to determine the occurrence and dietary exposure to alternariol (AOH), alternariol monomethyl ether (AME), and tenuazonic acid (TeA). TeA was identified as the predominant contaminant, present in 94.89% (353/372) of samples, with concentrations ranging from 1.00 to 601.16 μg/kg. AOH and AME exhibited similarly low detection frequencies (23.66%, 88/372 and 25.27%, 94/372), with concentrations ranging from 0.42 to 93.17 μg/kg and 0.36 to 25.39 μg/kg, respectively. All three analytes were generally found at higher levels in wheat flour compared with in processed products. Co-occurrence was observed in 41.4% (154/372) of positive samples, most frequently as AME-TeA. Based on contamination levels and local consumption data, both deterministic and probabilistic assessments indicated that the estimated daily intakes of AOH and AME exceeded the threshold of toxicological concern (TTC, 0.0025 μg/kg b.w./day), suggesting potential health risks. TeA exposure appeared to pose potential risks only under extreme exposure scenarios (high contamination and high consumption) among children and adolescents. This work provides the first comprehensive evidence of Alternaria toxin contamination in wheat flour-based products from Shanghai, together with an initial assessment of their potential health risks to residents, particularly minors. Our findings underscore the urgent need to establish regulatory limits and to conduct further toxicological investigations to refine TTC values and define tolerable daily intake (TDI) for more robust risk assessment.
