# Host hydraulics constrain mistletoe resistance to drought-induced embolism.

Increasing drought frequency and intensity under climate change are intensifying forest mortality world-wide, yet how biotic interactions modulate plant hydraulic vulnerability remains poorly understood. Mistletoes are obligate stem hemiparasites that operate at low leaf water potentials and sustain high transpiration while relying on host xylem water supply, raising questions about how their hydraulic safety is achieved and constrained. Here, we employed xylem vulnerability curves, vessel and pit anatomy, water-use indicators, and phylogenetically informed models to examine embolism resistance across 41 mistletoe-host species pairs in tropical China.
