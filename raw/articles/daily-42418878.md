# Effects of superheated steam-modified oat insoluble dietary fiber on the pasting, rheological, and retrogradation properties of potato starch.

This study modified oat insoluble dietary fiber (IDF) via superheated steam (SS) to obtain SSIDF, and systematically explored its impacts on the pasting, rheological and retrogradation properties of potato starch (PS). Results showed SSIDF significantly optimized these properties, with the 180 °C-treated SSIDF at 2% concentration exhibiting the most prominent effect. It reduced PS's peak viscosity by 25.40% and increased its pasting temperature. SSIDF also retarded short-term retrogradation (especially a 7-day retrogradation enthalpy of 42.61 J/g with 2% SSIDF-3), enhanced storage modulus (G') and reduced creep compliance of PS gel, forming a more compact 3D network. FTIR analysis suggested potential SSIDF-amylose complex formation via hydrogen bond reorganization, while LF-NMR showed an increased bound water proportion. Both are conducive to structural stability. In conclusion, SSIDF has significant advantages in optimizing PS properties, providing valuable parameters for starch-based food development.
