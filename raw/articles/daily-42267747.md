# Domestic wastewater supplementation enhances biomass, nutrient uptake, and water use efficiency of tomato in non-circulating hydroponic systems.

Within a circular nutrient economy framework, supplementing wastewater with commercial hydroponic fertilizer mix has been proposed to reduce nutrient variability, enhance phytoremediation, and support nutrient recovery for crop production. However, the resource-use (wastewater and nutrient) efficiency of such practice in non-circulating hydroponic systems compared to conventional freshwater and fertilizer use remains unclear. This study provides novel evidence on integrating wastewater reuse with non-circulating hydroponic systems by evaluating anaerobic filter effluent supplemented with reduced fertilizer levels. Unlike previous studies focused on recirculating systems or single metrics, it demonstrates that 50% supplementation sustains yield, nutrient uptake, and water-use efficiency. The findings support a low-cost strategy for phytoremediation, nutrient recovery, and sustainable crop production.
