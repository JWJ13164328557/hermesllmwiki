# Antifungal activity and action mechanisms of 2, 4-Di-tert-butylphenol from Bacillus amyloliquefaciens Z-7 volatile organic compounds against Verticillium dahliae.

Verticillium dahliae is a devastating soil-borne pathogen causing potato verticillium wilt, resulting in substantial yield losses. Prolonged reliance on chemical fungicides has risen environmental and health concerns, underscoring the need for sustainable biocontrol alternatives. A biocontrol strain of Bacillus amyloliquefaciens Z-7 was isolated and identified from maize rhizosphere soil. Potato plants exposure to volatile organic compounds (VOCs) of Z-7 showed significantly reduced wilt symptoms. Twenty-one VOCs of Z-7 were identified via HS-SPME-GC-MS detection. Recognizing the complex and partially uncertain chemical composition of microbial VOC mixtures, a bioassay-directed approach was employed to evaluate selected commercially available constituents. Among these, 2, 4-di-tert-butylphenol (2, 4-DTBP) showed higher antifungal activity on V. dahliae and the hyphae treated with 2, 4-DTBP showed significantly increased activities of chitinase and β-1, 3-glucanase. Scanning electron microscopy (SEM), transmission electron microscopy (TEM), fluorescent white (CFW) and propyl iodide (PI) staining analysis showed that 2, 4-DTBP damaged mycelial morphology, together with integrity of cell walls and membranes of V. dahliae. RNA-seq analysis revealed the involvement of oxidative phosphorylation and steroid biosynthesis in the antifungal mechanism of 2, 4-DTBP against V. dahliae. Results in this study demonstrate the complex VOC mixture from B. amyloliquefaciens Z-7 as promising biocontrol agents against potato verticillium wilt, with 2,4-DTBP identified via bioassay-directed evaluation as a major contributor to the observed antifungal effects.
