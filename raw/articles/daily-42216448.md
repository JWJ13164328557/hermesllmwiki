# S-nitrosylation-mediated differential regulation of STOP1 and STAR1 coordinates external and internal aluminum resistance in Arabidopsis.

Aluminum (Al) toxicity severely limits crop productivity on acidic soils, which account for ∼40% of the world's arable land. In Arabidopsis, the transcription factor STOP1 promotes external Al detoxification primarily by inducing ALMT1-mediated malate secretion, whereas the ATP-binding cassette transporter complex STAR1/ALS3 mediates internal detoxification through vacuolar sequestration of Al. However, how these two detoxification pathways are coordinately regulated remains largely unclear, particularly with respect to the role of nitric oxide (NO). Here, through a forward genetic screen, we identified the Al-sensitive mutant rae8/cnx2-3, which is defective in molybdenum cofactor biosynthesis and exhibits reduced NO production. Using genetic, biochemical, and physiological analyses, we show that NO regulates Al resistance through S-nitrosylation-mediated modulation of both STOP1 and STAR1. NO promotes S-nitrosylation of STOP1 at Cys-8, enhancing its interaction with the F-box protein RAE1 and thereby accelerating STOP1 degradation. In contrast, S-nitrosylation of STAR1 at Cys-231 stabilizes the protein and promotes its accumulation. Consequently, in the rae8/cnx2-3 mutant, excessive accumulation of STOP1 together with reduced STAR1 abundance disrupts the balance between external and internal Al detoxification, ultimately leading to enhanced Al sensitivity. Together, our findings uncover a coordinated regulatory mechanism by which NO fine-tunes external and internal Al resistance pathways through targeted S-nitrosylation of STOP1 and STAR1, providing important insights into plant adaptation to acidic soils.
