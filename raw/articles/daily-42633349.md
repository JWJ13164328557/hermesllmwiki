# Toxic Anterior Segment Syndrome following XEN Gel Stent Implantation: A Rare Complication in Minimally Invasive Glaucoma Surgery - Case Report.

INTRODUCTION: Toxic anterior segment syndrome (TASS) is a rare, sterile, acute inflammatory reaction of the anterior segment following intraocular surgery. Although it is most commonly associated with cataract surgery, it has been reported after a variety of ophthalmic procedures. To our knowledge, no case of TASS following XEN Gel Stent Implantation has been previously described in the literature.

CASE PRESENTATION: An 85-year-old pseudophakic woman with primary open-angle glaucoma developed TASS after uneventful XEN63 implantation. Within 24 h postoperatively, the patient presented with diffuse limbus-to-limbus corneal edema, anterior chamber inflammation, and absence of pain or posterior segment involvement. Despite prompt and intensive topical corticosteroid therapy, she developed irreversible corneal decompensation, iris atrophy, and fixed nonreactive mydriasis. No additional cases were identified in the same surgical setting, suggesting a patient- or procedure-specific trigger. Potential causative factors included intracameral agents (lidocaine, acetylcholine, cefuroxime, viscoelastic material), possible migration of mitomycin C through the XEN stent lumen, or an inflammatory response to the gelatin-based device itself.

CONCLUSIONS: This case demonstrates that TASS can occur following minimally invasive glaucoma surgery, including XEN Gel Stent implantation, and may result in severe and irreversible ocular sequelae. It underscores the importance of heightened postoperative vigilance and strict preventive protocols even in procedures perceived as low risk.

Eye surgery is generally considered safe, but in rare cases, certain complications can occur even in routine procedures. One such complication is toxic anterior segment syndrome (TASS), a noninfectious inflammation of the front part of the eye that typically develops within 24 h after surgery. TASS is caused by a reaction to substances that enter the eye during the operation, such as medications, fluids, or residues from surgical instruments. Although TASS has been described mainly after cataract surgery, it can occur following other types of eye surgery as well. In this report, we describe the first known case of TASS occurring after a glaucoma surgery called XEN63 Gel Stent implantation. Glaucoma is a disease that damages the optic nerve, often due to elevated pressure inside the eye. The XEN Gel Stent is a tiny tube inserted into the eye to help lower the pressure. The procedure is widely used. Our patient was an 85-year-old woman who developed severe eye inflammation the day after her surgery. Despite prompt treatment with anti-inflammatory eye drops, she suffered permanent damage to the cornea (the transparent front surface of the eye), resulting in significant visual loss. This case highlights that even procedures considered low-risk can, on rare occasions, lead to serious complications. Eye care professionals should be aware of TASS as a possible complication following this type of glaucoma surgery, so that it can be recognized and treated as early as possible.
