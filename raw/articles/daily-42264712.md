# Reducing expression of salivary protein genes by insecticides contributed to suppressing feeding and reproduction in Laodelphax striatellus.

The small brown planthopper (SBPH), Laodelphax striatellus, is one of the most serious pests on many crops. During feeding, SBPH uses its stylet to penetrate the phloem while secreting saliva, in which salivary proteins play a critical role in facilitating feeding behavior and mediating interactions with the host. Flonicamid, pymetrozine, and two pyrethroid insecticides (etofenprox and deltamethrin) are commonly used insecticides against sucking insect pests. These four insecticides have been reported to be closely associated with insect feeding behavior. Therefore, we aim to test whether the feeding changes by these insecticides are through regulating salivary protein genes. In this study, we found that treatment with pymetrozine resulted in the downregulation of expression levels of 4 salivary protein genes, LsG14, LsDNAJB9, LssaMP and LsMLP. Etofenprox treatment only downregulated the expression levels of LsG14. And deltamethrin treatment downregulated the expression levels of LssaMP, LsMLP and LsVgC. LsG14 was selected for further functional analysis due to its unknown function and its downregulation by two insecticides. RNA interference (RNAi) of LsG14 significantly reduced survival within 8 days. Moreover, silencing LsG14 led to an 81.7% reduction in honeydew excretion and a 76.8% decrease in egg production. Bioassays corresponded with the RNAi results that SBPH feeding on rice treated with pymetrozine and etofenprox produced less honeydew (pymetrozine: 86.7%; etofenprox: 66.1%) and laid fewer eggs (pymetrozine: 51.7%; etofenprox: 15.4%). These findings suggested that the inhibition of pymetrozine and etofenprox on the feeding and oviposition behavior in L. striatellus might be partially attributed to their effects on the expression of important salivary protein genes. This study elucidates the mechanisms of action of pymetrozine and etofenprox against insect pests, offering a novel target for SBPH management and providing a theoretical foundation for the identification and development of new insecticides.
